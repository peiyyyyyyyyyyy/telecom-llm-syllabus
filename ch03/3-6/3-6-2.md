## 正文

In real‑world network design a single formula rarely solves a problem. Engineers must combine several physical relationships—propagation loss, noise, modulation limits, etc.—to answer questions such as “What data rate can this link support?” or “How much transmit power is needed to achieve a target throughput?”. Writing out each algebraic step and keeping track of units is essential for three reasons:

1. **Transparency** – A reviewer can follow the logic and spot mistakes without guessing what intermediate values were used.  
2. **Error control** – Rounding or unit conversion errors introduced early can be amplified through later steps; keeping full precision until the final answer limits this effect.  
3. **Learning transfer** – When a student or a language model sees the explicit chain of reasoning, the pattern can be reused for new problems.

Below we present the most common symbols, the governing equations, and a disciplined workflow that you can apply to any multi‑step telecom problem.

---

### 1. Key Definitions and Formulas  

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit | Typical range |
|--------|---------|------|---------------|
| $P_t$ | Transmit power (linear) | W | $10^{-3}$ – $10^{3}$ W |
| $G_t$, $G_r$ | Antenna gains (linear) | – | $1$ – $100$ (often given in dBi) |
| $f$ | Carrier frequency | Hz | $10^{6}$ – $10^{11}$ Hz |
| $\lambda$ | Wavelength, $\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f}$ | m | $c = 3\times10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$ |
| $d$ | Separation distance | m | $10^{0}$ – $10^{7}$ m |
| $L$ | Miscellaneous system loss factor (cable, polarization, etc.) | – | $1$ – $10$ |
| $B$ | Signal bandwidth | Hz | $10^{3}$ – $10^{9}$ Hz |
| $k$ | Boltzmann constant, $1.38\times10^{-23}\,\text{J/K}$ | J/K | – |
| $T$ | System noise temperature (often $290\,$K) | K | – |
| $N_0$ | Thermal noise spectral density, $kT$ | W/Hz | $≈4.0\times10^{-21}\,$W/Hz |
| $N$ | Total noise power, $N = N_0 B$ | W | – |
| $\text{SNR}$ | Signal‑to‑noise ratio, linear | – | – |
| $C$ | Channel capacity (Shannon), $C = B\log_2(1+\text{SNR})$ | bit/s | – |

**Friis transmission equation (free‑space):**  

$$
P_r = \frac{P_t\,G_t\,G_r\,\lambda^{2}}{(4\pi d)^{2}\,L}
$$

All quantities must be in linear units; if an antenna gain is given in dBi, convert with  

$$
G_{\text{linear}} = 10^{\frac{G_{\text{dBi}}}{10}} .
$$

**Thermal noise power:**  

$$
N = kTB .
$$

**Shannon capacity:**  

$$
C = B\log_{2}\!\bigl(1+\text{SNR}\bigr),\qquad 
\text{SNR}= \frac{P_r}{N}.
$$

---

### 2. Step‑by‑Step Workflow  

1. **Convert all dB values to linear.**  
2. **Compute wavelength** $\lambda = c/f$.  
3. **Apply Friis** to obtain $P_r$. Keep at least 6‑7 significant figures.  
4. **Compute noise power** $N = kTB$.  
5. **Form SNR** as a ratio $P_r/N$.  
6. **Insert SNR into Shannon** to obtain $C$.  
7. **Round only the final result** to the required number of significant figures or the format demanded by the problem.

---

### 3. Worked Example  

*Problem:* A point‑to‑point microwave link operates at $f = 2.4\,$GHz over a distance $d = 5\,$km. The transmitter outputs $P_t = 10\,$W, the transmit antenna gain is $G_t = 15\,$dBi, the receive antenna gain is $G_r = 10\,$dBi, and the system loss factor is $L = 2$. The allocated bandwidth is $B = 20\,$MHz. Determine the achievable data rate $C$ (in Mbps) assuming a noise temperature $T = 290\,$K.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|------|-------------|--------|
| 1. Linear gains | $G_t = 10^{15/10}=31.62$, $G_r = 10^{10/10}=10.00$ | $G_t=31.62$, $G_r=10.00$ |
| 2. Wavelength | $\lambda = \dfrac{3\times10^{8}}{2.4\times10^{9}} = 0.125\,$m | $\lambda=0.125\,$m |
| 3. Friis | $P_r = \dfrac{10\;\text{W}\times31.62\times10.00\times(0.125)^2}{(4\pi\times5\,000)^2\times2}$ | $P_r = 5.86\times10^{-8}\,$W |
| 4. Noise power | $N = kTB = (1.38\times10^{-23})(290)(20\times10^{6}) = 8.00\times10^{-14}\,$W | $N = 8.00\times10^{-14}\,$W |
| 5. SNR | $\text{SNR}=P_r/N = \dfrac{5.86\times10^{-8}}{8.00\times10^{-14}} = 7.33\times10^{5}$ (linear) | $\text{SNR}=7.33\times10^{5}$ |
| 6. Capacity | $C = 20\times10^{6}\,\log_{2}(1+7.33\times10^{5})$ | $C = 2.05\times10^{8}\,$bit/s |
| 7. Final answer | Convert to Mbps | $\boxed{205\;\text{Mbps}}$ |

*Key observations:*  
- No unit conversion error occurred because $d$ was expressed in metres to match $\lambda$ in metres.  
- The linear SNR is very large; the logarithm compresses it to a modest increase in capacity.  

---

### 4. Common Mistakes & Practical Tips  

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Remedy |
|---------|----------------|--------|
| **Using dBi directly in the Friis equation** | dBi is a logarithmic gain; Friis requires a linear factor. | Convert with $G_{\text{lin}} = 10^{G_{\text{dBi}}/10}$ before substitution. |
| **Mixing distance units (km vs. m)** | $\lambda$ is in metres; $d$ must be in the same unit. | Always write $d$ in metres; keep a conversion table handy. |
| **Rounding after each intermediate step** | Rounding propagates and can change the final result noticeably. | Retain full calculator precision (or at least 6–7 digits) until the final step. |
| **Neglecting system loss $L$** | $L$ is often omitted because it seems “minor”. | Include $L$ explicitly; treat it as a linear factor $>1$. |
| **Applying Shannon’s formula with SNR in dB** | The logarithm in Shannon expects a linear ratio. | Convert SNR from dB to linear: $\text{SNR}_{\text{lin}} = 10^{\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}}/10}$. |

**Practical note:** When a problem supplies bandwidth in MHz and noise temperature in Kelvin, compute $N = kTB$ in watts, then convert to dBm only if the answer format asks for it. This avoids an extra conversion that can introduce rounding error.

---

### 5. Summary  

A disciplined multi‑step calculation consists of (i) **unit consistency**, (ii) **linear‑scale arithmetic**, (iii) **minimal rounding**, and (iv) **clear documentation of each algebraic transformation**. Mastering this workflow not only yields correct numerical answers but also builds a reasoning chain that can be verified, audited, and reused across the many diverse problems encountered in telecommunications engineering.

**5.** For a point‑to‑point link at $f = 2.1\,$GHz, distance $d = 3\,$km, bandwidth $B = 5\,$MHz, required capacity $C = 10\,$Mbps, transmit antenna gain $G_t = 10\,$dBi, receive antenna gain $G_r = 5\,$dBi, system loss $L = 2$, and $T=290\,$K, determine the minimum transmit power $P_t$ in **mW**. (Use $c=3\times10^{8}$ m/s, $k=1.38\times10^{-23}$ J/K.)


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Convert gains: $G_t = 10^{10/10}=10$, $G_r = 10^{5/10}=3.1623$.  
2. Required SNR from Shannon: $C = B\log_2(1+\text{SNR}) \Rightarrow \text{SNR}=2^{C/B}-1 = 2^{10/5}-1 = 2^{2}-1 = 3$.  
3. Noise power: $N = kTB = 1.38\times10^{-23}\times290\times5\times10^{6}=2.001\times10^{-14}\,$W.  
4. Required receive power: $P_r = \text{SNR}\times N = 3\times2.001\times10^{-14}=6.003\times10^{-14}\,$W.  
5. Wavelength: $\lambda = c/f = 3\times10^{8}/2.1\times10^{9}=0.142857\,$m, $\lambda^{2}=0.020408$.  
6. Friis rearranged for $P_t$: $P_t = \dfrac{P_r L (4\pi d)^{2}}{G_t G_r \lambda^{2}}$.  
   Compute $(4\pi d)^{2}= (4\pi\times3000)^{2}= (37699.1)^{2}=1.420\times10^{9}$.  
   Numerator: $P_r L (4\pi d)^{2}=6.003\times10^{-14}\times2\times1.420\times10^{9}=1.706\times10^{-4}\,$W.  
   Denominator: $G_t G_r \lambda^{2}=10\times3.1623\times0.020408=0.645$.  
   Hence $P_t = 1.706\times10^{-4}/0.645 = 2.64\times10^{-4}\,$W.  
7. Convert to mW: $P_t = 0.264\,$mW.  
$\boxed{0.264}$

</details>
