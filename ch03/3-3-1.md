# 3.3.1 發射功率與 EIRP

所屬：[3.3 鏈路預算](3-3.md)

## 內文

#### 為什麼要學

鏈路預算（link budget）是一張把「發射端送出多少功率、路上損失多少、接收端至少要收到多少」逐項列出的收支表，第一列就是發射端。基地臺的功率放大器輸出多少瓦，並不等於天線主波束方向上實際送出去的強度：中間還要扣掉饋線與接頭的損耗，再加上天線把能量集中到某個方向所帶來的增益。把這三件事合成一個數字，就是等效全向輻射功率（equivalent isotropically radiated power, EIRP）。涵蓋規劃、干擾評估、電磁波暴露評估與頻率主管機關的發射限值，多半都以 EIRP（或它的近親 ERP）為準，所以這是讀規格書、站臺資料表與法規時最先要會算的量。

#### 定義與符號

| 符號 | 意思 | 單位 |
|---|---|---|
| $P_t$ | 發射機（功率放大器）輸出功率 | W 或 dBm、dBW |
| $L_t$ | 發射端損耗：饋線、接頭、合路器等，從功放輸出到天線輸入端 | dB（正值） |
| $G_t$ | 發射天線在指定方向的增益，相對於等向性天線 | dBi |
| $G_{t,d}$ | 發射天線在指定方向的增益，相對於半波偶極天線 | dBd |
| EIRP | 等效全向輻射功率 | W 或 dBm、dBW |
| ERP | 有效輻射功率（effective radiated power），以半波偶極為基準 | W 或 dBm、dBW |
| $S$ | 距離 $d$ 處的功率通量密度 | W/m² |
| $d$ | 與發射天線的距離（遠場） | m |

#### 公式與推導

國際電信聯合會《無線電規則》對 EIRP 的定義是：送進天線的功率，乘上天線在指定方向相對於等向性天線（isotropic antenna）的增益。送進天線的功率是功放輸出扣掉饋線損耗之後的值，因此用線性量寫成

$$\text{EIRP} = \frac{P_t\,G_t}{L_t}\quad(\text{W；}G_t\text{、}L_t\text{ 為線性倍數})$$

取對數後乘除變加減：

$$\text{EIRP (dBm)} = P_t\,(\text{dBm}) + G_t\,(\text{dBi}) - L_t\,(\text{dB})$$

功率單位的換算是 $P(\text{dBm}) = 10\log_{10}\!\big(P/1\,\text{mW}\big)$、$P(\text{dBW}) = 10\log_{10}\!\big(P/1\,\text{W}\big)$，兩者相差 30 dB（見 3.1.1）。

EIRP 的物理意義是：若把真實天線換成一支向所有方向均勻輻射的假想天線，要在「指定方向」產生同樣的場強，需要送進多少功率。因此在遠場、自由空間條件下，距離 $d$ 處的功率通量密度為

$$S = \frac{\text{EIRP}}{4\pi d^2}$$

ERP 的定義完全相同，只是比較基準改成半波偶極天線（half-wave dipole）。無損耗半波偶極本身相對於等向性天線的增益是 1.64 倍，即 $10\log_{10}(1.64) = 2.15$ dB，所以

$$\text{ERP} = \frac{\text{EIRP}}{1.64},\qquad \text{ERP (dBm)} = \text{EIRP (dBm)} - 2.15$$

同一支天線、同一個方向，EIRP 的數字永遠比 ERP 大 2.15 dB，兩者描述的是同一個實體輻射量，只是基準不同。

使用時要注意三件事。第一，EIRP 是方向的函數，沒有特別說明時指主波束最大增益方向；偏離主波束的方向要改用該方向的增益。第二，$P_t$ 必須和討論的訊號對應：整個載波的總功率、每個資源區塊或每 MHz 的功率，算出來的 EIRP 意義不同，法規常以「每若干 MHz 的 EIRP」設限。第三，多支天線元件同相合成時，若每個元件的功率固定為 $P$、共 $N$ 個元件，總功率是 $NP$，理想陣列增益又是 $N$ 倍，主波束方向的 EIRP 會隨 $N^2$ 成長（即 $20\log_{10}N$ dB）；這是理想同相合成的上限，實際值要扣掉效率與校正誤差。

#### 算例

**算例 1（由功放功率求 EIRP 與 ERP）**　已知：基地臺功放輸出 40 W；饋線與接頭損耗合計 2 dB（假設）；天線增益 17 dBi（假設）。求主波束方向的 EIRP 與 ERP。

1. 功率換成 dBm：$P_t = 10\log_{10}(40{,}000\ \text{mW}) = 46.02$ dBm。
2. 代入：$\text{EIRP} = 46.02 + 17 - 2 = 61.02$ dBm $= 31.02$ dBW。
3. 換回瓦：用未取位的 31.0206 dBW，$10^{3.10206} = 1{,}264.9$ W，約 1.26 kW（若用已取位的 31.02 會得到 1,264.7 W，差異來自取位）。
4. ERP：$61.02 - 2.15 = 58.87$ dBm，即 $1{,}264.9 / 1.64 = 771.3$ W。

合理性檢查：淨增益為 $17 - 2 = 15$ dB，約 31.6 倍，$40 \times 31.6 = 1{,}264$ W，與第 3 步一致。EIRP 遠大於功放的 40 W，並不代表能量變多，而是能量被集中到主波束。

**算例 2（由 ERP 換算 EIRP）**　已知：某站臺資料表登記 ERP 為 500 W。求 EIRP。

1. 線性：$\text{EIRP} = 500 \times 1.64 = 820$ W。
2. 以 dB 驗算：$10\log_{10}(500) = 26.99$ dBW，加 2.15 dB 得 29.14 dBW，即 59.14 dBm；$10^{2.914} = 820$ W，兩種算法一致。

#### 常見錯誤

- **把 ERP 當 EIRP 直接比較**：兩者差 2.15 dB（1.64 倍）。比較限值或不同文件的數字前，先確認基準是等向性還是半波偶極。
- **dBm 與 dBW 混用**：46 dBm 是 16 dBW，不是 46 dBW；漏掉 30 dB 相當於差一千倍。
- **損耗的正負號弄反**：$L_t$ 定義為正值並用減的；若把損耗寫成負的增益又再減一次，結果會多出兩倍的損耗量。
- **以為 EIRP 是實際輻射的總功率**：實際輻射的總功率不會超過送進天線的功率。EIRP 只在指定方向有意義，其他方向的等效值比較小。

#### 和其他小節的關係

功率單位換算用 3.1.1；$G_t$ 的來源與 dBi、dBd 的差別在 3.3.2 說明；$L_t$ 與其他損耗、餘量的整理在 3.3.3。本節算出的 EIRP 是 3.3.5 最大允許路徑損耗的起點，並在 3.3.6 分別用於上行與下行；功率通量密度與自由空間路徑損耗的關係見 3.1.2。

**出處**　ITU《無線電規則》（Radio Regulations）第 1 條「名詞與定義」第 1.161 款（e.i.r.p.）與第 1.162 款（e.r.p.）；Balanis, *Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design*，天線基本參數一章（半波偶極指向性 1.64）；Rappaport, *Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice*，行動無線電傳播（大尺度路徑損耗）一章。

#### 單題：EIRP of phased arrays

**3.3.1S008**　Why must the gain be entered in dBi, not dBd, in the formula EIRP = P_t + G_t − L_t? What error results if the dBd figure is used directly?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　EIRP is defined relative to an isotropic radiator, so the antenna gain must also be referred to the isotropic antenna (dBi). Entering a dBd value directly makes the result 2.15 dB too low; what is obtained is in fact the ERP, not the EIRP.

**詳解**　$G(\text{dBi}) = G(\text{dBd}) + 2.15$. Using dBd gives $P_t + G_{\text{dBd}} - L_t = \text{EIRP} - 2.15 = \text{ERP}$.

**出處**　ITU Radio Regulations, Article 1, Nos. 1.161 and 1.162.

</details>

**3.3.1S009**　The new antenna gets its extra 3.65 dB mainly from a narrower vertical beamwidth. With the transmitter power reduced so that the boresight EIRP is unchanged, will users located well below the main beam, close to the mast, receive the same signal as before? Explain.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Not necessarily. Only the boresight EIRP has been held constant. EIRP is a function of direction: a narrower vertical beam falls off faster away from boresight, and the transmitter power has been cut by 3.65 dB, so users close to the mast, served by the lower part of the pattern or by side lobes, may receive less signal than before. The pattern of the new antenna in their direction must be checked.

**詳解**　EIRP in direction $(\theta,\phi)$ is $P_t + G(\theta,\phi) - L_t$. Off boresight, $G(\theta,\phi)$ of the new antenna may be lower than that of the old one, while $P_t$ is 3.65 dB lower.

**出處**　ITU Radio Regulations, Article 1, No. 1.161 (EIRP "in a given direction"); C. A. Balanis, *Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design*.

</details>

**答案**　The EIRP limit applies only to the transmitting side. **詳解**　$P_{t,\max} = 53 - 29 + 0.5 = 24.5$ dBm. The EIRP stays at 53 dBm.

**3.3.1S025**　The installer calculated the maximum power setting for the 29 dBi dish as follows. Identify the wrong step, explain why, and give the corrected value in dBm.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

#### 情境題：Power flux density and RF exposure

**Scenario | Rooftop compliance boundary**

A 3.5 GHz sector antenna, 1.4 m tall, is mounted on a rooftop. It is fed with 40 W, and the antenna gain minus the transmit-side loss is 17 dB (17.8 dBi gain, 0.8 dB loss). For this problem, take the exposure reference level for the general public as 10 W/m², and estimate the power flux density on boresight with the free-space far-field formula $S = \text{EIRP}/(4\pi d^2)$. Take c = 3×10^8 m/s.

**3.3.1S031**　Find the boresight EIRP in watts, rounded to one decimal place.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　2004.7 W

**詳解**　Net gain 17 dB, linear $10^{1.7} = 50.1187$. EIRP $= 40 \times 50.1187 = 2004.75$ W, which rounds to 2004.7 W. (In dBm: $46.0206 + 17 = 63.0206$ dBm.)

**出處**　ITU Radio Regulations, Article 1, No. 1.161.

</details>

**3.3.1S032**　Using the far-field formula, at what boresight distance does the power flux density fall to the 10 W/m² reference level? Give the answer in metres, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　3.99 m

**詳解**　$d = \sqrt{\text{EIRP}/(4\pi S)} = \sqrt{2004.75/(4\pi\times10)} = \sqrt{2004.75/125.664} = \sqrt{15.953} = 3.9942$ m, which rounds to 3.99 m.

**出處**　T. S. Rappaport, *Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice* (free-space propagation, power flux density).

</details>

**3.3.1S033**　If a second carrier doubles the total EIRP of this sector, does the boresight compliance distance double? Explain.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　No. Power flux density falls as $1/d^2$, so the distance at which a given density is reached grows with the square root of the EIRP. Doubling the EIRP increases the distance by a factor of $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$, from 3.99 m to about 5.65 m.

**詳解**　$d \propto \sqrt{\text{EIRP}}$; $3.9942 \times 1.4142 = 5.649$ m.

**出處**　T. S. Rappaport, *Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice* (free-space propagation).

</details>

**3.3.1S034**　Is the far-field formula $S = \text{EIRP}/(4\pi d^2)$ strictly valid at a distance of about 4 m from this antenna? What does that mean for the result?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　No. The usual far-field distance $2D^2/\lambda$ for a 1.4 m antenna at 3.5 GHz is about 46 m, so 4 m is well inside the radiating near field, where the main beam has not yet formed and the gain is not fully realised. Close to a large panel the far-field formula generally overestimates the on-axis power flux density, so the 3.99 m boundary is a conservative estimate rather than an exact value.

**詳解**　$\lambda = 3\times10^8/3.5\times10^9 = 0.08571$ m. $2D^2/\lambda = 2\times1.4^2/0.08571 = 45.73$ m.

**出處**　C. A. Balanis, *Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design* (field regions, far-field distance $2D^2/\lambda$).

</details>

**3.3.1S038**　International rules for satellites often limit the power flux density at the Earth's surface rather than the satellite EIRP itself. Why is PFD the more useful quantity for protecting terrestrial receivers?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　What a terrestrial receiver actually experiences is the power arriving per unit area at its location, which depends on both the EIRP in that direction and the distance (and elevation angle) to the satellite. A PFD limit controls the interference directly where it occurs, independently of how a particular satellite combines antenna gain and power.

**詳解**　$\text{PFD} = \text{EIRP}(\theta) - 10\log_{10}(4\pi d^2)$; the same EIRP gives different PFD values at different slant ranges, and the same PFD can be produced by many combinations of $P_t$ and $G_t$.

**答案**　The satellite antenna gain towards the edge is 3 dB lower than at beam centre, so the EIRP in that direction is 3 dB lower and, at the same slant range, the power flux density is also 3 dB lower, i.e. about half of the beam-centre value in linear terms. #### 情境題：EIRP of antenna arrays

**Scenario | Millimetre-wave fixed wireless terminal**

A 28 GHz fixed wireless terminal has a 4×4 array (16 elements). Each element has its own power amplifier delivering 8 dBm, each element gain is 5 dBi, and a beamforming implementation loss of 1 dB is assumed relative to ideal in-phase combining, so $\text{EIRP} = P_{\text{elem}}G_{\text{elem}}N^2/L$. For this problem, assume the terminal class allows a boresight EIRP of up to 43 dBm.

**3.3.1S046**　Find the boresight EIRP of the terminal in dBm, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　36.08 dBm

**詳解**　$20\log_{10}(16) = 24.0824$ dB. EIRP $= 8 + 5 + 24.0824 - 1 = 36.0824$ dBm, which rounds to 36.08 dBm.

**出處**　C. A. Balanis, *Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design* (arrays); ITU Radio Regulations, Article 1, No. 1.161.

</details>

**3.3.1S047**　Keeping the same element power, element gain and implementation loss, what is the smallest number of elements that reaches the 43 dBm EIRP allowance?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　36 elements

**詳解**　Required $20\log_{10}N \ge 43 - 8 - 5 + 1 = 31$ dB, so $N^2 \ge 10^{3.1} = 1258.9$ and $N \ge 35.48$. The smallest integer is 36. Check: $N = 36$ gives $12 + 20\log_{10}(36) = 12 + 31.126 = 43.13$ dBm, which exceeds 43; $N = 35$ gives $12 + 30.881 = 42.88$ dBm, which falls short. **出處**　C. A. Balanis, *Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design* (arrays).

</details>

**3.3.1S048**　The total conducted power of all 16 amplifiers is only about 0.1 W, yet the EIRP is about 4 W. Does this violate conservation of energy? Explain.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　No. EIRP is not the radiated power; it is the power an isotropic antenna would need to produce the same flux density as the array does in the beam direction. The array radiates at most about 0.1 W in total, but concentrates it into a narrow beam, so the flux density in that one direction equals that of a 4 W isotropic source.

**詳解**　Total conducted: $16 \times 10^{0.8} = 16\times6.3096 = 100.95$ mW. EIRP: $10^{3.60824}$ mW $= 4.057$ W. The ratio, about 16 dB, is the array's directive concentration (12.04 dB array gain + 5 dBi element gain − 1 dB loss).

**出處**　C. A. Balanis, *Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design* (gain and directivity); ITU Radio Regulations, Article 1, No. 1.161.

</details>

**3.3.1S049**　When the terminal steers its beam 45° away from the array broadside, what happens to the EIRP in the beam direction, and why?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　It decreases. The array factor can still add the elements coherently in the new direction, but each element's own gain is lower at 45° off broadside, and the projected aperture of the array is smaller, so the total gain in the steered direction is lower than on broadside (scan loss).

**詳解**　$\text{EIRP}(\theta) = P_{\text{elem}}N^2 G_{\text{elem}}(\theta)/L$, and $G_{\text{elem}}(\theta) < G_{\text{elem}}(0)$ for a patch-type element; the effective aperture falls roughly as $\cos\theta$.

**出處**　C. A. Balanis, *Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design* (arrays, scanning).

</details>

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　No. The two figures describe the same gain: 0 dBd means the gain of a half-wave dipole, which is 2.15 dBi. The vendors only use different reference antennas.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

For a small chip antenna this is mainly due to low radiation efficiency: part of the input power is dissipated as heat in the antenna and nearby circuit board rather than radiated. The EIRP is therefore lower than the conducted power.

**3.3.1S062**　Residents are told "the site has an EIRP of about 800 W". Why is this figure not the right one for estimating exposure at the school?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　The 800 W figure is the boresight EIRP, i.e. in the direction of maximum gain. **3.3.1S063**　The scenario adds the vertical (15 dB) and horizontal (3 dB) pattern attenuations to get the attenuation in the direction of the school. Under what condition is this a reasonable approximation, and what is the risk?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　It assumes the three-dimensional pattern can be separated into the product of a horizontal and a vertical pattern (a sum in dB). <details><summary>看答案</summary>

**3.3.1S065**　A second building lies in the same direction but at twice the distance (120 m). The consultant writes the following. Identify the wrong step, explain why, and give the corrected power flux density in mW/m² rounded to four decimal places.

1. At 60 m, $S = 0.2789$ mW/m².
2. Doubling the distance halves $S$, so at 120 m, $S = 0.1395$ mW/m².

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 2 is wrong: in free space the power flux density falls as $1/d^2$, so doubling the distance divides $S$ by 4. The corrected value is 0.0697 mW/m².

**出處**　T. S. Rappaport, *Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice* (free-space propagation).
