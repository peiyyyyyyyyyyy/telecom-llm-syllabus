# 3.4.6 多重存取：FDMA、TDMA、CDMA、OFDMA

所屬：[3.4 訊務與存取](3-4.md)

## 內文

#### 為什麼要學

一個細胞只有有限的頻譜，卻要同時服務很多用戶。多重存取（multiple access）就是「怎麼把無線資源切開分給大家」的規則：依頻率切是 FDMA，依時間切是 TDMA，依展頻碼區分是 CDMA，把頻率與時間切成細格再動態分配是 OFDMA。各代行動通訊的主要差別之一就在這裡。會算「一段頻譜可以切出多少通道、每個用戶分到多少資源」，才能估計細胞容量、解讀資源區塊（resource block, RB）使用率這類 KPI，也才能把頻譜換算成 3.4.2 所需要的通道數。

#### 定義與符號

| 符號 | 意思 | 單位 |
|---|---|---|
| $B_t$ | 系統總頻寬 | Hz |
| $B_g$ | 頻段邊緣的保護頻帶（單側） | Hz |
| $B_c$ | 每個載波（頻道）的頻寬 | Hz |
| $N_c$ | 載波數 | 個 |
| $N_s$ | 每個 TDMA 訊框的時槽數 | 個 |
| $K$ | 頻率重用因子（一個重用群的細胞數） | 無因次 |
| $W$ | CDMA 展頻後的頻寬（約等於碼片速率） | Hz 或 chip/s |
| $R$ | 用戶資訊速率 | bit/s |
| $G_p$ | 處理增益 $W/R$ | 無因次或 dB |
| $E_b/I_0$ | 每位元能量對干擾功率密度比（需求值） | 無因次或 dB |
| $\alpha$ | 語音活動因子 | 無因次 |
| $f$ | 其他細胞干擾相對於本細胞干擾的比值 | 無因次 |
| $\Delta f$ | OFDM 子載波間隔 | Hz |
| $N_{\text{RB}}$ | 資源區塊數 | 個 |

#### 公式與推導

**FDMA（分頻多重存取）**　每個用戶佔一個載波。可用載波數為

$$N_c=\left\lfloor\frac{B_t-2B_g}{B_c}\right\rfloor$$

相鄰載波之間的保護間隔通常已包含在 $B_c$（載波間距）內。

**TDMA（分時多重存取）**　每個載波再切成 $N_s$ 個輪流使用的時槽，通常與 FDMA 併用。總通道數為 $N_c\times N_s$；採用頻率重用時，每個細胞只分到 $N_c/K$ 個載波：

$$N_{\text{ch/cell}}=\left\lfloor\frac{N_c}{K}\right\rfloor\times N_s$$

每個用戶只在自己的時槽傳送，平均速率約為載波總速率的 $1/N_s$，還要再扣掉保護時間與訓練序列等開銷。

**CDMA（分碼多重存取）**　所有用戶同時使用同一段頻譜，靠展頻碼區分；別人的訊號在解展頻後表現為干擾，所以容量受干擾限制而不是受通道數限制。考慮單一細胞的上行，假設功率控制完美（基地臺收到每個用戶的功率相同，都是 $P$）且忽略熱雜訊，則某用戶受到的干擾是其餘 $N-1$ 個用戶的功率：

$$\frac{E_b}{I_0}=\frac{P/R}{(N-1)P/W}=\frac{W/R}{N-1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N=1+\frac{W/R}{E_b/I_0}$$

式中各量都是線性值，不是 dB。再考慮語音活動因子 $\alpha$（用戶不說話時降低發射）與其他細胞的干擾 $f$：

$$N\approx1+\frac{W/R}{E_b/I_0}\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha\,(1+f)}$$

這是「軟容量」：多一個用戶不會被硬性拒絕，而是所有人的干擾稍微上升。

**OFDMA（正交分頻多重存取）**　把頻寬切成許多彼此正交的子載波，時間上切成 OFDM 符碼，一個子載波乘一個符碼是一個資源單元（resource element, RE）。排程器每個時槽把資源區塊分給不同用戶。依 3GPP TS 38.211：子載波間隔 $\Delta f=2^{\mu}\times15$ kHz（$\mu=0$ 至 6 對應 15、30、60、120、240、480、960 kHz）；一個資源區塊是頻域上連續 12 個子載波；一般循環字首下每個時槽 14 個 OFDM 符碼；每個 1 ms 子訊框有 $2^{\mu}$ 個時槽。因此

$$B_{\text{RB}}=12\,\Delta f,\qquad B_{\text{occ}}=N_{\text{RB}}\times12\,\Delta f,\qquad N_{\text{RE/slot}}=N_{\text{RB}}\times12\times14$$

$N_{\text{RB}}$ 的上限由通道頻寬與子載波間隔決定，列在 3GPP TS 38.101-1 的表 5.3.2-1；佔用頻寬比通道頻寬小，差額是兩側的保護頻帶。

#### 算例

**算例 1：FDMA／TDMA 的通道數**

已知（皆為假設值）：總頻寬 10 MHz，兩側各留 100 kHz 保護頻帶，載波間距 200 kHz，每個載波 8 個時槽，頻率重用因子 $K=7$。

- $N_c=\lfloor(10000-2\times100)/200\rfloor=\lfloor49\rfloor=49$ 個載波。
- 每細胞載波數：$\lfloor49/7\rfloor=7$ 個。
- 每細胞通道數：$7\times8=56$ 個時槽通道（未扣控制通道）。
- 若每個載波的總速率為 240 kbit/s（假設），每個用戶在扣除開銷前約分到 $240/8=30$ kbit/s。
- 合理性檢查：全系統 $49\times8=392$ 個通道，每細胞 56 個是它的七分之一，符合 $K=7$。

**算例 2：CDMA 上行容量**

已知（皆為假設值）：$W=1.2288$ Mchip/s，$R=9.6$ kbit/s，需求 $E_b/I_0=7$ dB，$\alpha=0.4$，$f=0.6$。

- 處理增益：$G_p=1228800/9600=128$，即 $10\log_{10}128=21.07$ dB。
- $E_b/I_0$ 換成線性：$10^{0.7}=5.012$。
- 只看單一細胞、不計語音活動：$N=1+128/5.012=1+25.54=26.54$，取整數 26 個用戶。
- 加入 $\alpha$ 與 $f$：$\alpha(1+f)=0.4\times1.6=0.64$；$N=1+25.54/0.64=1+39.91=40.91$，取整數 40 個用戶。
- 合理性檢查：以 dB 看，$21.07-7=14.07$ dB，$10^{1.407}=25.5$，與線性算法一致。語音活動使容量上升、他細胞干擾使容量下降，兩者合起來的係數是 $1/0.64=1.5625$ 倍，結果比 26 高，方向正確。

**算例 3：NR 的 OFDMA 資源**

已知：通道頻寬 20 MHz、子載波間隔 15 kHz（$\mu=0$）；依 TS 38.101-1 表 5.3.2-1，$N_{\text{RB}}=106$。

- 子載波數：$106\times12=1272$ 個。
- 佔用頻寬：$1272\times15=19{,}080$ kHz＝19.08 MHz。
- 每時槽 RE 數：$1272\times14=17{,}808$ 個；$\mu=0$ 時每秒 1,000 個時槽，每秒 $1.7808\times10^7$ 個 RE。
- 單側保護頻帶：依 TS 38.101-1 表 5.3.3-1 的附註公式，$(20000-19080)/2-15/2=460-7.5=452.5$ kHz，與該表所列數值相同。
- 某用戶在一個時槽分到 20 個 RB：$20\times12\times14=3360$ 個 RE，佔全部的 $20/106=18.9\%$。
- 同樣算法用在 100 MHz、30 kHz（$\mu=1$，$N_{\text{RB}}=273$）：$273\times12=3276$ 個子載波，佔用 $3276\times30=98{,}280$ kHz＝98.28 MHz；每時槽 $3276\times14=45{,}864$ 個 RE，每秒 2,000 個時槽，共 $9.1728\times10^7$ 個 RE。
- 合理性檢查：佔用頻寬都略小於通道頻寬。RE 數是資源總量的上限，其中一部分要給參考訊號與控制通道，不能全部換算成用戶資料。

#### 常見錯誤

1. **通道頻寬當成佔用頻寬**：20 MHz 的 NR 載波在 15 kHz 間隔下是 106 個 RB（19.08 MHz），不是 $20000/180=111$ 個。RB 數要查表，不能直接相除。
2. **CDMA 公式裡 dB 與線性混用**：$W/R$ 與 $E_b/I_0$ 相除前要都是線性值；若用 dB 就要相減後再換回線性。
3. **忘了頻率重用**：FDMA／TDMA 系統每個細胞只能用全部載波的 $1/K$。
4. **把 RE 數直接當成資料量**：RE 數還要乘上每個符碼的位元數與編碼率，並扣除參考訊號與控制開銷，才是實際的資料位元數（見 3.2）。

#### 和其他小節的關係

本節算出的每細胞通道數是 3.4.2 Erlang B 的 $N$。OFDMA 的 RE 數乘上 3.2 的調變階數與編碼率，可得傳輸區塊大小與峰值速率。CDMA 的 $E_b/I_0$ 與處理增益和 3.1 的訊號雜訊比、3.3 的鏈路預算是同一套 dB 運算。3.4.4 的隨機存取則是取得這些排程資源之前的步驟。

#### 單題：NR resource blocks and occupied bandwidth

**3.4.6G021**　An NR carrier has a 40 MHz channel bandwidth with 30 kHz subcarrier spacing, for which TS 38.101-1 Table 5.3.2-1 gives $N_{RB} = 106$. #### 情境題：TDMA capacity

**Scenario | 2G network frequency plan**

A 2G TDMA operator holds 2 × 15 MHz. In each direction it keeps a 100 kHz guard band at each edge, uses 200 kHz carrier spacing and 8 timeslots per carrier, and plans with a frequency reuse factor $K = 12$, each cell receiving $\lfloor N_c/K \rfloor$ carriers. In each cell, 2 timeslots are reserved for control channels.

**3.4.6S011**　How many traffic channels does each cell have?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　46 traffic channels

**詳解**　$N_c = \lfloor (15{,}000 - 200)/200 \rfloor = 74$. Carriers per cell $= \lfloor 74/12 \rfloor = 6$. Timeslots $= 6 \times 8 = 48$. Traffic channels $= 48 - 2 = 46$.

**出處**　Rappaport, Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice (frequency reuse; TDMA capacity)

</details>

**3.4.6S012**　With frequency hopping and power control, the operator tightens the reuse to $K = 4$. How many traffic channels per cell are then available?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　142 traffic channels

**詳解**　Carriers per cell $= \lfloor 74/4 \rfloor = 18$. Timeslots $= 18 \times 8 = 144$. Traffic channels $= 144 - 2 = 142$.

**出處**　Rappaport, Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice (frequency reuse; TDMA capacity)

</details>

**3.4.6S013**　Why does a smaller reuse factor give more channels per cell, and what is the price?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Each cell gets a larger share ($1/K$) of the carriers, but co-channel cells are closer: the reuse distance ratio $D/R = \sqrt{3K}$ falls (from 6 to about 3.46), so the carrier-to-co-channel-interference ratio drops.

**詳解**　With $K = 12$, $D/R = \sqrt{36} = 6$; with $K = 4$, $D/R = \sqrt{12} = 3.46$. Co-channel interference grows as $D/R$ shrinks, roughly as $(D/R)^{\gamma}$ with path-loss exponent $\gamma$. The network then relies on frequency hopping, power control and discontinuous transmission to keep C/I acceptable.

**出處**　Rappaport, Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice (co-channel reuse ratio $Q = D/R = \sqrt{3N}$)

</details>

**3.4.6S014**　Going from 46 to 142 traffic channels roughly triples the channel count. Does the carried voice traffic (in Erlang at a fixed blocking probability) also only triple? Explain.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　It more than triples, because of trunking efficiency: with Erlang B, a larger group of channels can be loaded to a higher average occupancy at the same blocking probability.

**詳解**　Erlang B capacity grows faster than linearly in the number of channels. At 2 % blocking, 46 channels carry about 36.5 Erlang (about 0.79 Erlang per channel), while 142 channels carry about 129 Erlang (about 0.91 Erlang per channel). The per-cell channel count from the frequency plan is the $N$ that goes into Erlang B (section 3.4.2).

**出處**　Rappaport, Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice (trunking and grade of service, Erlang B)

</details>

**Scenario | TDMA fixed-wireless uplink with guard times**

A rural fixed-wireless system uses TDMA on its uplink: a 5 ms frame contains 10 slots of 500 µs, and the channel bit rate is 2 Mbit/s. <details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　220 bits

**詳解**　Round trip $= 2 \times 15{,}000/(3 \times 10^8) = 1 \times 10^{-4}$ s $= 100$ µs. Guard $= 100 + 10 = 110$ µs. In bits: $110 \times 10^{-6} \times 2 \times 10^6 = 220$ bits.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　143.20 kbit/s

**詳解**　Slot $= 500 \times 10^{-6} \times 2 \times 10^6 = 1000$ bits. Data $= 1000 - 220 - 64 = 716$ bits per frame. Rate $= 716/0.005 = 143{,}200$ bit/s $= 143.20$ kbit/s.

**出處**　Rappaport, Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice (efficiency of TDMA)

</details>

**3.4.6S018**　How would timing advance change the guard period, and why?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　With timing advance, each terminal starts transmitting earlier by its own round-trip delay, so all bursts arrive aligned at the base station; the guard then only needs to cover timing errors and ramping, not the full 100 µs round trip.

**詳解**　Without timing advance, a burst from a distant terminal arrives up to 100 µs late and would overlap the next slot. The base station measures each terminal's delay and instructs it to advance its transmissions accordingly. The residual guard covers only the measurement error and power ramping, so most of the 200 bits used for the round trip become available for data.

**出處**　Rappaport, Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice (TDMA synchronization and timing advance)

</details>

**3.4.6S019**　What happens to the per-terminal data rate if the cell radius is doubled to 30 km without timing advance, and why is this a fundamental limit of TDMA cell size?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　The guard grows to 210 µs (420 bits), leaving 516 data bits per slot, so the rate falls to 103.2 kbit/s; the guard grows linearly with radius and eventually consumes the slot.

**詳解**　Round trip at 30 km $= 200$ µs, guard $= 210$ µs $= 420$ bits. Data $= 1000 - 420 - 64 = 516$ bits, rate $= 516/0.005 = 103.2$ kbit/s. Without timing advance, the guard must grow with the round-trip time, so large cells require either timing advance or longer slots, which is why TDMA systems specify a maximum timing advance and hence a maximum cell range.

**3.4.6S023**　The cell is at 96 users and a 97th user is admitted. What happens, and why is CDMA capacity called "soft"?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　No hard blocking occurs: every user's interference rises a little, so all users need slightly more power and their $E_b/I_0$ margin shrinks; at some point users at the cell edge can no longer reach the target. Capacity is soft because it is set by tolerable interference, not by a fixed number of channels.

**詳解**　In FDMA/TDMA the 47th call in a 46-channel cell is simply blocked. In CDMA all users share the band, and each new user adds interference to all others. Power control raises everyone's transmit power to keep $E_b/I_0$; this works until users at the edge run out of power. Admission control therefore limits load (noise rise) rather than a channel count, and the limit can be traded against quality or coverage.

**出處**　Holma & Toskala, WCDMA for UMTS (soft capacity, admission control)

</details>

**3.4.6S024**　Why does the capacity formula assume perfect power control, and what happens without it?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Because the formula assumes every user is received with equal power. Without power control, a user near the base station would be received tens of dB stronger than an edge user and would drown it out (near–far problem), collapsing capacity.

**詳解**　The derivation sets every user's received power to $P$, so the interference seen by any user is $(N-1)P$. Path loss between a near and a far user can differ by 60–80 dB, so with equal transmit powers the near user's signal would dominate the interference at the base station. Fast closed-loop power control keeps received powers equal to within about 1 dB.

**出處**　Gilhousen et al., "On the Capacity of a Cellular CDMA System," IEEE Trans. Veh. Technol., 1991 (power control)

</details>

**3.4.6S028**　Why does sectorization multiply CDMA capacity nearly by the number of sectors, while in an FDMA/TDMA system with a fixed carrier allocation it does not by itself add channels?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　In CDMA, capacity is interference-limited; a directional sector antenna receives only about one third of the interference, so each sector supports nearly a full cell's load on the same carrier. In FDMA/TDMA, the number of channels is set by the carriers assigned to the cell; splitting them among sectors does not create new channels.

**詳解**　In CDMA every sector reuses the same carrier, and each sees roughly $1/3$ of the users' interference, so per-sector capacity ≈ cell capacity (reduced somewhat by sector overlap). In FDMA/TDMA, sectorization reduces co-channel interference and so may allow a tighter reuse factor, but on its own the cell's carriers are just divided among the sectors (and trunking efficiency falls).

**出處**　Rappaport, Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice (sectoring); Gilhousen et al., 1991

</details>

**3.4.6S029**　Why does a voice activity factor of 0.45 increase CDMA capacity, while silence periods do not increase the capacity of a TDMA system with fixed slot assignment?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　In CDMA, a silent user transmits at reduced power and so creates less interference for everyone, which directly frees capacity. In TDMA with fixed slots, a silent user's slot stays reserved and unused, so nobody else benefits.

**詳解**　CDMA capacity is set by the average total interference, which scales with $\alpha$, giving the factor $1/\alpha$. A fixed TDMA/FDMA channel is a dedicated resource; to exploit silence it needs statistical multiplexing schemes that reassign slots during speech pauses.

**出處**　Gilhousen et al., "On the Capacity of a Cellular CDMA System," IEEE Trans. Veh. Technol., 1991 (voice activity)

#### 情境題：CDMA spreading codes and rates

**Scenario | Downlink code tree of a WCDMA cell**

A WCDMA cell transmits its downlink at 3.84 Mchip/s using orthogonal variable spreading factor (OVSF) codes. Each downlink symbol carries 2 coded bits (QPSK). A code of spreading factor SF has the symbol rate $3.84 \times 10^6/\text{SF}$ and blocks all codes below it in the code tree, so it uses up $128/\text{SF}$ of the 128 codes at SF = 128. Common channels occupy the equivalent of 4 codes at SF = 128 (stated value). One high-rate data user is given a code with SF = 8, and voice users each use one SF = 128 code.

**3.4.6S031**　What is the channel (coded) bit rate of the SF = 8 code, in kbit/s, rounded to two decimal places?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　960.00 kbit/s

**詳解**　Symbol rate $= 3.84 \times 10^6/8 = 480$ ksymbol/s. Coded bit rate $= 480 \times 2 = 960.00$ kbit/s.

**出處**　Holma & Toskala, WCDMA for UMTS (spreading factor and channel bit rate)

</details>

**3.4.6S032**　What is the maximum number of SF = 128 voice codes that remain available?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　108 codes

**詳解**　The SF = 8 code blocks $128/8 = 16$ codes at SF = 128. Common channels use 4. Remaining $= 128 - 16 - 4 = 108$.

**出處**　Holma & Toskala, WCDMA for UMTS (OVSF code tree)

</details>

**3.4.6S033**　OVSF codes are perfectly orthogonal. Why do downlink users still interfere with each other in a multipath channel?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Orthogonality holds only for time-aligned codes. Delayed multipath copies are shifted in time, so they are no longer orthogonal to the other users' codes and appear as interference (described by an orthogonality factor below 1).

**詳解**　In the downlink all codes leave the base station synchronized, so on a single path they do not interfere. Each delayed path is a time-shifted version of the composite signal; its cross-correlation with the wanted code at the receiver's sampling instant is not zero. The more energy in delayed paths, the lower the effective orthogonality and the higher the intra-cell interference.

**出處**　Holma & Toskala, WCDMA for UMTS (downlink orthogonality factor)

</details>

**3.4.6S034**　Under what conditions does the code tree, rather than transmit power or interference, limit the number of downlink users?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　When the users have good radio conditions and need little power, for example many low-rate users close to the base station, or when a few high-rate (low-SF) codes block large parts of the tree; then codes run out before the power does.

**詳解**　Normally the downlink is power-limited: edge users consume a large share of the base-station power. If most users are near the cell centre, each needs little power, and the 128-code tree (minus blocked branches) becomes the bottleneck. Low-SF codes for high-rate users block whole branches, accelerating code shortage. A second scrambling code adds code space, at the cost of losing orthogonality between the two trees.

**出處**　Holma & Toskala, WCDMA for UMTS (code limitation in downlink)

</details>

**答案**　Step 2 is wrong: an SF = 8 code blocks $128/8 = 16$ codes at SF = 128 (it uses 1/8 of the tree), not 8. Correct: 108 voice codes.

**3.4.6S057**　Why can NR use the whole carrier in every cell (reuse 1) when GSM needed $K = 4$?

**答案**　NR adapts modulation and coding to each user's SINR and relies on channel coding, HARQ, interference-aware scheduling and coordination, so it can work at low SINR at the cell edge; GSM voice needs roughly 9 dB C/I on a fixed modulation, which requires co-channel cells to be several cells apart.

**3.4.6S058**　The ratio of about 9.6 compares channel counts. Why should it not be read as a 9.6-fold capacity gain without further checks?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Because the NR figure assumes 1 RB per packet for every user, which only holds at good SINR; edge users need more RBs, and control, reference-signal and interference margins are simplified. A fair comparison uses the same voice quality, blocking or outage target and a realistic SINR distribution.

**詳解**　The RB need per VoIP packet depends on the user's MCS; at the cell edge one packet may need several RBs, cutting the user count. The comparison also ignores PDCCH limits, codec differences (GSM full rate vs a modern codec) and Erlang trunking. System-level simulations with the same quality criteria are needed for a fair figure.

**出處**　Dahlman, Parkvall & Sköld, 5G NR: The Next Generation Wireless Access Technology (VoIP capacity evaluation)

**3.4.6S059**　A planner computes the GSM side:

#### 情境題：Numerology choice

**Scenario | 50 MHz carrier: 30 kHz or 60 kHz spacing**

An operator can run a 50 MHz FR1 carrier with 30 kHz spacing ($N_{RB} = 133$) or with 60 kHz spacing ($N_{RB} = 65$), both from TS 38.101-1 Table 5.3.2-1. A resource block has 12 subcarriers; with normal cyclic prefix a slot has 14 symbols and a 1 ms subframe has $2^{\mu}$ slots (TS 38.211 Table 4.3.2-1), with $\mu = 1$ for 30 kHz and $\mu = 2$ for 60 kHz. Assume the normal cyclic prefix lasts about 2.34 µs at 30 kHz and about 1.17 µs at 60 kHz.

**3.4.6S061**　By what percentage are the resource elements per second lower with 60 kHz than with 30 kHz? Give the answer in percent, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　2.26 %

**詳解**　30 kHz: $133 \times 12 \times 14 \times 2000 = 44{,}688{,}000$ REs/s. 60 kHz: $65 \times 12 \times 14 \times 4000 = 43{,}680{,}000$ REs/s. Reduction $= (44{,}688{,}000 - 43{,}680{,}000)/44{,}688{,}000 = 1{,}008{,}000/44{,}688{,}000 = 0.022556 = 2.26$ %.

**出處**　3GPP TS 38.211 V18.10.0, Table 4.3.2-1; 3GPP TS 38.101-1 V18.15.0, Table 5.3.2-1

</details>

**3.4.6S062**　Why might the operator still choose 60 kHz despite having 2.26 % fewer resources?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Because the slot is half as long (0.25 ms instead of 0.5 ms), which shortens scheduling and HARQ timelines for low-latency services, and the wider spacing is more robust to phase noise and Doppler.

**詳解**　Slot duration $= 1\text{ ms}/2^{\mu}$: 0.5 ms at 30 kHz, 0.25 ms at 60 kHz. Shorter slots reduce the time to the next transmission opportunity and the HARQ round trip. A wider spacing also makes inter-carrier interference from frequency errors, phase noise and Doppler smaller relative to the spacing.

**出處**　3GPP TS 38.211 V18.10.0, Tables 4.2-1 and 4.3.2-1

</details>

**3.4.6S063**　The site serves a large outdoor area where the channel delay spread can reach 2 µs. Which spacing is safer, and why?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　30 kHz: its cyclic prefix (about 2.34 µs) covers a 2 µs delay spread, while the 60 kHz cyclic prefix (about 1.17 µs) is shorter than the delay spread, causing inter-symbol interference and loss of subcarrier orthogonality.

**詳解**　OFDM remains free of inter-symbol interference only if the cyclic prefix is longer than the channel's delay spread. Doubling the subcarrier spacing halves the symbol and the normal cyclic prefix. With 2 µs of delay spread, the 60 kHz prefix would be exceeded by about 0.8 µs, so 30 kHz (or extended CP at 60 kHz, at the cost of 12 symbols per slot) is the robust choice.

**出處**　3GPP TS 38.211 V18.10.0, Tables 4.2-1 and 4.3.2-2; Dahlman, Parkvall & Sköld, 5G NR (cyclic prefix and numerology)

</details>

**3.4.6S064**　A planner computes the 60 kHz resources:
1. Subcarriers $= 65 \times 12 = 780$.
2. Slots per second $= 2000$.
3. REs per second $= 780 \times 14 \times 2000 = 21.84$ million, about half of the 30 kHz figure.

Which step is wrong, and what is the correct result?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 2 is wrong: for 60 kHz ($\mu = 2$) there are $2^2 = 4$ slots per 1 ms subframe, i.e. 4000 slots per second. Correct: 43.68 million REs per second, only 2.26 % fewer than at 30 kHz.

**詳解**　$780 \times 14 \times 4000 = 43{,}680{,}000$ REs/s.

**出處**　3GPP TS 38.211 V18.10.0, Table 4.3.2-1

</details>

**3.4.6S065**　Another planner estimates the 60 kHz occupied bandwidth:
1. At 30 kHz, $N_{RB} = 133$.
2. Doubling the spacing halves the RB count: $N_{RB} = \lfloor 133/2 \rfloor = 66$.
3. Occupied $= 66 \times 12 \times 60 = 47{,}520$ kHz.

Which step is wrong, and what is the correct occupied bandwidth?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 2 is wrong: $N_{RB}$ is not exactly half; the larger minimum guard band at 60 kHz means TS 38.101-1 Table 5.3.2-1 gives 65 RBs. Correct occupied bandwidth: 46.80 MHz.

**詳解**　$65 \times 12 \times 60 = 46{,}800$ kHz $= 46.80$ MHz. (Check with Table 5.3.3-1: $(50{,}000 - 46{,}800)/2 - 30 = 1570$ kHz, the listed minimum guard band; 66 RBs would leave only $(50{,}000 - 47{,}520)/2 - 30 = 1210$ kHz.)

**出處**　3GPP TS 38.101-1 V18.15.0, Tables 5.3.2-1 and 5.3.3-1

</details>
