# 3.1.6 訊號雜訊比與 SINR

所屬：[3.1 訊號與通道](3-1.md)

## 內文

#### 為什麼要學

一條鏈路能跑多快、錯誤率多高，取決於想要的訊號相對於「不想要的東西」有多強。只考慮熱雜訊時用訊號雜訊比（signal-to-noise ratio, SNR）；行動網路裡鄰近細胞用同樣的頻率，干擾常常比雜訊還大，所以要用訊號對干擾加雜訊比（signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio, SINR）。SINR 是調變編碼等級的選擇、吞吐量估算、換手判斷與網路最佳化最核心的指標。另外，比較不同調變方式的效能時，要用和頻寬、速率無關的每位元能量對雜訊密度比 $E_b/N_0$，三者之間的換算是基本功。

#### 定義與符號

| 符號 | 意思 | 單位 |
|---|---|---|
| $S$ | 想要訊號的接收功率 | W 或 mW（或 dBm） |
| $N$ | 雜訊功率，$N = N_0B$（含接收機雜訊指數） | W 或 mW（或 dBm） |
| $I_i$ | 第 $i$ 個干擾源的接收功率 | W 或 mW（或 dBm） |
| $I$ | 干擾總功率，$I = \sum_i I_i$ | W 或 mW（或 dBm） |
| $\text{SNR}$、$\text{SIR}$、$\text{SINR}$ | 訊號對雜訊、對干擾、對干擾加雜訊的比值 | 無因次，或 dB |
| $B$ | 計算雜訊所用的頻寬 | Hz |
| $R_b$ | 資訊位元率 | bit/s |
| $E_b$ | 每個位元的能量，$E_b = S/R_b$ | J |
| $N_0$ | 雜訊功率頻譜密度 | W/Hz |
| $C/N_0$ | 載波功率對雜訊密度比 | Hz（或 dB-Hz） |

#### 公式與推導

三個比值的線性定義：

$$\text{SNR} = \frac{S}{N},\qquad \text{SIR} = \frac{S}{I},\qquad \text{SINR} = \frac{S}{I + N} = \frac{S}{\sum_i I_i + N}$$

用 dB 表示時是相減：$\text{SNR(dB)} = S(\text{dBm}) - N(\text{dBm})$。但 **分母裡的干擾和雜訊是功率相加**，必須先把每一項換成 mW 加總，再換回 dBm，最後才和 $S$ 相減。這裡假設各干擾源和雜訊互不相關，所以功率可以直接相加；而且把干擾當成像雜訊一樣處理。

把 SINR 的倒數拆開可以得到一個很有用的關係：

$$\frac{1}{\text{SINR}} = \frac{1}{\text{SNR}} + \frac{1}{\text{SIR}}$$

所以 SINR 一定比 SNR 和 SIR 都小，並且由比較小的那個主導。$I \ll N$ 時 $\text{SINR} \approx \text{SNR}$，稱為雜訊受限（noise-limited），常見於涵蓋邊緣或鄉村，加大發射功率有幫助；$I \gg N$ 時 $\text{SINR} \approx \text{SIR}$，稱為干擾受限（interference-limited），常見於密集市區，所有基地臺一起加大功率並不會改善 SINR，要靠天線傾角、干擾協調或波束成形。SNR 與 SIR 剛好相等時，SINR 比它們低 3.01 dB。

**$E_b/N_0$ 與 SNR 的換算。** 訊號功率是每秒送出的能量，$S = E_bR_b$；雜訊功率 $N = N_0B$，所以

$$\text{SNR} = \frac{E_b}{N_0}\cdot\frac{R_b}{B}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \frac{E_b}{N_0}(\text{dB}) = \text{SNR(dB)} + 10\log_{10}\frac{B}{R_b}$$

$R_b/B$ 就是頻譜效率（bit/s/Hz）。$E_b/N_0$ 是無因次量，和選用的頻寬無關，適合比較不同調變與編碼；SNR 則會隨量測頻寬改變。同理，$C/N_0 = \text{SNR}\times B$，用 dB 表示是 $\text{SNR(dB)} + 10\log_{10}B$，單位 dB-Hz，例如 1 MHz 頻寬內 SNR 為 10 dB 相當於 $C/N_0 = 70$ dB-Hz。

#### 算例

**算例 1：兩個干擾源加雜訊的 SINR**

已知（假設值）：服務細胞訊號 $S = -85$ dBm；兩個鄰近細胞的干擾分別為 −95 dBm、−100 dBm；頻寬 20 MHz，接收機雜訊指數 7 dB，溫度 290 K。求 SNR、SIR、SINR（dB，取到小數第 2 位）。

- 雜訊：$N = -174 + 10\log_{10}(20\times10^6) + 7 = -174 + 73.01 + 7 = -93.99$ dBm，線性值 $10^{-9.399} = 3.991\times10^{-10}$ mW。
- 干擾：$10^{-9.5} = 3.162\times10^{-10}$ mW，$10^{-10} = 1.000\times10^{-10}$ mW，合計 $4.162\times10^{-10}$ mW，即 $-93.81$ dBm。
- 干擾加雜訊：$4.162\times10^{-10} + 3.991\times10^{-10} = 8.153\times10^{-10}$ mW，即 $-90.89$ dBm。
- $\text{SNR} = -85 - (-93.99) = 8.99$ dB；$\text{SIR} = -85 - (-93.81) = 8.81$ dB；$\text{SINR} = -85 - (-90.89) = 5.89$ dB（線性值 3.88）。
- 合理性檢查：干擾和雜訊幾乎一樣大（差 0.18 dB），所以 SINR 應該比 SNR、SIR 低大約 3 dB；$8.99 - 5.89 = 3.10$ dB、$8.81 - 5.89 = 2.92$ dB，吻合。用倒數關係驗算：$1/10^{0.899} + 1/10^{0.881} = 0.1262 + 0.1315 = 0.2577$，倒數為 3.88，一致。

**算例 2：SNR 與 $E_b/N_0$ 互換**

已知：頻寬 10 MHz 內量到 SNR＝3 dB，資訊位元率 2 Mbit/s。求 $E_b/N_0$。

- $B/R_b = 10\times10^6/(2\times10^6) = 5$，$10\log_{10}5 = 6.99$ dB。
- $E_b/N_0 = 3 + 6.99 = 9.99$ dB。
- 檢查：頻譜效率只有 0.2 bit/s/Hz，每個位元分到的能量多，所以 $E_b/N_0$ 比 SNR 高，方向正確。反過來，若在 1 MHz 內傳 2 Mbit/s（頻譜效率 2 bit/s/Hz）且 SNR＝10 dB，則 $E_b/N_0 = 10 + 10\log_{10}0.5 = 10 - 3.01 = 6.99$ dB，比 SNR 低。

#### 常見錯誤

- **把干擾和雜訊的 dBm 值直接相加，或取其中最大的就算數**：一定要換成線性功率加總。
- **線性與 dB 混用**：例如把 $\text{SINR} = 5.89$ dB 直接代進需要線性值的公式（如 Shannon 容量），正確要代 3.88。
- **$E_b/N_0$ 換算方向弄反**：$E_b/N_0 = \text{SNR}\times B/R_b$；頻寬大於位元率時 $E_b/N_0$ 比 SNR 高。
- **雜訊頻寬和訊號頻寬不一致**：算 $N$ 用的 $B$ 要和量 $S$ 的頻寬相同（例如都是整個載波，或都是單一資源區塊）。

#### 和其他小節的關係

分母的雜訊來自 3.1.5 的 $kTB$ 與雜訊指數；功率加總與 dB 換算的規則來自 3.1.1；訊號與干擾的強度由 3.1.2、3.1.3 的路徑損耗決定。本節算出的 SNR、SINR 會代入 3.2.1 的 Shannon 容量、3.2.4 的編碼率與 MCS 選擇；$E_b/N_0$ 是 3.2.3 錯誤率公式的自變數；3.3.3 的干擾餘量與 3.3.4 的接收靈敏度也建立在本節之上。

#### 單題：SNR from signal and noise powers

**3.1.6G001**　A narrowband receiver measures a wanted signal power of 2.5 pW and a noise power of 0.04 pW, both in the same 200 kHz bandwidth. Determine the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in dB, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　17.96

**詳解**　Both powers are linear and refer to the same bandwidth, so the ratio can be taken directly.
SNR (linear) = 2.5 / 0.04 = 62.5.
SNR (dB) = 10·log10(62.5) = 17.9588 dB ≈ 17.96 dB.
\boxed{17.96}

**出處**　S. Haykin, *Communication Systems* (definition of signal-to-noise ratio as a power ratio).

</details>

**3.1.6G002**　A receiver with a noise figure of 7 dB and a noise bandwidth of 10 MHz receives a wanted signal of −87 dBm. The source noise temperature is 290 K and Boltzmann's constant is k = 1.380649×10⁻²³ J/K. Compute the thermal noise density from kT (do not use a rounded −174 dBm/Hz) and determine the SNR in the 10 MHz noise bandwidth, in dB, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　9.98

**詳解**　Step 1: kT = 1.380649×10⁻²³ × 290 = 4.00388×10⁻²¹ W/Hz = 4.00388×10⁻¹⁸ mW/Hz, i.e. 10·log10(4.00388×10⁻¹⁸) = −173.975 dBm/Hz.
Step 2: bandwidth term 10·log10(10×10⁶) = 70.000 dB.
Step 3: noise power N = −173.975 + 70.000 + 7 = −96.975 dBm.
Step 4: SNR = −87 − (−96.975) = 9.975 dB ≈ 9.98 dB.
\boxed{9.98}

**3.1.6G003**　At the input of a demodulator the wanted signal has an rms voltage of 12 µV and the noise has an rms voltage of 1.5 µV. Both are measured across the same 50 Ω resistance and in the same 25 kHz bandwidth. Determine the SNR in dB, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　18.06

**詳解**　Power is proportional to the square of the rms voltage when the resistance is the same, so SNR = (V_s/V_n)².
Voltage ratio = 12 / 1.5 = 8; power ratio = 8² = 64.
SNR (dB) = 10·log10(64) = 20·log10(8) = 18.0618 dB ≈ 18.06 dB.
\boxed{18.06}

#### 單題：Summing interferers and SINR

**3.1.6G006**　A downlink receiver measures, all in the same 9 MHz bandwidth, a wanted signal of −85 dBm, three interfering signals of −95 dBm, −98 dBm and −103 dBm, and a noise power of −104 dBm. The interferers and the noise are mutually uncorrelated, so their powers add. Determine the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) in dB, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　7.48

**詳解**　Step 1: convert to mW. −95 dBm = 3.1623×10⁻¹⁰ mW; −98 dBm = 1.5849×10⁻¹⁰ mW; −103 dBm = 5.0119×10⁻¹¹ mW; noise −104 dBm = 3.9811×10⁻¹¹ mW.
Step 2: I + N = (3.1623 + 1.5849 + 0.5012 + 0.3981)×10⁻¹⁰ = 5.6465×10⁻¹⁰ mW.
Step 3: back to dBm: 10·log10(5.6465×10⁻¹⁰) = −92.4822 dBm.
Step 4: SINR = −85 − (−92.4822) = 7.4822 dB ≈ 7.48 dB.
\boxed{7.48}

**出處**　A. Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications* (SINR with interference treated as noise).

</details>

**3.1.6G008**　For a link, the signal-to-noise ratio (interference ignored) is 15 dB and the signal-to-interference ratio (noise ignored) is 10 dB. Both ratios refer to the same 20 MHz bandwidth, and interference and noise are uncorrelated. Determine the SINR in dB, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**3.1.6S017**　What is the SINR at the laptop, in dB, rounded to two decimal places?

**3.1.6S018**　The laptop vendor offers a new radio whose noise figure is 3 dB lower. How much would the SINR improve in this apartment, and why?

#### 情境題：Eb/N0, Es/N0 and SNR

**Scenario | Satellite broadcast carrier**

A satellite carrier uses QPSK with a rate-3/4 channel code at a symbol rate of 30 Msymbol/s; ignore framing and pilot overhead, so the information bit rate is 30 × 2 × 3/4 = 45 Mbit/s. With a roll-off of 0.2 the carrier occupies 36 MHz. A monitoring receiver reports a carrier-to-noise ratio C/N = 6.5 dB, with the noise measured in a noise bandwidth equal to the symbol rate (30 MHz). The noise is white.

**3.1.6S031**　What is Eb/N0 per information bit, in dB, rounded to two decimal places?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　4.74 dB

**詳解**　Eb/N0 = (C/N)·(B/Rb), with B = 30 MHz and Rb = 45 Mbit/s.
B/Rb = 30/45 = 0.6667 → 10·log10(0.6667) = −1.7609 dB.
Eb/N0 = 6.5 − 1.7609 = 4.7391 dB ≈ 4.74 dB.

**出處**　B. Sklar, *Digital Communications: Fundamentals and Applications* (Eb/N0 = (S/N)·(W/R)).

</details>

**3.1.6S032**　A second instrument measures the noise over the full 36 MHz occupied bandwidth. What C/N does it report for the same carrier, in dB, rounded to two decimal places?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　5.71 dB

**詳解**　The carrier power is the same; the noise power rises by 36/30 = 1.2 → 10·log10(1.2) = 0.7918 dB.
C/N (36 MHz) = 6.5 − 0.7918 = 5.7082 dB ≈ 5.71 dB.

**出處**　J. G. Proakis and M. Salehi, *Digital Communications* (white noise: N = N0·B).

</details>

**3.1.6S033**　With the noise bandwidth set equal to the symbol rate, the reported C/N of 6.5 dB is numerically equal to Es/N0. Why?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　The carrier power is the symbol energy times the symbol rate, C = Es·Rs, and the noise power is N = N0·B. Hence C/N = (Es/N0)·(Rs/B), and when B = Rs the factor Rs/B is 1, so C/N = Es/N0 = 6.5 dB. With any other noise bandwidth the two differ by 10·log10(Rs/B).

**詳解**　C/N = Es·Rs / (N0·B). For B = 36 MHz: C/N = 6.5 + 10·log10(30/36) = 5.71 dB, while Es/N0 remains 6.5 dB.

**出處**　J. G. Proakis and M. Salehi, *Digital Communications* (signal power and energy per symbol).

</details>

**3.1.6S034**　The two instruments report different C/N values (6.5 dB and 5.71 dB) for the same carrier. Do they also imply different Eb/N0 values?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　No. Eb/N0 is the energy per information bit divided by the noise density; neither quantity depends on the bandwidth in which someone chooses to measure the noise. Each C/N converts to the same Eb/N0 once its own bandwidth is used: 6.5 − 10·log10(45/30) = 4.74 dB and 5.71 − 10·log10(45/36) = 4.74 dB. This is why Eb/N0 is preferred for comparing modulation and coding schemes, while SNR must always be quoted with its bandwidth.

**詳解**　10·log10(45/30) = 1.7609 dB; 10·log10(45/36) = 0.9691 dB; 6.5 − 1.7609 = 4.7391 dB; 5.7082 − 0.9691 = 4.7391 dB.

**出處**　B. Sklar, *Digital Communications: Fundamentals and Applications* (Eb/N0 as a bandwidth-independent figure of merit).

</details>

**3.1.6S035**　The carrier is reconfigured to 8-PSK with a rate-2/3 code at the same 30 Msymbol/s, giving an information bit rate of 30 × 3 × 2/3 = 60 Mbit/s. The C/N in the 30 MHz noise bandwidth is now 9 dB. An operator computes Eb/N0 as follows. Which step is wrong, why, and what is the correct Eb/N0 in dB (two decimal places)?
1. Rb/B = 60/30 = 2, i.e. 10·log10(2) = 3.01 dB.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**詳解**　Eb/N0 = 9 − 3.0103 = 5.9897 dB ≈ 5.99 dB. (When more than one bit per hertz of noise bandwidth is carried, Eb/N0 is lower than the SNR.)

**出處**　B. Sklar, *Digital Communications: Fundamentals and Applications* (Eb/N0 = (S/N)·(W/R)).

</details>

**詳解**　In the 18 kHz bandwidth the required SNR is 9 + 10·log10(36/18) = 12.01 dB and the noise is −165.9752 + 42.5527 = −123.4225 dBm, giving −111.41 dBm: the same sensitivity as from Eb/N0 directly. Changing the bookkeeping bandwidth changes the required SNR by the same amount as the noise.

**3.1.6S048**　Which of the four noise-and-interference contributions is the largest, and what would a terminal with 10 dB better ACS (43 dB) gain?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　The adjacent-channel leakage is the largest: −93 dBm is 5.01×10⁻¹⁰ mW, about 58 % of the total 8.66×10⁻¹⁰ mW, even though the interferer is in another channel, because it is received 22 dB stronger than the wanted signal. With ACS = 43 dB the leakage falls to −103 dBm, I + N falls to −93.82 dBm and the SINR rises from 8.62 dB to about 11.82 dB, a gain of about 3.2 dB. The gain is less than 10 dB because the co-channel cells and the noise then dominate.

**詳解**　−103 dBm = 5.012×10⁻¹¹ mW; I + N = (0.5012 + 1.9953 + 1.2589 + 0.3981)×10⁻¹⁰ = 4.1535×10⁻¹⁰ mW = −93.8159 dBm; SINR = 11.8159 dB.

**出處**　3GPP TS 38.101-1 (adjacent channel selectivity); A. Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications*.

</details>

**3.1.6S049**　Why must the adjacent-channel power be reduced by the ACS before it is added to the co-channel interference, instead of adding the −60 dBm directly, or ignoring it because it is "on another frequency"?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　SINR counts only the unwanted power that falls inside the wanted channel after the receive filtering. The receive filter attenuates the adjacent channel by a finite amount (the ACS), so a fraction 10^(−33/10) of the −60 dBm reaches the demodulator: it cannot be ignored, but it is far less than −60 dBm. Every term in I + N must be referred to the same point and the same bandwidth as the wanted signal.

**詳解**　−60 dBm − 33 dB = −93 dBm in-channel; adding −60 dBm directly would overstate the interference by a factor of about 2000.

**出處**　3GPP TS 38.101-1 (adjacent channel selectivity); ITU-R Recommendation SM.337 (frequency and distance separations; off-channel rejection).

</details>

**3.1.6S050**　A second base station of the other operator appears on the other adjacent channel at −55 dBm (ACS also 33 dB); everything else is as in the scenario, where I + N was 8.6642×10⁻¹⁰ mW. A student recomputes the SINR as follows. Which step is wrong, why, and what is the correct SINR in dB (two decimal places)?
1. Leakage from the new interferer: −55 + 33 = −22 dBm = 6.3096×10⁻³ mW.

**詳解**　−88 dBm = 1.5849×10⁻⁹ mW.

SINR = −82 − (−86.1060) = 4.1060 dB ≈ 4.11 dB.

**3.1.6S057**　Before despreading, the signal is almost 20 dB below the noise in the front-end bandwidth, yet the receiver tracks it reliably. How is this possible?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　The receiver correlates the input with a local copy of the spreading code and integrates. The wanted signal adds coherently and collapses into a bandwidth of the order of the inverse of the integration time, while the noise remains spread, so only the noise in that narrow bandwidth counts. Integrating over 1 ms, for instance, corresponds to a noise bandwidth of 1 kHz, where the SNR is 43.5 − 30 = 13.5 dB. The quantity that matters is C/N0 (or Eb/N0), not the SNR in the wide front-end bandwidth.

**詳解**　SNR after coherent integration over T = C/N0 − 10·log10(1/T). For T = 1 ms: 43.5 − 10·log10(1000) = 13.5 dB, an improvement of 33.11 dB over −19.61 dB, equal to 10·log10(2.046×10⁶/1000).

**出處**　E. D. Kaplan and C. J. Hegarty (eds.), *Understanding GPS/GNSS: Principles and Applications*; B. Sklar, *Digital Communications: Fundamentals and Applications* (processing gain of direct-sequence systems).

**3.1.6S058**　If the coherent integration time is extended from 1 ms to 20 ms, by how much does the post-correlation SNR improve, and what limits a further extension for this signal?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　It improves by 10·log10(20) = 13.01 dB (from 13.5 dB to 26.51 dB), because the noise bandwidth of the integration shrinks from 1 kHz to 50 Hz while the signal power is unchanged. Going beyond 20 ms is limited by the data: at 50 bit/s a bit lasts 20 ms, and a sign change inside the integration interval cancels part of the signal unless the data bits are known. Residual frequency error and oscillator instability also cause loss as the integration gets longer.

**詳解**　10·log10(20 ms / 1 ms) = 13.0103 dB; 13.5 + 13.0103 = 26.5103 dB = C/N0 − 10·log10(50).

**出處**　E. D. Kaplan and C. J. Hegarty (eds.), *Understanding GPS/GNSS: Principles and Applications* (coherent integration and data-bit limits).

**Scenario | Handset with a main and a diversity antenna**

A handset has two receive antennas. On the main antenna the SNR is 8 dB. The diversity antenna has 4 dB less gain, so its SNR is 4 dB. Both SNRs refer to the same 9 MHz noise bandwidth; the two receiver chains have equal noise power and independent noise, there is no interference, and the channel gains are known perfectly. Under these assumptions the output SNR of maximal-ratio combining (MRC) is the sum of the linear branch SNRs.

**3.1.6S061**　What is the SNR after MRC in dB, rounded to two decimal places?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　9.46 dB

**詳解**　Step 1: convert: 8 dB = 10^(0.8) = 6.3096; 4 dB = 10^(0.4) = 2.5119.
Step 2: sum = 6.3096 + 2.5119 = 8.8215.
Step 3: 10·log10(8.8215) = 9.4554 dB ≈ 9.46 dB (1.46 dB better than the main antenna alone).

**出處**　A. Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications* (maximal-ratio combining: output SNR is the sum of the branch SNRs).

</details>

**3.1.6S062**　The rule "MRC output SNR = sum of branch SNRs" was used under stated assumptions. Name the assumptions, and say what happens when the dominant impairment is a strong interferer seen by both antennas instead of receiver noise.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Assumptions: the noise on the branches is independent (uncorrelated) with known power (here equal), the receiver knows each branch's channel gain and phase so that it can co-phase and weight the branches optimally, and the SNRs refer to the same bandwidth. If the impairment is an interferer received on both antennas, the unwanted components are correlated between the branches; they can add coherently in the combiner, so the sum rule no longer holds and MRC can give much less gain. A combiner that takes the interference correlation into account (interference rejection combining / MMSE) is then needed and can do better than MRC.

**詳解**　MRC weights are proportional to h_i*/N_i; the output SNR Σ|h_i|²/N_i follows only when the noise terms are independent. With spatially correlated interference the optimal weights are w = R⁻¹h, where R is the interference-plus-noise covariance matrix.

**出處**　A. Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications* (receiver diversity); D. Tse and P. Viswanath, *Fundamentals of Wireless Communication* (receive beamforming and linear MMSE receivers).

</details>

**3.1.6S063**　For two branches under these assumptions, what is the largest possible MRC gain over the stronger branch alone, when does it occur, and what happens to the gain as the diversity antenna gets weaker?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　The largest gain is 3.01 dB and occurs when the two branch SNRs are equal (the sum is then twice either one). As the second branch gets weaker the gain shrinks toward 0 dB: here, with a 4 dB imbalance, it is 1.46 dB; with a 10 dB imbalance it would be only 10·log10(1.1) = 0.41 dB. MRC never makes the output worse than the best branch, because a useless branch simply receives a near-zero weight.

**詳解**　Gain = 10·log10(1 + γ₂/γ₁) with γ₂ ≤ γ₁; maximum 10·log10(2) = 3.0103 dB at γ₂ = γ₁; here 10·log10(1 + 10^(−0.4)) = 10·log10(1.3981) = 1.4554 dB.

**出處**　A. Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications* (array gain of maximal-ratio combining).

</details>
