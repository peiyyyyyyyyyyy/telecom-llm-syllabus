# 3.4.3 排隊模型：Little 定理、M/M/1、M/M/c、M/M/1/K

所屬：[3.4 訊務與存取](3-4.md)

## 內文

#### 為什麼要學

封包網路的每個節點（路由器輸出埠、基地臺排程器的緩衝區、核心網的信令處理、UPF 的轉送佇列）都是「到達、等待、被服務、離開」的排隊系統。排隊模型把到達率、服務率和緩衝區大小，換算成平均延遲、佇列長度、利用率和丟棄率。它可以解釋為什麼鏈路利用率接近 100% 時延遲會急遽上升，也是設定緩衝區大小、估計端到端延遲、判讀 KPI（延遲、丟包率）的基礎。

#### 定義與符號

| 符號 | 意思 | 單位 |
|---|---|---|
| $\lambda$ | 平均到達率 | 個/秒 |
| $\mu$ | 每個伺服器的平均服務率（平均服務時間的倒數） | 個/秒 |
| $c$ | 伺服器數 | 個 |
| $K$ | 系統容量（含正在被服務的那一個） | 個 |
| $a$ | 提供負載 $\lambda/\mu$ | Erl |
| $\rho$ | 每個伺服器的利用率 | 無因次 |
| $L$ | 系統內平均數量（等待中＋服務中） | 個 |
| $L_q$ | 佇列中平均數量（只算等待中） | 個 |
| $W$ | 在系統內的平均停留時間 | 秒 |
| $W_q$ | 在佇列中的平均等待時間 | 秒 |
| $P_n$ | 穩態時系統內有 $n$ 個的機率 | 無因次 |
| $P_K$ | 系統滿的機率（阻塞／丟棄機率） | 無因次 |
| $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ | 實際進入系統的有效到達率 | 個/秒 |

Kendall 記號 A/S/c/K 依序是到達過程、服務時間分布、伺服器數、系統容量；M 表示無記憶性（卜瓦松到達或指數服務時間）。

#### 公式與推導

**Little 定理**　對任何處於穩態的排隊系統，不論到達與服務的分布為何：

$$L=\lambda W,\qquad L_q=\lambda W_q$$

另外 $W = W_q + 1/\mu$ 永遠成立（停留時間＝等待＋服務）。有容量上限、會丟棄時，式中的 $\lambda$ 要改用 $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$。

以下三個模型都假設卜瓦松到達、指數分布的服務時間、先到先服務，而且都用同一個方法推導：穩態時，系統從「有 $n$ 個」升到「有 $n+1$ 個」的頻率，必須等於從 $n+1$ 降回 $n$ 的頻率（生滅過程的平衡方程式），由此解出每個狀態的機率 $P_n$，再用 $L=\sum nP_n$ 求平均數量，最後用 Little 定理換成時間。

**M/M/1**（單一伺服器、無限緩衝）　$\rho=\lambda/\mu$，穩態條件 $\rho<1$。平衡方程式 $\lambda P_n=\mu P_{n+1}$ 給出穩態機率 $P_n=(1-\rho)\rho^n$，伺服器忙碌的機率 $1-P_0$ 剛好等於 $\rho$，由此得到

$$L=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho},\quad L_q=\frac{\rho^2}{1-\rho},\quad W=\frac{1}{\mu-\lambda},\quad W_q=\frac{\rho}{\mu-\lambda}$$

分母的 $1-\rho$ 說明了延遲為何在高負載時暴增：$\rho$ 從 0.8 升到 0.9，$L$ 從 4 變成 9。

**M/M/c**（$c$ 個相同伺服器、無限緩衝）　$a=\lambda/\mu$，$\rho=a/c$，穩態條件 $\rho<1$（即 $a<c$）。到達時必須等待的機率就是 3.4.2 的 Erlang C，$C(c,a)$：

$$L_q=C(c,a)\,\frac{\rho}{1-\rho},\quad W_q=\frac{L_q}{\lambda}=\frac{C(c,a)}{c\mu-\lambda},\quad W=W_q+\frac{1}{\mu},\quad L=L_q+a$$

**M/M/1/K**（單一伺服器、系統最多容納 $K$ 個，滿了就丟棄）　因為容量有限，任何 $\rho=\lambda/\mu$ 都有穩態，不需要 $\rho<1$。當 $\rho\ne1$：

$$P_n=\frac{(1-\rho)\rho^n}{1-\rho^{K+1}}\ (0\le n\le K),\qquad P_K=\frac{(1-\rho)\rho^K}{1-\rho^{K+1}}$$

$$L=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}-\frac{(K+1)\rho^{K+1}}{1-\rho^{K+1}}$$

當 $\rho=1$ 時每個狀態機率相等，$P_K=1/(K+1)$，$L=K/2$。有效到達率 $\lambda_{\text{eff}}=\lambda(1-P_K)$，伺服器利用率是 $1-P_0=\lambda_{\text{eff}}/\mu$（不是 $\rho$），$W=L/\lambda_{\text{eff}}$，$L_q=L-(1-P_0)$。

#### 算例

**算例 1：M/M/1 與 M/M/1/K**

已知：某輸出埠平均每秒到達 $\lambda=800$ 個封包，平均每秒可送出 $\mu=1000$ 個（假設值；卜瓦松到達、指數服務時間）。

（a）緩衝區視為無限：
- $\rho=800/1000=0.8<1$，有穩態。
- $L=0.8/0.2=4$ 個；$L_q=0.64/0.2=3.2$ 個。
- $W=1/(1000-800)=0.005$ 秒＝5 ms；$W_q=0.8/200=0.004$ 秒＝4 ms。
- Little 定理檢查：$\lambda W=800\times0.005=4=L$；$\lambda W_q=800\times0.004=3.2=L_q$；$W-W_q=1$ ms＝$1/\mu$。全部吻合。

（b）系統容量 $K=5$（1 個在傳送、4 個在等）：
- $\rho^5=0.32768$，$\rho^6=0.262144$。
- $P_5=0.2\times0.32768/(1-0.262144)=0.065536/0.737856=0.0888$，丟棄率約 8.88%。
- $P_0=0.2/0.737856=0.2711$，利用率 $1-P_0=0.7289$。
- $L=4-6\times0.262144/0.737856=4-2.1317=1.868$ 個。
- $\lambda_{\text{eff}}=800\times(1-0.0888)=728.9$ 個/秒；$W=1.868/728.9=0.002563$ 秒＝2.563 ms。
- $L_q=1.868-0.7289=1.139$ 個；$W_q=1.139/728.9=1.563$ ms。
- 檢查：$W-W_q=1.000$ ms＝$1/\mu$；$\lambda_{\text{eff}}/\mu=0.7289=1-P_0$。吻合。
- 合理性：緩衝區變小，延遲從 5 ms 降到 2.56 ms，代價是 8.88% 的封包被丟棄，這是延遲與丟包之間的取捨。把 $K$ 加大到 10，丟棄率降到 2.35%，$W$ 回升到 3.80 ms。

**算例 2：M/M/c**

已知：$c=2$ 個處理單元，每個 $\mu=1000$ 個/秒，總到達率 $\lambda=1600$ 個/秒（假設值）。

- $a=1600/1000=1.6$ Erl，$\rho=1.6/2=0.8<1$。
- Erlang B 遞迴：$B(1)=1.6/2.6=0.6154$；$B(2)=1.6\times0.6154/(2+1.6\times0.6154)=0.9846/2.9846=0.3299$。
- $C(2,1.6)=2\times0.3299/(2-1.6\times0.6701)=0.6598/0.9278=0.7111$。
- $L_q=0.7111\times0.8/0.2=2.844$ 個；$W_q=2.844/1600=1.778$ ms。
- $W=1.778+1=2.778$ ms；$L=2.844+1.6=4.444$ 個。
- Little 定理檢查：$\lambda W=1600\times0.002778=4.444=L$，吻合。
- 合理性：兩個各自獨立的 M/M/1（各收 800 個/秒）的 $W$ 是 5 ms；共用佇列降到 2.78 ms；若換成一個速度 2 倍的單一伺服器（$\mu=2000$），$W=1/(2000-1600)=2.5$ ms。資源合併共用比分開好，這和 Erlang B 的中繼效率是同一個道理。

#### 常見錯誤

1. **$L$ 與 $L_q$、$W$ 與 $W_q$ 混用**：題目問「等待時間」是 $W_q$，問「停留時間、延遲」通常是 $W$，兩者差一個服務時間 $1/\mu$。
2. **$\rho\ge1$ 還套無限緩衝的公式**：會得到負數。先檢查穩態條件。
3. **M/M/1/K 用 $\lambda$ 而不是 $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ 套 Little 定理**：被丟棄的封包沒有進入系統，不該計入。
4. **$K$ 的意思弄錯**：$K$ 是系統容量，包含正在服務的那一個；等待位置只有 $K-1$ 個。

#### 和其他小節的關係

M/M/c 的等待機率就是 3.4.2 的 Erlang C；M/M/c/c 的阻塞機率是 Erlang B。本節的 $W_q$ 是 3.4.5 四種延遲中的排隊延遲，$1/\mu$ 對應傳輸延遲。$a=\lambda/\mu$ 與 3.4.1 的話務量是同一個量。

**出處**　Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1: Theory*，生滅過程與 M/M/1、M/M/m、M/M/1/K 系統；Bertsekas & Gallager, *Data Networks*，延遲模型一章（Little 定理、M/M/1、M/M/m 及有限容量系統）。

#### 單題：Little's law

**3.4.3G001**　An edge-router output port is observed in steady state. A monitoring probe reports that on average 18 packets are in the port (waiting plus being transmitted), and that the port carries 12,000 packets/s with no packet loss. Using Little's law, determine the mean time a packet spends in the port, in ms, rounded to three decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　1.500

**詳解**　Little's law: $L = \lambda W$, valid for any queueing system in steady state.
W = L / λ = 18 / 12 000 = 0.0015 s = 1.500 ms.
\boxed{1.500}

**出處**　J. D. C. Little, "A Proof for the Queuing Formula: L = λW," *Operations Research*, 1961; D. Bertsekas and R. Gallager, *Data Networks* (Little's theorem).

**3.4.3G002**　A core-network signalling node processes 450 registration transactions per second in steady state, with no rejections. The mean time a transaction spends in the node (queueing plus processing) is 36 ms, and the mean processing time once a processor picks the transaction up is 8 ms. Determine the mean number of transactions waiting in the queue (not being processed), rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　12.60

**詳解**　Step 1: the time in the node is waiting plus processing, so $W_q = W - 1/\mu$ = 36 − 8 = 28 ms = 0.028 s.
Step 2: Little's law applied to the queue only: $L_q = \lambda W_q$ = 450 × 0.028 = 12.60.
Check: number in processing = λ × 8 ms = 3.60; total L = λW = 450 × 0.036 = 16.20 = 12.60 + 3.60.
\boxed{12.60}

**出處**　L. Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1: Theory* (Little's result $L_q = \lambda W_q$, $W = W_q + \bar{x}$).

</details>

**3.4.3G003**　A small-cell gateway receives 2400 packets/s. Because its buffer is finite, 1.2 % of arriving packets are dropped on arrival and never enter the system. The mean number of packets in the gateway is 6.5. Assuming steady state, determine the mean time an accepted packet spends in the gateway, in ms, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　2.74

**詳解**　Step 1: only accepted packets enter the system, so Little's law must use the effective arrival rate: λ_eff = 2400 × (1 − 0.012) = 2371.2 packets/s.
Step 2: W = L / λ_eff = 6.5 / 2371.2 = 0.0027412 s = 2.7412 ms ≈ 2.74 ms.
(Using 2400 packets/s instead would give 2.708 ms, which is wrong because dropped packets spend no time in the system.)
\boxed{2.74}

**出處**　D. Bertsekas and R. Gallager, *Data Networks* (Little's theorem applied to systems with blocking: use the accepted arrival rate).

</details>

Assuming steady state, use Little's law to determine the mean rate at which new sessions are set up, in sessions per second, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**3.4.3G005**　A flow of 800 packets/s passes in sequence through three network nodes without any loss. In steady state the mean numbers of packets inside the nodes (queued plus in service) are 2.4, 1.1 and 0.5. In addition, the links between the nodes add a fixed total propagation delay of 2 ms. Determine the mean end-to-end delay of a packet, in ms, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　7.00

**詳解**　Step 1: Little's law holds for the three nodes taken together as one system, and the flow through each is 800 packets/s. Total mean number in the nodes = 2.4 + 1.1 + 0.5 = 4.0.
Step 2: mean time in the nodes = 4.0 / 800 = 0.005 s = 5.00 ms.
Step 3: add the propagation delay (no queueing involved): 5.00 + 2 = 7.00 ms.
\boxed{7.00}

**3.4.3G008**　An M/M/1 queue has an arrival rate of 600 customers/s and, in steady state, a mean of 3 customers in the system. Determine the mean waiting time in the queue (excluding service), in ms, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　3.75

**詳解**　Step 1: from $L = \rho/(1-\rho)$, ρ = L/(1+L) = 3/4 = 0.75.

**3.4.3G011**　Packets with exponentially distributed length (mean 1000 bytes) arrive as a Poisson process at 2000 packets/s to a link modelled as M/M/1. <details><summary>看答案</summary>

**3.4.3S003**　The probe vendor says Little's law cannot be applied because UPF traffic is bursty and not Poisson. Is that right?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　No. L = λW holds for any arrival process, any service-time distribution and any service order, as long as the system is in steady state (or the averages are taken over a long window that starts and ends with similar occupancy) and L, λ and W refer to the same system boundary. Poisson arrivals are needed for the M/M/1 formulas, not for Little's law.

**詳解**　Little's proof is a sample-path (counting) argument: the area under the number-in-system curve equals the sum of the times spent by all customers, so no distribution is assumed.

**出處**　J. D. C. Little, "A Proof for the Queuing Formula: L = λW," *Operations Research*, 1961.

</details>

**3.4.3S004**　During another window the occupancy rose steadily from 10 to 400 packets. Why should the averages from that window not be fed into Little's law?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Because the system was not in steady state: arrivals exceeded departures for the whole window, so the backlog was growing. The forwarded rate (departures) differs from the arrival rate, and the average occupancy depends on the window length. W computed as L/λ would describe neither the packets that left nor those still queued. Little's law needs a stationary period (or a window whose start and end occupancy are negligible compared with the totals).

**詳解**　In a growing queue, arrivals − departures = 400 − 10 = 390 packets accumulated; the sample-path identity then has a boundary term that does not vanish.

**出處**　D. Bertsekas and R. Gallager, *Data Networks* (Little's theorem and its conditions).

</details>

**Scenario | Backbone link during a live-streaming event**

**3.4.3S056**　Under normal load, what is the mean waiting time in the queue (excluding transmission), in µs, rounded to two decimal places? Use the M/M/1 model.

**答案**　56.67 µs

**詳解**　ρ = 85 000/100 000 = 0.85 < 1.

**3.4.3S057**　Why does the steady-state mean become a poor description of the link as ρ approaches 1, even if ρ stays just below 1?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Because both the mean and the variability of the queue grow like 1/(1 − ρ), and the time the queue needs to settle into its steady state also grows sharply (roughly like 1/(1 − ρ)²). Near ρ = 1 a measurement window of minutes may never reach steady state, and short traffic bursts produce queue excursions far above the mean; percentiles and transient behaviour matter more than the mean.

**詳解**　M/M/1: E[N] = ρ/(1 − ρ), Var[N] = ρ/(1 − ρ)².

**出處**　L. Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1: Theory* (M/M/1 system; transient behaviour).

</details>

**3.4.3S058**　During the event the arrival rate exceeds the link rate. Assuming an unlimited buffer and that the queue is negligible when the event starts, how large does the backlog become after the 10 minutes, and how long would a packet arriving at that moment wait?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　About 2.4 million packets, i.e. about 24 s of waiting. There is no steady state; the backlog grows on average by λ − μ = 4000 packets/s. Real routers would drop packets long before this, so the event must be handled by capacity or traffic engineering, not by buffering.

**詳解**　Backlog ≈ (104 000 − 100 000) × 600 s = 2.4×10⁶ packets; drain time = 2.4×10⁶/100 000 = 24 s.

**3.4.3S062**　What does each symbol in "M/M/2" assume here, and if each job took exactly 50 ms (no randomness), would the mean wait be higher or lower than 64.3 ms?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　First M: Poisson arrivals (exponential inter-arrival times); second M: exponential service times; 2: two parallel servers; capacity unlimited and FCFS by default. **詳解**　Waiting time grows with the variance of the service time; for one server W_q(M/D/1) = ½ W_q(M/M/1).

**3.4.3S063**　The site software is changed so that a job is rejected when 10 jobs are already present. Which model now applies, and which of the M/M/2 results remain valid?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　M/M/2/10 (finite capacity 10). The Erlang C results and L_q = C·ρ/(1 − ρ) no longer apply; P_n must be recomputed over n = 0 … 10, and Little's law must use λ_eff = λ(1 − P₁₀). **詳解**　Finite capacity truncates the birth–death chain; stability is then automatic, and the normalization changes.

**出處**　L. Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1: Theory* (M/M/m/K systems).
