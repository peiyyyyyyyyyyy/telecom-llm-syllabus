## 正文

In any wireless system the quality of a received data stream is ultimately limited by the ratio of the useful signal power to the power of unwanted disturbances. Two closely related metrics are used:

* **Signal‑to‑Noise Ratio (SNR)** – compares the received signal power to the thermal‑noise power that is present in the receiver front‑end.  
* **Signal‑to‑Interference‑plus‑Noise Ratio (SINR)** – extends SNR by also accounting for co‑channel or adjacent‑channel interference that arrives at the receiver together with the desired signal.

Both quantities dictate the achievable modulation order, coding rate, and consequently the bit‑error rate (BER) or packet error rate (PER). Network planners use SNR/SINR maps to dimension cell radii, to set transmit powers, and to allocate resources in dense deployments such as 5G NR or LTE‑Advanced.

---

### 1. Definitions and basic formulas  

1. **Linear SNR**  

$$
\mathrm{SNR}_{\text{lin}}=\frac{P_{\text{s}}}{P_{\text{n}}}
$$

where  

* $P_{\text{s}}$ – received signal power (W).  
* $P_{\text{n}}$ – total noise power in the receiver bandwidth (W).

2. **SNR in decibels**  

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{P_{\text{s}}}{P_{\text{n}}}\right)}
$$

3. **Thermal noise power**  

$$
P_{\text{n,\,thermal}} = k\,T\,B
$$

* $k = 1.380\,648\,52\times10^{-23}\,\text{J/K}$ – Boltzmann constant.  
* $T$ – noise temperature (K), typically $290\;\text{K}$ for room temperature.  
* $B$ – receiver bandwidth (Hz).

4. **Effective noise power with a receiver noise figure (NF)**  

$$
P_{\text{n}} = kTB \; 10^{\frac{\text{NF}}{10}}
$$

* $\text{NF}$ – noise figure (dB), a measure of how much the receiver degrades the thermal noise.

5. **Linear SINR**  

$$
\mathrm{SINR}_{\text{lin}} = \frac{P_{\text{s}}}{P_{\text{n}}+P_{\text{i}}}
$$

* $P_{\text{i}}$ – total interference power (W) from all unwanted transmitters that fall inside the receiver’s band.

6. **SINR in decibels**  

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{SINR}_{\text{dB}} = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{P_{\text{s}}}{P_{\text{n}}+P_{\text{i}}}\right)}
$$

All power quantities appearing in the logarithmic expressions must be expressed in the same linear units (W or mW). When powers are given in dBm, conversion to linear scale uses  

$$
P_{\text{lin}}[\text{mW}] = 10^{\frac{P_{\text{dBm}}}{10}}.
$$

---

### 2. Worked example  

A small‑cell base station transmits at $P_{\text{t}} = 20\;\text{dBm}$. The link budget is:

| Item                     | Value (dB) |
|--------------------------|------------|
| Transmit antenna gain $G_{\text{t}}$ | $+5$ dBi |
| Receive antenna gain $G_{\text{r}}$  | $+5$ dBi |
| Path loss $L$          | $100$ dB |
| Receiver bandwidth $B$ | $10\;\text{MHz}$ |
| Receiver noise figure $\text{NF}$ | $5$ dB |
| Interfering signal power $\;P_{\text{i}}$ | $-90$ dBm |

**Step 1 – Received signal power**  

$$
P_{\text{s,dBm}} = P_{\text{t}} + G_{\text{t}} + G_{\text{r}} - L = 20 + 5 + 5 - 100 = -70\;\text{dBm}.
$$

**Step 2 – Thermal noise floor**  

$$
P_{\text{n,thermal}} = kTB = 1.38\times10^{-23}\times 290 \times 10^{7}
                \approx 4.0\times10^{-15}\;\text{W}.
$$

Convert to dBm:

$$
P_{\text{n,thermal,dBm}} = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4.0\times10^{-15}}{1\;\text{mW}}\right)
                         \approx -114\;\text{dBm}.
$$

**Step 3 – Effective noise (including NF)**  

$$
P_{\text{n,dBm}} = P_{\text{n,thermal,dBm}} + \text{NF}
                 = -114 + 5 = -109\;\text{dBm}.
$$

**Step 4 – SNR**  

$$
\mathrm{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = P_{\text{s,dBm}} - P_{\text{n,dBm}}
                        = -70 - (-109) = 39\;\text{dB}.
$$

**Step 5 – SINR**  

First convert all powers to linear mW:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\text{s}} &= 10^{-70/10} = 10^{-7}\;\text{mW}=1.0\times10^{-7}\;\text{mW},\\
P_{\text{n}} &= 10^{-109/10}=10^{-10.9}\;\text{mW}\approx1.26\times10^{-11}\;\text{mW},\\
P_{\text{i}} &= 10^{-90/10}=10^{-9}\;\text{mW}=1.0\times10^{-9}\;\text{mW}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Total disturbance $P_{\text{n}}+P_{\text{i}} \approx 1.0126\times10^{-9}\;\text{mW}$.

$$
\mathrm{SINR}_{\text{lin}} = \frac{1.0\times10^{-7}}{1.0126\times10^{-9}}
                         \approx 98.8,
\qquad
\mathrm{SINR}_{\text{dB}} = 10\log_{10}(98.8) \approx 20\;\text{dB}.
$$

**Interpretation** – Even though the pure SNR is 39 dB, the presence of a –90 dBm interferer reduces the usable SINR to about 20 dB, which may force the system to drop to a lower‑order modulation.

---

### 3. Practical notes and common pitfalls  

| Pitfall | Why it hurts | Correct practice |
|---------|--------------|------------------|
| **Mixing linear and dB values** (e.g., adding dBm directly to a linear‑scale noise figure) | Logarithmic addition is invalid; results are meaningless. | Convert every quantity to the same domain (linear W or mW) before arithmetic, then back to dB for reporting. |
| **Using $20\log_{10}$ instead of $10\log_{10}$ for power ratios** | $20\log_{10}$ applies to voltage or field‑strength ratios, not power. | Remember SNR and SINR are power ratios → use $10\log_{10}$. |
| **Neglecting bandwidth in the noise calculation** | Thermal noise is proportional to $B$; ignoring it over‑estimates SNR in narrowband systems. | Always compute $P_{\text{n}} = kTB$ with the exact receiver bandwidth. |
| **Omitting the receiver noise figure** | The front‑end adds extra noise; ignoring NF yields an optimistic SNR. | Add NF in dB after the thermal‑noise calculation, or multiply by $10^{\text{NF}/10}$ in linear form. |
| **Treating interference as “noise” only when it is far below the noise floor** | In dense deployments interference often dominates; treating it as negligible leads to under‑dimensioned link budgets. | Include all significant interferers in $P_{\text{i}}$ and compute SINR, not just SNR. |

Finally, remember that regulatory documents (e.g., 3GPP TS 36.133) specify reference noise temperatures and typical NF values for different device classes; using those standards ensures that your SNR/SINR calculations are comparable across vendors and studies.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following expressions correctly defines the linear Signal‑to‑Interference‑plus‑Noise Ratio (SINR)?

- A. $\displaystyle \frac{P_{\text{s}}}{P_{\text{n}}-P_{\text{i}}}$
- B. $\displaystyle \frac{P_{\text{s}}+P_{\text{i}}}{P_{\text{n}}}$
- C. $\displaystyle \frac{P_{\text{s}}}{P_{\text{n}}+P_{\text{i}}}$
- D. $\displaystyle \frac{P_{\text{s}}}{\max(P_{\text{n}},P_{\text{i}})}$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。SINR compares the desired signal power to the *sum* of noise and interference powers.

</details>

**2.** A base station transmits 23 dBm with a 5 dBi antenna. The receiver has a 2 dBi antenna, path loss of 95 dB, bandwidth 10 MHz, receiver NF = 4 dB, and an interfering signal of –85 dBm. Compute the SINR in dB at the receiver.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Received signal power:  
   $P_{\text{s,dBm}} = 23 + 5 + 2 - 95 = -65\;\text{dBm}$  
2. Thermal noise for 10 MHz: $P_{\text{n,thermal,dBm}} \approx -114\;\text{dBm}$  
3. Effective noise with NF: $P_{\text{n,dBm}} = -114 + 4 = -110\;\text{dBm}$  
4. Convert to linear mW:  
   $P_{\text{s}} = 10^{-65/10}=10^{-6.5}=3.1623\times10^{-7}\;\text{mW}$  
   $P_{\text{n}} = 10^{-110/10}=10^{-11}=1.0\times10^{-11}\;\text{mW}$  
   $P_{\text{i}} = 10^{-85/10}=10^{-8.5}=3.1623\times10^{-9}\;\text{mW}$  
5. Total disturbance: $P_{\text{n}}+P_{\text{i}} = 1.0\times10^{-11}+3.1623\times10^{-9}
   = 3.1723\times10^{-9}\;\text{mW}$  
6. Linear SINR: $\displaystyle \frac{3.1623\times10^{-7}}{3.1723\times10^{-9}}\approx 99.7$  
7. SINR in dB: $10\log_{10}(99.7) \approx 20\;\text{dB}$  

$\boxed{20}$

</details>

**3.** A user equipment receives a signal of –75 dBm. The receiver bandwidth is 20 MHz, noise temperature 290 K, and receiver NF is 6 dB. Assuming no interference, compute the SNR in dB.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Thermal noise: $P_{\text{n,thermal}} = kTB = 1.38\times10^{-23}\times290\times20\times10^{6}
   \approx 8.0\times10^{-15}\;\text{W}$  
   Convert to dBm: $P_{\text{n,thermal,dBm}} = 10\log_{10}(8.0\times10^{-12}) \approx -111.07\;\text{dBm}$  
2. Effective noise with NF: $P_{\text{n,dBm}} = -111.07 + 6 = -105.07\;\text{dBm}$  
3. SNR (dB): $P_{\text{s,dBm}} - P_{\text{n,dBm}} = -75 - (-105.07) = 30.07\;\text{dB}$  

$\boxed{30.07}$

</details>
