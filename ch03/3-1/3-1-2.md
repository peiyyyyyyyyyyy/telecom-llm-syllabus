## 正文

In any wireless link the transmitted electromagnetic wave spreads out as it travels from the transmitter (Tx) to the receiver (Rx). Even in an ideal, unobstructed environment the power reaching the Rx is reduced simply because the same amount of energy is distributed over a larger spherical surface. This reduction is called *free‑space path loss* (FSPL). FSPL is the baseline attenuation against which all additional losses (building penetration, foliage, rain, etc.) are measured, so a precise understanding of it is essential for link‑budget calculations, cell‑planning, and the design of satellite or terrestrial point‑to‑point systems.

---

### 1. Fundamental definition  

Consider an isotropic radiator that emits a total radiated power $P_{\text{t}}$ (in watts). At a distance $d$ (metres) the power density $S$ (W·m$^{-2}$) on the surface of a sphere of radius $d$ is  

$$
S = \frac{P_{\text{t}}}{4\pi d^{2}} .
$$

If the Rx antenna has an effective aperture $A_{\text{e}}$ (m$^2$), the received power is  

$$
P_{\text{r}} = S \, A_{\text{e}} .
$$

Using the relationship between effective aperture and antenna gain $G_{\text{r}}$,

$$
A_{\text{e}} = \frac{G_{\text{r}} \lambda^{2}}{4\pi},
$$

where $\lambda$ (metres) is the wavelength, the *Friis transmission equation* for free space becomes  

$$
\boxed{P_{\text{r}} = P_{\text{t}} \, G_{\text{t}} \, G_{\text{r}} \left(\frac{\lambda}{4\pi d}\right)^{2}} \tag{1}
$$

with $G_{\text{t}}$ the Tx antenna gain (dimensionless, often expressed in dBi).  

The *path loss* $L$ is defined as the ratio of transmitted to received power (linear scale),

$$
L = \frac{P_{\text{t}}}{P_{\text{r}}} = \frac{1}{G_{\text{t}} G_{\text{r}}}\left(\frac{4\pi d}{\lambda}\right)^{2}.
$$

Expressed in decibels (dB),

$$
\boxed{L_{\text{dB}} = 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi d}{\lambda}\right) - 10\log_{10}(G_{\text{t}} G_{\text{r}})} \tag{2}
$$

If isotropic antennas are assumed ($G_{\text{t}} = G_{\text{r}} = 1$), the second term vanishes and we obtain the classic *free‑space path loss* expression:

$$
\boxed{L_{\text{FS}}(dB) = 20\log_{10}(d) + 20\log_{10}(f) + 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi}{c}\right)} \tag{3}
$$

where  

* $d$ – separation distance (metres)  
* $f$ – carrier frequency (hertz)  
* $c = 2.9979\times10^{8}$ m·s$^{-1}$ – speed of light  

Because the constant term $20\log_{10}(4\pi/c) = -147.55$ dB, a convenient engineering form that uses kilometres and megahertz is  

$$
\boxed{L_{\text{FS}}(dB) = 32.44 + 20\log_{10}(d_{\text{km}}) + 20\log_{10}(f_{\text{MHz}})} \tag{4}
$$

Equation (4) is widely used in link‑budget spreadsheets and in the 3GPP *TR 36.814* reference for LTE air‑interface simulations.

---

### 2. Worked example  

*Problem:* A point‑to‑point microwave link operates at $f = 2.4$ GHz with isotropic antennas separated by $d = 10$ m. The Tx power is $P_{\text{t}} = 1$ W (i.e., $30$ dBm). Compute (a) the free‑space path loss in dB, and (b) the received power in dBm.

*Solution:*  

1. Convert the distance to kilometres and the frequency to megahertz:  

$$
d_{\text{km}} = 10\text{ m} = 0.01\text{ km},\qquad f_{\text{MHz}} = 2400\text{ MHz}.
$$

2. Apply (4):

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{\text{FS}} &= 32.44 + 20\log_{10}(0.01) + 20\log_{10}(2400)\\
&= 32.44 + 20(-2) + 20\log_{10}(2.4\times10^{3})\\
&= 32.44 - 40 + 20\bigl(\log_{10}2.4 + 3\bigr)\\
&= 32.44 - 40 + 20(0.3802 + 3)\\
&= 32.44 - 40 + 20(3.3802)\\
&= 32.44 - 40 + 67.604\\
&\approx 60.04\;\text{dB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

3. Received power (isotropic gains = 0 dBi):

$$
P_{\text{r}}(\text{dBm}) = P_{\text{t}}(\text{dBm}) - L_{\text{FS}} = 30\;\text{dBm} - 60.04\;\text{dB} \approx -30.0\;\text{dBm}.
$$

*Result:* FSPL ≈ 60 dB; received power ≈ −30 dBm.

---

### 3. Practical notes & common pitfalls  

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | How to avoid |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| **Unit mismatch** – using km for $d$ in (3) or Hz for $f$ in (4) | The logarithm argument must be dimensionless; the constant 32.44 only works with km / MHz. | Always convert to the unit system that matches the constant, or use the full SI form (3). |
| **Omitting the $4\pi$ factor** | Some texts present a simplified $L = (d/\lambda)^2$ which is correct only when the antenna gains already include the $4\pi$ term. | Keep the full $(4\pi d/\lambda)^2$ expression unless you explicitly account for it in the gains. |
| **Mixing linear and dB values** | Adding a linear gain (e.g., 3) to a dB loss (e.g., 100 dB) yields nonsense. | Convert all quantities to the same domain (all dB or all linear) before arithmetic. |
| **Neglecting antenna gains** | Assuming isotropic antennas when real antennas have >0 dBi leads to overly pessimistic link budgets. | Insert the Tx/Rx gains (in dBi) as additive terms: $P_{\text{r}} = P_{\text{t}} + G_{\text{t}} + G_{\text{r}} - L_{\text{FS}}$. |
| **Rounding the constant** | Using 32.4 instead of 32.44 introduces a small error that can accumulate in large‑scale planning. | Keep at least two decimal places for the constant; most calculators output it automatically. |

A final tip: when performing a quick sanity check, remember that **doubling the distance adds 6 dB** to the free‑space loss (because $20\log_{10}(2)=6.02$). Similarly, **increasing the frequency by a factor of ten adds 20 dB**. These rules of thumb help spot unreasonable numbers before a full calculation is run.

## 練習題

**1.** In the FSPL formula $L_{\text{FS}}(dB)=32.44+20\log_{10}(d_{\text{km}})+20\log_{10}(f_{\text{MHz}})$, which term directly reflects the carrier frequency?

- A. The constant 32.44 dB
- B. $20\log_{10}(d_{\text{km}})$
- C. $20\log_{10}(f_{\text{MHz}})$
- D. None of the above

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The frequency dependence appears in the term $20\log_{10}(f_{\text{MHz}})$; higher frequency yields larger loss.

</details>

**2.** If the separation distance in a free‑space link is increased from 5 km to 20 km, how much does the FSPL increase (approximately)?

- A. 3 dB
- B. 6 dB
- C. 9 dB
- D. 12 dB

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：D。FSPL scales with $20\log_{10}(d)$; a factor of 4 increase adds $20\log_{10}(4)=12.04$ dB.

</details>

**3.** Two identical antennas each have a gain of 3 dBi. Compared with isotropic antennas (0 dBi), the received power in a free‑space link will be:

- A. 3 dB higher
- B. 6 dB higher
- C. 3 dB lower
- D. Unchanged

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Gains add in dB: $G_{\text{t}}+G_{\text{r}}=3+3=6$ dB, raising received power by 6 dB.

</details>

**4.** Compute the free‑space path loss (in dB) for a carrier frequency of 5 GHz over a distance of 3 km. Use the standard formula with km and MHz units.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Convert frequency: $f_{\text{MHz}} = 5000$ MHz.  
2. Use (4): $L_{\text{FS}} = 32.44 + 20\log_{10}(3) + 20\log_{10}(5000)$.  
3. $20\log_{10}(3) = 9.542$ dB.  
4. $\log_{10}(5000)=3.69897$; $20\log_{10}(5000)=73.979$ dB.  
5. Sum: $32.44 + 9.542 + 73.979 = 115.961$ dB.  
$\boxed{115.96}$

</details>

**5.** A link operates at 900 MHz with Tx power $P_{\text{t}} = 20$ dBm, Tx antenna gain $G_{\text{t}} = 2$ dBi, Rx antenna gain $G_{\text{r}} = 5$ dBi, and separation $d = 2$ km. Determine the received power in dBm using free‑space loss.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Frequency in MHz: $f_{\text{MHz}} = 900$.  
2. FSPL: $L_{\text{FS}} = 32.44 + 20\log_{10}(2) + 20\log_{10}(900)$.  
   - $20\log_{10}(2)=6.0206$ dB.  
   - $\log_{10}(900)=2.95424$; $20\log_{10}(900)=59.0848$ dB.  
   - Sum: $L_{\text{FS}} = 32.44 + 6.0206 + 59.0848 = 97.5454$ dB.  
3. Received power: $P_{\text{r}} = P_{\text{t}} + G_{\text{t}} + G_{\text{r}} - L_{\text{FS}}$.  
   - $20 + 2 + 5 - 97.5454 = -70.5454$ dBm.  
$\boxed{-70.55}$

</details>
