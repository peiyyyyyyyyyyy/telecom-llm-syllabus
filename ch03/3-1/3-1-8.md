## 正文

In modern wired and wireless networks almost every analog waveform—voice, video, sensor outputs—must be represented digitally before it can be processed, stored or transmitted over packet‑switched or circuit‑switched links. The three operations that enable this conversion are **sampling**, **quantization**, and **coding** (most commonly PCM). Their performance determines the required channel bandwidth, the achievable signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR), and ultimately the quality of service perceived by the end‑user.

---

### 1. Sampling  

Sampling is the process of measuring a continuous‑time signal $x(t)$ at discrete instants $t=nT_s$, where $T_s$ is the **sampling interval** and $f_s=1/T_s$ the **sampling frequency** (Hz).  

The **Nyquist‑Shannon sampling theorem** states that a band‑limited signal with highest frequency component $B$ (Hz) can be perfectly reconstructed from its samples **iff**  

$$
f_s \ge 2B \qquad\text{(Nyquist rate)} .
$$

If $f_s$ is lower, higher‑frequency components **alias** into the baseband, producing irreversible distortion. In practice an **anti‑aliasing filter** (low‑pass with cutoff $B$) is placed before the sampler to enforce the band‑limited assumption.

---

### 2. Quantization  

Each sample value, which is still continuous in amplitude, is mapped to one of $2^{L}$ discrete levels, where $L$ is the **number of bits per sample**. For a uniform mid‑rise quantizer spanning a voltage range $[V_{\min},V_{\max}]$ the **quantization step size** is  

$$
\Delta = \frac{V_{\max}-V_{\min}}{2^{L}} \quad\text{[V]} .
$$

The quantization error $e_q$ is the difference between the original sample and its quantized representation. Assuming the error is uniformly distributed in $[-\Delta/2,\Delta/2]$, its **mean‑square value** (quantization noise power) is  

$$
P_q = \frac{\Delta^{2}}{12}\quad\text{[W]}\; .
$$

The **signal‑to‑quantization‑noise ratio (SQNR)** for a full‑scale sinusoid of peak amplitude $A = (V_{\max}-V_{\min})/2$ becomes  

$$
\mathrm{SQNR} = \frac{A^{2}}{2P_q}= \frac{3}{2}\,2^{2L} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{SQNR}_{\mathrm{dB}} = 6.02\,L + 1.76\;\text{dB}.
$$

Thus each additional bit improves SQNR by roughly **6 dB**.

---

### 3. Pulse Code Modulation (PCM)  

PCM concatenates sampling, quantization and binary **coding**. The **bit rate** required for PCM is  

$$
R_b = f_s \, L \quad\text{[bits s}^{-1}\text{]} .
$$

If the binary symbols are transmitted using a baseband **NRZ (non‑return‑to‑zero)** line code, the required **minimum Nyquist bandwidth** is approximately  

$$
B_{\text{PCM}} \approx \frac{R_b}{2} \quad\text{[Hz]} .
$$

More spectrally efficient line codes (e.g., Manchester, duobinary) or passband modulation alter this factor, but the relationship above provides a quick engineering estimate.

---

### 4. Worked Example  

A telephony system must digitise a voice signal whose useful bandwidth is $B = 3.4\;\text{kHz}$. The design follows the ITU‑G.711 standard:  

* Sampling frequency $f_s = 8\;\text{kHz}$ (slightly above Nyquist).  
* Quantizer: $L = 8$ bits, spanning $V_{\min}=-1\;\text{V}$ to $V_{\max}=+1\;\text{V}$.

**Step 1 – Quantization step**  

$$
\Delta = \frac{V_{\max}-V_{\min}}{2^{L}}
       = \frac{2\;\text{V}}{256}
       = 7.8125\times10^{-3}\;\text{V}.
$$

**Step 2 – Quantization noise power**  

$$
P_q = \frac{\Delta^{2}}{12}
    = \frac{(7.8125\times10^{-3})^{2}}{12}
    \approx 5.08\times10^{-6}\;\text{W}.
$$

**Step 3 – SQNR (dB)**  

$$
\mathrm{SQNR}_{\mathrm{dB}} = 6.02\,L + 1.76 = 6.02\times8 + 1.76 \approx 49.9\;\text{dB}.
$$

**Step 4 – PCM bit rate and bandwidth**  

$$
R_b = f_s L = 8000 \times 8 = 64\,000\;\text{bits s}^{-1}.
$$
$$
B_{\text{PCM}} \approx \frac{R_b}{2}=32\;\text{kHz}.
$$

Thus an 8‑kHz sampled, 8‑bit PCM stream needs roughly **64 kbps** and occupies about **32 kHz** of baseband spectrum.

---

### 5. Common Pitfalls and Practical Tips  

| Pitfall | Why it hurts | Remedy |
|---------|--------------|--------|
| **Sampling below $2B$** | Aliasing folds high‑frequency energy into the baseband, destroying intelligibility. | Always enforce the Nyquist condition; use a proper anti‑alias filter with a margin (e.g., 10 % excess). |
| **Ignoring quantizer overload** | If the signal exceeds $[V_{\min},V_{\max}]$, samples clip, causing large distortion not captured by SQNR formulas. | Choose a range that comfortably contains the expected peak‑to‑peak amplitude (e.g., 1.5–2 × nominal amplitude). |
| **Assuming quantization noise is white** | In low‑bit systems the error distribution deviates from uniform, especially for speech with non‑Gaussian statistics. | For high‑resolution (≥8 bits) the uniform model is adequate; otherwise perform empirical noise measurements. |
| **Counting only payload bits** | PCM frames often include synchronization, framing, and error‑correction bits, increasing the actual $R_b$. | Add overhead (typically 5‑10 %) when sizing channels. |
| **Mismatching line‑code bandwidth** | Using NRZ while the channel budget is based on a more efficient code leads to over‑provisioning. | Adjust $B_{\text{PCM}}$ according to the chosen line code or passband modulation factor. |

Understanding these relationships lets engineers trade off **sampling rate**, **resolution**, and **channel resources** to meet quality‑of‑service targets while respecting spectrum constraints.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following statements correctly describes the Nyquist sampling theorem?

- A. A signal can be perfectly reconstructed if the sampling frequency is at least equal to the signal’s highest frequency component.
- B. A signal can be perfectly reconstructed if the sampling frequency is at least twice the signal’s highest frequency component.
- C. A signal can be perfectly reconstructed only if the sampling frequency is greater than ten times the signal’s bandwidth.
- D. A signal can be perfectly reconstructed regardless of the sampling frequency if an anti‑alias filter is used.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The theorem requires $f_s \ge 2B$, where $B$ is the highest frequency (bandwidth) of the signal.

</details>

**2.** For a uniform mid‑rise quantizer with $L$ bits, the quantization step size $\Delta$ is given by:

- A. $\Delta = \dfrac{V_{\max}+V_{\min}}{2^{L}}$
- B. $\Delta = \dfrac{V_{\max}-V_{\min}}{2^{L-1}}$
- C. $\Delta = \dfrac{V_{\max}-V_{\min}}{2^{L}}$
- D. $\Delta = \dfrac{V_{\max}-V_{\min}}{2^{L}+1}$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The range is divided into $2^{L}$ equal intervals, giving $\Delta = (V_{\max}-V_{\min})/2^{L}$.

</details>

**3.** Increasing the number of quantization bits $L$ by one bit improves the SQNR by approximately:

- A. 3 dB
- B. 6 dB
- C. 9 dB
- D. 12 dB

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。SQNR$_{\text{dB}} = 6.02L + 1.76$, so each additional bit adds about 6 dB.

</details>

**4.** A voice channel has a useful bandwidth of $3\;\text{kHz}$. It is sampled at the Nyquist rate and quantized with $10$ bits per sample. What is the required PCM bit rate $R_b$ in bits per second?


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Nyquist sampling frequency: $f_s = 2B = 2 \times 3000 = 6000\;\text{Hz}$.  
2. Bits per sample: $L = 10$.  
3. Bit rate: $R_b = f_s \times L = 6000 \times 10 = 60000\;\text{bits/s}$.  
$\boxed{60000}$

</details>

**5.** A uniform quantizer spans $-1\;\text{V}$ to $+1\;\text{V}$ and uses $12$ bits. Compute the quantization step size $\Delta$ (in volts) and the quantization noise power $P_q$ (in watts). Give $P_q$ as the final answer.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

2. Number of levels: $2^{12}=4096$.  
3. Step size: $\Delta = 2/4096 = 0.00048828125\;\text{V}$.  
4. Quantization noise power: $P_q = \Delta^{2}/12 = (0.00048828125)^{2}/12$.  
5. Compute: $(0.00048828125)^{2}=2.384185791015625e-07$.  
6. Divide by 12: $P_q = 1.986821492512... \times 10^{-8}\;\text{W}$.  
$\boxed{1.9868214925e-08}$

</details>
