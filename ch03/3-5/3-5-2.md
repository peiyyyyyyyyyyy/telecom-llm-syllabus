# 3.5.2 機率與隨機變數

所屬：[3.5 基礎數理](../3-5.md)

## 正文

In any communication system, the quantities we observe—packet arrivals, bit errors, fading amplitudes, inter‑arrival times—are not deterministic. They fluctuate because of thermal noise, user behavior, propagation phenomena, and scheduling policies. Probability theory provides the language to model, analyse, and ultimately design systems that are robust to such uncertainty. A *random variable* (RV) is a measurable mapping from an underlying sample space (the set of all possible elementary outcomes) to the real numbers. By assigning probabilities to the values (or intervals of values) that the RV can take, we can quantify performance metrics such as outage probability, bit‑error rate (BER), or throughput.

---

### 1. Basic definitions  

*Sample space* $\Omega$: the set of all elementary outcomes (e.g., all possible sequences of received symbols).  

*Event* $A\subseteq\Omega$: a subset of outcomes; probability $P(A)$ satisfies $0\le P(A)\le 1$ and $P(\Omega)=1$.

*Random variable* $X:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$: assigns a real number $x$ to each outcome $\omega\in\Omega$.

*Probability mass function* (PMF) – for a **discrete** RV:  
$$p_X(x)=P\{X=x\},\qquad x\in\mathcal{X}$$  
where $\mathcal{X}$ is the countable support of $X$.  

*Probability density function* (PDF) – for a **continuous** RV:  
$$f_X(x)=\frac{d}{dx}F_X(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}$$  
with $F_X(x)$ the cumulative distribution function (CDF).  

*Cumulative distribution function* (CDF):  
$$F_X(x)=P\{X\le x\}=\int_{-\infty}^{x} f_X(t)\,dt\quad\text{(continuous)}$$  
or $F_X(x)=\sum_{t\le x}p_X(t)$ for discrete $X$.

All symbols are dimensionless unless otherwise stated; $x$ inherits the unit of the underlying physical quantity (e.g., seconds for inter‑arrival time, volts for amplitude).

---

### 2. Expectation, variance, and higher moments  

The **expected value** (mean) of $X$ is  
$$\mu_X = \mathbb{E}[X]=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}} x\,p_X(x), & X\ \text{discrete}\\[6pt]
\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,f_X(x)\,dx, & X\ \text{continuous}
\end{cases}$$  

The **variance** measures spread:  
$$\sigma_X^{2}= \operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb{E}\big[(X-\mu_X)^2\big]=\mathbb{E}[X^{2}] - \mu_X^{2}$$  

Higher‑order moments $\mathbb{E}[X^{k}]$ are useful for skewness, kurtosis, and for moment‑generating functions.

*Linearity*: for any constants $a,b$,
$$\mathbb{E}[aX+b]=a\,\mathbb{E}[X]+b.$$

If $X$ and $Y$ are **independent**, then  
$$\mathbb{E}[XY]=\mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y],\qquad\operatorname{Var}(X+Y)=\operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{Var}(Y).$$

---

### 3. Common distributions in telecom  

| Distribution | Typical use case | PMF / PDF | Parameters | Mean | Variance |
|--------------|------------------|-----------|------------|------|----------|
| Bernoulli $B(p)$ | Single bit error (0/1) | $p_X(1)=p$, $p_X(0)=1-p$ | $p\in[0,1]$ | $p$ | $p(1-p)$ |
| Binomial $Bin(n,p)$ | Number of errors in $n$ bits | $p_X(k)=\binom{n}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k}$ | $n\in\mathbb{N},p$ | $np$ | $np(1-p)$ |
| Poisson $\text{Poi}(\lambda)$ | Packet arrivals in a short interval | $p_X(k)=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}$ | $\lambda>0$ (mean arrivals) | $\lambda$ | $\lambda$ |
| Exponential $Exp(\lambda)$ | Inter‑arrival time of a Poisson process | $f_X(x)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x},\ x\ge0$ | $\lambda>0$ (rate) | $1/\lambda$ | $1/\lambda^{2}$ |
| Gaussian $N(\mu,\sigma^{2})$ | Thermal noise, fading (after Central Limit Theorem) | $f_X(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\!\big[-\frac{(x-\mu)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\big]$ | $\mu$, $\sigma^{2}>0$ | $\mu$ | $\sigma^{2}$ |

These models appear in 3GPP specifications (e.g., the Poisson traffic model in TS 36.331) and ITU‑R recommendations for channel modeling.

---

### 4. Worked example  

*Problem*: A base station observes packet arrivals that follow a Poisson process with average rate $\lambda = 2$ packets · ms$^{-1}$.  

1. What is the probability that exactly three packets arrive in a 1‑ms interval?  
2. What is the expected number of arrivals in that interval?  

*Solution*:  
For a Poisson process, the number of arrivals $N$ in a time window of length $T$ is $\text{Poi}(\lambda T)$.  
Here $T = 1\text{ ms}=10^{-3}\text{ s}$, but the rate is already per millisecond, so $\lambda T = 2\times1 = 2$.  

1. $$P\{N=3\}=e^{-\lambda T}\frac{(\lambda T)^{3}}{3!}=e^{-2}\frac{2^{3}}{6}=e^{-2}\frac{8}{6}= \frac{4}{3}e^{-2}\approx 0.180.$$

2. The mean of a Poisson RV equals its parameter: $\mathbb{E}[N]=\lambda T = 2$ packets.

Thus, in a typical 1‑ms slot we expect two packets, and the chance of seeing exactly three packets is about **18 %**.

---

### 5. Practical notes and common pitfalls  

| Pitfall | Why it happens | How to avoid it |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Confusing PMF and PDF** | Both are denoted $p_X$ or $f_X$ in textbooks. | Explicitly state “discrete” vs “continuous”; remember that a PDF can exceed 1 because it is a density, not a probability. |
| **Ignoring units in rate parameters** | $\lambda$ may be given per second, per millisecond, etc. | Convert the observation interval to the same time unit before plugging into formulas. |
| **Treating dependent variables as independent** | Independence is rarely guaranteed in network traffic (burstiness). | Verify independence (e.g., by checking autocorrelation) before applying $\operatorname{Var}(X+Y)=\operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{Var}(Y)$. |
| **Misapplying Bayes’ rule with continuous variables** | The denominator may be a probability density, not a probability. | Use the proper form: $f_{X|Y}(x|y)=\frac{f_{Y|X}(y|x)f_X(x)}{f_Y(y)}$, where $f_Y(y)=\int f_{Y|X}(y|x)f_X(x)dx$. |
| **Rounding intermediate results** | Small rounding errors can produce probabilities >1 or <0. | Keep full floating‑point precision until the final step; only round the final answer for reporting. |

Understanding probability and random variables equips telecom engineers to predict outage probabilities, dimension buffers, and evaluate link‑layer reliability—all essential for designing modern cellular and IoT networks.

## 練習題

**1.** If $N_1\sim\text{Poi}(\lambda_1)$ and $N_2\sim\text{Poi}(\lambda_2)$ are independent, what is the variance of $N=N_1+N_2$?

- A. $\lambda_1+\lambda_2$
- B. $\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2$
- C. $\sqrt{\lambda_1+\lambda_2}$
- D. $\lambda_1\lambda_2$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。The variance of a Poisson RV equals its mean; for independent sums variances add, giving $\lambda_1+\lambda_2$.

</details>

**2.** A Poisson arrival process has rate $\lambda = 5\ \text{packets/s}$. Find the probability that exactly three packets arrive during a 200 ms interval. (Answer is dimensionless probability.)


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Convert interval to seconds: $T = 200\text{ ms}=0.2\text{ s}$.  
2. Effective mean count: $\mu = \lambda T = 5 \times 0.2 = 1.0$.  
3. Poisson probability: $P\{N=3\}=e^{-\mu}\frac{\mu^{3}}{3!}=e^{-1}\frac{1^{3}}{6}= \frac{e^{-1}}{6}$.  
4. Numerically, $e^{-1}\approx0.367879$, so $P\approx0.367879/6\approx0.061313$.  
$\boxed{0.061313240}$

</details>

**3.** An exponential random variable $X$ models the inter‑arrival time of packets with rate $\lambda = 2\ \text{s}^{-1}$. What is the probability that the inter‑arrival time exceeds $1\ \text{s}$? (Answer is dimensionless probability.)


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

For $X\sim\text{Exp}(\lambda)$, $P\{X>t\}=e^{-\lambda t}$.  
With $t=1\text{ s}$ and $\lambda=2\ \text{s}^{-1}$:  
$P\{X>1\}=e^{-2\cdot1}=e^{-2}\approx0.135335$.  
$\boxed{0.135335283}$

</details>
