# 3.5.7 線性代數與矩陣：轉移矩陣、穩態向量、MIMO 通道矩陣

所屬：[3.5 基礎數理](../3-5.md)

## 正文

Linear algebra provides the language for describing how signals and probabilities evolve in communication networks. Three concrete matrix concepts appear repeatedly:

1. **Transition matrix** – the one‑step probability matrix of a discrete‑time Markov chain that models packet‑level state evolution (e.g., “idle”, “transmitting”, “collision”).  
2. **Steady‑state (stationary) vector** – a probability row vector that remains unchanged after multiplication by the transition matrix; it describes long‑run occupancy probabilities of the network states.  
3. **MIMO channel matrix** – a complex (or real) matrix **H** that maps the vector of transmitted symbols **x** to the received vector **y** in a multiple‑input multiple‑output (MIMO) link:  

$$
\mathbf{y}= \mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{x}+ \mathbf{n},
$$

where **n** is additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN).  

Understanding these matrices enables the design of scheduling policies, the evaluation of long‑term throughput, and the computation of channel capacity for spatial multiplexing.

---

### 1. Transition Matrix  

For a system with *S* discrete states, the **transition matrix** $\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{S\times S}$ contains the one‑step conditional probabilities  

$$
p_{ij}= \Pr\{X_{k+1}=j \mid X_{k}=i\},\qquad i,j\in\{1,\dots ,S\}.
$$

*Properties*  

* $0\le p_{ij}\le 1$ for all entries.  
* Each **row** sums to one: $\sum_{j=1}^{S}p_{ij}=1$.  Such a matrix is called **row‑stochastic**.  
* If the chain is *irreducible* and *aperiodic*, a unique steady‑state vector exists (see below).

The matrix form of the Chapman‑Kolmogorov equation for $k$ steps is  

$$
\mathbf{P}^{(k)} = \mathbf{P}^k .
$$

---

### 2. Steady‑State Vector  

A row vector $\boldsymbol{\pi} = [\pi_1,\dots ,\pi_S]$ is a **steady‑state vector** if  

$$
\boldsymbol{\pi}\,\mathbf{P}= \boldsymbol{\pi}, \qquad
\sum_{i=1}^{S}\pi_i = 1,\qquad \pi_i\ge 0.
$$

In matrix notation this is an eigenvalue problem  

$$
\boldsymbol{\pi}\,(\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{I}) = \mathbf{0},
$$

where $\mathbf{I}$ is the identity matrix and the eigenvalue associated with $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ is $\lambda = 1$.  

A common computational technique is to solve  

$$
(\mathbf{P}^\top - \mathbf{I})\boldsymbol{\pi}^\top = \mathbf{0},
$$

together with the normalisation constraint. The **power method** also converges to $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ for an aperiodic, irreducible chain:

$$
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{(n+1)} = \boldsymbol{\pi}^{(n)}\mathbf{P},\qquad
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{(0)}\text{ arbitrary probability vector}.
$$

---

### 3. MIMO Channel Matrix  

In a narrow‑band flat‑fading MIMO link with $N_t$ transmit and $N_r$ receive antennas, the channel is described by  

$$
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_r\times N_t},\qquad
h_{ij}= \text{complex gain from transmit antenna }j\text{ to receive antenna }i.
$$

Key linear‑algebraic concepts:

| Concept | Definition | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Rank** | $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{H})$ = dimension of column space. | Determines the number of independent data streams that can be sent simultaneously. |
| **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)** | $\mathbf{H}= \mathbf{U}\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\,\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}$ where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}= \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\dots ,\sigma_{\min(N_t,N_r)})$. | The singular values $\sigma_i$ quantify channel gains for the parallel eigen‑modes. |
| **Capacity** (assuming equal power allocation) | $$
C = \log_2\!\bigl(\det\bigl(\mathbf{I}_{N_r}+ \frac{P}{N_0}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}\bigr)\bigr)\;\text{[bits/s/Hz]},
$$ where $P$ is total transmit power (W) and $N_0$ is noise power (W). | Provides the theoretical upper bound on achievable spectral efficiency. |

---

### Worked Example – Steady‑State Distribution  

Consider a three‑state MAC protocol:  

*State 1*: idle, *State 2*: transmitting, *State 3*: collision.  

The one‑step transition matrix is  

$$
\mathbf{P}= \begin{bmatrix}
0.7 & 0.2 & 0.1\\
0.3 & 0.4 & 0.3\\
0.2 & 0.3 & 0.5
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Find the steady‑state vector $\boldsymbol{\pi}=[\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3]$.

**Solution**  

Solve $\boldsymbol{\pi}\mathbf{P}= \boldsymbol{\pi}$ and $\pi_1+\pi_2+\pi_3=1$.  

The linear system reduces to  

$$
\begin{cases}
3\pi_1-3\pi_2-2\pi_3=0,\\
-2\pi_1+6\pi_2-3\pi_3=0,\\
\pi_1+\pi_2+\pi_3=1.
\end{cases}
$$

From the first two equations we obtain  

$$
\pi_2 = \frac{13}{12}\pi_3,\qquad
\pi_1 = \frac{7}{4}\pi_3.
$$

Substituting into the normalisation condition gives  

$$
\frac{7}{4}\pi_3 + \frac{13}{12}\pi_3 + \pi_3 = 1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\pi_3 = \frac{6}{23}\approx 0.2609.
$$

Hence  

$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol{\pi}\approx[0.4565,\;0.2826,\;0.2609]}.
$$

Interpretation: in the long run the link is idle about 45 % of the time, successfully transmitting 28 %, and in collision 26 %.

---

### Worked Example – MIMO Capacity  

A 2 × 2 real MIMO channel is  

$$
\mathbf{H}= \begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0.5\\
0.3 & 1.2
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Assume total transmit power $P=10\;\text{W}$ and noise power $N_0=1\;\text{W}$. Compute the capacity.

1. Compute $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\!T}$:  

$$
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\!T}= \begin{bmatrix}
1.25 & 0.9\\
0.9 & 1.53
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

2. Form $\mathbf{I}+ \frac{P}{N_0}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\!T}= 
\begin{bmatrix}
13.5 & 9.0\\
9.0 & 16.3
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

3. Determinant: \(\det = 13.5\times16.3 - 9.0^2 = 139.05$.

4. Capacity:  

$$
C = \log_2(139.05) \approx 7.11\;\text{bits/s/Hz}.
$$

Thus the channel can theoretically support about **7.1 bits/s/Hz** with equal power allocation.

### Common Mistakes & Practical Notes  

| Mistake | Why it matters | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| **Treating a transition matrix as column‑stochastic** | Leads to solving the wrong eigenvalue problem; the steady‑state vector would be a **right** eigenvector instead of a left eigenvector. | Verify that each **row** sums to 1; use $\boldsymbol{\pi}\mathbf{P} = \boldsymbol{\pi}$. |
| **Neglecting the normalisation constraint** when solving $\boldsymbol{\pi}\mathbf{P} = \boldsymbol{\pi}$ | The homogeneous system yields a whole subspace; the resulting vector may not be a probability distribution. | Append $\sum_i \pi_i = 1$ as an extra equation or normalise the eigenvector after solving. |
| **Assuming full rank for any MIMO matrix** | In practice antenna correlation or line‑of‑sight can make $\mathbf{H}$ rank‑deficient, reducing spatial multiplexing gain. | Compute the rank or singular values; design precoders only on the non‑zero modes. |
| **Using the determinant formula for capacity with correlated noise** | The formula $C=\log_2\det(\mathbf{I}+ \frac{P}{N_0}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger})$ assumes white noise and equal power per antenna. | If noise covariance $\mathbf{R}_n\neq N_0\mathbf{I}$ or power allocation is non‑uniform, replace the term by $\mathbf{I}+ \mathbf{H}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}$ where $\mathbf{Q}$ is the transmit covariance matrix. |
| **Rounding intermediate results too early** | Small rounding errors can shift eigenvalues and produce an inaccurate steady‑state distribution, especially for nearly‑reducible chains. | Keep at least double‑precision (≈ 15 decimal digits) throughout calculations; round only the final answer. |

By mastering these matrix tools, telecom engineers can predict long‑term network performance, design robust scheduling algorithms, and exploit spatial dimensions in modern wireless standards.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following statements about a discrete‑time Markov chain transition matrix **P** is always true?

- A. All columns of **P** sum to one.
- B. **P** is always symmetric.
- C. All rows of **P** sum to one.
- D. The determinant of **P** equals one.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。By definition a transition matrix is row‑stochastic; each row represents a probability distribution over next states, so rows sum to one.

</details>

**2.** The steady‑state vector **π** of an irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain satisfies which equation?

- A. **π** = **P** **π** (right eigenvector)
- B. **π** **P** = **π** (left eigenvector)
- C. **π** **P** = **0**
- D. **π** = **π** **P** **P**

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The steady‑state distribution is a left eigenvector of **P** associated with eigenvalue 1, i.e., **π** **P** = **π**.

</details>

**3.** For a 2 × 2 MIMO channel matrix **H**, which condition guarantees that two independent data streams can be transmitted simultaneously without loss of rank?

- A. $\det(\mathbf{H}) = 0$
- B. $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{H}) = 1$
- C. $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{H}) = 2$
- D. All entries of **H** are equal.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Full rank (rank = min$N_t,N_r$=2) means the two column vectors are linearly independent, allowing two spatial streams.

</details>

**4.** A three‑state Markov chain has transition matrix  
$$
\mathbf{P}= \begin{bmatrix}
0.7 & 0.2 & 0.1\\
0.3 & 0.4 & 0.3\\
0.2 & 0.3 & 0.5
\end{bmatrix}.
$$  
Find the steady‑state probability of being in state 2 (i.e., $\pi_2$).  Answer in decimal form (rounded to four decimal places).


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

Solve $\boldsymbol{\pi}\mathbf{P}= \boldsymbol{\pi}$ with $\sum_i\pi_i=1$. From the linear system we obtain $\pi_2 = \frac{13}{12}\pi_3$ and $\pi_3 = \frac{6}{23}$. Hence $\pi_2 = \frac{13}{12}\times\frac{6}{23}= \frac{78}{276}=0.2826087$. Rounded to four decimals: $\boxed{0.2826}$.

</details>

**5.** A 2 × 2 real MIMO channel has matrix  
$$
\mathbf{H}= \begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0.5\\
0.3 & 1.2
\end{bmatrix}.
$$  
With total transmit power $P = 10\;\text{W}$ and noise power $N_0 = 1\;\text{W}$, compute the channel capacity $C$ in bits/s/Hz using  
$C = \log_2\!\bigl(\det(\mathbf{I}+ \frac{P}{N_0}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\!T})\bigr)$.  Give the answer rounded to two decimal places.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Compute $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\!T}= \begin{bmatrix}1.25 & 0.9\\ 0.9 & 1.53\end{bmatrix}$.  
2. Form $\mathbf{I}+10\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\!T}= \begin{bmatrix}13.5 & 9.0\\ 9.0 & 16.3\end{bmatrix}$.  
3. Determinant = $13.5\times16.3 - 9.0^2 = 139.05$.  
4. Capacity $C = \log_2(139.05)= \frac{\ln(139.05)}{\ln(2)}$.  
5. $\ln(139.05) ≈ 4.934$; $\ln(2)≈0.6931$; ratio ≈ 7.112. Rounded to two decimals: $\boxed{7.11}$.

</details>
