# 3.5.6 馬可夫決策過程

所屬：[3.5 基礎數理](../3-5.md)

## 正文

A Markov Decision Process provides a mathematically rigorous framework for sequential decision‑making under uncertainty. In a communication network an MDP can model, for example, adaptive link‑layer scheduling, dynamic spectrum access, or power‑control policies that evolve over time in response to stochastic channel conditions. By solving the MDP the engineer obtains a *policy* – a rule that maps the observed network state to an action – that maximizes a long‑term performance metric such as throughput, energy efficiency, or a weighted combination of quality‑of‑service (QoS) indicators.

---

### 1. Core definitions  

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit / Range |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| $\mathcal{S}$ | Finite set of *states* (e.g., channel quality levels) | – |
| $\mathcal{A}$ | Finite set of *actions* available in each state (e.g., transmit, wait, change power) | – |
| $P(s' \mid s,a)$ | *Transition probability*: probability that the next state is $s'$ when action $a$ is taken in state $s$ | $0\le P\le1$, $\sum_{s'}P(s' \mid s,a)=1$ |
| $R(s,a,s')$ | *Immediate reward* obtained after moving from $s$ to $s'$ by action $a$ | reward units (e.g., bits, joules) |
| $\gamma$ | *Discount factor* that weighs near‑term vs. far‑term rewards; $0\le\gamma<1$ | – |
| $\pi(a\mid s)$ | *Policy*: probability of choosing action $a$ in state $s$ | $0\le\pi\le1$, $\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)=1$ |
| $V^\pi(s)$ | *State‑value function* under policy $\pi$: expected discounted return when starting in $s$ | reward units |
| $Q^\pi(s,a)$ | *Action‑value function*: expected discounted return when first taking $a$ in $s$, then following $\pi$ | reward units |
| $V^*(s),\;Q^*(s,a)$ | *Optimal* value functions (maximum achievable) | reward units |
| $\pi^*$ | *Optimal policy* that attains $V^*$ | – |

The **discounted return** from time $t$ onward is  

$$
G_t = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\,R_{t+k+1},
$$

where $R_{t+k+1}$ is the reward received $k+1$ steps after $t$.

---

### 2. Bellman equations  

For a *fixed* policy $\pi$ the state‑value satisfies the **Bellman expectation equation**

$$
V^{\pi}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a\mid s)\Bigl[\,\underbrace{\sum_{s'\in\mathcal{S}}P(s'\mid s,a)\bigl(R(s,a,s')+\gamma V^{\pi}(s')\bigr)}_{\text{expected one‑step return}}\Bigr].
\tag{1}
$$

The corresponding action‑value is  

$$
Q^{\pi}(s,a)=\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\Bigl(R(s,a,s')+\gamma\!\sum_{a'}\!\pi(a'\mid s')\,Q^{\pi}(s',a')\Bigr).
\tag{2}
$$

The *optimal* state‑value obeys the **Bellman optimality equation**

$$
V^{*}(s)=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\;\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\bigl(R(s,a,s')+\gamma V^{*}(s')\bigr).
\tag{3}
$$

An optimal deterministic policy can be extracted as  

$$
\pi^{*}(s)=\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\;\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\bigl(R(s,a,s')+\gamma V^{*}(s')\bigr).
\tag{4}
$$

Equations (3)–(4) are the basis for classic solution methods such as **value iteration** and **policy iteration**.

---

### 3. Worked example  

Consider a simple link‑layer controller with two channel states  

* $s_G$ (good) – high SNR, successful transmission yields 1 bit of reward.  
* $s_B$ (bad) – low SNR, transmission fails (reward 0).  

Two actions are possible  

* $a_T$ – *Transmit* now.  
* $a_W$ – *Wait* (no transmission, no immediate reward).

Transition probabilities (independent of the action)  

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(s_G\mid s_G,a_T)&=0.8, & P(s_B\mid s_G,a_T)&=0.2,\\
P(s_G\mid s_B,a_T)&=0.1, & P(s_B\mid s_B,a_T)&=0.9,\\
P(s_G\mid s,a_W)&=0.5, & P(s_B\mid s,a_W)&=0.5\quad\forall s.
\end{aligned}
$$

Immediate rewards  

$$
R(s_G,a_T,s')=1,\qquad R(s_B,a_T,s')=0,\qquad R(s,a_W,s')=0.
$$

Discount factor $\gamma=0.9$.

---

**Step 1 – write Bellman optimality equations.**  

Let $V_G\triangleq V^*(s_G)$ and $V_B\triangleq V^*(s_B)$.  

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_G &= \max\Bigl\{\,1+\gamma\bigl[0.8V_G+0.2V_B\bigr],\; \gamma\bigl[0.5V_G+0.5V_B\bigr]\Bigr\},\\[2mm]
V_B &= \max\Bigl\{\,\gamma\bigl[0.1V_G+0.9V_B\bigr],\; \gamma\bigl[0.5V_G+0.5V_B\bigr]\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

**Step 2 – guess the optimal actions.**  
Because transmitting in a good state yields an immediate reward, it is reasonable to assume $a_T$ is optimal in $s_G$. In the bad state, waiting avoids a likely transition to the same bad state, so we test $a_W$ as optimal in $s_B$.

**Step 3 – solve the resulting linear system.**  

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_G &= 1 + 0.9\bigl(0.8V_G+0.2V_B\bigr)=1+0.72V_G+0.18V_B,\\
V_B &= 0.9\bigl(0.5V_G+0.5V_B\bigr)=0.45V_G+0.45V_B.
\end{aligned}
$$

Rearranging,

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.28V_G &= 1 + 0.18V_B \quad\Rightarrow\quad V_G = \frac{1+0.18V_B}{0.28},\\
0.55V_B &= 0.45V_G \quad\Rightarrow\quad V_B = \frac{0.45}{0.55}V_G\approx0.8182V_G.
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the second relation into the first gives  

$$
V_G = \frac{1+0.18(0.8182V_G)}{0.28}
      = \frac{1+0.1473V_G}{0.28}
      \;\Longrightarrow\; 0.28V_G-0.1473V_G = 1
      \;\Longrightarrow\; 0.1327V_G = 1,
$$

hence  

$$
\boxed{V_G \approx 7.53\;\text{bits}},\qquad 
\boxed{V_B \approx 6.16\;\text{bits}}.
$$

**Step 4 – verify the action choice.**  

*Transmit in $s_G$* yields $1+0.9(0.8V_G+0.2V_B)=7.53$, whereas *wait* gives $0.9(0.5V_G+0.5V_B)=6.16$.  

*Transmit in $s_B$* gives $0.9(0.1V_G+0.9V_B)=5.67$, while *wait* gives $6.16$.  

Thus the optimal deterministic policy is  

$$
\pi^{*}(s_G)=a_T,\qquad \pi^{*}(s_B)=a_W.
$$

The example illustrates how an MDP captures the trade‑off between immediate reward (transmit now) and future opportunity (wait for a better channel).

---

### 4. Practical notes & common pitfalls  

| Pitfall | Why it hurts | Remedy |
|---------|--------------|--------|
| **Omitting the discount factor** ($\gamma=1$) in a non‑terminating network | The return may diverge or give equal weight to arbitrarily distant events, obscuring the effect of near‑term QoS constraints. | Choose $\gamma$ based on the desired time horizon (e.g., $\gamma=0.9$ corresponds to an effective horizon of $\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\approx10$ steps). |
| **Assuming deterministic transitions** when the channel is actually fading | Leads to overly optimistic policies that fail in practice. | Model $P(s'|s,a)$ from measurements or standard channel models (e.g., Rayleigh fading). |
| **Mixing up $V(s)$ and $Q(s,a)$** | Errors in policy extraction; a policy derived from $V$ may ignore action‑specific information. | Use (4) to compute the maximizing action explicitly, or work directly with $Q$-values. |
| **Neglecting constraint handling** (e.g., power budget) | The optimal policy may violate regulatory or hardware limits. | Incorporate constraints as part of the reward (penalties) or formulate a *constrained MDP* and solve via Lagrangian methods. |
| **State‑space explosion** | Computational infeasibility for realistic networks (e.g., many users, sub‑carriers). | Apply aggregation, function approximation, or reinforcement‑learning variants (deep Q‑network, policy gradient). |

When deploying an MDP‑derived controller, validate the policy with Monte‑Carlo simulations over realistic traffic and channel traces before field trials.

## 練習題

**1.** In the Bellman optimality equation $V^{*}(s)=\max_{a}\sum_{s'}P(s'|s,a)[R(s,a,s')+\gamma V^{*}(s')]$, what does the term $\gamma V^{*}(s')$ represent?

- A. The discounted immediate reward obtained in the next state.
- B. The discounted expected future return starting from the next state.
- C. The probability of staying in the current state.
- D. The variance of the reward distribution.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。$\gamma V^{*}(s')$ is the discounted value of the future return after moving to $s'$; it does not include the immediate reward.

</details>

**2.** Which of the following statements is *always* true for a finite‑horizon undiscounted MDP ($\gamma=1$) with at least one absorbing (terminal) state?

- A. The optimal policy is unique.
- B. The value function of any state is bounded by the maximum possible cumulative reward.
- C. Transition probabilities must sum to less than 1 for non‑absorbing states.
- D. The optimal action in a state never depends on the time step.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。With $\gamma=1$ the total reward cannot exceed the sum of the per‑step maximal rewards, so the value is bounded; uniqueness and time‑dependence are not guaranteed.

</details>

**3.** In a communication‑network MDP, the set $\mathcal{A}$ (actions) typically includes which of the following?

- A. Only the physical layer modulation formats.
- B. Any controllable network parameter such as transmit power, scheduling decision, or spectrum band.
- C. The random channel realizations observed by the receiver.
- D. The user equipment identifiers.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Actions are the controllable decisions the network can make; channel realizations are part of the state, not the action set.

</details>

**4.** Consider the two‑state MDP described in the textbook example (states $s_G$, $s_B$; actions transmit $a_T$ and wait $a_W$; $\gamma=0.9$). Compute the optimal state‑value $V^{*}(s_G)$ and give the answer to two decimal places.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Write Bellman equations (5).  
2. Assume optimal actions: transmit in $s_G$, wait in $s_B$.  
3. Solve linear system:  
   $V_G = 1 + 0.9(0.8V_G+0.2V_B)$ → $0.28V_G = 1 + 0.18V_B$.  
   $V_B = 0.9(0.5V_G+0.5V_B)$ → $0.55V_B = 0.45V_G$ → $V_B = 0.818181V_G$.  
4. Substitute $V_B$ into first equation:  
   $0.28V_G = 1 + 0.18(0.818181V_G)$ → $0.28V_G = 1 + 0.147273V_G$ → $0.132727V_G = 1$.  
   $V_G = 1/0.132727 \approx 7.5336$.  
5. Rounded to two decimals: $\boxed{7.53}$.

</details>

**5.** In a different MDP there are two states $s_0$ and $s_1$ with two actions $a_{\text{stay}}$ and $a_{\text{switch}}$. Discount factor $\gamma=0.9$. Rewards and transition probabilities are:  

*In $s_0$*:  
 $a_{\text{stay}}$: $R=2$, $P(s_0|s_0,a_{\text{stay}})=0.6$, $P(s_1|s_0,a_{\text{stay}})=0.4$.  
 $a_{\text{switch}}$: $R=0$, $P(s_1|s_0,a_{\text{switch}})=1.0$.  

*In $s_1$*:  
 $a_{\text{stay}}$: $R=1$, $P(s_1|s_1,a_{\text{stay}})=0.7$, $P(s_0|s_1,a_{\text{stay}})=0.3$.  
 $a_{\text{switch}}$: $R=0$, $P(s_0|s_1,a_{\text{switch}})=1.0$.  

Assuming the optimal policy is to *stay* in both states, compute the optimal value $V^{*}(s_0)$ and give the answer rounded to two decimal places.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Write Bellman optimality equations for staying:  

   $V_0 = 2 + \gamma\bigl(0.6V_0 + 0.4V_1\bigr)$,  
   $V_1 = 1 + \gamma\bigl(0.7V_1 + 0.3V_0\bigr)$.  

2. Substitute $\gamma=0.9$:  

   $V_0 = 2 + 0.9(0.6V_0+0.4V_1)=2+0.54V_0+0.36V_1$ → $0.46V_0 = 2 + 0.36V_1$ → $V_0 = (2+0.36V_1)/0.46$.  

   $V_1 = 1 + 0.9(0.7V_1+0.3V_0)=1+0.63V_1+0.27V_0$ → $0.37V_1 = 1 + 0.27V_0$ → $V_1 = (1+0.27V_0)/0.37$.  

3. Substitute $V_1$ into $V_0$:  

   $V_0 = \frac{2 + 0.36\frac{1+0.27V_0}{0.37}}{0.46}
        = \frac{2 + 0.972973(1+0.27V_0)}{0.46}
        = \frac{2.972973 + 0.262703V_0}{0.46}$.  

4. Multiply both sides by $0.46$:  

   $0.46V_0 = 2.972973 + 0.262703V_0$ → $0.197297V_0 = 2.972973$ → $V_0 = 2.972973/0.197297 \approx 15.066$.  

5. Rounded to two decimals: $\boxed{15.07}$.

</details>
