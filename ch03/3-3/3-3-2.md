# 3.3.2 天線增益與方向性

所屬：[3.3 鏈路預算](../3-3.md)

## 正文

In a wireless link the antenna is the device that converts electrical power into a propagating electromagnetic wave (and vice‑versa).  The *gain* of an antenna quantifies how much power is concentrated in a particular direction compared with an ideal isotropic radiator.  Because the received power $P_{\text{rx}}$ appears in the link‑budget equation as a product of the transmit power, the path loss and the two antenna gains, an accurate gain value is essential for realistic coverage predictions, spectrum efficiency calculations and for meeting regulatory EIRP limits.

### Definitions

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $G$ | **Linear antenna gain** (dimensionless) | – |
| $G_{\text{dBi}}$ | Antenna gain expressed in decibels relative to an isotropic radiator | dBi |
| $D$ | **Directivity** (dimensionless) – the gain of a loss‑less antenna | – |
| $\eta$ | **Radiation efficiency** (ratio of radiated power to input power) | – (0 ≤ $\eta$ ≤ 1) |
| $A_e$ | **Effective aperture** – area that captures power from a plane wave | $\text{m}^2$ |
| $\lambda$ | Wavelength of the operating frequency | m |
| $f$ | Frequency | Hz |
| $\Omega_A$ | **Beam solid angle** (integral of the normalized power pattern over $4\pi$ steradians) | sr |
| $\theta_{\text{HPBW}}$ | **Half‑power beamwidth** (angle where power drops to –3 dB) | ° |

### Core Relationships

1. **Gain and Directivity**  
   $$ G = \eta \, D $$  
   The gain is the directivity reduced by the radiation efficiency.  For a perfectly lossless antenna ($\eta = 1$) we have $G = D$.

2. **Directivity and Beam Solid Angle**  
   $$ D = \frac{4\pi}{\Omega_A} $$  
   A narrower main lobe (smaller $\Omega_A$) yields a larger directivity.

3. **Effective Aperture**  
   $$ A_e = \frac{G \, \lambda^{2}}{4\pi} = \frac{D \, \lambda^{2}}{4\pi}\,\eta $$  
   $A_e$ is the “capture area’’ that a receiving antenna presents to an incident plane wave.

4. **Conversion to dBi**  
   $$ G_{\text{dBi}} = 10\log_{10}(G) \qquad\text{or}\qquad G = 10^{G_{\text{dBi}}/10} $$  

5. **Approximate Gain from Beamwidth** (useful for sector antennas with a single main lobe)  
   $$ G_{\text{dBi}} \approx 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{32400}{\theta_{\text{H}} \, \theta_{\text{V}}}\right) $$  
   where $\theta_{\text{H}}$ and $\theta_{\text{V}}$ are the horizontal and vertical HPBWs in degrees.  This expression assumes a rectangular‑sinc pattern and neglects side‑lobe contributions.

### Worked Example

A cellular base‑station uses a sector antenna at **2.6 GHz** with the following specifications:

* Gain (quoted) $G_{\text{dBi}} = 15\ \text{dBi}$  
* Radiation efficiency $\eta = 0.90$  
* Horizontal HPBW $\theta_{\text{H}} = 65^{\circ}$ (vertical HPBW is the same)

**Step 1 – Linear gain**  
$$
G = 10^{15/10}=31.62
$$

**Step 2 – Directivity**  
$$
D = \frac{G}{\eta}= \frac{31.62}{0.90}=35.14
$$

**Step 3 – Directivity in dBi**  
$$
D_{\text{dBi}} = 10\log_{10}(35.14)=15.46\ \text{dBi}
$$

**Step 4 – Wavelength**  
$$
\lambda = \frac{c}{f}= \frac{3\times10^{8}\ \text{m/s}}{2.6\times10^{9}\ \text{Hz}} = 0.1154\ \text{m}
$$

**Step 5 – Effective aperture**  
$$
A_e = \frac{G\lambda^{2}}{4\pi}= \frac{31.62\,(0.1154)^{2}}{12.566}=0.0335\ \text{m}^{2}
$$

**Step 6 – Check the beamwidth formula** (optional)  
$$
G_{\text{dBi,\,approx}} = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{32400}{65\times65}\right)=10\log_{10}(7.68)=8.85\ \text{dBi}
$$  
The discrepancy shows that the simple beam‑width approximation under‑estimates the gain because the actual pattern contains side‑lobe suppression and a slightly narrower effective beam than the rectangular model assumes.  In practice the quoted manufacturer gain (15 dBi) is the reliable figure for link budgeting.

### Practical Notes & Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it is wrong | Correct practice |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| Confusing **dBi** (relative to isotropic) with **dBd** (relative to a half‑wave dipole). | dBd = dBi – 2.15 dB. Using the wrong reference adds a systematic error. | Always state the reference; convert when necessary: $G_{\text{dBi}} = G_{\text{dBd}} + 2.15$. |
| Ignoring radiation efficiency and treating the quoted gain as directivity. | The gain already includes loss; assuming $\eta=1$ over‑estimates received power. | Use the relation $G = \eta D$ if you need the underlying directivity. |
| Assuming gain is independent of frequency. | $G$ depends on $\lambda^{2}$ through $A_e$; a physically sized antenna becomes electrically smaller at higher frequencies, reducing gain unless the aperture is scaled. | Re‑calculate $G$ when the operating frequency changes, or use the manufacturer’s frequency‑specific gain curve. |
| Using the HPBW approximation for antennas with significant side‑lobes (e.g., printed‑array panels). | The formula assumes all power is in the main lobe. | Verify the pattern; if side‑lobe level > –10 dB, employ measured gain or full‑pattern integration. |
| Forgetting to convert linear quantities before applying logarithmic formulas. | $10\log_{10}$ expects a dimensionless ratio; feeding a value in dB leads to nonsense. | Keep a clear separation: convert dBi ↔ linear first, then perform arithmetic. |

Accurate gain values are the cornerstone of any link‑budget analysis.  When in doubt, rely on measured antenna patterns or the manufacturer’s frequency‑specific data sheets rather than on generic approximations.

## 練習題

**1.** Which equation correctly relates antenna gain $G$, directivity $D$, and radiation efficiency $\eta$?

- A. $G = D + \eta$
- B. $G = \eta \, D$
- C. $G = D / \eta$
- D. $G = \eta - D$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。By definition, gain equals directivity multiplied by the radiation efficiency: $G = \eta D$.

</details>

**2.** An antenna has a quoted gain of 12 dBi and a radiation efficiency of 0.8. What is its directivity $D$ in dBi?

- A. 13.2 dBi
- B. 12.0 dBi
- C. 10.8 dBi
- D. 14.5 dBi

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。Convert gain to linear: $G=10^{12/10}=15.85$. Divide by $\eta$: $D=G/\eta=15.85/0.8=19.81$. Convert back: $10\log_{10}(19.81)=13.2\,$dBi.

</details>

**3.** For a rectangular‑sinc antenna pattern with horizontal and vertical HPBWs of $50^{\circ}$ and $60^{\circ}$, the approximate gain is given by $G_{\text{dBi}}\approx10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{32400}{\theta_H\theta_V}\right)$. What is the approximate gain?

- A. 8.2 dBi
- B. 10.3 dBi
- C. 12.5 dBi
- D. 14.1 dBi

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。$\frac{32400}{50\times60}=10.8$, $10\log_{10}(10.8)=12.5$ dBi.

</details>

**4.** A transmitter outputs 10 W (40 dBm). Feed‑line loss is 2 dB and the antenna efficiency is 85 %. The network requires an EIRP of 50 dBm. What is the minimum antenna gain in dBi?


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Convert power to dBm: $P_{\text{tx}}=40$ dBm.  
2. Subtract feed‑line loss: $P_{\text{after loss}} = 40 - 2 = 38$ dBm.  
3. Account for efficiency: $10\log_{10}(\eta)=10\log_{10}(0.85) = -0.70$ dB.  
4. Required gain: $G_{\text{dBi}} = \text{EIRP} - P_{\text{after loss}} - 10\log_{10}(\eta) = 50 - 38 - (-0.70) = 12.7$ dBi.  
$\boxed{12.7}$

</details>

**5.** An antenna operating at 3.5 GHz has a gain of 18 dBi and a radiation efficiency of 0.80. Determine its effective aperture $A_e$ in square meters (rounded to three decimal places).


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Wavelength: $\lambda = c/f = 3\times10^{8} / 3.5\times10^{9} = 0.085714\,$m.  
2. Linear gain: $G = 10^{18/10}=63.0957$.  
3. Effective aperture: $A_e = \dfrac{G\lambda^{2}}{4\pi} = \dfrac{63.0957 \times (0.085714)^{2}}{12.5664}=0.03686\,$m$^{2}$.  
Rounded to three decimals: $0.037\,$m$^{2}$.  
$\boxed{0.037}$

</details>
