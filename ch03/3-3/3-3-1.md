# 3.3.1 發射功率與 EIRP

所屬：[3.3 鏈路預算](../3-3.md)

## 正文

In any wireless system the *transmit power* is the starting point of the link‑budget. It determines how much electromagnetic energy leaves the transmitter before any antenna or propagation effect is taken into account. However, engineers rarely work directly with raw power alone; they need a figure that already incorporates the directional gain of the radiating antenna. This figure is the **Equivalent Isotropically Radiated Power (EIRP)**, which represents the power that would have to be emitted by an ideal isotropic radiator (gain = 0 dBi) to achieve the same power density in the direction of maximum radiation as the actual antenna system.

Why EIRP matters:

* **Regulatory compliance** – many spectrum regulators (e.g., ITU‑R, national agencies) set limits on EIRP rather than on transmitter output, because EIRP directly reflects the potential for interference.
* **Coverage planning** – the received power at a given distance depends on EIRP and the path‑loss model; therefore the cell radius can be estimated from EIRP and the receiver sensitivity.
* **Inter‑system compatibility** – uplink and downlink power allocations are often expressed as EIRP to make the comparison between different antenna configurations straightforward.

---

### 1. Key definitions  

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit | Typical range |
|--------|---------|------|---------------|
| $P_{\text{tx}}$ | Transmit (output) power of the RF chain, before the antenna | Watt (W) or dBm | 0.001 W – 100 W |
| $G_{\text{t}}$ | Gain of the transmitting antenna (relative to an isotropic radiator) | dBi (decibels‑isotropic) | 0 dBi – 30 dBi |
| $E_{\text{IRP}}$ | Equivalent isotropically radiated power | dBm (or W) | –30 dBm – +80 dBm |
| $L_{\text{c}}$ | Cable, connector, and filter losses between the PA and the antenna (expressed as a positive dB value) | dB | 0 dB – 5 dB |
| $P_{\text{tx,dBm}}$ | Transmit power expressed in dBm | dBm | –30 dBm – +50 dBm |

The conversion between linear power (W) and decibel‑milliwatts (dBm) is

$$
P_{\text{dBm}} = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{P_{\text{W}}}{1\ \text{mW}}\right)
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
P_{\text{W}} = 10^{\frac{P_{\text{dBm}}-30}{10}} .
$$

---

### 2. EIRP formula  

When the antenna is fed through loss elements, the **net EIRP** is

$$
\boxed{E_{\text{IRP}} \;[\text{dBm}] \;=\; P_{\text{tx,dBm}} \;+\; G_{\text{t}} \;-\; L_{\text{c}} } \tag{1}
$$

*All terms are in dB‑type units, so addition/subtraction is valid.*  
If the losses are negligible ($L_{\text{c}} = 0$ dB), the expression simplifies to

$$
E_{\text{IRP}} = P_{\text{tx,dBm}} + G_{\text{t}} .
$$

When a linear result (Watts) is required, first compute $E_{\text{IRP}}$ in dBm, then convert using the inverse relation above.

---

### 3. Worked example  

**Scenario**: A base‑station transmitter outputs $P_{\text{tx}} = 5\ \text{W}$. The feeder cable incurs $L_{\text{c}} = 2\ \text{dB}$ loss, and the mounted antenna has a gain of $G_{\text{t}} = 18\ \text{dBi}$.

1. Convert $P_{\text{tx}}$ to dBm  

$$
P_{\text{tx,dBm}} = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{5\ \text{W}}{1\ \text{mW}}\right)
= 10\log_{10}(5000) \approx 10 \times 3.6990 = 36.99\ \text{dBm}.
$$

2. Apply Eq. (1)

$$
E_{\text{IRP}} = 36.99\ \text{dBm} + 18\ \text{dBi} - 2\ \text{dB}
= 52.99\ \text{dBm} \approx 53\ \text{dBm}.
$$

3. Convert back to Watts (optional)

$$
E_{\text{IRP,W}} = 10^{\frac{53-30}{10}} = 10^{2.3} \approx 199.5\ \text{W}.
$$

Thus the system radiates the same power density as a 199 W isotropic source, even though the actual PA output is only 5 W.

---

### 4. Practical notes and common pitfalls  

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | Correct practice |
|---------|---------------|------------------|
| **Mixing linear and dB quantities** | Adding a Watt value directly to a dBi gain is mathematically invalid. | Always convert linear power to dBm (or dBW) before adding gains or subtracting losses. |
| **Neglecting feeder loss** | Cable loss can be several dB, especially at higher frequencies, leading to over‑optimistic EIRP estimates. | Measure or consult manufacturer data for $L_{\text{c}}$ and include it in Eq. (1). |
| **Using antenna “gain” in dBd instead of dBi** | Some datasheets quote gain relative to a dipole (dBd). 1 dBd = 2.15 dBi. | Convert dBd to dBi before using the formula. |
| **Assuming EIRP = radiated power** | EIRP is a *reference* power, not the actual power emitted in all directions. | Remember that EIRP only describes the power density in the direction of maximum gain. |
| **Forgetting regulatory limits** | Designers may calculate a high EIRP without checking local limits, risking non‑compliance. | Cross‑check the computed EIRP against the applicable spectrum‑allocation rules (e.g., ITU‑R SM.1546). |

**Tip**: When performing a link‑budget, keep a spreadsheet column for $P_{\text{tx,dBm}}$, $G_{\text{t}}$, $L_{\text{c}}$, and $E_{\text{IRP}}$ so that any change in hardware (e.g., a new antenna) propagates automatically through the calculations.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following expressions correctly gives the EIRP in dBm when feeder loss is present?

- A. $E_{\text{IRP}} = P_{\text{tx,dBm}} + G_{\text{t}} + L_{\text{c}}$
- B. $E_{\text{IRP}} = P_{\text{tx,dBm}} - G_{\text{t}} - L_{\text{c}}$
- C. $E_{\text{IRP}} = P_{\text{tx,dBm}} + G_{\text{t}} - L_{\text{c}}$
- D. $E_{\text{IRP}} = P_{\text{tx,dBm}} - G_{\text{t}} + L_{\text{c}}$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。EIRP adds antenna gain and subtracts any loss; thus Eq. (1) is $P_{\text{tx,dBm}} + G_{\text{t}} - L_{\text{c}}$.

</details>

**2.** An antenna is specified as 12 dBd. What is its gain in dBi?

- A. 9.85 dBi
- B. 12 dBi
- C. 14.15 dBi
- D. 16 dBi

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Convert dBd to dBi by adding 2.15 dB: $12 + 2.15 = 14.15$ dBi.

</details>

**3.** A transmitter outputs 0.5 W. What is its power expressed in dBm?

- A. 23 dBm
- B. 26.99 dBm
- C. 27 dBm
- D. 30 dBm

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。$10\log_{10}(0.5\ \text{W}/1\text{mW}) = 10\log_{10}(500) \approx 26.99$ dBm.

</details>

**4.** A base‑station has $P_{\text{tx}} = 10\ \text{W}$, antenna gain $G_{\text{t}} = 15\ \text{dBi}$, and feeder loss $L_{\text{c}} = 1\ \text{dB}$. Compute the EIRP in dBm.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Convert $P_{\text{tx}}$ to dBm: $P_{\text{tx,dBm}} = 10\log_{10}(10\ \text{W}/1\text{mW}) = 10\log_{10}(10\,000) = 40\ \text{dBm}$.  
2. Apply Eq. (1): $E_{\text{IRP}} = 40\ \text{dBm} + 15\ \text{dBi} - 1\ \text{dB} = 54\ \text{dBm}$.  
$\boxed{54}$

</details>

**5.** Desired EIRP is $60\ \text{dBm}$, antenna gain $G_{\text{t}} = 20\ \text{dBi}$, feeder loss $L_{\text{c}} = 2\ \text{dB}$. What transmit power $P_{\text{tx}}$ (in Watts) is required?


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Rearrange Eq. (1) to find $P_{\text{tx,dBm}}$: $P_{\text{tx,dBm}} = E_{\text{IRP}} - G_{\text{t}} + L_{\text{c}} = 60 - 20 + 2 = 42\ \text{dBm}$.  
2. Convert $42\ \text{dBm}$ to Watts: $P_{\text{tx,W}} = 10^{(42-30)/10} = 10^{1.2} \approx 15.8489\ \text{W}$.  
$\boxed{15.8489}$

</details>
