## 正文

In a cellular system the radio link is bidirectional: the *uplink* (UL) carries traffic from the mobile terminal (user equipment, UE) to the base‑station (BS), while the *downlink* (DL) carries traffic from the BS to the UE. Although the same physical channel (frequency, bandwidth, modulation) may be used for both directions, the link‑budget components differ considerably because the transmitter and receiver roles are swapped and because regulatory, hardware, and ergonomic constraints are not symmetric. Understanding the quantitative difference between UL and DL link budgets is essential for:

* **Cell planning** – the cell radius is limited by the weaker of the two directions.  
* **Power‑control design** – uplink power control must compensate for UE‑limited transmit power, while downlink power control is used mainly to manage inter‑cell interference.  
* **Equipment specification** – antenna gain, feeder loss, and allowable EIRP are defined separately for UE and BS.

---

### 1.  Fundamental Link‑Budget Equation

For any point‑to‑point radio link the received power $P_{\text{RX}}$ (in dBm) can be expressed as  

$$
\boxed{P_{\text{RX}} = P_{\text{TX}} + G_{\text{TX}} + G_{\text{RX}} - L_{\text{FS}} - L_{\text{misc}} + M}
$$

where  

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $P_{\text{TX}}$ | Transmit power (including PA output) | dBm |
| $G_{\text{TX}}$ | Transmit antenna gain (including any feeder loss) | dBi |
| $G_{\text{RX}}$ | Receive antenna gain (including any feeder loss) | dBi |
| $L_{\text{FS}}$ | Free‑space (or empirical) path loss | dB |
| $L_{\text{misc}}$ | Sum of all additional losses (cable, connector, polarization, atmospheric) | dB |
| $M$ | System margin (fading, implementation loss) | dB |

The **Effective Isotropic Radiated Power (EIRP)** is defined as  

$$
\text{EIRP}=P_{\text{TX}}+G_{\text{TX}}\quad\text{[dBm]}
$$

and the **Receiver Sensitivity** $S_{\text{RX}}$ is the minimum $P_{\text{RX}}$ required for a target bit‑error rate (BER) or block‑error rate (BLER).  

---

### 2.  Direction‑Specific Terms

| Direction | Typical $P_{\text{TX}}$ | Typical $G_{\text{TX}}$ | Typical $G_{\text{RX}}$ | Remarks |
|-----------|------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|---------|
| **Uplink** | UE limited to $23\,$dBm (max) or lower by power control | UE antenna ≈ $0\,$dBi (omnidirectional) | BS sector antenna ≈ $15\,$dBi (highly directional) | UE power is strictly limited by SAR and battery; BS can use high‑gain panels. |
| **Downlink** | BS can transmit up to $46\,$dBm (EIRP) in many bands | BS antenna ≈ $15\,$dBi | UE antenna ≈ $0\,$dBi | BS EIRP is often limited by regulatory masks; UE sensitivity is the limiting factor. |

Because the antenna gains appear on opposite sides of the equation, the **link asymmetry** is largely driven by the disparity between UE and BS antenna directivity.

---

### 3.  Path‑Loss Model

For a line‑of‑sight (LOS) link the Friis free‑space loss is  

$$
L_{\text{FS}} = 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi d f}{c}\right)\quad\text{[dB]}
$$

where  

* $d$ – distance between terminals (m)  
* $f$ – carrier frequency (Hz)  
* $c = 3\times10^{8}\,$m/s – speed of light  

In non‑LOS or urban environments a distance‑dependent exponent $n$ (typically $3\!-\!4$) is added:  

$$
L_{\text{PL}} = L_{\text{FS}} + 10n\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{d}{d_0}\right)
$$

with reference distance $d_0 = 1\,$m.

---

### 4.  Worked Example

**Scenario** – A 2.6 GHz LTE cell.  
* UE transmit power $P_{\text{TX,UL}} = 23\,$dBm.  
* UE antenna gain $G_{\text{TX,UL}} = 0\,$dBi, feeder loss $L_{\text{f,UL}} = 2\,$dB.  
* BS antenna gain $G_{\text{RX,UL}} = 15\,$dBi, feeder loss $L_{\text{f,UL}}^{\text{BS}} = 1\,$dB.  
* Required BS receiver sensitivity $S_{\text{BS}} = -95\,$dBm.  
* System margin $M = 5\,$dB.  
* Assume free‑space loss.

**Step 1 – Combine gains and losses**  

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{\text{TX,eff}} &= G_{\text{TX,UL}} - L_{\text{f,UL}} = 0 - 2 = -2\;\text{dBi}\\
G_{\text{RX,eff}} &= G_{\text{RX,UL}} - L_{\text{f,UL}}^{\text{BS}} = 15 - 1 = 14\;\text{dBi}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 – Write the UL budget**  

$$
-95 = 23 + (-2) + 14 - L_{\text{FS}} + 5
$$

Solve for $L_{\text{FS}}$:

$$
L_{\text{FS}} = 23 -2 +14 +5 +95 = 135\;\text{dB}
$$

**Step 3 – Convert loss to distance**  

$$
L_{\text{FS}} = 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi d f}{c}\right)
$$

$$
d = \frac{c}{4\pi f}\;10^{L_{\text{FS}}/20}
$$

Insert $f = 2.6\times10^{9}\,$Hz, $L_{\text{FS}} = 135\,$dB:

$$
d \approx \frac{3\times10^{8}}{4\pi\cdot2.6\times10^{9}}\;10^{135/20}
\approx 2.2\;\text{km}
$$

Thus the **uplink cell radius** limited by the BS receiver is roughly **2.2 km**.

**Downlink comparison** – Using the same distance, BS transmit power $P_{\text{TX,DL}} = 46\,$dBm, BS antenna gain $15\,$dBi, BS feeder loss $1\,$dB, UE antenna gain $0\,$dBi, UE feeder loss $2\,$dB, and UE sensitivity $S_{\text{UE}} = -100\,$dBm, the received DL power computes to  

$$
P_{\text{RX,DL}} = 46 + (15-1) + (0-2) -135 + 5 = -72\;\text{dBm}
$$

which is *well above* the UE sensitivity, confirming that for this configuration the **uplink** is the bottleneck.

---

### 5.  Common Pitfalls and Practical Notes

| Pitfall | Why it matters | Remedy |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| **Double‑counting antenna gain** – Adding $G_{\text{TX}}$ and $G_{\text{RX}}$ while also including the same gain in the EIRP term. | Inflates received power, leading to unrealistic cell sizes. | Use either the EIRP formulation **or** the separate $P_{\text{TX}}+G_{\text{TX}}$ expression, never both. |
| **Mixing linear and dB units** – Subtracting a linear loss (e.g., 0.5) from a dB quantity. | Produces mathematically invalid results. | Keep all terms in dB; convert linear factors with $10\log_{10}(\cdot)$. |
| **Ignoring polarization loss** – Assuming perfect alignment (0 dB) when cross‑polarization may be 3 dB. | Over‑estimates link margin, especially for sector antennas. | Add a typical $L_{\text{pol}} = 3\,$dB unless polarization is known to be matched. |
| **Neglecting uplink power control** – Using the UE’s maximum transmit power for all users. | Over‑states coverage for cell‑edge users that are power‑controlled down. | Apply a typical power‑control headroom (e.g., 6 dB below max) when estimating worst‑case UL. |
| **Assuming the same margin for UL and DL** – Using a single $M$ value for both directions. | UL often needs larger margin due to fading and mobility. | Choose $M_{\text{UL}} \ge M_{\text{DL}}$, or compute margins from link‑specific link‑level simulations. |

Finally, remember that **regulatory limits** (e.g., ITU‑R M.1645 for EIRP) apply to the BS side, while **SAR / battery constraints** dominate the UE side. A realistic link‑budget comparison must respect both sets of limits.

## 練習題

**1.** Which regulatory reference primarily limits the maximum downlink Effective Isotropic Radiated Power (EIRP) that a base‑station may emit?

- A. ITU‑R M.1645 (Maximum EIRP limits)
- B. IEEE 802.11 ax (Wi‑Fi) specifications
- C. FCC Part 15 (unlicensed devices)
- D. SAR (Specific Absorption Rate) limits for handheld devices

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。ITU‑R M.1645 defines the global maximum permissible EIRP for mobile‑service base stations; the other documents relate to different services or to UE‑side constraints.

</details>

**2.** Which of the following is a common pitfall that causes an over‑optimistic (i.e., too large) estimated cell radius in link‑budget calculations?

- A. Double‑counting antenna gain (adding it both to EIRP and again as a separate term)
- B. Ignoring free‑space loss in the path‑loss term
- C. Mixing linear and dB units without conversion
- D. Assuming zero polarization loss when antennas are cross‑polarized

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。Adding the same antenna gain twice inflates the received‑power term, leading to unrealistically high coverage predictions.

</details>

**3.** Compute the maximum uplink cell‑radius (in meters) for a 2.6 GHz LTE cell given:
- UE transmit power = 23 dBm  
- UE antenna gain = 0 dBi, UE feeder loss = 2 dB  
- BS antenna gain = 15 dBi, BS feeder loss = 1 dB  
- BS receiver sensitivity = –95 dBm  
- System margin = 5 dB  
- Path‑loss exponent n = 3.5 (non‑LOS) with reference distance d₀ = 1 m  
Use the Friis free‑space loss at d₀.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解：

1. Effective UE transmit gain: $G_{\text{TX,eff}} = 0 - 2 = -2$ dBi.  
2. Effective BS receive gain: $G_{\text{RX,eff}} = 15 - 1 = 14$ dBi.  
3. Required total loss:  
$$
L_{\text{total}} = P_{\text{TX}} + G_{\text{TX,eff}} + G_{\text{RX,eff}} + M - S_{\text{BS}}
= 23 + (-2) + 14 + 5 - (-95) = 135\;\text{dB}
$$  
4. Free‑space loss at the reference distance (1 m):  
$$
L_{\text{FS}}(d_0) = 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi d_0 f}{c}\right)
= 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi \times 1 \times 2.6\times10^{9}}{3\times10^{8}}\right)
\approx 40.74\;\text{dB}
$$  
5. Path‑loss model: $L_{\text{total}} = L_{\text{FS}}(d_0) + 10n\log_{10}(d/d_0)$.  
Solve for $d$:  
$$
10n\log_{10}d = L_{\text{total}} - L_{\text{FS}}(d_0) \\
\log_{10}d = \frac{135 - 40.74}{10 \times 3.5}
= \frac{94.26}{35} \approx 2.6931
$$  
$$
d = 10^{2.6931} \approx 4.93 \times 10^{2} = 4.93 \times 10^{2}\,\text{m}
$$  
$$
\boxed{493}
$$

</details>

**4.** Using the distance obtained above (≈ 493 m), calculate the downlink received power (in dBm) for the same cell with:
- BS transmit power = 46 dBm  
- BS antenna gain = 15 dBi, BS feeder loss = 1 dB  
- UE antenna gain = 0 dBi, UE feeder loss = 2 dB  
- System margin = 5 dB  
- Same path‑loss exponent n = 3.5 (so total loss remains 135 dB).


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解：

1. Effective BS transmit gain: $G_{\text{TX,eff}} = 15 - 1 = 14$ dBi.  
2. Effective UE receive gain: $G_{\text{RX,eff}} = 0 - 2 = -2$ dBi.  
3. Received power:  
$$
P_{\text{RX}} = P_{\text{TX}} + G_{\text{TX,eff}} + G_{\text{RX,eff}} - L_{\text{total}} + M
$$  
$$
= 46 + 14 + (-2) - 135 + 5 = -72\;\text{dBm}
$$  
$$
\boxed{-72}
$$

</details>
