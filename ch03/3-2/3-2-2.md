## 正文

In a wireless system the *spectral efficiency* quantifies how many useful information bits can be transmitted per unit of radio spectrum. It is the key figure of merit that network planners use to decide how many users can be accommodated in a given carrier, how much capacity a cell can deliver, and whether a new service (e.g., high‑definition video) is feasible without allocating additional bandwidth. Because spectrum is a scarce and costly resource, every increase in spectral efficiency directly translates into higher revenue or lower operating cost.

---

### 1. Basic definition  

The most common definition is  

$$
\boxed{\eta = \frac{R}{B}}\qquad\text{[bits·s}^{-1}\text{·Hz}^{-1}]
$$

where  

* $R$ – net information bit rate (bits s$^{-1}$), after error‑correction decoding and after removing all protocol overhead.  
* $B$ – occupied radio bandwidth (Hz).  

The unit “bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$” is often abbreviated **bits/s/Hz** or **b/s/Hz**.

---

### 2. Relationship to modulation, coding and overhead  

For a digital baseband symbol that carries $k=\log_2 M$ bits (where $M$ is the modulation order, e.g. $M=64$ for 64‑QAM), and a forward error‑correction (FEC) code of rate $r$ (dimensionless, $0<r\le 1$), the *raw* spectral efficiency would be

$$
\eta_{\text{raw}} = r\,k \quad\text{[bits·s}^{-1}\text{·Hz}^{-1}]
$$

In practice the symbol stream is further reduced by protocol overhead such as cyclic prefix (CP), guard bands, pilot symbols, and control channels. If the total overhead fraction is denoted by $\alpha$ (dimensionless, $0\le\alpha<1$), the *effective* spectral efficiency becomes  

$$
\boxed{\eta = r\,k\,(1-\alpha)} \tag{1}
$$

All symbols in (1) are defined as follows  

| Symbol | Meaning | Typical unit / range |
|--------|---------|----------------------|
| $M$ | Modulation order (e.g. 4, 16, 64, 256) | – |
| $k=\log_2 M$ | Bits per constellation symbol | bits/symbol |
| $r$ | FEC code rate (e.g. 1/2, 3/4) | – |
| $\alpha$ | Fraction of time/frequency occupied by non‑data symbols | – |
| $\eta$ | Spectral efficiency | bits·s$^{-1}$·Hz$^{-1}$ |
| $R$ | Net bit rate | bits·s$^{-1}$ |
| $B$ | Occupied bandwidth | Hz |

Equation (1) is the backbone of *Adaptive Modulation and Coding* (AMC): the network monitors the instantaneous signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) and selects the pair $(M,r)$ that yields the highest $\eta$ while keeping the block error rate within target limits.

---

### 3. Shannon limit as an upper bound  

Claude Shannon proved that no coding scheme can surpass the capacity  

$$
C = B\log_2\!\bigl(1+\mathsf{SNR}\bigr) \quad\text{[bits·s}^{-1}]
$$

Dividing by $B$ gives the *theoretical* maximum spectral efficiency  

$$
\boxed{\eta_{\max}= \log_2\!\bigl(1+\mathsf{SNR}\bigr)} \tag{2}
$$

where $\mathsf{SNR}$ is the **linear** signal‑to‑noise ratio (ratio of signal power to noise power, dimensionless). Equation (2) is useful for sanity checks: any practical $\eta$ must satisfy $\eta \le \eta_{\max}$.

---

### 4. Worked example  

*Scenario*: A 20 MHz LTE carrier is to be configured for a user experiencing good channel conditions. The scheduler selects 64‑QAM ($M=64$) and a turbo code of rate $r=0.75$. The CP and control overhead together occupy $\alpha = 0.07$ (i.e., 7 % of the symbol interval).

1. Compute $k$:  

   $$
   k = \log_2 64 = 6\;\text{bits/symbol}
   $$

2. Apply (1) to obtain spectral efficiency  

   $$
   \eta = 0.75 \times 6 \times (1-0.07)
        = 0.75 \times 6 \times 0.93
        = 4.185\;\text{bits·s}^{-1}\text{·Hz}^{-1}
   $$

3. Net data rate  

   $$
   R = \eta \, B = 4.185 \times 20\!\times\!10^{6}
     = 83.7\!\times\!10^{6}\;\text{bits·s}^{-1}
     \approx \boxed{83.7\ \text{Mbps}}
   $$

If the same carrier used 16‑QAM ($k=4$) with the same code rate, $\eta$ would drop to $2.79$ bits/s/Hz, yielding only $55.8$ Mbps.

---

### 5. Common pitfalls and practical notes  

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | Correct practice |
|---------|---------------|------------------|
| **Ignoring $\alpha$** – treating the whole bandwidth as data‑carrying. | Overhead (CP, pilots, guard bands) is often a few percent but can be larger in high‑mobility or carrier‑aggregation scenarios. | Always subtract the known overhead fraction before reporting $\eta$. |
| **Mixing linear and dB SNR** when using (2). | $\log_2(1+\mathsf{SNR}_{\text{dB}})$ is meaningless. | Convert dB to linear: $\mathsf{SNR}_{\text{lin}} = 10^{\mathsf{SNR}_{\text{dB}}/10}$. |
| **Equating modulation order with spectral efficiency** (e.g., saying 64‑QAM ⇒ 6 bits/s/Hz). | This neglects coding rate and overhead, and assumes a symbol rate equal to the Nyquist rate. | Use (1) to incorporate $r$ and $(1-\alpha)$. |
| **Assuming $\eta$ is independent of channel bandwidth**. | In practice, larger $B$ may require larger guard bands, reducing the effective bandwidth. | Re‑evaluate $\alpha$ for each bandwidth allocation. |
| **Using Shannon bound as a design target**. | Real codes achieve only a fraction (≈80–90 %) of the bound, especially at low SNR. | Treat (2) as an upper limit; design AMC tables with a margin (e.g., 0.8 × $\eta_{\max}$). |

**Practical tip**: When reporting spectral efficiency to non‑technical stakeholders, express it both as *bits/s/Hz* and as *Mbps per carrier* (e.g., “4.2 bits/s/Hz ≈ 84 Mbps on a 20 MHz channel”). This dual presentation clarifies the abstract metric and its tangible impact on user experience.

## 練習題

**1.** A wireless link has an SNR of 15 dB. What is the theoretical maximum spectral efficiency according to Shannon’s formula?

- A. $\log_2(1+15)$
- B. $\log_2(1+31.62)$
- C. $\log_2(1+10)$
- D. $\log_2(1+100)$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。15 dB corresponds to a linear SNR of $10^{15/10}=31.62$, then $\eta_{\max}= \log_2(1+31.62)$.

</details>

**2.** An OFDM system uses 16‑QAM, a code rate of 0.5, and a total overhead of 7 %. What is its net spectral efficiency (bits/s/Hz)?

- A. 2.00
- B. 1.86
- C. 2.93
- D. 3.00

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。$k=\log_2 16=4$, so $\eta =0.5\times4\times(1-0.07)=1.86$ bits/s/Hz.

</details>

**3.** Which of the following does **not** directly reduce the spectral efficiency of a radio link?

- A. A coding rate lower than 1
- B. The cyclic prefix in OFDM
- C. Antenna gain
- D. Guard‑band allocation

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Antenna gain improves link budget but does not change the number of bits transmitted per Hertz.

</details>

**4.** A 10 MHz carrier uses 64‑QAM (k = 6), code rate 0.8, and 10 % total overhead. Compute the net data rate in Mbps. (Answer in Mbps)


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解：

1. Compute spectral efficiency: $\eta = r\,k\,(1-\alpha)=0.8\times6\times(1-0.10)=0.8\times6\times0.90=4.32\;$bits/s/Hz.  
2. Net data rate: $R = \eta \times B = 4.32 \times 10\times10^{6}=43.2\times10^{6}\;$bits/s.  
3. Convert to Mbps: $43.2\times10^{6}\,$bits/s $= 43.2\,$Mbps.  
\boxed{43.2}

</details>

**5.** A link achieves a spectral efficiency of 3.5 bits/s/Hz over a 5 MHz bandwidth. What is the minimum linear SNR required to meet this efficiency according to Shannon’s bound? (Answer is a linear ratio, not dB)


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解：

Shannon bound: $\eta_{\max}= \log_2(1+\mathsf{SNR})$.  
Set $\eta_{\max}=3.5$ and solve for $\mathsf{SNR}$:  
$1+\mathsf{SNR}=2^{3.5}\;$ → $\mathsf{SNR}=2^{3.5}-1$.  
Compute $2^{3.5}=2^{3}\times2^{0.5}=8\times1.41421356=11.3137085$.  
Thus $\mathsf{SNR}=11.3137085-1=10.3137085$.  
\boxed{10.3137085}

</details>
