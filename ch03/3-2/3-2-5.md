## 正文

Orthogonal Frequency‑Division Multiplexing (OFDM) is the dominant multicarrier waveform in modern cellular systems (e.g., LTE, NR) and many broadband wireless standards. By splitting a wideband channel into many narrow, mutually orthogonal sub‑carriers, OFDM converts a frequency‑selective fading channel into a set of flat‑fading sub‑channels. This simplifies equalisation, enables flexible spectrum allocation, and provides a natural framework for mapping higher‑layer resources such as modulation and coding schemes (MCS) onto a *resource grid*.

---

### 1. Core definitions

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $B$ | Total allocated channel bandwidth (including guard) | Hz |
| $\Delta f$ | Sub‑carrier spacing (distance between adjacent sub‑carriers) | Hz |
| $N$ | Number of *useful* sub‑carriers (FFT size after removing DC and guard) | – |
| $T_\text{u}$ | Useful OFDM symbol duration (inverse of $\Delta f$) | s |
| $T_\text{CP}$ | Cyclic‑prefix (CP) duration | s |
| $T_\text{sym}=T_\text{u}+T_\text{CP}$ | Full OFDM symbol duration | s |
| $N_\text{FFT}$ | Size of the IFFT/FFT (including null carriers) | – |
| $N_\text{RB}$ | Number of resource blocks (RB) in the frequency dimension | – |
| $N_\text{SC/RB}=12$ | Sub‑carriers per RB (LTE/NR definition) | – |
| $N_\text{sym}$ | Number of OFDM symbols that fit into a given time interval (e.g., a 1‑ms subframe) | – |
| $T_\text{subframe}$ | Duration of a subframe (typically 1 ms in LTE/NR) | s |
| $\alpha$ | CP‑to‑useful‑symbol ratio, $T_\text{CP}=\alpha T_\text{u}$ | – |

The **sub‑carrier spacing** is usually a fixed multiple of a reference value (e.g., 15 kHz in LTE, 30 kHz, 60 kHz,… in NR). The useful symbol time follows directly from orthogonality:

$$
T_\text{u}= \frac{1}{\Delta f}.
$$

A cyclic prefix, generated by copying the last $T_\text{CP}$ seconds of the time‑domain OFDM symbol to its front, protects against inter‑symbol interference (ISI) caused by multipath delay spread. Its length is often expressed as a fraction $\alpha$ of $T_\text{u}$:

$$
T_\text{CP}= \alpha\,T_\text{u},\qquad 0<\alpha<1.
$$

The **total OFDM symbol duration** is therefore

$$
T_\text{sym}= T_\text{u}+T_\text{CP}= (1+\alpha)T_\text{u}= \frac{1+\alpha}{\Delta f}.
$$

---

### 2. From bandwidth to FFT size

Given a channel bandwidth $B$ and a chosen sub‑carrier spacing $\Delta f$, the *theoretical* number of sub‑carriers is

$$
N_\text{theo}= \frac{B}{\Delta f}.
$$

In practice the IFFT length $N_\text{FFT}$ must be a power of two (or a supported radix) and must accommodate guard sub‑carriers (unused edge sub‑carriers) and the DC (zero‑frequency) carrier. The number of *useful* sub‑carriers $N$ is therefore

$$
N = N_\text{FFT} - N_\text{guard} - 1,
$$

where $N_\text{guard}$ counts the total edge nulls on both sides.

---

### 3. Resource Grid

The **resource grid** is a two‑dimensional lattice that enumerates all possible *resource elements* (REs). An RE is the smallest unit that can carry a complex modulation symbol (e.g., a QPSK point). Its axes are:

* **Frequency axis** – groups of $N_\text{SC/RB}=12$ adjacent sub‑carriers form a *resource block* (RB).  
  $$
  N_\text{RB}= \Big\lfloor\frac{N}{N_\text{SC/RB}}\Big\rfloor .
  $$

* **Time axis** – a set of $N_\text{sym}$ OFDM symbols (including CP) forms a *slot* (e.g., 0.5 ms in LTE) and a collection of slots forms a *subframe* (1 ms).

Thus the total number of REs in a subframe is

$$
N_\text{RE}= N_\text{RB}\times N_\text{sym}.
$$

Higher‑layer schedulers allocate one or more RBs to a user equipment (UE) per subframe and assign an appropriate MCS, which determines the number of bits per RE.

---

### 4. Worked example

*System parameters*  

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Bandwidth $B$ | 10 MHz |
| Sub‑carrier spacing $\Delta f$ | 15 kHz |
| CP ratio $\alpha$ (normal CP) | 0.07 (≈ 4.7 µs) |
| Guard band (each side) | 0.5 MHz |
| Subframe duration $T_\text{subframe}$ | 1 ms |

1. **Effective bandwidth** (after guard):  
   $$
   B_\text{eff}= B-2\times0.5\text{ MHz}=9\text{ MHz}.
   $$

2. **Number of useful sub‑carriers**:  
   $$
   N = \frac{B_\text{eff}}{\Delta f}= \frac{9\times10^{6}}{15\times10^{3}} = 600.
   $$

3. **FFT size**: the smallest power‑of‑two ≥ 600 is $N_\text{FFT}=1024$.  
   Edge guards: $N_\text{guard}=1024-600-1=423$ (distributed equally on both sides).

4. **Useful symbol time**:  
   $$
   T_\text{u}= \frac{1}{\Delta f}= \frac{1}{15\,000}=66.666\;\mu\text{s}.
   $$

5. **CP duration**:  
   $$
   T_\text{CP}= \alpha T_\text{u}=0.07\times66.666\;\mu\text{s}\approx4.667\;\mu\text{s}.
   $$

6. **Full OFDM symbol time**:  
   $$
   T_\text{sym}=T_\text{u}+T_\text{CP}\approx71.33\;\mu\text{s}.
   $$

7. **OFDM symbols per subframe**:  
   $$
   N_\text{sym}= \Big\lfloor\frac{T_\text{subframe}}{T_\text{sym}}\Big\rfloor
               =\Big\lfloor\frac{1000\;\mu\text{s}}{71.33\;\mu\text{s}}\Big\rfloor = 14.
   $$

8. **Resource blocks**:  
   $$
   N_\text{RB}= \Big\lfloor\frac{N}{12}\Big\rfloor = \Big\lfloor\frac{600}{12}\Big\rfloor = 50.
   $$

9. **Total REs per subframe**:  
   $$
   N_\text{RE}= N_\text{RB}\times N_\text{sym}= 50\times14 = 700.
   $$

A scheduler can therefore assign up to 700 modulation symbols (e.g., 2 bits per QPSK symbol → 1400 bits) to a single UE in that subframe, subject to control‑channel overhead and other users.

---

### 5. Common pitfalls and practical notes

| Pitfall | Why it hurts | Remedy |
|---------|--------------|--------|
| **Ignoring guard bands** – using $B$ directly in $N=B/\Delta f$ leads to over‑estimating sub‑carriers and may cause spectral leakage into adjacent bands. | Always subtract the guard‑band width before computing $N$. |
| **Treating $T_\text{CP}$ as a constant** – CP length varies with numerology (e.g., NR supports short CP for high $\Delta f$). | Use the CP‑to‑useful‑symbol ratio $\alpha$ appropriate for the chosen numerology. |
| **Mismatching FFT size and sub‑carrier count** – selecting an FFT that is too small forces spectral aliasing; too large wastes power and increases PAPR. | Choose the smallest power‑of‑two $N_\text{FFT}\ge N+1$ and verify that the resulting guard sub‑carriers meet spectral mask requirements. |
| **Assuming integer number of symbols per subframe** – rounding errors may lead to a leftover time that is silently dropped, reducing throughput. | Compute $N_\text{sym}$ with floor operation and, if needed, allocate the residual time to a *partial* symbol (e.g., for special control bursts). |
| **Confusing sub‑carrier index with physical frequency** – forgetting that the DC sub‑carrier is usually unused for PAPR reasons. | Remember the “‑1” term in $N = N_\text{FFT} - N_\text{guard} - 1$. |

Finally, the resource grid is the bridge between the PHY layer (OFDM symbols) and higher layers (scheduling, HARQ). Accurate bookkeeping of sub‑carriers, CP, and guard bands is essential for realistic capacity analysis and for training domain‑specific language models that must understand telecom terminology.
