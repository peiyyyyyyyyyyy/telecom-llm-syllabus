# 3.2.1 Shannon 通道容量

所屬：[3.2 容量](../3-2.md)

## 正文

In any digital communication system the ultimate question is: *how many information bits can be transmitted reliably over a given physical link?*  
The answer is provided by Claude Shannon’s landmark result of 1948, often called the **Shannon–Hartley theorem**. It gives an *upper bound*—the **channel capacity** $C$—on the achievable information rate (bits per second) for an additive white Gaussian‑noise (AWGN) channel of bandwidth $B$ when the received signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) is $\gamma$. Capacity is a theoretical benchmark; practical systems (modulation, coding, multiple‑access, fading, etc.) strive to approach it.

### Key definitions

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $C$    | Channel capacity (maximum achievable information rate) | bits · s$^{-1}$ (bit/s) |
| $B$    | One‑sided (positive‑frequency) bandwidth of the channel | hertz (Hz) |
| $S$    | Average received signal power | watt (W) |
| $N$    | Average noise power within $B$ | watt (W) |
| $\gamma$| Signal‑to‑noise ratio, linear | dimensionless |
| $\gamma_{\text{dB}}$| SNR expressed in decibels | dB |

The linear SNR is defined as  

$$
\gamma = \frac{S}{N}\;,\qquad
\gamma_{\text{dB}} = 10\log_{10}\!\gamma .
$$

### Shannon‑Hartley formula

For a real‑valued AWGN channel the capacity is  

$$
\boxed{ C = B\;\log_{2}\!\bigl(1+\gamma\bigr) }\tag{1}
$$

where $\log_{2}$ is the base‑2 logarithm.  

*If the channel is complex (baseband) the usable bandwidth is $2B$ and the capacity becomes $C = 2B\log_{2}(1+\gamma)$, but the textbook convention is to use the one‑sided $B$ and (1).*

**Spectral efficiency** $\eta$ follows directly:  

$$
\eta = \frac{C}{B}= \log_{2}\!\bigl(1+\gamma\bigr) \quad \text{[bits · s$^{-1}$ · Hz$^{-1}$]}.
$$

### Worked example

A cellular base‑station allocates a **20 MHz** carrier to a user. The measured SNR at the receiver is **10 dB**.

1. Convert SNR to linear scale  

$$
\gamma = 10^{\,10/10}=10 .
$$

2. Apply (1)  

$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= 20\times10^{6}\;\log_{2}\!\bigl(1+10\bigr)\\
  &= 20\times10^{6}\;\log_{2}(11)\\
  &\approx 20\times10^{6}\times 3.459\,\; \text{bits/s}\\
  &= 6.918\times10^{7}\;\text{bits/s}\; \approx 69.2\;\text{Mbps}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, under the ideal AWGN assumption the user could not exceed **≈ 69 Mbps** on that carrier, regardless of the modulation or coding used.

### Practical notes

* **Conversion pitfall** – SNR must be *linear* inside the logarithm. A common error is to insert $\gamma_{\text{dB}}$ directly, which yields a grossly over‑estimated capacity.  
* **Bandwidth definition** – $B$ in (1) is the *one‑sided* bandwidth (the positive‑frequency part). If a system specification gives a *double‑sided* bandwidth, halve it before using (1).  
* **Noise power** – $N = kTB$ for thermal noise ($k$ Boltzmann constant, $T$ absolute temperature). In practice a receiver’s noise figure $F$ adds a multiplicative factor: $N = FkTB$.  
* **Upper bound nature** – Real codes have finite block length and decoding complexity, so they achieve rates *below* $C$. Nevertheless, modern low‑density parity‑check (LDPC) and polar codes can operate within a few tenths of a dB of the Shannon limit.  
* **Fading channels** – Equation (1) assumes a stationary SNR. In mobile radio the instantaneous SNR varies; capacity is then expressed as an *average* over the fading distribution (e.g., ergodic capacity).  
* **Design implication** – Knowing $C$ guides the selection of modulation order $M$ (bits per symbol $\log_{2}M$) and coding rate $R_c$ so that $R_c\log_{2}M \le \eta$.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it’s wrong |
|---------|----------------|
| Using $\log_{10}$ instead of $\log_{2}$ | Capacity is measured in bits; base‑2 log is required. |
| Adding noise figure in dB to SNR in dB without conversion | Noise figure adds *linearly* to noise power, not to dB‑SNR. |
| Ignoring that $B$ must be in **Hz** (e.g., inserting MHz directly) | The logarithm is dimension‑less, but $B$ must be expressed in Hz to obtain bits/s. |
| Assuming capacity can be reached with any modulation | Capacity is independent of modulation; only coding can approach it. |
| Forgetting the “+1” inside the log when $\gamma \ll 1$ | For low SNR the term “1” dominates; dropping it leads to negative or undefined capacities. |

Understanding Shannon capacity equips engineers with a clear performance ceiling, enabling rational trade‑offs between bandwidth, power, and complexity in modern wireless systems.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following expressions correctly gives the Shannon capacity of a real‑valued AWGN channel with bandwidth $B$ (Hz) and linear SNR $\gamma$?

- A. $C = B\log_{2}(1+\gamma)$
- B. $C = 2B\log_{2}(1+\gamma)$
- C. $C = B\log_{10}(1+\gamma)$
- D. $C = B\,(1+\gamma)$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。The Shannon–Hartley theorem for a real‑valued channel is $C = B\log_{2}(1+\gamma)$. Option B applies to a complex baseband representation, C uses the wrong logarithm base, and D omits the logarithm entirely.

</details>

**2.** A channel has a one‑sided bandwidth of $5\,$MHz and an SNR of $20\,$dB. What is its theoretical Shannon capacity (rounded to the nearest megabit per second)?

- A. $33\,$Mbps
- B. $16\,$Mbps
- C. $5\,$Mbps
- D. $100\,$Mbps

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。$20\,$dB → $\gamma=10^{2}=100$. $C=5\!\times\!10^{6}\log_{2}(1+100)\approx5\!\times\!10^{6}\times6.658\approx33.3\,$Mbps.

</details>

**3.** Which statement about Shannon capacity is **FALSE**?

- A. It assumes additive white Gaussian noise.
- B. It is independent of the modulation format used.
- C. It can be achieved with sufficiently long and complex codes.
- D. It increases without bound as the transmitted power grows while bandwidth stays fixed.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：D。Capacity grows as $\log_{2}(1+\gamma)$; even with infinite power the increase is logarithmic, not unbounded. The other statements are true.

</details>

**4.** Compute the Shannon capacity (in Mbps) for a channel with bandwidth $B = 10\,$MHz and SNR $= 15\,$dB.  (Give the answer to one decimal place.)


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Convert SNR to linear: $\gamma = 10^{15/10}=10^{1.5}=31.6227766$.
2. Apply (1): $C = 10\times10^{6}\,\log_{2}(1+\gamma)$.
3. $1+\gamma = 32.6227766$.
4. $\log_{2}(32.6227766)=\dfrac{\ln 32.6227766}{\ln 2}\approx\frac{3.485}{0.6931}=5.025$.
5. $C = 10\times10^{6}\times5.025 = 5.025\times10^{7}\,\text{bit/s}=50.25\,$Mbps.
$\boxed{50.3}$

</details>

**5.** A link has signal power $S = 5\,$mW, noise power $N = 0.5\,$mW, and allocated bandwidth $B = 2.5\,$MHz. Compute its Shannon capacity in Mbps (rounded to two decimal places).


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Linear SNR: $\gamma = S/N = 5/0.5 = 10$.
2. Use (1): $C = 2.5\times10^{6}\,\log_{2}(1+10)$.
3. $1+10 = 11$.
4. $\log_{2}(11)=\dfrac{\ln 11}{\ln 2}\approx\frac{2.3979}{0.6931}=3.45943$.
5. $C = 2.5\times10^{6}\times3.45943 = 8.648575\times10^{6}\,$bit/s = $8.648575$ Mbps.
$\boxed{8.65}$

</details>
