# 3.2.6 MIMO 容量

所屬：[3.2 容量](../3-2.md)

## 正文

Multiple‑Input Multiple‑Output (MIMO) technology exploits spatial multiplexing by employing several transmit and receive antennas on the same carrier frequency. In a real cellular or wireless LAN network, MIMO is the main engine behind the dramatic increase of data rates from a few megabits per second (Mbps) to several gigabits per second (Gbps) without requiring additional spectrum. Understanding the *capacity* of a MIMO link—i.e., the theoretical maximum achievable spectral efficiency under given physical constraints—allows system designers to dimension antenna arrays, power amplifiers, and coding schemes, and to evaluate the benefit of advanced techniques such as beamforming or water‑filling.

---

### 1. Channel model and basic definitions  

Consider a narrow‑band (flat‑fading) point‑to‑point MIMO channel with  

* $N_t$ = number of transmit antennas,  
* $N_r$ = number of receive antennas,  

and a complex channel matrix  

$$
\mathbf H \in \mathbb{C}^{N_r\times N_t},
$$

whose entry $h_{ij}$ models the complex gain from transmit antenna $j$ to receive antenna $i$. The baseband input–output relation is  

$$
\mathbf y = \mathbf H\mathbf x + \mathbf n,
$$

where  

* $\mathbf x\in\mathbb{C}^{N_t}$ is the transmitted signal vector (average total power $P$ [W]),  
* $\mathbf y\in\mathbb{C}^{N_r}$ is the received vector,  
* $\mathbf n\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\sigma^2\mathbf I_{N_r})$ is additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) with variance $\sigma^2$ [W] per receive dimension.

Define the **signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR)** per receive antenna  

$$
\rho \;=\; \frac{P}{\sigma^2}\quad\text{(dimensionless)}.
$$

When the transmitter has no channel state information (CSI) and distributes its power equally over the $N_t$ antennas, the per‑antenna SNR becomes $\rho/N_t$.

---

### 2. MIMO capacity with equal power allocation  

With perfect CSI at the receiver (but not at the transmitter) and a total power constraint $P$, the *ergodic* capacity (bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$) is given by the celebrated Telatar‑Foschini formula  

$$
\boxed{%
C = \log_2\!\det\!\Bigl(\mathbf I_{N_r}
      + \frac{\rho}{N_t}\,\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}\Bigr)
      \;\;[\text{bits/s/Hz}]
}
\tag{1}
$$

where  

* $\mathbf I_{N_r}$ is the $N_r\times N_r$ identity matrix,  
* $\mathbf H^{\dagger}$ denotes the Hermitian (conjugate‑transpose) of $\mathbf H$,  
* $\det(\cdot)$ is the matrix determinant,  
* $\log_2(\cdot)$ is the base‑2 logarithm.

Equation (1) can also be expressed through the eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ of $\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}$ (there are $\min\{N_t,N_r\}$ non‑zero eigenvalues):

$$
C = \sum_{i=1}^{\min(N_t,N_r)} \log_2\!\Bigl(1+\frac{\rho}{N_t}\lambda_i\Bigr).
\tag{2}
$$

The term *spatial multiplexing gain* (or *degrees of freedom*) equals $\min(N_t,N_r)$; at high SNR the capacity grows linearly with this number.

---

### 3. Worked example (identity channel)

Assume a $2\times2$ MIMO link with the following parameters  

* $N_t = N_r = 2$,  
* total transmit power $P = 1\;\text{W}$,  
* noise variance $\sigma^2 = 0.1\;\text{W}$,  
* channel matrix $\mathbf H = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0\\0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ (perfectly orthogonal sub‑channels).

First compute the SNR  

$$
\rho = \frac{P}{\sigma^2}= \frac{1}{0.1}=10.
$$

Because $N_t=2$, the per‑antenna SNR is $\rho/N_t = 5$.

$$
\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}= \mathbf I_{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathbf I_{2}+ \frac{\rho}{N_t}\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}
= \mathbf I_{2}+5\mathbf I_{2}=6\mathbf I_{2}.
$$

The determinant of a diagonal matrix is the product of its diagonal entries, hence  

$$
\det\bigl(6\mathbf I_{2}\bigr)=6^2 = 36.
$$

Finally, the capacity per Hertz is  

$$
C = \log_2(36)=2\log_2(6)\approx 2\times2.585 = 5.17\;\text{bits/s/Hz}.
$$

Thus a $2\times2$ orthogonal MIMO link can theoretically deliver more than **5 bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$**, roughly double the capacity of a single‑antenna link under the same SNR.

---

### 4. Practical notes and common pitfalls  

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | How to avoid it |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Omitting the $1/N_t$ factor** | The formula (1) assumes *equal* power across all transmit antennas. Forgetting the division by $N_t$ over‑estimates capacity, especially when $N_t\gg N_r$. | Always start from the total SNR $\rho=P/\sigma^2$ and explicitly write $\rho/N_t$ before forming the matrix inside the determinant. |
| **Using the raw channel matrix instead of $\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}$** | Capacity depends on the *singular values* of $\mathbf H$, not on individual entries. | Compute $\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}$ (or perform an SVD) to obtain eigenvalues $\lambda_i$. |
| **Assuming the same result for correlated channels** | Spatial correlation reduces the eigenvalue spread, shrinking the determinant and thus capacity. | Model correlation (e.g., Kronecker model) and include it in $\mathbf H$; verify the eigenvalue distribution. |
| **Neglecting the effect of CSI at the transmitter** | If the transmitter knows $\mathbf H$, water‑filling across eigen‑modes yields higher capacity than equal power. | Distinguish between “no‑CSI” (eq. 1) and “full‑CSI” (water‑filling) scenarios; use the appropriate formula. |
| **Confusing bits/s/Hz with absolute data rate** | Capacity in (1) is *spectral efficiency*. To obtain an absolute bit rate, multiply by the occupied bandwidth $B$ (Hz). | Remember $R = C \times B$ (bits s$^{-1}$). |

In practice, the achievable throughput is further limited by modulation order, coding rate (see Sections 3.2.3–3.2.4), and the overhead of OFDM resource grids (Section 3.2.5). Nevertheless, the MIMO capacity expression provides a fundamental benchmark for any multi‑antenna system design.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following expressions correctly gives the capacity (bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$) of a narrow‑band $N_t\times N_r$ MIMO channel with total transmit power $P$, noise variance $\sigma^2$, and equal power allocation across the transmit antennas?

- A. $C = \log_2\!\det\!\bigl(\mathbf I_{N_r} + \frac{P}{\sigma^2}\,\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}\bigr)$
- B. $C = \log_2\!\det\!\bigl(\mathbf I_{N_r} + \frac{P}{N_t\sigma^2}\,\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}\bigr)$
- C. $C = N_t\,\log_2\!\bigl(1+\frac{P}{N_t\sigma^2}\bigr)$
- D. $C = \sum_{i=1}^{\min(N_t,N_r)}\log_2\!\bigl(1+\frac{P}{\sigma^2}\lambda_i\bigr)$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The equal‑power constraint divides the total SNR $\rho=P/\sigma^2$ by $N_t$, giving the factor $\frac{P}{N_t\sigma^2}$ inside the determinant.

</details>

**2.** For a $4\times4$ i.i.d. Rayleigh MIMO channel at asymptotically high SNR, the multiplexing (spatial‑degrees‑of‑freedom) gain equals

- A. 1
- B. 2
- C. 3
- D. 4

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：D。The maximum number of independent data streams equals $\min(N_t,N_r)=4$.

</details>

**3.** Which statement about water‑filling power allocation in a MIMO system with full CSI at the transmitter is true?

- A. Water‑filling always allocates the same power to every eigen‑mode, regardless of channel gains.
- B. Water‑filling concentrates all power on the strongest eigen‑mode and turns off the others.
- C. Water‑filling distributes power inversely proportional to the eigen‑values of $\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}$.
- D. Water‑filling allocates more power to eigen‑modes with larger eigen‑values, but still uses all modes unless a mode is extremely weak.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：D。Water‑filling raises a water level over the inverse of each eigen‑value; stronger modes receive more power, weaker ones may be turned off only if their inverse exceeds the water level.

</details>

**4.** Compute the MIMO capacity (bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$) for a $2\times2$ system with $P=5\,$W, $\sigma^2=0.5\,$W, and channel matrix $\mathbf H=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. SNR: $\rho = P/\sigma^2 = 5/0.5 = 10$.  
2. Per‑antenna factor: $\rho/N_t = 10/2 = 5$.  
3. $\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}= \begin{bmatrix}4&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$.  
4. Inside determinant: $\mathbf I_2 + 5\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}= \begin{bmatrix}1+5\cdot4 & 0\\0 & 1+5\cdot1\end{bmatrix}= \begin{bmatrix}21&0\\0&6\end{bmatrix}$.  
5. Determinant: $21\times6 =126$.  
6. Capacity: $C=\log_2(126) \approx 6.97\,$bits/s/Hz.  
$\boxed{6.97}$

</details>

**5.** A $3\times2$ MIMO link has total transmit power $P=2\,$W, noise variance $\sigma^2=0.2\,$W, and channel matrix  
$$
\mathbf H=\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0.5 & -0.3\\
0.2 & -1 & 0.4
\end{bmatrix}.
$$  
Compute the capacity (bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$).


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. $\rho = P/\sigma^2 = 2/0.2 = 10$.  
2. $\rho/N_t = 10/3 \approx 3.3333$.  
3. Compute $\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}$:  
   * Row 1·Row 1$^{\!*}$ = $1^2+0.5^2+(-0.3)^2 = 1.34$.  
   * Row 1·Row 2$^{\!*}$ = $1\cdot0.2 + 0.5\cdot(-1) + (-0.3)\cdot0.4 = -0.42$.  
   * Row 2·Row 2$^{\!*}$ = $0.2^2+(-1)^2+0.4^2 = 1.20$.  
   Hence $\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}= \begin{bmatrix}1.34 & -0.42\\ -0.42 & 1.20\end{bmatrix}$.  
4. Multiply by $\rho/N_t$: $3.3333\!\times\!\mathbf H\mathbf H^{\dagger}= \begin{bmatrix}4.4667 & -1.4\\ -1.4 & 4.0\end{bmatrix}$.  
5. Add identity: $\mathbf I_2 + \dots = \begin{bmatrix}5.4667 & -1.4\\ -1.4 & 5.0\end{bmatrix}$.  
6. Determinant: $5.4667\times5.0 - (-1.4)^2 = 27.3335 - 1.96 = 25.3735$.  
7. Capacity: $C = \log_2(25.3735) \approx 4.66\,$bits/s/Hz.  
$\boxed{4.66}$

</details>
