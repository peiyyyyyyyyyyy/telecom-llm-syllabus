## 正文

In modern digital communication systems the relationship among **bit rate** ($R_b$), **symbol rate** (or **baud rate**, $R_s$), **bandwidth** ($B$) and the **Nyquist criterion** determines how efficiently a physical channel can be utilized.  Engineers use these fundamentals to dimension links, allocate spectrum, and select modulation formats that meet latency, throughput, and regulatory constraints.

---

### 1. Key definitions  

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $R_b$ | Net information **bit rate** (payload bits per second) | bits s$^{-1}$ |
| $R_s$ | **Symbol rate** (how many signalling intervals are transmitted per second) | symbols s$^{-1}$ (baud) |
| $M$ | Modulation order (size of the constellation) | – |
| $k$ | Bits per symbol, $k=\log_2 M$ | bits symbol$^{-1}$ |
| $B$ | **Two‑sided** signal bandwidth (frequency range that contains the bulk of the signal power) | Hz |
| $\alpha$ | **Roll‑off factor** of a raised‑cosine (RC) or root‑raised‑cosine (RRC) pulse, $0\le\alpha\le 1$ | – |

The **spectral efficiency** of a signalling scheme is the ratio  

$$
\eta = \frac{R_b}{B}\quad\text{[bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$]}.
$$

---

### 2. Bit rate and symbol rate  

The fundamental relationship is  

$$
\boxed{R_b = k\,R_s}\tag{1}
$$

because each transmitted symbol carries $k$ bits of information.  For binary signalling ($M=2$, $k=1$) the bit rate equals the symbol rate; for higher‑order constellations the bit rate is multiplied by $\log_2 M$.

---

### 3. Nyquist bandwidth for zero ISI  

Nyquist showed that, with an ideal **sinc** pulse (infinite time support), inter‑symbol interference (ISI) can be eliminated if the minimum **Nyquist bandwidth** is  

$$
\boxed{B_{\text{Nyq}} = \frac{R_s}{2}}\tag{2}
$$

for a **baseband** (real‑valued) signal.  The factor ½ appears because a real baseband signal occupies only the positive‑frequency half of the spectrum; the negative half mirrors it.

For a **passband** (carrier‑modulated) signal the occupied bandwidth doubles, giving  

$$
B_{\text{Nyq,pass}} = R_s.\tag{3}
$$

Equation (2) is a theoretical lower bound; practical pulse shaping introduces excess bandwidth.

---

### 4. Raised‑cosine pulse shaping  

A raised‑cosine (RC) or root‑raised‑cosine (RRC) pulse with roll‑off $\alpha$ expands the required bandwidth to  

$$
\boxed{B = \frac{R_s}{2}\,(1+\alpha)}\tag{4}
$$

for a baseband signal.  When $\alpha=0$ the RC pulse reduces to the ideal sinc, achieving the Nyquist limit.  Typical values are $\alpha=0.2$–$0.35$, balancing spectral compactness and timing tolerance.

Combining (1) and (4) yields the **ideal spectral efficiency** (with $\alpha=0$)  

$$
\eta_{\max}= \frac{R_b}{B_{\text{Nyq}}}= \frac{kR_s}{R_s/2}=2k\quad\text{[bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$]}.
$$

With excess bandwidth the efficiency degrades to  

$$
\eta = \frac{2k}{1+\alpha}.
$$

---

### 5. Worked example  

A cellular downlink uses **64‑QAM** ($M=64$, $k=\log_2 64 = 6$ bits/symbol) with a symbol rate of $R_s = 5\,$Msps.  The transmitter employs a root‑raised‑cosine filter with roll‑off $\alpha = 0.20$.

1. **Occupied bandwidth** (baseband) from (4):  

$$
B = \frac{5\times10^6}{2}\,(1+0.20)=2.5\times10^6 \times 1.20 = 3.0\times10^6\ \text{Hz}=3\ \text{MHz}.
$$

2. **Net bit rate** from (1):  

$$
R_b = k\,R_s = 6 \times 5\times10^6 = 30\times10^6\ \text{bits/s}=30\ \text{Mbps}.
$$

3. **Spectral efficiency**:  

$$
\eta = \frac{R_b}{B}= \frac{30\ \text{Mbps}}{3\ \text{MHz}} = 10\ \text{bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$}.
$$

If the same system were forced to use an ideal sinc pulse ($\alpha=0$), the bandwidth would shrink to $B_{\text{Nyq}} = 2.5\,$MHz and the efficiency would increase to $12$ bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$.

---

### 6. Practical notes & common pitfalls  

| Pitfall | Why it is wrong | Correct approach |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Confusing $R_b$ and $R_s$** | Assuming a 16‑QAM link with $R_s=10$ Msps also has $R_b=10$ Mbps. | Use $R_b = kR_s$ with $k=\log_2 M$. |
| **Neglecting roll‑off** | Computing bandwidth as $B=R_s/2$ for any RC‑shaped signal. | Apply (4): $B = (1+\alpha)R_s/2$. |
| **Using the wrong factor of 2** | Applying (2) to a passband carrier system and under‑estimating required spectrum. | For passband, bandwidth is $B=R_s$ (or $B=(1+\alpha)R_s$ with excess). |
| **Assuming Nyquist limit is always reachable** | Believing a real‑world system can use a sinc pulse without penalty. | Real filters need $\alpha>0$; design for the required roll‑off and margin for filter implementation. |
| **Forgetting units** | Mixing symbols/s with bits/s or Hz with MHz. | Keep units consistent; convert MHz → Hz before substitution. |

Understanding the interplay of these quantities allows engineers to **trade off** modulation order, symbol timing, and spectral occupancy, which is the essence of link budgeting and spectrum planning in 4G/5G NR, Wi‑Fi, and satellite systems.

## 練習題

**1.** A baseband system uses a raised‑cosine pulse with roll‑off factor $\alpha=0.25$ and a symbol rate of $10\,$Msps. What is the minimum 3‑dB bandwidth required?

- A. $5\,$MHz
- B. $6.25\,$MHz
- C. $12.5\,$MHz
- D. $15\,$MHz

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Using $B = (1+\alpha)R_s/2 = 1.25 \times 5\,$MHz = $6.25\,$MHz.

</details>

**2.** An ideal Nyquist channel carries 16‑QAM at a symbol rate of $8\,$Msps. What is the maximum achievable net bit rate?

- A. $16\,$Mbps
- B. $32\,$Mbps
- C. $64\,$Mbps
- D. $128\,$Mbps

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。16‑QAM carries $k=\log_2 16=4$ bits/symbol, so $R_b = 4 \times 8\,$Msps = $32\,$Mbps.

</details>

**3.** Which of the following statements about the Nyquist criterion is true?

- A. It limits the bit rate only for binary signalling.
- B. It requires a rectangular pulse shape.
- C. It gives the minimum bandwidth for zero ISI when sinc pulses are used.
- D. It is irrelevant for passband modulation.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Nyquist’s first criterion states that a sinc‑shaped pulse achieves zero ISI with the minimum bandwidth $B=R_s/2$.

</details>

**4.** A QPSK system (M = 4) transmits at a symbol rate of $7\,$Msps using a raised‑cosine filter with roll‑off factor $\alpha = 0.35$. Compute the occupied bandwidth $B$ in Hz.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Determine bits per symbol: $k = \log_2 4 = 2$ (not needed for bandwidth).  
2. Apply the raised‑cosine bandwidth formula (4): $B = \frac{R_s}{2}\,(1+\alpha)$.  
3. Insert $R_s = 7\times10^{6}\,$sym/s and $\alpha = 0.35$:  
   $B = \frac{7\times10^{6}}{2}\times(1+0.35) = 3.5\times10^{6}\times1.35 = 4.725\times10^{6}\,$Hz.

</details>

**5.** A link uses 256‑QAM ($M=256$) and occupies a $20\,$MHz channel. The pulse shaping is a raised‑cosine with roll‑off $\alpha = 0.15$. Assuming ideal Nyquist signalling, compute the maximum net bit rate $R_b$ (in bits per second) that can be carried.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Bits per symbol: $k = \log_2 256 = 8$ bits/symbol.  
2. For a baseband raised‑cosine, the relation between bandwidth and symbol rate is $B = \frac{R_s}{2}(1+\alpha)$. Solve for $R_s$: $R_s = \frac{2B}{1+\alpha}$.  
3. Insert $B = 20\times10^{6}$ Hz and $\alpha = 0.15$:  
   $R_s = \frac{2 \times 20\times10^{6}}{1.15} = \frac{40\times10^{6}}{1.15} \approx 34.7826087\times10^{6}$ sym/s.  
4. Net bit rate: $R_b = k\,R_s = 8 \times 34.7826087\times10^{6} \approx 278.2608696\times10^{6}$ bits/s.  
$\boxed{278260870}$

</details>
