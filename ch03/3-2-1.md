# 3.2.1 Shannon 通道容量

所屬：[3.2 容量](3-2.md)

## 內文

#### 為什麼要學

Shannon 通道容量（channel capacity）回答一個最根本的問題：給定頻寬與訊號雜訊比，一條鏈路最多能可靠傳多少位元。電信網路裡，它是所有速率估算的上限參考：做鏈路預算時，用它把接收端的 SNR 換成「最多可能的速率」；評估新頻段或載波聚合時，用它判斷加頻寬划不划算；看基地台的調變與編碼方案（MCS）選得保不保守時，也是拿實際頻譜效率和 Shannon 上限相比。它同時說明了兩種完全不同的系統設計方向：頻寬不夠時要靠提高 SNR 與高階調變，功率不夠時則要靠加大頻寬與低碼率編碼。

#### 定義與符號

| 符號 | 意思 | 單位 |
|---|---|---|
| $C$ | 通道容量，可靠傳輸速率的上限 | bit/s |
| $B$ | 通道頻寬 | Hz |
| $S$（或 $P$） | 接收到的平均訊號功率 | W |
| $N_0$ | 單邊雜訊功率頻譜密度 | W/Hz |
| $N = N_0 B$ | 頻寬 $B$ 內的雜訊功率 | W |
| $\text{SNR} = S/N$ | 訊號雜訊比（線性值，不是 dB） | 無單位 |
| $E_b$ | 每個資訊位元的接收能量，$E_b = S/R$ | J |
| $R$ | 實際資訊速率 | bit/s |
| $\eta = R/B$ | 頻譜效率 | bit/s/Hz |

#### 公式與推導

對於頻寬受限、平均功率受限的加成性白高斯雜訊（additive white Gaussian noise, AWGN）通道，容量為

$$C = B\log_2\!\left(1+\frac{S}{N}\right) = B\log_2\!\left(1+\frac{S}{N_0 B}\right)$$

這條公式的前提要記清楚：雜訊是白色高斯雜訊、通道增益固定且已知、發射訊號平均功率受限、輸入採高斯分布、而且允許任意長的編碼。它是「速率低於 $C$ 時錯誤率可以任意小」的界線，不是任何一種實際調變的速率公式。公式裡的 SNR 一定要用線性值。

依 SNR 的大小可分成兩個區域：

- **頻寬受限區**（$\text{SNR}\gg 1$）：$C \approx B\log_2(\text{SNR})$。SNR 每增加 3 dB（功率加倍），每 Hz 只多 1 bit/s；頻寬加倍則容量近乎加倍。這時頻寬是稀缺資源。
- **功率受限區**（$\text{SNR}\ll 1$）：利用 $\ln(1+x)\approx x$，得 $C \approx \dfrac{S}{N_0 \ln 2} \approx 1.4427\,\dfrac{S}{N_0}$。容量和功率成正比，幾乎和頻寬無關；頻寬趨近無限大時，容量收斂到這個有限值。

**Shannon 極限**：令速率等於容量 $R=C$，並把 $S = E_b R$ 代入，得 $\eta = \log_2\!\left(1+\eta\,E_b/N_0\right)$，也就是

$$\frac{E_b}{N_0} = \frac{2^{\eta}-1}{\eta}$$

這是在頻譜效率 $\eta$ 下可靠通訊所需的最小 $E_b/N_0$。當 $\eta\to 0$（頻寬無限大）時，$2^{\eta}-1 \approx \eta\ln 2$，所以

$$\left.\frac{E_b}{N_0}\right|_{\min} = \ln 2 = 0.6931,\qquad 10\log_{10}(0.6931) = -1.59\ \text{dB}$$

不論用多大的頻寬、多強的編碼，$E_b/N_0$ 低於 −1.59 dB 就不可能可靠傳輸。$\eta$ 越高，所需的 $E_b/N_0$ 越大：$\eta=1$ 時為 0 dB，$\eta=2$ 時為 $1.5$（1.76 dB），$\eta=6$ 時為 $10.5$（10.21 dB）。

#### 算例

**算例 1（頻寬受限區）** 已知頻寬 $B=20$ MHz，接收 SNR 為 15 dB，求容量。

1. SNR 換成線性：$10^{15/10} = 31.62$。
2. 代入：$\log_2(1+31.62) = \log_2(32.62) = 5.028$ bit/s/Hz。
3. $C = 20\times10^{6}\times 5.028 = 1.0056\times10^{8}$ bit/s，約 100.6 Mbps（取到小數第 1 位）。
4. 合理性檢查：SNR 接近 32，$\log_2 32 = 5$，結果略大於 5 bit/s/Hz，合理。實際系統因為編碼長度有限、參考訊號與控制通道占用資源，單層速率會明顯低於這個值。

**算例 2（功率受限區與頻寬的效益）** 假設接收功率 $S=-100$ dBm，雜訊密度 $N_0=-174$ dBm/Hz。比較頻寬 1 MHz 與 100 MHz 的容量，並求頻寬無限大時的極限。

1. $S/N_0 = -100-(-174) = 74$ dB·Hz，線性值 $10^{7.4} = 2.512\times10^{7}$ Hz。
2. $B=1$ MHz：$\text{SNR} = 2.512\times10^{7}/10^{6} = 25.12$（14 dB），$C = 10^{6}\times\log_2(26.12) = 4.707$ Mbps。
3. $B=100$ MHz：$\text{SNR} = 0.2512$（−6 dB），$C = 10^{8}\times\log_2(1.2512) = 32.33$ Mbps。
4. 極限：$C_\infty = 2.512\times10^{7}/0.6931 = 3.624\times10^{7}$ bit/s，即 36.24 Mbps。
5. 合理性檢查：頻寬放大 100 倍，容量只變成 $32.33/4.707 = 6.87$ 倍，而且已經達到極限值的 89%；再加頻寬幾乎沒有好處，這時該做的是提高接收功率。

#### 常見錯誤

- 把 dB 值直接代進 $\log_2(1+\text{SNR})$。15 dB 要先換成 31.62，不能代 15。
- 把 $\log_2$ 算成 $\log_{10}$ 或 $\ln$。換底：$\log_2 x = \ln x/\ln 2 = \log_{10}x/0.30103$。
- 以為頻寬加倍、容量一定加倍。接收功率固定時，頻寬加倍會讓雜訊功率也加倍、SNR 減半；只有在高 SNR 區才近似加倍。
- 把 −1.59 dB 當成 SNR 的下限。它是 $E_b/N_0$ 的下限；展頻或低碼率系統的 SNR 可以遠低於 0 dB 仍正常運作。

#### 和其他小節的關係

公式用到的 SNR 與 $E_b/N_0$ 換算在 3.1.6，雜訊功率 $N_0B$ 在 3.1.5。把容量除以頻寬就是 3.2.2 的頻譜效率上限；3.2.3、3.2.4 的調變與 MCS 是逼近這個上限的實際手段；3.2.6 把公式推廣到多天線；3.7 細項則從資訊理論說明容量的由來。

**3.2.1S033**　The table shows the required Eb/N0 rising from 2.5 dB to 12.0 dB as η goes from 1 to 5. Is that a weakness of the team's codes, or is it fundamental? Explain.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　It is fundamental. **詳解**　(2¹−1)/1 = 1 → 0 dB; 7/3 → 3.68 dB; 31/5 = 6.2 → 7.92 dB. Gaps: 2.5 − 0 = 2.5 dB; 3.12 dB; 12.0 − 7.92 = 4.08 dB.

**詳解**　(2^η − 1)/η → ln 2 as η → 0; 2^η − 1 → 0.

#### 情境題：Capacity with interference and several carriers

**Scenario | Interference-limited urban cell edge**

A user at the edge of an urban cell on a 10 MHz carrier receives the wanted signal at −95 dBm. The total interference from neighbouring cells is −97 dBm and the receiver noise is −104 dBm, all in the same 10 MHz. Interference and noise are uncorrelated, so their powers add, and for this estimate the interference is treated as additional Gaussian noise. Use $C = B\log_2(1+\text{SINR})$ with a base-2 logarithm and a linear SINR.

**3.2.1S056**　What is the achievable rate with interference treated as noise? Give the answer in Mbit/s, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　12.15 Mbit/s

**詳解**　Step 1: I = 10^(−9.7) = 1.99526×10⁻¹⁰ mW; N = 10^(−10.4) = 3.98107×10⁻¹¹ mW; I + N = 2.39337×10⁻¹⁰ mW = −96.2099 dBm.
Step 2: SINR = −95 − (−96.2099) = 1.2099 dB → 1.32127.
Step 3: C = 10×10⁶ × log2(2.32127) = 10×10⁶ × 1.21491 = 12.15 Mbit/s.

**出處**　A. Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications* (SINR and capacity with interference treated as noise).

</details>

**3.2.1S057**　To show the cost of interference, an engineer computes the capacity the user would have without any interference. Which step is wrong, why, and what is the correct capacity in Mbit/s (two decimal places)?
1. SNR = −95 − (−104) = 9 dB.
2. SNR (linear) = 10^0.9 = 7.943.
3. C = 10×10⁶ × ln(1 + 7.943) = 21.91 Mbit/s.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 3 is wrong: capacity in bit/s needs a base-2 logarithm; ln gives nats/s. Correct capacity = 31.61 Mbit/s.

**詳解**　log2(8.94328) = ln(8.94328)/ln 2 = 2.19090/0.693147 = 3.16080 bit/s/Hz.
C = 10×10⁶ × 3.16080 = 31.61 Mbit/s (so interference removes about 19.5 Mbit/s).

**出處**　T. M. Cover and J. A. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory* (bits versus nats).

</details>

**3.2.1S058**　Management proposes to raise the transmit power of every cell in the cluster by 6 dB. What happens to this user's SINR and rate, and why?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Very little improves. The wanted signal and the interference both rise by 6 dB, so only the noise share of the denominator shrinks: SINR rises from 1.21 dB to 1.79 dB and the rate from 12.15 to 13.27 Mbit/s (+9 %). The user is interference-limited; the SINR can never exceed S/I = 2 dB however much power is used. Interference coordination, beamforming or a better serving cell would help more.

**詳解**　S = −89 dBm, I = −91 dBm, N = −104 dBm: I + N = 8.34139×10⁻¹⁰ mW = −90.7876 dBm; SINR = 1.7876 dB → 1.50925; C = 10×10⁶ × log2(2.50925) = 13.27 Mbit/s.

**出處**　A. Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications* (interference-limited cellular systems).

</details>

**3.2.1S059**　Under what condition is it reasonable to put SINR into the Shannon formula, and what does the result represent?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　It is reasonable when the interference is the sum of many independent signals (so it looks approximately Gaussian), when the receiver does not try to decode or cancel it, and when the SINR is stable over a code block. The result is then an achievable rate with single-user decoding ("interference treated as noise"), not the true capacity of the interference channel: a receiver that decodes or cancels a strong interferer could do better, while a bursty or structured interferer could make the estimate optimistic for a practical receiver.

**詳解**　C = B·log2(1 + S/(I + N)) follows from the AWGN formula when I + N is modelled as Gaussian noise of power I + N.

**出處**　A. Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications* (interference treated as noise); D. Tse and P. Viswanath, *Fundamentals of Wireless Communication* (interference management).

</details>

**3.2.1S060**　A second user in the same area receives −92 dBm with the same interference (−97 dBm) and noise (−104 dBm). A student computes as follows. Which step is wrong, why, and what is the correct rate in Mbit/s (two decimal places)?
1. I + N = 2.393×10⁻¹⁰ mW = −96.21 dBm.

**答案**　Step 3 is wrong: the SINR was inserted in dB. It must be converted to linear first (10^0.421 = 2.636). Correct rate = 18.62 Mbit/s.

**詳解**　SINR = 4.2099 dB → 2.63627.
C = 10×10⁶ × log2(3.63627) = 10×10⁶ × 1.86246 = 18.62 Mbit/s.

**出處**　A. Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications* (SINR as a linear ratio).

**Scenario | Spreading power over two carriers in a small cell**

A small cell has two 20 MHz carriers in the same band. For a given user both carriers have the same path gain, and the noise in each carrier is −100 dBm (noise figure included). If the full transmit power is put on one carrier, the user receives −85 dBm on it. The channels are AWGN and independent; use $C = B\log_2(1+\text{SNR})$ per carrier (base 2, linear SNR) and add the capacities of the carriers.

**3.2.1S061**　What is the Shannon capacity if all the power is put on a single carrier? Give the answer in Mbit/s, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　100.56 Mbit/s

**詳解**　Step 1: SNR = −85 − (−100) = 15 dB → 31.6228.
Step 2: log2(32.6228) = 5.02781 bit/s/Hz.
Step 3: C = 20×10⁶ × 5.02781 = 100.56 Mbit/s.

**出處**　C. E. Shannon (1949).

</details>

**3.2.1S062**　The power is instead split equally between the two carriers. How much more total capacity does that give? Give the answer in Mbit/s, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　62.30 Mbit/s

**詳解**　Step 1: each carrier receives half the power: SNR = 31.6228/2 = 15.8114 (11.99 dB).
Step 2: per carrier C = 20×10⁶ × log2(16.8114) = 20×10⁶ × 4.07137 = 81.4273 Mbit/s; two carriers = 162.8547 Mbit/s.
Step 3: gain = 162.8547 − 100.5562 = 62.2985 Mbit/s ≈ 62.30 Mbit/s.

**出處**　T. M. Cover and J. A. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory* (parallel Gaussian channels).

</details>

**3.2.1S063**　Why is the equal split optimal here, and how would the optimal split change if the second carrier had 10 dB more noise?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　For parallel Gaussian channels the optimum is water-filling: each channel gets power P_i = max(0, μ − N_i/g_i), where N_i/g_i is the noise referred to the input. With equal gains and equal noise, the "floors" are equal and the optimal powers are equal. If carrier 2 had 10 dB more noise, its floor would be higher, so it would get less power (here still a positive share, about 36 % of the total) and carrier 1 more; if the noise difference were large enough, carrier 2 would get no power at all.

**詳解**　Water level μ = (P + N₁ + N₂)/2 with P = 10^(−8.5) mW, N₁ = 10^(−10) mW, N₂ = 10^(−9) mW: μ = 2.1311×10⁻⁹; P₁ = 2.0311×10⁻⁹ mW, P₂ = 1.1311×10⁻⁹ mW (P₂/P = 0.358).

**出處**　T. M. Cover and J. A. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory* (water-filling for parallel Gaussian channels).

</details>

**3.2.1S064**　When is it correct to add the Shannon capacities of the two carriers to obtain the total capacity?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　When the carriers act as independent parallel channels: their noises are independent, there is no mutual interference (orthogonal frequencies with adequate guard bands), and the transmitter and receiver can code over each carrier separately. Then the mutual information of the pair is the sum of the individual terms. If the carriers interfered with each other, or the noise were correlated, the simple sum would not apply.

**詳解**　C_total = Σ B_i·log2(1 + SNR_i) for independent parallel AWGN channels.

**出處**　T. M. Cover and J. A. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory* (parallel Gaussian channels).

</details>

**3.2.1S065**　Another user is served on a 20 MHz carrier at 15 dB SNR and a 10 MHz carrier at 9 dB SNR. A planner computes the total capacity as follows. Which step is wrong, why, and what is the correct total in Mbit/s (two decimal places)?
1. Linear SNRs: 31.623 and 7.943.
2. Total bandwidth = 20 + 10 = 30 MHz.

**出處**　T. M. Cover and J. A. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory* (parallel Gaussian channels).

</details>
