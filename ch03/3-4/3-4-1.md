# 3.4.1 話務量（Erlang）

所屬：[3.4 訊務與存取](../3-4.md)

## 正文

In any telecommunication system, the most fundamental question a planner must answer is: *how much simultaneous usage will the network experience?*  The answer determines how many radio carriers, trunk circuits, or processing cores are required to keep the probability of call loss (or excessive delay) within acceptable limits.  The industry’s answer to this question is the **traffic load**, measured in **Erlangs** (symbol E).  An Erlang is a dimension‑less unit that represents the average number of concurrent calls (or sessions) offered to a resource.  It is named after A. K. Erlang, whose pioneering work on telephone traffic in the early 20th century still underpins modern capacity planning.

---

### 1. Core definitions

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $ \lambda $ | **Call arrival rate** – average number of call *attempts* per unit time (e.g. calls · h$^{-1}$) | calls · h$^{-1}$ |
| $ h $ | **Mean holding (or service) time** – average duration of a call (or session) | h (hours) |
| $ A $ | **Offered traffic** – product $ \lambda h $ | Erlangs (E) |
| $ N $ | Number of identical servers (channels, time slots, codes, etc.) | – |
| $ P_k $ | Probability that exactly $k$ calls are simultaneously active | – |
| $ P_{\text{block}} $ | Blocking probability (probability a call finds all $N$ servers busy) | – |

The fundamental relationship is  

$$
\boxed{A = \lambda \, h}
$$

where $A$ is expressed in Erlangs.  Because $ \lambda $ has units of *calls per hour* and $h$ has units of *hours*, the product is dimension‑less; the Erlang therefore quantifies “average concurrent calls”.

---

### 2. Poisson model for call arrivals

If call attempts follow a **Poisson process** (the standard assumption for large, aggregated traffic), the number of active calls $k$ at any instant is a Poisson‑distributed random variable with mean $A$:

$$
\boxed{P_k = \Pr\{K = k\}= \frac{A^{\,k}}{k!}\,e^{-A}},\qquad k = 0,1,2,\dots
$$

*Derivation sketch*: The superposition of independent exponential inter‑arrival times yields the Poisson distribution for the count of arrivals in a fixed interval.  Since each call occupies a server for an exponentially distributed holding time, the **Erlang loss system** (also called *M/M/N/N*) has the same steady‑state distribution as a Poisson count with mean $A$.

---

### 3. Worked example

A small business exchange receives **120 call attempts per hour**.  The average call lasts **3 minutes**.

1. **Convert holding time to hours**  

   $$
   h = 3\ \text{min} = \frac{3}{60}\ \text{h}=0.05\ \text{h}
   $$

2. **Compute offered traffic**  

   $$
   A = \lambda h = 120\ \frac{\text{calls}}{\text{h}}\times 0.05\ \text{h}=6\ \text{Erlangs}
   $$

   The exchange therefore experiences, on average, six simultaneous calls.

3. **Probability that more than 10 channels are needed**  

   We need  

   $$
   P_{>10}=1-\sum_{k=0}^{10} P_k
   $$

   Using the Poisson formula with $A=6$:

   $$
   P_k = \frac{6^{k}}{k!}e^{-6}
   $$

   Computing the cumulative sum (e.g. with a calculator or a spreadsheet) gives  

   $$
   \sum_{k=0}^{10} P_k \approx 0.9999
   $$

   Hence  

   $$
   P_{>10}\approx 1-0.9999 = 0.0001\;(0.01\%)
   $$

   The chance that the traffic exceeds ten concurrent calls is negligible, so provisioning ten channels would meet a typical 1 % blocking target.

---

### 4. Why Erlangs matter in real networks

* **Capacity planning** – Operators dimension radio cells, PSTN trunks, and VoIP gateways by converting forecasted call volumes into Erlangs and then selecting the number of resources that keep blocking below a Service Level Agreement (SLA) threshold.
* **Performance benchmarking** – Comparing traffic loads across technologies (e.g., GSM vs. LTE) is possible because Erlangs abstract away the underlying physical medium.
* **Cost optimisation** – Over‑provisioning (too many channels) inflates CAPEX/OPEX, while under‑provisioning harms QoS.  Accurate Erlang calculations strike the right balance.

---

### 5. Common pitfalls and practical notes

| Pitfall | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Unit mismatch** | Forgetting to convert holding time to **hours** yields a traffic value off by a factor of 60 (or 3600). Always express $h$ in hours when using $A=\lambda h$. |
| **Assuming Poisson for bursty traffic** | The Poisson model assumes independent, memoryless arrivals.  In bursty data‑centric networks (e.g., video streaming), arrivals may be correlated, leading to under‑estimation of peak $k$ values. |
| **Treating Erlang as a physical unit** | Erlang is a *dimension‑less* measure of load, not a time or bandwidth unit.  Do not multiply Erlangs by seconds or Hertz. |
| **Neglecting call retries** | Blocked call attempts often retry, effectively increasing $\lambda$ and thus $A$.  Ignoring retries can underestimate required capacity. |
| **Using the wrong mean holding time** | For mixed services (voice + data), a single $h$ may not represent the system.  Separate traffic classes should be modelled independently or aggregated using weighted averages. |

In practice, engineers validate Erlang‑based forecasts with **traffic measurements** (e.g., CDR analysis) and, when necessary, adjust the model to reflect observed arrival patterns.  This iterative approach ensures that the theoretical load aligns with real‑world performance.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following expressions correctly defines the offered traffic $A$ in Erlangs?

- A. $A = \lambda + h$
- B. $A = \lambda / h$
- C. $A = \lambda \, h$
- D. $A = \lambda - h$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Offered traffic is the product of the call arrival rate $\lambda$ (calls per hour) and the mean holding time $h$ (hours), giving $A=\lambda h$.

</details>

**2.** In a Poisson traffic model with offered load $A=5$ Erlangs, what is the probability that exactly three calls are simultaneously active?

- A. $\displaystyle \frac{5^3}{3!}e^{-5}$
- B. $\displaystyle \frac{5^3}{3!}e^{5}$
- C. $\displaystyle \frac{5^2}{2!}e^{-5}$
- D. $\displaystyle \frac{5^4}{4!}e^{-5}$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。The Poisson probability for $k$ concurrent calls is $P_k = \frac{A^{k}}{k!}e^{-A}$; substituting $k=3$, $A=5$ yields option A.

</details>

**3.** A telecom node receives $200$ call attempts per hour, and the average call duration is $2$ minutes. Compute the offered traffic $A$ in Erlangs.  (Answer in Erlangs, rounded to three decimal places.)


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Convert holding time to hours: $h = 2\ \text{min} = \frac{2}{60}\ \text{h}=0.033\overline{3}\ \text{h}$.  
2. Apply $A = \lambda h$ with $\lambda = 200\ \text{calls/h}$:  
   $A = 200 \times 0.033\overline{3}=6.\overline{6}$ Erlangs.  
3. Rounded to three decimals: $A = 6.667$ E.  
$\boxed{6.667}$

</details>

**4.** For an offered traffic of $A = 4$ Erlangs, find the probability that exactly $3$ calls are simultaneously active.  (Answer as a decimal rounded to four places.)


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

Use the Poisson formula $P_k = \frac{A^{k}}{k!}e^{-A}$ with $A=4$, $k=3$:  
$$
P_3 = \frac{4^{3}}{3!}\,e^{-4}= \frac{64}{6}\,e^{-4}=10.\overline{6}\,e^{-4}.
$$  
Compute $e^{-4}\approx 0.0183156389$.  
Thus $P_3 \approx 10.6667 \times 0.0183156389 \approx 0.1950$.  
Rounded to four decimal places: $0.1950$.  
$\boxed{0.1950}$

</details>
