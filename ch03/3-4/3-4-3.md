# 3.4.3 排隊模型：Little 定理、M/M/1、M/M/c、M/M/1/K

所屬：[3.4 訊務與存取](../3-4.md)

## 正文

In a packet‑ or call‑oriented network every request first **arrives**, then **waits** (if the server is busy) and finally **receives service**.  The statistical description of this process is the **queueing model**.  Queueing theory provides the tools to predict *average delay*, *buffer occupancy* and *probability of loss*, which are essential for dimensioning radio access nodes, routers, and core‑network elements.  If the model is mismatched to reality, a network may appear to meet its design traffic but actually suffer excessive latency or high drop rates.

### Little’s Law

For any **stable** (i.e., the average arrival rate is less than the average service capacity) queueing system observed in steady state, Little’s law relates three fundamental averages:

$$
L = \lambda \, W
$$

* $L$ – mean number of customers (calls, packets) **in the system** (both waiting and being served) [unit: Erlangs or “customers”]  
* $\lambda$ – **effective** arrival rate (customers per unit time) [unit: s$^{-1}$]  
* $W$ – mean **sojourn time** (total time a customer spends in the system) [unit: s]

A closely related expression for the queue itself is $L_q = \lambda \, W_q$, where $L_q$ and $W_q$ refer only to the waiting line (excluding the one in service).  Little’s law holds for *any* queue discipline (FIFO, LIFO, priority) as long as the system is stationary; it is frequently used to check the internal consistency of analytical results.

### The M/M/1 Model

The notation **M/M/1** denotes:

* **M** – Poisson (Markovian) arrival process with rate $\lambda$ (s$^{-1}$).  
* **M** – Exponential (Markovian) service time distribution with mean $1/\mu$ (s), i.e., service rate $\mu$ (s$^{-1}$).  
* **1** – A single server.

The traffic intensity (or utilization) is

$$
\rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu}, \qquad 0 \le \rho < 1 .
$$

Stability requires $\rho<1$; otherwise the queue grows without bound.

Key performance measures (derived from the birth–death process) are:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L      &= \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}, &
L_q    &= \frac{\rho^{2}}{1-\rho}, \\
W      &= \frac{1}{\mu-\lambda}, &
W_q    &= \frac{\lambda}{\mu(\mu-\lambda)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

These formulas are consistent with Little’s law: $L = \lambda W$ and $L_q = \lambda W_q$.

### The M/M/c Model

**M/M/c** extends the single‑server case to *c* identical parallel servers (e.g., *c* channels in a base station).  The arrival process remains Poisson with rate $\lambda$, and each server offers exponential service at rate $\mu$.  The **traffic intensity per server** is still $\rho = \lambda/(c\mu)$, and stability now requires $\rho<1$.

The probability that an arriving customer finds all servers busy (i.e., must wait) is given by the **Erlang‑C formula**:

$$
P_{\text{wait}} = \frac{\displaystyle \frac{(c\rho)^{c}}{c!\,}\frac{1}{1-\rho}}%
{\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{c-1}\frac{(c\rho)^{n}}{n!} \;+\; \frac{(c\rho)^{c}}{c!}\frac{1}{1-\rho}} .
$$

From $P_{\text{wait}}$ we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_q &= P_{\text{wait}} \,\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}, &
W_q &= \frac{L_q}{\lambda}, \\
L   &= L_q + \frac{\lambda}{\mu}, &
W   &= W_q + \frac{1}{\mu}.
\end{aligned}
$$

When $c=1$, the Erlang‑C expression collapses to the M/M/1 results.

### The M/M/1/K Model (Finite Buffer)

Real equipment cannot store an infinite number of waiting customers.  **M/M/1/K** models a single server with a finite buffer that can hold at most $K-1$ waiting customers; the system capacity (including the one in service) is $K$.

The stationary probability of having exactly $n$ customers in the system ($0\le n\le K$) is

$$
P_n = \frac{(1-\rho)\,\rho^{\,n}}{1-\rho^{\,K+1}}, \qquad (\rho\neq1).
$$

The **blocking probability** (probability that an arrival finds the system full and is lost) is simply

$$
P_{\text{block}} = P_K = \frac{(1-\rho)\,\rho^{\,K}}{1-\rho^{\,K+1}} .
$$

Because some arrivals are rejected, the **effective** arrival rate is reduced:

$$
\lambda_{\text{eff}} = \lambda\,(1-P_{\text{block}}).
$$

The mean number in system and the mean waiting time are obtained by substituting $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ into the Little’s‑law relationships:

$$
L = \sum_{n=0}^{K} n P_n , \qquad 
W = \frac{L}{\lambda_{\text{eff}}}.
$$

### Worked Example

A small cell serves voice calls with an average call arrival rate $\lambda = 20\ \text{calls/s}$ and an average call duration of $33\ \text{ms}$ (i.e., service rate $\mu = 1/0.033\approx30\ \text{calls/s}$).  Assume a single server (M/M/1).

1. **Utilization**  
   $$
   \rho = \frac{20}{30}=0.667 < 1 \quad\text{(stable)}.
   $$

2. **Mean number in system**  
   $$
   L = \frac{0.667}{1-0.667}=2.0\ \text{calls}.
   $$

3. **Mean waiting time in queue**  
   $$
   W_q = \frac{20}{30(30-20)} = \frac{20}{30\times10}=0.0667\ \text{s}=66.7\ \text{ms}.
   $$

4. **Mean total time in system** (Little’s law)  
   $$
   W = \frac{L}{\lambda}= \frac{2.0}{20}=0.10\ \text{s}=100\ \text{ms}.
   $$

Thus, on average a call spends 100 ms in the cell, of which about 67 ms are spent waiting for the channel.  If the operator wants the average waiting time below 30 ms, the service capacity must be increased (e.g., add a second channel, moving to an M/M/2 model).

### Common Pitfalls & Practical Notes

| Mistake | Why it matters | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| **Using $\rho\ge 1$** | Violates the stability condition; formulas give infinite or negative values. | Always check $\lambda < c\mu$ (or $\lambda < \mu$ for M/M/1) before applying closed‑form results. |
| **Confusing $\lambda$ with $\lambda_{\text{eff}}$ in finite buffers** | Ignoring blocked arrivals underestimates delay and overestimates throughput. | Compute $P_{\text{block}}$ first, then $\lambda_{\text{eff}} = \lambda(1-P_{\text{block}})$. |
| **Applying M/M/1 formulas to multi‑server systems** | Leads to under‑estimation of waiting time because parallel servers reduce contention. | Use the Erlang‑C expression for $P_{\text{wait}}$ and derived $L_q$, $W_q$. |
| **Assuming exponential service when traffic is deterministic** | Real voice codecs often have near‑constant packet lengths; exponential assumption may over‑estimate variance. | Validate the exponential assumption with measurements; if not appropriate, consider M/D/1 or G/G/1 approximations. |
| **Neglecting the impact of bursty arrivals** | Poisson arrivals smooth traffic; burstiness can temporarily violate $\rho<1$ locally, causing queue spikes. | Use traffic shaping or admission control; for analysis, consider a Markov Modulated Poisson Process (MMPP) if needed. |

In practice, engineers often start with the simple M/M/1 or M/M/c models to obtain quick insights, then refine the analysis with simulation or more sophisticated queueing models when the assumptions (Poisson arrivals, exponential service, infinite buffers) are not met.

## 練習題

**1.** In an M/M/1 queue the traffic intensity $\rho$ is 0.8. What is the average number of customers **in the queue** (excluding the one in service)?

- A. $0.8$
- B. $1.6$
- C. $3.2$
- D. $4.0$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。$L_q = \rho^2/(1-\rho) = 0.8^2/0.2 = 0.64/0.2 = 3.2$.

</details>

**2.** For an M/M/c system with $c=3$, $\lambda=12$ calls/s and $\mu=5$ calls/s per server, which statement is true?

- A. The system is unstable because $\lambda > c\mu$.
- B. The probability that an arrival must wait is zero.
- C. The traffic intensity per server $\rho$ equals 0.8.
- D. The average waiting time $W_q$ is negative.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。$\rho = \lambda/(c\mu) = 12/(3·5)=0.8$, and since $\rho<1$ the system is stable.

</details>

**3.** In an M/M/1/K queue with $K=4$, $\lambda=6$ s$^{-1}$, $\mu=10$ s$^{-1}$, what is the blocking probability $P_{\text{block}}$?

- A. $0.043$
- B. $0.067$
- C. $0.089$
- D. $0.111$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。$\rho=0.6$, $P_K = (1-\rho)\rho^{K}/(1-\rho^{K+1}) = 0.4·0.6^{4}/(1-0.6^{5})≈0.067$.

</details>

**4.** An M/M/1 queue has arrival rate $\lambda = 10\ \text{s}^{-1}$ and service rate $\mu = 15\ \text{s}^{-1}$. Compute the average number of customers in the system $L$ (unitless).


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Compute traffic intensity: $\rho = \lambda/\mu = 10/15 = 0.666\overline{6}$.\ 
2. Use $L = \rho/(1-\rho)$.  
   $1-\rho = 1 - 0.666\overline{6} = 0.333\overline{3}$.\ 
   $L = 0.666\overline{6} / 0.333\overline{3} = 2.0$.\ 
$\boxed{2.0}$

**5.** For an M/M/1/K system with capacity $K=5$, arrival rate $\lambda = 8\ \text{s}^{-1}$ and service rate $\mu = 12\ \text{s}^{-1}$, find the blocking probability $P_{\text{block}}$ (dimensionless, rounded to three decimal places).


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Compute traffic intensity: $\rho = \lambda/\mu = 8/12 = 0.666\overline{6}$.\ 
2. Compute numerator: $(1-\rho)\rho^{K} = (1-0.666\overline{6})·(0.666\overline{6})^{5} = 0.333\overline{3}·(2/3)^{5} = 0.333\overline{3}·32/243 ≈ 0.0439$.\ 
3. Compute denominator: $1-\rho^{K+1} = 1-(0.666\overline{6})^{6} = 1-(2/3)^{6} = 1-64/729 ≈ 0.9122$.\ 
4. Blocking probability: $P_{\text{block}} = 0.0439/0.9122 ≈ 0.0481$.\ 
Rounded to three decimals: $\boxed{0.048}$
