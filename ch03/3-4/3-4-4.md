# 3.4.4 隨機存取：ALOHA、CSMA

所屬：[3.4 訊務與存取](../3-4.md)

## 正文

Random‑access protocols are the foundation of many shared‑medium networks (e.g., satellite links, Ethernet, Wi‑Fi). Unlike circuit‑switched or scheduled multiple‑access schemes, the users do not reserve a time‑slot or frequency before transmitting; they simply “listen” (if possible) and transmit whenever they have data. Because transmissions may collide, the analysis of random access focuses on **offered traffic**, **collision probability**, and the resulting **throughput** and **delay**.

---

### 1. Pure ALOHA  

In pure ALOHA each node transmits immediately when a packet becomes ready. The channel is a *purely* shared medium; there is no carrier sensing.  

*Offered load* $G$ (Erlangs) is the average number of packet transmission attempts per packet time $T$ (the time to send one packet).  
*Throughput* $S$ (successful packets per packet time) is the fraction of attempts that survive without collision.

A collision occurs if any other transmission starts within a **vulnerability period** of length $2T$ (the original packet’s transmission time plus the overlapping packet’s time). Assuming Poisson arrivals, the probability that **no** other transmission starts in that interval is $e^{-2G}$. Hence  

$$
\boxed{S = G\,e^{-2G}} \qquad (1)
$$

The maximum of (1) occurs at $G=0.5$, giving  

$$
S_{\max}=0.5\,e^{-1}\approx 0.184\;(18.4\%).
$$

---

### 2. Slotted ALOHA  

Time is divided into discrete slots of length $T$. A node may transmit only at slot boundaries, halving the vulnerability period to $T$. The success probability becomes $e^{-G}$, yielding  

$$
\boxed{S = G\,e^{-G}} \qquad (2)
$$

Maximum throughput occurs at $G=1$, giving  

$$
S_{\max}=e^{-1}\approx 0.368\;(36.8\%).
$$

Slotted ALOHA therefore doubles the efficiency of pure ALOHA, at the cost of requiring slot synchronization.

---

### 3. Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA)  

CSMA improves on ALOHA by listening to the channel before transmitting. The most common variants are:

| Variant | Behaviour when channel is busy |
|---------|--------------------------------|
| **1‑persistent** | Transmit immediately when the channel becomes idle (waits zero time). |
| **Non‑persistent** | Wait a random back‑off time, then retry. |
| **p‑persistent** (slotted) | In each slot, transmit with probability $p$, otherwise defer. |

The analysis below concentrates on **1‑persistent CSMA** on an *unslotted* channel, which is the classic model for Ethernet (CSMA/CD).  

Define:

* $\tau$ – one‑way **propagation delay** (seconds).  
* $T$ – **packet transmission time** (seconds).  
* $a = \tau/T$ – *normalized propagation delay* (dimensionless).  
* $G$ – offered load in Erlangs (attempts per $T$).  

When a node finishes sensing an idle channel and begins transmission, a collision can still occur if another node, located up to a distance of one round‑trip propagation time $2\tau$, has already started transmitting but the first node has not yet sensed it. The vulnerable interval is therefore $2\tau$. Assuming Poisson arrivals, the probability that **no** other transmission starts during this interval is $e^{-2aG}$. Consequently, the throughput of 1‑persistent CSMA is approximated by  

$$
\boxed{S \approx G\,e^{-2aG}} \qquad (3)
$$

When $a\ll 1$ (e.g., a short LAN where propagation time is tiny compared with packet time), $S$ approaches $G$, i.e., near‑perfect channel utilization. As $a$ grows (longer propagation paths or shorter packets), the exponential term reduces throughput, converging toward the ALOHA limits.

---

### 4. Worked Example  

A local Ethernet segment operates at $R = 1\ \text{Mbps}$. The average packet length is $L = 1{,}000\ \text{bits}$, and the maximum distance between two stations yields a one‑way propagation delay $\tau = 0.25\ \text{ms}$.

1. **Compute packet transmission time**  

$$
T = \frac{L}{R}= \frac{1{,}000\ \text{bits}}{1\times10^{6}\ \text{bits/s}} = 1\ \text{ms}.
$$

2. **Normalized propagation delay**  

$$
a = \frac{\tau}{T}= \frac{0.25\ \text{ms}}{1\ \text{ms}} = 0.25.
$$

3. **Assume offered load $G = 0.8$ Erlangs.**  

   *Pure ALOHA throughput* (1):  

$$
S_{\text{pure}} = 0.8\,e^{-2\times0.8}=0.8\,e^{-1.6}\approx 0.8\times0.2019=0.1615\;(16.2\%).
$$

   *Slotted ALOHA throughput* (2):  

$$
S_{\text{slotted}} = 0.8\,e^{-0.8}=0.8\times0.4493=0.3594\;(35.9\%).
$$

   *1‑persistent CSMA throughput* (3):  

$$
S_{\text{CSMA}} = 0.8\,e^{-2aG}=0.8\,e^{-2\times0.25\times0.8}=0.8\,e^{-0.4}\approx 0.8\times0.6703=0.5362\;(53.6\%).
$$

Thus, with the same offered load, CSMA delivers markedly higher utilization because the carrier‑sense step eliminates many collisions that ALOHA cannot avoid.

---

### 5. Practical Notes & Common Mistakes  

1. **Treating $G$ as a pure number** – $G$ is dimensionless only after normalizing by the packet time $T$. Always express $G$ in Erlangs (attempts per $T$) to keep formulas consistent.  

2. **Ignoring propagation delay in CSMA** – Using (3) with $a=0$ yields $S=G$, which is unrealistic for any real LAN. Even a few microseconds of $\tau$ can noticeably reduce throughput for short packets.  

3. **Assuming slotted ALOHA needs no synchronization** – Slots must be globally aligned (e.g., via a beacon or a common clock). Without proper synchronization the system reverts to pure ALOHA performance.  

4. **Confusing “throughput” $S$ with “channel utilization”** – $S$ is measured in successful packets per packet time. To obtain bits per second, multiply $S$ by the packet size $L$ and divide by $T$ (or simply use $R\times$ utilization).  

5. **Over‑estimating CSMA performance in high‑load regimes** – The exponential approximation (3) assumes Poisson arrivals and a single collision domain. In Ethernet with **collision detection (CSMA/CD)**, retransmission back‑off further reduces effective throughput when $G$ approaches or exceeds 1 Erlang.  

Understanding these subtleties helps engineers select the appropriate access method for a given physical medium and traffic profile, and it provides a solid basis for the performance models used later in the chapter (queueing delay, packet loss, etc.).

## 練習題

**1.** In pure ALOHA the vulnerability period is:

- A. $T$
- B. $2T$
- C. $T/2$
- D. $3T$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。A collision can start any time within one packet time before or after the transmission, giving a total vulnerable interval of $2T$.

</details>

**2.** Which of the following statements about slotted ALOHA is FALSE?

- A. It requires all stations to be synchronized to slot boundaries.
- B. Its maximum throughput is $e^{-1}\approx0.37$ packets per slot.
- C. Its throughput expression is $S = G e^{-2G}$.
- D. It halves the vulnerable period compared with pure ALOHA.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The correct throughput formula for slotted ALOHA is $S = G e^{-G}$; the $e^{-2G}$ term belongs to pure ALOHA.

</details>

**3.** For a 1‑persistent CSMA network the normalized propagation delay $a$ is defined as:

- A. $a = \tau \times T$
- B. $a = \tau / T$
- C. $a = T / \tau$
- D. $a = \tau^{2} / T$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。$a$ is the ratio of one‑way propagation delay $\tau$ to packet transmission time $T$, i.e., $a=\tau/T$.

</details>

**4.** A wireless sensor network uses pure ALOHA. Each packet is 800 bits and the channel rate is 200 kbps. If the offered load is $G=0.4$ Erlangs, compute the throughput $S$ (in packets per second).


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Packet transmission time $T = L/R = 800\ \text{bits} / 200\,000\ \text{bits/s}=0.004\ \text{s}=4\ \text{ms}$.  
2. Throughput in packets per packet time: $S_{\text{norm}} = G e^{-2G}=0.4\,e^{-0.8}$.  
3. Compute $e^{-0.8}\approx0.449329$.  
4. $S_{\text{norm}} = 0.4 \times 0.449329 = 0.1797316\ \text{packets per }T$.  
5. Convert to packets per second: $S = S_{\text{norm}} / T = 0.1797316 / 0.004 = 44.933\ \text{packets/s}$.  
$\boxed{44.933}$

</details>

**5.** In a 1‑persistent CSMA LAN the link rate is 10 Mbps, the average packet length is 5 000 bits, and the maximum one‑way propagation delay is 10 µs. If the offered load is $G=0.6$ Erlangs, calculate the channel utilization $U$ (dimensionless, i.e., the throughput fraction $S/G$).


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

1. Transmission time $T = L/R = 5{,}000\ \text{bits} / 10{,}000{,}000\ \text{bits/s}=0.0005\ \text{s}=0.5\ \text{ms}$.  
2. Normalized propagation delay $a = \tau/T = 10\ \mu\text{s} / 0.5\ \text{ms}=10^{-5}\ \text{s} / 5\times10^{-4}\ \text{s}=0.02$.  
3. Throughput using (3): $S = G e^{-2aG}=0.6\,e^{-2\times0.02\times0.6}=0.6\,e^{-0.024}=0.6\times0.9763=0.5858$.  
4. Utilization $U = S/G = 0.5858 / 0.6 = 0.9763$.  
$\boxed{0.9763}$

</details>
