# 3.7.1 熵與互資訊

所屬：[3.7 資訊理論與編碼](../3-7.md)

## 正文

In any digital communication system the *information* carried by a signal is what we ultimately want to convey, store, or process.  Quantifying that information is the cornerstone of modern network design: it tells us how much a source can be compressed (Shannon’s source‑coding theorem), how much noise a channel can tolerate before reliable transmission becomes impossible (Shannon’s channel‑coding theorem), and how to allocate bits across multiple services (e.g., video, voice, control) in a heterogeneous network.  The two fundamental metrics that enable these analyses are **entropy** and **mutual information**.

### Entropy of a Discrete Random Variable

Let $X$ be a discrete random variable (RV) taking values in the alphabet $\mathcal{X}$ with probability mass function (PMF) $p_X(x)=\Pr\{X=x\}$.  
The **entropy** of $X$ (Shannon entropy) is defined as  

$$
\boxed{ H(X) = -\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}} p_X(x)\,\log_{2} p_X(x) }\qquad\text{[bits/symbol]}
$$

* **$p_X(x)$** – probability of symbol $x$ (unitless, $0\le p_X(x)\le 1$).  
* **$\log_{2}$** – logarithm base 2; the unit is **bits**.  
* The sum runs over all symbols with non‑zero probability; terms with $p_X(x)=0$ are defined as zero because $\lim_{p\to0}p\log_2p =0$.

Entropy measures the *average uncertainty* of the source.  A perfectly deterministic source ($p_X(x_0)=1$) has $H(X)=0$ bits, whereas a uniformly distributed source over $M$ symbols attains the maximum $H(X)=\log_{2}M$ bits.

### Conditional Entropy

If a second RV $Y$ is observed, the remaining uncertainty about $X$ is the **conditional entropy**

$$
\boxed{ H(X|Y)= -\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}} p_{XY}(x,y)\,\log_{2} p_{X|Y}(x|y) }
$$

where $p_{XY}(x,y)$ is the joint PMF and $p_{X|Y}(x|y)=\frac{p_{XY}(x,y)}{p_Y(y)}$.  By construction  

$$
0\le H(X|Y)\le H(X)
$$

with equality on the left when $Y$ determines $X$ (no remaining uncertainty) and on the right when $X$ and $Y$ are independent.

### Mutual Information

The amount of information that **$X$ and $Y$ share** is the **mutual information**  

$$
\boxed{ I(X;Y)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}} p_{XY}(x,y)\,
\log_{2}\!\frac{p_{XY}(x,y)}{p_X(x)\,p_Y(y)} }
$$

Equivalently, mutual information can be expressed through entropies:

$$
I(X;Y)=H(X)-H(X|Y)=H(Y)-H(Y|X)=H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)
$$

* Units: bits per channel use (or bits per symbol).  
* **Symmetry:** $I(X;Y)=I(Y;X)$.  
* **Non‑negativity:** $I(X;Y)\ge 0$, with equality iff $X$ and $Y$ are independent.

In a communication system, if $X$ denotes the transmitted symbol and $Y$ the received symbol, $I(X;Y)$ quantifies the *information rate* that survives the noisy channel.  The **channel capacity** $C$ is the supremum of $I(X;Y)$ over all admissible input distributions.

### Worked Example

Consider a memoryless source that emits four symbols $\{a,b,c,d\}$ with probabilities  

$$
p(a)=0.1,\; p(b)=0.2,\; p(c)=0.3,\; p(d)=0.4 .
$$

**(a) Entropy of the source**  

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(X) &=-\bigl[0.1\log_2 0.1+0.2\log_2 0.2+0.3\log_2 0.3+0.4\log_2 0.4\bigr] \\
&= -\bigl[0.1(-3.3219)+0.2(-2.3219)+0.3(-1.7369)+0.4(-1.3219)\bigr]\\
&\approx 1.846\ \text{bits/symbol}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(b) Mutual information of a binary symmetric channel (BSC)**  

Suppose the source symbols are mapped to bits and transmitted over a BSC with crossover probability $p=0.1$.  With a uniform input ($\Pr\{0\}= \Pr\{1\}=0.5$), the mutual information per channel use is  

$$
I(X;Y)=1-H_2(p), \qquad 
H_2(p)=-p\log_2 p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p).
$$

Evaluating $H_2(0.1)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_2(0.1)&=-(0.1\log_2 0.1+0.9\log_2 0.9)\\
&\approx -(0.1(-3.3219)+0.9(-0.1520))\\
&\approx 0.469\ \text{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence  

$$
I(X;Y)=1-0.469\approx 0.531\ \text{bits/channel use}.
$$

The BSC can therefore support reliable transmission at any rate below $0.531$ bits per symbol, in accordance with Shannon’s channel‑coding theorem.

### Practical Notes & Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it Happens | Correct Practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Using natural logarithm** (`ln`) without converting units | Many engineers default to `ln` in calculators. | Always use $\log_2$ for bits; if `ln` is used, multiply the result by $\frac{1}{\ln 2}$. |
| **Including zero‑probability symbols in the sum** | Direct substitution yields $\log_2 0$ → undefined. | Omit symbols with $p=0$ or treat the term as zero by the limit $p\log_2 p \to 0$. |
| **Confusing $H(X|Y)$ with $H(Y|X)$** | Both are conditional entropies but generally differ. | Keep the conditioning variable explicit; verify with the joint distribution. |
| **Assuming independence when $I(X;Y)=0$** | Some think $I=0$ implies independence *and* identical distributions. | $I=0$ ⇔ statistical independence; identical marginal distributions are unrelated. |
| **Neglecting the base of the logarithm in capacity calculations** | Capacity formulas are often given in bits, but some textbooks use nats. | Check the logarithm base; convert nats to bits by dividing by $\ln 2$. |

**Practical tip:** When designing a source coder (e.g., Huffman or arithmetic coding), the achievable average code length $L$ satisfies  

$$
H(X) \le L < H(X)+1 \quad\text{(bits per symbol)},
$$

so measuring $H(X)$ accurately tells you how close a practical code can get to the theoretical optimum.

## 練習題

**1.** A discrete memoryless source emits symbols $\{a,b,c\}$ with probabilities $\{0.5,0.3,0.2\}$. What is the entropy $H(X)$ in bits?

- A. 1.485 bits
- B. 1.571 bits
- C. 1.846 bits
- D. 2.000 bits

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。Using $H=-\sum p\log_2 p$ gives $0.5\!*(-1)+0.3\!*(-1.7369)+0.2\!*(-2.3219)=1.485$ bits.

</details>

**2.** For a binary symmetric channel with crossover probability $p=0.1$ and uniformly distributed input bits, the mutual information $I(X;Y)$ equals:

- A. 0.47 bits
- B. 0.53 bits
- C. 0.61 bits
- D. 0.71 bits

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。$I=1-H_2(p)$; $H_2(0.1)\approx0.469$ bits, thus $I\approx0.531$ bits.

</details>

**3.** Which of the following statements about conditional entropy is always true?

- A. $H(X|Y) \le H(X)$ for any pair $(X,Y)$.
- B. $H(X|Y) \ge H(X)$ for any pair $(X,Y)$.
- C. $H(X|Y)=0$ iff $X$ and $Y$ are independent.
- D. $H(X|Y)=H(Y)$ when $X$ and $Y$ are identically distributed.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。Conditioning can never increase uncertainty, so $H(X|Y)\le H(X)$.

</details>

**4.** Compute the entropy (in bits) of a source with alphabet $\{s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4\}$ and probabilities $\{0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4\}$.


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

$$
\begin{aligned}
H &= -\bigl[0.1\log_2 0.1 + 0.2\log_2 0.2 + 0.3\log_2 0.3 + 0.4\log_2 0.4\bigr] \\
&= -\bigl[0.1(-3.321928) + 0.2(-2.321928) + 0.3(-1.736966) + 0.4(-1.321928)\bigr] \\
&= 0.3321928 + 0.4643856 + 0.5210898 + 0.5287712 \\
&\approx 1.84644\ \text{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$\boxed{1.84644}$

</details>

**5.** A binary erasure channel (BEC) has erasure probability $\varepsilon = 0.2$. The input bits are uniformly distributed. What is the mutual information $I(X;Y)$ (in bits per channel use)?


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

For a BEC, the output is either the transmitted bit (probability $1-\varepsilon$) or an erasure symbol (probability $\varepsilon$).  
With uniform input, the mutual information equals the channel capacity:  
\[
I(X;Y) = 1 - \varepsilon = 1 - 0.2 = 0.8\ \text{bits}.
$$
$\boxed{0.8}$

</details>
