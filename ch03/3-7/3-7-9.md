# 3.7.9 離散通道容量：BSC、BEC

所屬：[3.7 資訊理論與編碼](../3-7.md)

## 正文

In any digital communication system the physical medium (wire, radio, optical fiber, etc.) can be abstracted as a *discrete memoryless channel* (DMC).  The DMC model assumes that each transmitted symbol is affected independently of the others, and that the statistics of the channel are completely described by a conditional probability matrix $P_{Y|X}(y|x)$.  The most elementary DMCs are binary: the input alphabet is $\mathcal{X}=\{0,1\}$ and the output alphabet is either $\{0,1\}$ (for error‑only channels) or $\{0,1,e\}$ where $e$ denotes an erasure.

The **channel capacity** $C$ of a DMC is the supremum of all achievable information rates (bits per channel use) for which the probability of decoding error can be driven arbitrarily close to zero by an appropriate coding scheme and sufficiently large block length.  Shannon’s noisy‑channel coding theorem guarantees that for any rate $R<C$ there exist codes that achieve reliable communication, while no code can surpass $C$.  Consequently, capacity is the ultimate benchmark for system designers: it tells us the maximum spectral efficiency that any practical coding‑modulation combination can hope to approach.

---

### 1. Binary Symmetric Channel (BSC)

A BSC flips each transmitted bit independently with probability $p$ (the *crossover probability*).  Its transition matrix is  

$$
P_{Y|X}(y|x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
1-p & p\\[2pt]
p   & 1-p
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad x,y\in\{0,1\}.
$$

The mutual information between the input $X$ (with distribution $P_X$) and the output $Y$ is maximized when the input is *uniform*, i.e. $P_X(0)=P_X(1)=\tfrac12$.  Under this optimal input, the capacity of the BSC is  

$$
\boxed{C_{\text{BSC}} = 1 - H_2(p)}\quad\text{[bits per channel use]},
$$

where $H_2(p)$ is the binary entropy function  

$$
H_2(p)= -p\log_2 p - (1-p)\log_2 (1-p),\qquad 0\le p\le 1.
$$

*Units:* bits per channel use (b/cu).  If the channel is used at a symbol rate $f_s$ (symbols / second), the *information rate* in bits / second is $R = f_s \, C_{\text{BSC}}$.

---

### 2. Binary Erasure Channel (BEC)

A BEC either delivers the transmitted bit correctly or declares an *erasure* $e$ with probability $\varepsilon$.  Its transition matrix is  

$$
P_{Y|X}(y|x)=
\begin{cases}
1-\varepsilon, & y=x\in\{0,1\},\\
\varepsilon,    & y=e,\\
0,              & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Again a uniform input maximizes the mutual information, and the capacity of the BEC is remarkably simple:

$$
\boxed{C_{\text{BEC}} = 1-\varepsilon}\quad\text{[bits per channel use]}.
$$

Because erasures are *detectable*, the decoder can treat the erased positions as missing symbols and recover them using redundancy; this property underlies the popularity of erasure‑correcting codes such as Reed‑Solomon and modern LDPC/Polar codes on packet‑switched networks.

---

### 3. Worked Example – BSC

*Problem:* A wireless link behaves as a BSC with crossover probability $p = 0.1$. The link is clocked at $f_s = 2\ \text{Msymbol/s}$. Compute (a) the channel capacity in bits per channel use, and (b) the maximum achievable information rate in megabits per second (Mbps).

*Solution:*  

1. Compute binary entropy:  

$$
H_2(0.1)= -0.1\log_2 0.1 -0.9\log_2 0.9.
$$  

$$
\log_2 0.1 = -3.3219,\qquad \log_2 0.9 = -0.1520.
$$  

Thus  

$$
H_2(0.1)= -0.1(-3.3219) -0.9(-0.1520)=0.3322+0.1368=0.4690\ \text{bits}.
$$

2. Capacity:  

$$
C_{\text{BSC}} = 1 - 0.4690 = 0.5310\ \text{bits per use}.
$$

3. Information rate:  

$$
R = f_s \, C_{\text{BSC}} = 2\times10^{6}\times0.5310 = 1.062\times10^{6}\ \text{bits/s}=1.062\ \text{Mbps}.
$$

*Interpretation:* Even though the raw symbol rate is 2 Msymbol/s, the noisy nature of the channel limits reliable throughput to just over 1 Mbps.  Modern capacity‑approaching codes (e.g., LDPC, Polar) can operate within a few percent of this bound when the block length is sufficiently large.

---

### 4. Common Mistakes & Practical Notes

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Correct Approach |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Using the raw bit‑error rate (BER) as the capacity loss.** | BER ≠ crossover probability for coded systems; coding changes the effective error statistics. | Always start from the *channel model* (BSC or BEC) and use the formulas above. |
| **Applying natural‑log entropy ($\ln$) without converting to base‑2.** | Many textbooks present $H(p) = -p\ln p - (1-p)\ln(1-p)$ (nats). | Multiply by $\frac{1}{\ln 2}$ or use $\log_2$ directly to obtain bits. |
| **Confusing erasure probability $\varepsilon$ with bit‑error probability.** | Both are denoted by “p” in informal discussions. | Remember that erasures are *detectable*; capacity is linear $1-\varepsilon$, unlike the BSC’s entropy expression. |
| **Assuming capacity is always achievable with short block lengths.** | Shannon’s theorem is asymptotic; finite‑length codes incur a *penalty* (dispersion). | For practical systems, consult finite‑block‑length bounds or use codes known to perform well at the target block size (e.g., short‑block Polar or protograph LDPC). |
| **Neglecting the effect of symbol rate.** | Capacity is expressed per channel use, not per second. | Multiply by the actual symbol (or channel use) rate to obtain bits / second. |

**Practical tip:** In modern cellular standards (e.g., 3GPP Release 15 and beyond), the link adaptation algorithm continuously estimates the effective crossover or erasure probability from pilot symbols and selects the modulation‑coding scheme (MCS) that operates as close as possible to the instantaneous capacity while respecting latency constraints.  This dynamic approach embodies the spirit of Shannon’s theorem: always strive to match the transmission rate to the current channel capacity.  

---  

**Key Take‑aways**

* The BSC capacity is $1-H_2(p)$; the BEC capacity is $1-\varepsilon$.  
* Uniform input distribution maximizes mutual information for both channels.  
* Capacity sets an absolute ceiling; practical codes approach it only with large block lengths and sophisticated decoding.  
* Correct handling of logarithm bases, distinction between errors and erasures, and conversion from per‑use to per‑second units are essential for accurate system design.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following expressions correctly gives the capacity of a binary erasure channel with erasure probability $\varepsilon$?

- A. $C = 1 - H_2(\varepsilon)$
- B. $C = (1-\varepsilon)\log_2(1-\varepsilon)$
- C. $C = 1 - \varepsilon$
- D. $C = \varepsilon$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The BEC capacity is linear in the non‑erasure probability: $C = 1-\varepsilon$ bits per channel use.

</details>

**2.** A binary symmetric channel has crossover probability $p = 0.2$. What is its capacity (in bits per channel use, rounded to three decimal places)?

- A. 0.279
- B. 0.500
- C. 0.721
- D. 0.850

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。$C = 1 - H_2(0.2) \approx 1 - 0.722 = 0.278$ bits, rounded to 0.279.

</details>

**3.** To approach the Shannon capacity of a BSC, which of the following conditions is *necessary* (though not sufficient) for the employed error‑correcting code?

- A. The code must be linear.
- B. The block length must tend to infinity.
- C. The decoder must be a hard‑decision Viterbi algorithm.
- D. The code rate must equal 1.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Shannon’s theorem guarantees capacity‑approaching performance only in the limit of infinite block length.

</details>

**4.** A BSC has crossover probability $p = 0.05$. The channel is used at a symbol rate of $10\ \text{Msymbol/s}$. Compute the maximum achievable information rate in megabits per second (Mbps). (Answer to three decimal places.)


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

Compute binary entropy:  
$H_2(0.05) = -0.05\log_2 0.05 - 0.95\log_2 0.95$.  
$\log_2 0.05 = -4.321928$, $\log_2 0.95 = -0.074000$.  
Thus $H_2 = 0.05*4.321928 + 0.95*0.074000 = 0.216096 + 0.070300 = 0.286396$ bits.  
Capacity $C = 1 - H_2 = 0.713604$ bits per use.  
Information rate $R = 10\times10^6 \times 0.713604 = 7.13604\times10^6$ bits/s = 7.136 Mbps.  
$$$\boxed{7.136}$$$

</details>

**5.** A binary erasure channel has erasure probability $\varepsilon = 0.30$. The symbol rate is $5\ \text{Msymbol/s}$. What redundancy (fraction of transmitted bits that are parity) is required to achieve a reliable throughput of $3\ \text{Mbps}$? (Answer as a decimal fraction, three decimal places.)


<details><summary>看答案</summary>

詳解（式號指本頁正文的公式）：

Required code rate $R = \frac{\text{throughput}}{\text{symbol rate}} = \frac{3\times10^6}{5\times10^6}=0.6$.  
Channel capacity $C = 1-\varepsilon = 0.7$ bits per use, which is larger than the required rate, so the target is feasible.  
Redundancy fraction = $1 - R = 0.4$.  
$$$\boxed{0.400}$$$

</details>
