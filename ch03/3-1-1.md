# 3.1.1 分貝與功率單位換算

所屬：[3.1 訊號與通道](3-1.md)

## 內文

#### 為什麼要學

電信網路裡的功率範圍非常大：基地臺一個載波發射數十瓦，手機收到的訊號卻常常只有 $10^{-12}$ 瓦上下，兩者相差十幾個數量級。分貝（decibel, dB）把「相乘、相除」變成「相加、相減」，所以鏈路預算、放大器增益、饋線損耗、天線增益、量測報表（例如 RSRP、接收靈敏度）全部用 dB 系列單位表示。讀規格、看路測資料、算涵蓋範圍，第一步都是把 dB、dBm、dBW、dBi、dBd 分清楚，並且知道哪些量可以直接相加、哪些一定要先換回線性值。

#### 定義與符號

| 符號 | 意思 | 單位 |
|---|---|---|
| $P$ | 功率（線性值） | W 或 mW |
| $P_{\text{ref}}$ | 參考功率 | W 或 mW |
| $G$、$L$ | 增益、損耗（兩個功率的比值） | 無因次，或 dB |
| $P_{\text{dBm}}$ | 以 1 mW 為參考的功率位準 | dBm |
| $P_{\text{dBW}}$ | 以 1 W 為參考的功率位準 | dBW |
| $G_{\text{dBi}}$ | 相對於等向性天線（isotropic antenna）的天線增益 | dBi |
| $G_{\text{dBd}}$ | 相對於半波長偶極天線（half-wave dipole）的天線增益 | dBd |
| $V$ | 電壓（方均根值） | V |

#### 公式與推導

分貝是兩個功率比值取常用對數再乘以 10：

$$X_{\text{dB}} = 10\log_{10}\frac{P_1}{P_2}$$

反過來，$P_1/P_2 = 10^{X_{\text{dB}}/10}$。dB 本身是「比值」，沒有絕對大小；把分母固定成某個參考功率，就得到絕對位準：

$$P_{\text{dBm}} = 10\log_{10}\frac{P}{1\ \text{mW}},\qquad P_{\text{dBW}} = 10\log_{10}\frac{P}{1\ \text{W}}$$

因為 1 W＝1000 mW，而 $10\log_{10}1000 = 30$，所以

$$P_{\text{dBm}} = P_{\text{dBW}} + 30$$

如果量的是電壓，而且兩個電壓跨在相同的阻抗上，功率和電壓平方成正比，所以 $X_{\text{dB}} = 20\log_{10}(V_1/V_2)$。阻抗不同時不能直接套用 20 倍的式子。

天線增益也是比值，只是參考對象不同。半波長偶極天線本身相對於等向性天線的增益是 1.64 倍，$10\log_{10}1.64 = 2.15$ dB，所以

$$G_{\text{dBi}} = G_{\text{dBd}} + 2.15$$

運算規則可以整理成三條：

1. **絕對位準 ± 比值＝絕對位準**：dBm ± dB＝dBm。例如發射功率（dBm）加天線增益（dBi）再減路徑損耗（dB），結果還是 dBm。
2. **絕對位準 − 絕對位準＝比值**：dBm − dBm＝dB。例如訊號功率減雜訊功率得到訊號雜訊比。
3. **兩個功率相加，必須先換成線性值（mW 或 W）再相加，最後再換回 dBm**。把兩個 dBm 數字直接相加沒有物理意義（那相當於把兩個功率相乘）。

常用的速算值：$10\log_{10}2 = 3.01$ dB，所以「加 3 dB 約等於功率變 2 倍」（精確地說 3 dB 是 1.995 倍）；加 10 dB 是 10 倍；加 20 dB 是 100 倍；減 3 dB 約為一半。任何 dB 值都可以拆成 10 和 3 的組合來心算，例如 46 dBm＝40＋3＋3 dBm，約為 10 W×2×2＝40 W。

#### 算例

**算例 1：W、dBm、dBW 互換**

已知：某基地臺每個載波的發射功率為 20 W（假設值）；另依 3GPP TS 38.101-1，功率等級 3（Power Class 3）的手機最大輸出功率為 23 dBm。求基地臺功率的 dBm 與 dBW 值，以及手機功率的 mW 值。

- 基地臺：20 W＝20,000 mW，$P_{\text{dBm}} = 10\log_{10}20000 = 43.01$ dBm；$P_{\text{dBW}} = 10\log_{10}20 = 13.01$ dBW，兩者差 30，符合。
- 手機：$P = 10^{23/10} = 199.5$ mW，約 0.2 W；換成 dBW 是 $23 - 30 = -7$ dBW。
- 合理性檢查：20 W 是 0.2 W 的 100 倍，應該差 20 dB；$43.01 - 23 = 20.01$ dB，吻合（0.01 的差來自 199.5 mW 與 200 mW 的差別）。

**算例 2：兩個訊號功率相加**

已知：接收端同時收到兩個互不相關的訊號，功率分別是 −90 dBm 和 −93 dBm。求總功率（dBm，取到小數第 2 位）。

- 先換成線性：$10^{-90/10} = 1.000\times10^{-9}$ mW；$10^{-93/10} = 5.012\times10^{-10}$ mW。
- 相加：$1.000\times10^{-9} + 0.5012\times10^{-9} = 1.5012\times10^{-9}$ mW。
- 換回 dBm：$10\log_{10}(1.5012\times10^{-9}) = -88.24$ dBm。
- 合理性檢查：總功率一定比較大的那個（−90 dBm）高，而且兩個相等功率相加才會高 3.01 dB；這裡第二個訊號比較弱，只高了 1.76 dB，合理。如果有人算出 −183 dBm（直接相加）或 −91.5 dBm（取平均），都明顯不對。

同樣的道理，兩個各 20 W（43.01 dBm）的載波合在一起是 40 W，也就是 $43.01 + 3.01 = 46.02$ dBm，而不是 86.02 dBm。

#### 常見錯誤

- **把 dBm 直接相加**：功率加總要先換回 mW。dBm 只能和 dB 相加減。
- **dB 和 dBm 混用**：「損耗 100 dBm」「SNR 15 dBm」都是錯的寫法；損耗、增益、SNR 是比值，單位是 dB。
- **功率比用了 20 倍、電壓比用了 10 倍**：功率比用 $10\log_{10}$，電壓比（同阻抗）才用 $20\log_{10}$。
- **dBi 和 dBd 沒有對齊**：廠商規格用 dBd、鏈路預算用 dBi 時，差 2.15 dB；另外 $\log_{10}$ 誤用成自然對數 $\ln$ 也會讓結果差 2.3 倍。

#### 和其他小節的關係

本節是整章的運算基礎：3.1.2、3.1.3 的路徑損耗、3.1.5 的雜訊底、3.1.6 的 SNR 與 SINR 都用 dB 表示；3.3.1 的 EIRP、3.3.2 的天線增益（dBi 與 dBd）、3.3.4 的接收靈敏度會直接用到本節的加減規則；3.6.3 的單位一致也以本節為例。

**出處**　ITU-R 建議書 V.574-5〈Use of the decibel and the neper in telecommunications〉（2015）；3GPP TS 38.101-1 V18.15.0 表 6.2.1-1（UE Power Class，功率等級 3 為 23 dBm）；Rappaport, *Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice*，行動無線電傳播（大尺度路徑損耗）一章中關於 dBm、dBW 與天線增益的說明。

**3.1.1G001**　The power amplifier of an outdoor small cell delivers 6.3 W to its antenna connector. Express this power in dBm, rounded to two decimal places.

\boxed{37.99}

**3.1.1G005**　A handset transmits at 23 dBm. A power-saving feature then reduces the transmitted power by 35% of its value in milliwatts. What is the new transmit power in dBm, rounded to two decimal places?

#### 單題：Adding and subtracting powers in the linear domain

**3.1.1G010**　The noise floor of a base-station receiver is −104 dBm. When an external interferer appears, the total of noise plus interference is 3.5 dB above the noise floor. Assuming noise and interference are uncorrelated, find the interferer power in dBm, rounded to two decimal places.

#### 單題：Voltage ratios, power ratios and impedance

**3.1.1G011**　An IF amplifier has a voltage gain of 18 (output volts per input volt). Its input and output impedances are both 50 Ω. Find its power gain in dB, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　25.11

**3.1.1S008**　A colleague writes: "The two settings differ by 3 dBm." What is the correct unit for the difference, and what would "3 dBm" actually mean?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　The difference is 3 dB, not 3 dBm. Subtracting two dBm values gives the logarithm of a power ratio, which is dimensionless and is expressed in dB. "3 dBm" is an absolute power of about 2 mW, which is not the difference between the two settings (that is about 198.6 mW).

**詳解**　$26\ \text{dBm} - 23\ \text{dBm} = 10\log_{10}(P_2/P_1) = 3$ dB, a ratio of $10^{0.3} = 1.9953$. By contrast, 3 dBm $= 10^{0.3}$ mW $= 1.9953$ mW.

**3.1.1S009**　The received power is found as $23\ \text{dBm} - 131\ \text{dB} = -108$ dBm. Why is it legitimate to subtract a dB value from a dBm value, while adding two dBm values to get a "total power" is not?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　A dB value is a pure ratio, so adding or subtracting it scales an absolute power by a factor, and the result is still an absolute power in dBm. Adding two dBm values corresponds to multiplying two powers (giving mW²), which is not a power. A total power is a sum of powers, which must be formed in linear units (mW or W) and then converted back to dBm.

**詳解**　$-108\ \text{dBm} = 10\log_{10}\!\big(P_{mW}\times10^{-13.1}\big)$ with $P_{mW} = 199.526$ mW: the loss multiplies the power by $10^{-13.1}$. In contrast, $\log(a) + \log(b) = \log(ab)$, so a sum of two dBm values is the logarithm of a product of powers, not of their sum.

**出處**　ITU-R Recommendation V.574; T. S. Rappaport, *Wireless Communications: Principles and Practice* (decibel arithmetic).

</details>

**3.1.1S013**　For the 4-branch radio the total can be written as $46 + 10\log_{10}(4)$ dBm. Under what condition is the shortcut "per-branch level plus $10\log_{10}(N)$" valid, and what must be done when the condition does not hold?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　It is valid only when all N branches have the same power and their powers add (uncorrelated signals), because then the total is N times one branch and the factor N becomes $10\log_{10}(N)$ dB. <details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Adding in dB is correct when the elements are in cascade, so that each one multiplies the power passing through it by its own transmission factor. Addition of dB values is equivalent to multiplication of the linear factors.

**詳解**　$10^{-0.32} = 0.478630$ and $10^{-0.14} = 0.724436$. Product: $0.478630\times0.724436 = 0.346737$, which equals $10^{-0.46}$, i.e. 4.6 dB. Because $\log(ab) = \log a + \log b$, cascaded factors add in dB.

#### 情境題：Voltage ratios, power ratios and impedance

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　26.02 dB

**詳解**　Voltage ratio: $400/20 = 20$. Voltage gain: $20\log_{10}(20) = 26.0206$ dB, which rounds to 26.02 dB.

**出處**　ITU-R Recommendation V.574 (decibel for field quantities).

</details>

**3.1.1S032**　Find the power gain of the amplifier (power delivered to the 200 Ω load divided by power delivered to the 50 Ω input) in dB, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　20.00 dB

**詳解**　Input power: $0.02^2/50 = 8\times10^{-6}$ W. Output power: $0.4^2/200 = 8\times10^{-4}$ W. Ratio: $8\times10^{-4}/8\times10^{-6} = 100$. Power gain: $10\log_{10}(100) = 20.00$ dB.

**出處**　D. M. Pozar, *Microwave Engineering* ($P = V_{rms}^2/R$; power gain); ITU-R Recommendation V.574.

**3.1.1S033**　The voltage gain in dB and the power gain in dB of this amplifier are not the same number. Explain why, and give the general relation between them.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　$20\log_{10}(V_2/V_1)$ equals the power gain only when input and load resistances are equal. With different resistances, $G_P = 20\log_{10}(V_2/V_1) + 10\log_{10}(R_1/R_2)$. Here the load resistance is four times the input resistance, so the same output voltage delivers one quarter of the power, and the power gain is 6.02 dB below the voltage gain.

**詳解**　$P_2/P_1 = (V_2^2/R_2)/(V_1^2/R_1) = (V_2/V_1)^2 (R_1/R_2)$. In dB: $26.0206 + 10\log_{10}(50/200) = 26.0206 - 6.0206 = 20.00$ dB.

**出處**　ITU-R Recommendation V.574 (relation between decibels for power and for voltage when impedances differ).

</details>

**3.1.1S034**　Suppose the load is changed to 50 Ω and the amplifier still produces 400 mV RMS across it. Does the power gain go up, go down or stay the same, and what does it become?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　It goes up, to 26.02 dB. For the same voltage, a lower resistance absorbs more power ($P = V^2/R$): the output power becomes four times larger. With equal input and load resistances the power gain equals the voltage gain in dB.

**詳解**　New output power: $0.4^2/50 = 3.2\times10^{-3}$ W. Ratio to the input power: $3.2\times10^{-3}/8\times10^{-6} = 400$. $10\log_{10}(400) = 26.0206$ dB, an increase of 6.02 dB over the 200 Ω case.

#### 情境題：Antenna gain references (dBi, dBd), EIRP and ERP

**Scenario | VHF repeater licensed by ERP**

A VHF land-mobile repeater has a transmitter output power of 50 W. Between the transmitter and the antenna there are a duplexer with 1.5 dB loss and a feeder with 2.0 dB loss. The collinear antenna has a gain of 6 dBd. The licence limits the effective radiated power (ERP, referenced to a half-wave dipole) to 100 W. Take the gain of a half-wave dipole as 2.15 dBi.

**3.1.1S036**　Find the ERP of the repeater in watts, rounded to one decimal place.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　88.9 W

**詳解**　Transmitter power: $10\log_{10}(50000) = 46.9897$ dBm. ERP $= 46.9897 - 1.5 - 2.0 + 6 = 49.4897$ dBm. In watts: $10^{4.94897}$ mW $= 88914.0$ mW $= 88.914$ W, which rounds to 88.9 W. (Equivalently $50\times10^{0.25} = 50\times1.77828$.)

**出處**　ITU Radio Regulations, Article 1, No. 1.162 (effective radiated power in a given direction).

</details>

**3.1.1S037**　Find the EIRP of the repeater in dBW, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　21.64 dBW

**詳解**　Transmitter power: $10\log_{10}(50) = 16.9897$ dBW. Antenna gain: $6 + 2.15 = 8.15$ dBi. EIRP $= 16.9897 - 1.5 - 2.0 + 8.15 = 21.6397$ dBW, which rounds to 21.64 dBW.

**出處**　ITU Radio Regulations, Article 1, No. 1.161 (equivalent isotropically radiated power); C. A. Balanis, *Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design* (half-wave dipole gain).

</details>

**3.1.1S038**　For this one station the EIRP figure is larger than the ERP figure. Does the station radiate more power when it is described by its EIRP? Explain.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　No. The radiated field is the same; only the reference antenna differs. EIRP is the power an isotropic radiator would need to produce the same field in the main direction, and ERP is the power a half-wave dipole would need. Because the dipole already has 2.15 dBi of gain, it needs 2.15 dB less power, so EIRP = ERP + 2.15 dB for the same station.

**詳解**　ERP $= 49.4897$ dBm $= 19.4897$ dBW; EIRP $= 21.6397$ dBW; the difference is exactly 2.15 dB, a linear factor of $10^{0.215} = 1.6406$ (88.9 W ERP corresponds to 145.9 W EIRP).

**出處**　ITU Radio Regulations, Article 1, Nos. 1.161 and 1.162.

</details>

**3.1.1S039**　The power reaching the antenna terminals is about 22.3 W, yet the ERP is about 88.9 W. Does the 6 dBd antenna add power to the signal? What does antenna gain express?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　No. A passive antenna cannot radiate more total power than it receives (22.3 W at most). Gain expresses how much the antenna concentrates the radiated power in its favoured direction compared with the reference antenna, at the cost of less radiation in other directions. ERP is the dipole input power that would give the same field strength in that direction, not the total radiated power.

**詳解**　Power at the antenna: $50\times10^{-0.35} = 50\times0.446684 = 22.334$ W. Gain of 6 dBd is a factor of $10^{0.6} = 3.9811$ in the main direction: $22.334\times3.9811 = 88.914$ W ERP. Integrated over all directions, the radiated power is still no more than 22.334 W.

**出處**　C. A. Balanis, *Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design* (directivity and gain).

</details>

1. Total power: 200 W = 53.01 dBm.
2. $10\log_{10}(273) \approx 24$ dB (rounded to a whole dB).
3. Power per resource block: $53.01 - 24 = 29.01$ dBm.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 2 is wrong: the intermediate value was rounded too early. $10\log_{10}(273) = 24.3616$ dB, and dropping 0.36 dB there makes the final result wrong in the first decimal place. The corrected result is 28.65 dBm.

**詳解**　$53.0103 - 24.3616 = 28.6487$ dBm, which rounds to 28.65 dBm. Linear check: $200/273 = 0.73260$ W $= 732.60$ mW, and $10\log_{10}(732.60) = 28.6487$ dBm.

**出處**　3GPP TS 38.104, Table 5.3.2-1; ITU-R Recommendation V.574.

</details>

**Scenario | Phase noise and a spur of a local oscillator**

A local oscillator delivers a carrier of +7 dBm. At an offset of 100 kHz from the carrier its single-sideband phase noise is −98 dBc/Hz; assume this density is flat across a 15 kHz slice centred on that offset. A discrete spur at another offset is measured at −62 dBc. The noise and the spur are uncorrelated.

**3.1.1S061**　Find the noise power contained in the 15 kHz slice in nanowatts, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　11.91 nW

**詳解**　Absolute noise density: $7 - 98 = -91$ dBm/Hz. Bandwidth: $10\log_{10}(15000) = 41.7609$ dB-Hz. Noise power: $-91 + 41.7609 = -49.2391$ dBm. In milliwatts: $10^{-4.92391} = 1.19149\times10^{-5}$ mW $= 11.9149$ nW, which rounds to 11.91 nW.

**出處**　D. M. Pozar, *Microwave Engineering* (oscillator phase noise, dBc/Hz); B. Sklar, *Digital Communications: Fundamentals and Applications* ($N = N_0 B$).

</details>

**3.1.1S062**　The oscillator output is passed through an ideal noiseless buffer amplifier with 10 dB gain. What happens to the phase noise expressed in dBc/Hz and to the noise density expressed in dBm/Hz?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　The value in dBc/Hz does not change (still −98 dBc/Hz), because carrier and noise sidebands are amplified by the same 10 dB and dBc is their ratio. The absolute density in dBm/Hz rises by 10 dB, from −91 dBm/Hz to −81 dBm/Hz, as does the carrier (from +7 dBm to +17 dBm).

**詳解**　After the buffer: carrier $7 + 10 = 17$ dBm; noise density $-91 + 10 = -81$ dBm/Hz; ratio $-81 - 17 = -98$ dBc/Hz. A relative level is unchanged by a gain or loss common to both quantities; an absolute level is not.

**出處**　D. M. Pozar, *Microwave Engineering* (oscillator phase noise); ITU-R Recommendation V.574 (relative and absolute levels).

</details>

**3.1.1S063**　An engineer says: "−98 is far below −62, so the phase noise is negligible compared with the spur." What is wrong with this comparison, and in what bandwidth would the flat noise equal the spur?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　The two numbers have different units: −98 dBc/Hz is a density (power per hertz) and −62 dBc is a power. The density must first be integrated over the bandwidth of interest. The flat noise equals the spur in a bandwidth of about 3981 Hz; in the 15 kHz slice the noise is −56.24 dBc, which is actually 5.76 dB stronger than the spur.

**詳解**　Equal power when $-98 + 10\log_{10}(B) = -62$, so $10\log_{10}(B) = 36$ and $B = 10^{3.6} = 3981.07$ Hz. In 15 kHz: $-98 + 41.7609 = -56.2391$ dBc, and $-56.2391 - (-62) = 5.7609$ dB.

**出處**　D. M. Pozar, *Microwave Engineering* (oscillator phase noise); ITU-R Recommendation SM.329 (levels relative to the carrier, dBc).

</details>

**3.1.1S064**　The noise in the 15 kHz slice relative to the carrier is computed as follows. Identify the wrong step, explain why, and give the corrected result in dBc rounded to two decimal places.

1. Phase noise density: −98 dBc/Hz.
2. Bandwidth factor: $10\log_{10}(15) = 11.76$ dB.
3. Noise in the slice: $-98 + 11.76 = -86.24$ dBc.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 2 is wrong. The density is per hertz, so the bandwidth must be in hertz: 15 kHz = 15000 Hz, not 15. The corrected result is −56.24 dBc.

**詳解**　$10\log_{10}(15000) = 41.7609$ dB. Noise: $-98 + 41.7609 = -56.2391$ dBc, which rounds to −56.24 dBc. The wrong result is exactly 30 dB too low, the factor of 1000 between kHz and Hz.

**出處**　B. Sklar, *Digital Communications: Fundamentals and Applications* ($N = N_0 B$); D. M. Pozar, *Microwave Engineering* (phase noise in dBc/Hz).

</details>

**3.1.1S065**　The combined level of the spur and the noise in the 15 kHz slice is computed as follows. Identify the wrong step, explain why, and give the corrected result in dBc rounded to two decimal places.

1. Noise in the 15 kHz slice: −56.24 dBc.
2. Spur: −62 dBc.
3. Combined level: $-56.24 + (-62) = -118.24$ dBc.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 3 is wrong. Adding two dB values multiplies the two ratios; the combined power of uncorrelated contributions is the sum of the linear ratios. The combined level must be higher than the larger of the two, not lower. The corrected result is −55.22 dBc.

**詳解**　$10^{-5.62391} = 2.37734\times10^{-6}$ and $10^{-6.2} = 6.30957\times10^{-7}$. Sum: $3.00830\times10^{-6}$. In dBc: $10\log_{10}(3.00830\times10^{-6}) = -55.2168$ dBc, which rounds to −55.22 dBc.

**出處**　ITU-R Recommendation V.574 (power summation in linear units); ITU-R Recommendation SM.329 (dBc).

</details>
