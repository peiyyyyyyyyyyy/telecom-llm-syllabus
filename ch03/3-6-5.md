# 3.6.5 驗算

所屬：[3.6 解題方法](3-6.md)

## 內文

#### 為什麼要學

算出答案不等於算對。電信計算常見的錯誤有固定的「指紋」：單位錯會差 30 dB 或 60 dB，bit 與 byte 錯會差 8 倍，功率比與電壓比弄混會差 2 倍的 dB 值，線性值當 dB 用則差得毫無規律。這些錯誤重算一次往往還是同樣的結果，因為重算走的是同一條路。驗算的意思是換一條路檢查：用粗略的量級估計、把答案代回原式、看極端情況下公式的行為、核對單位。實務上，鏈路預算審查、容量規劃覆核、告警門檻設定都靠這種快速檢查來攔住離譜的數字；對語言模型而言，這也是在輸出答案前自我檢查的主要手段。

#### 定義與符號

| 項目 | 意思 | 例子 |
|---|---|---|
| 量級估計（order-of-magnitude estimate） | 用好記的近似值快速算出答案的大概範圍 | 距離每增為 10 倍，自由空間損耗加 20 dB |
| 反推代回（back-substitution） | 把答案當作已知，倒著算回題目給的條件 | 由路徑損耗反推距離，看是否等於題目的距離 |
| 極端情況（limiting case） | 讓某個變數趨近 0、無限大或臨界值，看公式的結果是否合理 | M/M/1 的到達率趨近 0 時，停留時間應趨近服務時間 |
| 界限檢查（bound check） | 答案是否落在理論上必須滿足的範圍內 | 機率在 0 與 1 之間；效率不超過 100% |
| 單位檢查 | 答案的單位是否由算式自然得出，並且是題目要的 | bit ÷ (bit/s) 得到 s |
| 錯誤指紋 | 特定錯誤造成的固定偏差量 | 差 60 dB 多半是 km 與 m 或 MHz 與 GHz 弄錯 |
| 獨立路徑 | 用另一條公式或另一組單位重算同一個量 | FSPL 用原式 $20\log_{10}(4\pi d/\lambda)$ 複核 |

#### 公式與推導

驗算有四種檢查，建議依下列順序進行，由快到慢。

**一、單位與界限檢查（幾秒鐘）。** 先看答案的單位是不是題目要的，再看數值是否違反基本界限：機率與利用率在 0 與 1 之間；被動元件的損耗為正；接收功率不會大於發射功率加上天線增益；可達速率不會超過 3.2.1 的 Shannon 容量；編碼效率不超過 100%（3.7.2）；穩態排隊系統要有 $\rho < 1$（3.4.3）。違反任何一條，答案一定有錯。

**二、量級估計。** 用幾個好記的參考值，心算出答案應該落在哪裡：

- dB 的心算：3 dB 約 2 倍、10 dB 是 10 倍，其餘用這兩個組合（3.1.1）。
- 自由空間損耗：距離或頻率每增為 10 倍加 20 dB，加倍加 6 dB（3.1.2）。可以先算 1 m 處的損耗 $20\log_{10}(4\pi/\lambda)$，再加上距離項。
- 熱雜訊：$-174$ dBm/Hz，1 MHz 頻寬為 $-114$ dBm，頻寬每增為 10 倍加 10 dB（3.1.5）。
- Shannon 容量：高 SNR 時每多 3 dB，頻譜效率約多 1 bit/s/Hz（3.2.1）。

估計值與答案差在 1～2 dB 或幾個百分點之內屬於正常；差到 3 dB、10 dB、30 dB、60 dB 或 8 倍，就對照錯誤指紋去找原因。常見的指紋如下：

| 偏差 | 可能原因 |
|---|---|
| 30 dB 或 1000 倍 | dBm 與 dBW、W 與 mW、km 與 m（一次方）、kHz 與 MHz（頻寬） |
| 60 dB | FSPL 中 km 與 m、MHz 與 GHz 弄錯（20log 使 1000 倍變成 60 dB） |
| 8 倍 | bit 與 byte |
| dB 值差 2 倍 | $10\log_{10}$ 與 $20\log_{10}$ 用錯（功率比與電壓比） |
| 約 2.3 倍或 0.43 倍 | $\ln$ 與 $\log_{10}$ 用錯 |
| 約 4.9% 或 7.4% | $10^6$ 與 $2^{20}$、$10^9$ 與 $2^{30}$ |

**三、反推代回。** 把算出的答案當成已知，從公式的另一端倒著算，看能不能得回題目的條件。反推的價值在於它會把錯誤「翻譯」成一個看得懂的量，例如反推出來的距離是 0.2 m 而不是 200 m，馬上知道錯在 1000 倍。另一種做法是用獨立的關係式交叉檢查，例如排隊問題用 Little 定理 $N = \lambda T$（3.4.3）核對平均人數與平均時間是否一致。

**四、極端情況。** 把某個輸入推到極端，看公式的行為是否合乎直覺：距離趨近無限大時接收功率應趨近零；SNR 趨近 0 時容量應趨近 0；到達率趨近服務率時等待時間應趨近無限大；通道數增加時阻塞機率應下降。如果自己用的公式在極端情況下給出不合理的結果，表示公式選錯或記錯。

四種檢查都通過，不能證明答案完全正確，但可以排除絕大多數的大錯。

#### 算例

**算例 1（量級估計加反推：找出 60 dB 的錯誤）** 題目：求 28 GHz、距離 200 m 的自由空間路徑損耗。有人算得 47.40 dB，請檢查。

量級估計：28 GHz 的波長 $\lambda = 3\times10^8 / (28\times10^9) = 0.01071$ m。1 m 處的損耗為

$$20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi}{0.01071}\right) = 61.38\ \text{dB}$$

200 m 再加 $20\log_{10}(200) = 46.02$ dB，合計約 107.4 dB。答案 47.40 dB 比 1 m 處的損耗還小，不可能正確，而且與估計值差 60 dB。

反推：把 47.40 dB 代回配 km 與 GHz 的式子，求對應的距離：

$$20\log_{10} d_{\text{km}} = 47.40 - 92.45 - 20\log_{10}(28) = 47.40 - 92.45 - 28.94 = -73.99$$

$$d = 10^{-73.99/20} = 10^{-3.6995}\ \text{km} = 2.0\times10^{-4}\ \text{km} = 0.2\ \text{m}$$

反推得到 0.2 m，是題目距離的千分之一。對照錯誤指紋，60 dB 來自單位與常數不配：原計算把頻率 28（GHz）代入了配 MHz 的常數 32.44，即 $32.44 + 20\log_{10}(0.2) + 20\log_{10}(28) = 32.44 - 13.98 + 28.94 = 47.40$ dB。

改正：

$$\text{FSPL} = 92.45 + 20\log_{10}(0.2) + 20\log_{10}(28) = 92.45 - 13.98 + 28.94 = 107.41\ \text{dB}$$

與量級估計的 107.4 dB 相符。

**算例 2（極端情況加反推：找出公式用錯）** 題目：某路由器輸出埠可視為 M/M/1 佇列，封包到達率 800 封包/秒，服務率 1000 封包/秒。求封包在系統中的平均停留時間（含服務）。有人算得 0.8 ms，請檢查。

界限檢查：平均服務時間為 $1/\mu = 1/1000 = 1$ ms。停留時間包含等待與服務，不可能小於服務時間，0.8 ms 小於 1 ms，一定有錯。

極端情況：錯誤答案等於 $\rho/\mu = 0.8/1000$ 秒。若公式真是 $\rho/\mu$，到達率趨近 0 時停留時間會趨近 0，但實際上即使沒有人排隊，封包也要花 1 ms 被服務；到達率趨近服務率時結果只會趨近 1 ms，但實際上佇列應該無限增長。兩個極端都不合理，表示公式用錯。

反推：用 Little 定理，$N = \lambda T = 800 \times 0.0008 = 0.64$ 個封包；但 M/M/1 的平均系統人數是 $\rho/(1-\rho) = 0.8/0.2 = 4$ 個封包，兩者不一致。

改正（3.4.3，條件 $\rho = 0.8 < 1$ 成立）：

$$T = \frac{1}{\mu - \lambda} = \frac{1}{1000 - 800} = 0.005\ \text{s} = 5\ \text{ms}$$

複核：$N = \lambda T = 800 \times 0.005 = 4$ 個封包，與 $\rho/(1-\rho)$ 一致；其中等待時間為 $5 - 1 = 4$ ms。極端情況：$\lambda \to 0$ 時 $T \to 1/\mu = 1$ ms，$\lambda \to \mu$ 時 $T \to \infty$，都合理。

#### 常見錯誤

1. **用同一個方法重算一次當作驗算。** 同樣的單位錯誤或公式錯誤會重現，要換一條路徑檢查。
2. **只看數字不看量級。** 47.40 dB 看起來像個正常的 dB 值，不和參考值比較就看不出差了 60 dB。
3. **把取位差異當成錯誤，或把大偏差當成取位差異。** 差 0.01～0.03 dB 多半來自常數取位（3.6.4）；差 3 dB 以上就不是取位問題。
4. **忽略公式的前提。** 例如 $\rho \ge 1$ 時仍套用穩態公式，得到負的等待時間卻沒有察覺。

#### 和其他小節的關係

驗算要靠 3.6.2 留下的中間值才能定位錯在哪一步；單位檢查對應 3.6.3，取位差異的判斷對應 3.6.4。量級估計用到的參考值來自 3.1.1、3.1.2、3.1.5 與 3.2.1；算例用到 3.1.2 的自由空間路徑損耗與 3.4.3 的 M/M/1 與 Little 定理；界限檢查用到 3.2.1、3.4.2、3.7.2 的理論界限。

**出處**　自由空間衰減公式見 ITU-R 建議書 P.525（Calculation of free-space attenuation）；M/M/1 平均停留時間與 Little 定理見 Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1: Theory* 與 Bertsekas & Gallager, *Data Networks*（延遲模型一章）；回顧與檢查解答的方法見 G. Pólya, *How to Solve It*。

Back-substitute the reported value into $N = -174 + 10\log_{10} B_{\text{Hz}} + NF$ to find the noise bandwidth it implies. Give the implied bandwidth in kHz, rounded to two decimal places.

#### 單題：Throughput claims against the Shannon bound

**3.6.5G011**　A vendor claims 600 Mbit/s of single-stream (one spatial layer) throughput in a 100 MHz channel at an SNR of 15 dB over an AWGN channel. Compute the Shannon capacity of this channel in Mbit/s, rounded to two decimal places, to check whether the claim is possible.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　502.78

**詳解**　Step 1: $\text{SNR} = 10^{1.5} = 31.6228$.
Step 2: $\log_2(1 + 31.6228) = \log_2(32.6228) = 5.02781$ bit/s/Hz.
Step 3: $C = 100\times10^{6} \times 5.02781 = 502.78$ Mbit/s. The claimed 600 Mbit/s exceeds the bound by about 19%, so it cannot be a single-stream result under these conditions.
\boxed{502.78}

**出處**　C. E. Shannon, "A Mathematical Theory of Communication," Bell Syst. Tech. J., 1948.

</details>

#### 單題：Queueing results cross-checked with Little's law

**3.6.5G016**　A router output port is modelled as an M/M/1 queue with packet arrival rate 1200 packets/s and service rate 1500 packets/s. A report gives the mean time in system as 0.8 ms. Use Little's law and the M/M/1 mean number in system $\rho/(1-\rho)$ to test the report, then give the correct mean time in system in ms, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　3.33

**詳解**　Step 1: $\rho = 1200/1500 = 0.8$; M/M/1 mean number in system $= 0.8/0.2 = 4$ packets.
Step 2 (Little): with the reported time, $N = \lambda T = 1200 \times 0.0008 = 0.96$ packets, which disagrees with 4, so the report is wrong (0.8 ms is $\rho/\mu$).
Step 3: $T = 1/(\mu - \lambda) = 1/300 = 0.0033333$ s $= 3.33$ ms. Check: $\lambda T = 1200 \times 0.0033333 = 4.00$ packets, consistent.
\boxed{3.33}

**出處**　L. Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1: Theory*; J. D. C. Little, "A Proof for the Queuing Formula L = λW," Operations Research, 1961.

</details>

Apply Little's law to the claim, compare with the M/M/1 mean number in system, and give the correct mean time in system in ms, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

</details>

**3.6.5S004**　A colleague "verifies" every tool output by typing the same inputs into the same spreadsheet a second time. Why is that not a real check, and what makes the reference-distance estimate a better one?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Repeating the same procedure repeats the same mistakes: a wrong unit pair or a wrong constant gives the same wrong number twice. #### 情境題：Limiting-case checks

**Scenario | Delay at a router port**

An edge router port is modelled as an M/M/1 queue. Packets arrive at 4500 packets/s on average and the port serves 5000 packets/s. The operations team uses the M/M/1 formulas from a handbook and wants to confirm that its delay spreadsheet behaves correctly.

**3.6.5S021**　Compute the mean time a packet spends in the system (waiting plus service), in ms, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　2.00 ms

**詳解**　$T = 1/(\mu - \lambda) = 1/(5000 - 4500) = 1/500 = 0.002$ s $= 2.00$ ms.

**出處**　L. Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1: Theory*.

</details>

**3.6.5S022**　Use Little's law to obtain the mean number of packets in the system from $T$, check it against $\rho/(1-\rho)$, and then give the mean number waiting in the queue (excluding the one in service), rounded to one decimal place.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　8.1 packets

**詳解**　$N = \lambda T = 4500 \times 0.002 = 9$ packets. $\rho = 0.9$; $\rho/(1-\rho) = 0.9/0.1 = 9$, consistent. $L_q = N - \rho = 9 - 0.9 = 8.1$ packets (check: $\rho^2/(1-\rho) = 0.81/0.1 = 8.1$).

**出處**　J. D. C. Little, "A Proof for the Queuing Formula L = λW," Operations Research, 1961; L. Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1*.

</details>

**3.6.5S023**　How can the team test the delay spreadsheet by letting the arrival rate approach zero, and what value must the time in system approach?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　With almost no traffic there is no queue, so each packet only experiences its own service time: $T$ must approach $1/\mu = 0.2$ ms. If the spreadsheet's formula gives 0 (as $\rho/\mu$ would) or some other value at $\lambda \to 0$, the wrong formula is coded.

**詳解**　$T = 1/(\mu - \lambda) \to 1/5000$ s $= 0.2$ ms as $\lambda \to 0$.

**出處**　L. Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1*; G. Pólya, *How to Solve It*.

</details>

**3.6.5S024**　What should the spreadsheet show as the arrival rate approaches the service rate, and what does it mean if the delay stays finite and small there?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　As $\lambda \to \mu$ ($\rho \to 1$) the queue grows without bound, so $T$ must tend to infinity. A formula that stays finite near $\rho = 1$ (for example one that only scales with $\rho$) does not describe a queue at all, so the coded formula is wrong even if it happens to give plausible numbers at moderate load.

**詳解**　For example, at $\lambda = 4990$ packets/s, $T = 1/10 = 100$ ms, fifty times the value at 4500 packets/s.

**出處**　L. Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1*.

</details>

**3.6.5S025**　For next year's forecast of 5500 packets/s on the same port, a colleague writes: Step 1: $\rho = 5500/5000 = 1.1$. Step 2: $T = 1/(5000 - 5500) = -0.002$ s. Step 3: "the delay is 2 ms, same as today". Identify the wrong step, explain it, and give the service rate the port would need to keep the mean time in system at 2 ms.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 2 is wrong: the M/M/1 formula only holds in steady state with $\rho < 1$. At $\rho = 1.1$ there is no steady state; the backlog grows by about 500 packets every second and the delay increases without limit, so the negative value (and its magnitude in Step 3) is meaningless. To keep $T = 2$ ms the port needs $\mu = 6000$ packets/s.

**詳解**　$\mu = \lambda + 1/T = 5500 + 1/0.002 = 6000$ packets/s; then $\rho = 0.917 < 1$ and $T = 1/(6000 - 5500) = 2$ ms.

**出處**　L. Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume 1*; D. Bertsekas and R. Gallager, *Data Networks*.

</details>

**Scenario | Checking an Erlang B routine for a rural site**

A rural site has 10 traffic channels. In the busy hour it receives 180 call attempts with a mean holding time of 2 minutes; blocked calls are cleared (Erlang B). The operator has just written its own Erlang B routine with the recursion $B_0 = 1$, $B_n = A B_{n-1}/(n + A B_{n-1})$ and wants to check it before trusting it.

**3.6.5S026**　Compute the offered traffic and the blocking probability for 10 channels, giving the blocking in percent, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　$A = 6$ Erl; $B = 4.31\%$.

**詳解**　$A = 180 \times 2/60 = 6$ Erl. Recursion: $B_1 = 0.85714$, $B_2 = 0.72000$, $B_3 = 0.59016$, $B_4 = 0.46957$, $B_5 = 0.36040$, $B_6 = 0.26492$, $B_7 = 0.18505$, $B_8 = 0.12188$, $B_9 = 0.075145$, $B_{10} = 6 \times 0.075145/(10 + 0.45087) = 0.45087/10.45087 = 0.043142$, i.e. 4.31%.

**出處**　ITU-D / ITU-T *Teletraffic Engineering Handbook* (Erlang B recursion); ITU-T Recommendation E.521.

</details>

**3.6.5S027**　Compute the carried traffic in Erl, rounded to three decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　5.741 Erl

**詳解**　Carried $= A(1 - B) = 6 \times (1 - 0.043142) = 5.7411$ Erl, which rounds to 5.741 Erl. It is below both the offered 6 Erl and the 10 channels, as it must be.

**出處**　ITU-D / ITU-T *Teletraffic Engineering Handbook*.

</details>

**3.6.5S028**　Which limiting cases with known closed-form answers can be used to test the new routine, and why are they good tests?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　$N = 0$ must give $B = 1$ (every call blocked); $N = 1$ must give $B = A/(1 + A)$; $A \to 0$ must give $B \to 0$; and for very large $N$ the blocking must vanish. Each has an answer that can be computed by hand independently of the recursion, so a wrong index, a wrong starting value or a swapped term shows up immediately.

**詳解**　For example, with $A = 6$ and $N = 1$: $B = 6/7 = 0.85714$, which must equal the routine's $B_1$.

**出處**　ITU-D / ITU-T *Teletraffic Engineering Handbook*; G. Pólya, *How to Solve It*.

</details>

**3.6.5S029**　The routine's output table shows the blocking for 6 Erl rising slightly from 9 to 10 channels. Without recomputing anything, why must the routine be wrong?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Erlang B blocking decreases strictly as channels are added for a fixed offered traffic: an extra server can only take calls that would otherwise be lost. Any increase in $B$ with $N$ violates this monotonicity and proves a bug, typically a loop index off by one or a value overwritten in the recursion.

**詳解**　In the correct recursion for 6 Erl the values fall at every step, e.g. $B_9 = 0.07514 > B_{10} = 0.04314$; a table in which $B_{10} > B_9$ cannot come from the Erlang B formula.

**出處**　ITU-D / ITU-T *Teletraffic Engineering Handbook*.

</details>

**3.6.5S030**　A test run reads: Step 1: $A = 180 \times 2 = 360$ Erl. Step 2: run the recursion with $N = 10$. Step 3: $B = 0.972$, "the site is hopelessly congested". Identify the wrong step, give the check that exposes it, and give the correct blocking.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 1 is wrong: 180 calls per hour multiplied by a holding time in minutes gives call-minutes per hour, not erlangs. The holding time must be in hours: $A = 180 \times 2/60 = 6$ Erl. A unit check exposes it (erlangs = calls per hour × hours per call), and so does a bound check: 360 Erl offered to 10 channels would mean 36 calls in progress per channel at all times. The correct blocking is 4.31%.

**詳解**　With $A = 6$ Erl the recursion gives $B_{10} = 0.043142$, i.e. 4.31% (with $A = 360$ it gives 0.9723).

**出處**　ITU-T Recommendation E.600 (terms and definitions of traffic engineering); ITU-D / ITU-T *Teletraffic Engineering Handbook*.

</details>

**Scenario | Vendor throughput claim on a 40 MHz channel**

A vendor's datasheet claims 320 Mbit/s of single-stream throughput for a point-to-point radio in a 40 MHz channel. The measured SNR on the operator's link is 18 dB, and the channel can be treated as AWGN. The operator wants to know whether the claim can apply to this link.

**3.6.5S031**　Compute the Shannon capacity of the link in Mbit/s, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　240.09 Mbit/s

**詳解**　$\text{SNR} = 10^{1.8} = 63.0957$. $\log_2(64.0957) = 6.00216$ bit/s/Hz. $C = 40 \times 6.00216 = 240.086$ Mbit/s, which rounds to 240.09 Mbit/s.

**出處**　C. E. Shannon, "A Mathematical Theory of Communication," Bell Syst. Tech. J., 1948.

</details>

**3.6.5S032**　Back-substitute the claim: what minimum SNR in dB would a single stream need to carry 320 Mbit/s in 40 MHz? Round to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　24.07 dB

**詳解**　$C/B = 320/40 = 8$ bit/s/Hz. $\text{SNR} = 2^{8} - 1 = 255$. $10\log_{10}(255) = 24.0654$ dB, which rounds to 24.07 dB, about 6 dB more than the link has.

**出處**　C. E. Shannon, Bell Syst. Tech. J., 1948.

</details>

**3.6.5S033**　The claim exceeds the Shannon bound for this link. Does that prove the datasheet is false? What should the operator check before rejecting it?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　No. The bound applies to one stream in the stated bandwidth at the stated SNR. The claim may assume a higher SNR (the back-substitution shows at least 24 dB), several MIMO streams or two polarisations, a wider channel, or count bits before protocol overhead. The operator should check those assumptions; under the operator's own conditions (one stream, 40 MHz, 18 dB), no scheme can exceed 240 Mbit/s.

**詳解**　For example, two polarisations at 18 dB would give a bound of $2 \times 240.09 = 480.17$ Mbit/s, which would allow the claim.

**出處**　C. E. Shannon, Bell Syst. Tech. J., 1948; A. Goldsmith, *Wireless Communications* (MIMO capacity).

</details>

**3.6.5S034**　A colleague's capacity routine returns 40 Mbit/s for this channel when the SNR is set to zero (linear). What limiting-case check does this fail, and what does it suggest about the code?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　With no signal power ($\text{SNR} \to 0$) the capacity must go to zero, because $\log_2(1 + 0) = 0$. Returning 40 Mbit/s, i.e. $B \times 1$, suggests the routine computes $\log_2(\text{SNR} + 2)$, adds 1 twice, or uses $\log_2(1 + \text{SNR})$ with SNR in a wrong form; in any case the formula is mis-coded.

**詳解**　At SNR = 0, $C = 40\times10^{6} \times \log_2(1) = 0$; a result of $40\times10^{6}$ bit/s corresponds to $\log_2(\cdot) = 1$.

**出處**　C. E. Shannon, Bell Syst. Tech. J., 1948; T. M. Cover and J. A. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*.

</details>

**3.6.5S035**　A junior engineer computes: Step 1: SNR $= 18$. Step 2: $\log_2(1 + 18) = 4.2479$. Step 3: $C = 40 \times 4.2479 = 169.92$ Mbit/s. Identify the wrong step and give the correct capacity to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 1 is wrong: 18 is the SNR in dB; the Shannon formula needs the linear ratio $10^{1.8} = 63.10$. The correct capacity is 240.09 Mbit/s. The high-SNR rule (about 1 bit/s/Hz per 3 dB, so about 6 bit/s/Hz at 18 dB) would have flagged 4.25 bit/s/Hz as too low.

**詳解**　$C = 40 \times \log_2(1 + 63.0957) = 40 \times 6.00216 = 240.086$ Mbit/s, which rounds to 240.09 Mbit/s.

**出處**　C. E. Shannon, Bell Syst. Tech. J., 1948.

</details>

**Scenario | Amplifier gain from voltage measurements**

A lab measures an RF amplifier with an RMS voltmeter: 50 mV at the input and 1.6 V at the output, both across 50 Ω. Several engineers compute the gain independently, and the lab lead compares their results.

**3.6.5S056**　Compute the power gain in dB, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　30.10 dB

**詳解**　Voltage ratio $= 1.6/0.05 = 32$. With equal impedances, $G = 20\log_{10}(32) = 30.1030$ dB, which rounds to 30.10 dB.

**3.6.5S057**　As an independent path, compute the input and output powers in dBm from $P = V^2/R$ and give the output power in dBm, rounded to two decimal places; confirm that the difference equals the gain.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Output 17.09 dBm (input −13.01 dBm; difference 30.10 dB).

**詳解**　$P_{out} = 1.6^2/50 = 0.0512$ W $= 51.2$ mW $= 10\log_{10}(51.2) = 17.0927$ dBm, rounded 17.09 dBm. $P_{in} = 0.05^2/50 = 5\times10^{-5}$ W $= 0.05$ mW $= -13.0103$ dBm. Difference $= 17.0927 + 13.0103 = 30.1030$ dB.

**3.6.5S058**　One engineer reports 15.05 dB. Without recomputing, what is the likely error, and how can the lead tell?

**詳解**　$10\log_{10}(32) = 15.05$ dB; $30.10/15.05 = 2$.

**Scenario | Sector throughput and daily volume review**

A 3-sector LTE site uses a 20 MHz carrier in each sector. Drive tests give an average spectral efficiency of 1.8 bit/s/Hz per sector. The capacity team estimates the site throughput and the volume carried in one fully loaded busy hour, with 1 GB = $10^{9}$ bytes.

**3.6.5S061**　Compute the site throughput in Mbit/s and the busy-hour volume in GB, rounded to one decimal place.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　108.0 Mbit/s; 48.6 GB.

**詳解**　Sector: $1.8 \times 20 = 36$ Mbit/s; site: $3 \times 36 = 108.0$ Mbit/s. Volume: $108\times10^{6} \times 3600 = 3.888\times10^{11}$ bit $= 4.86\times10^{10}$ bytes $= 48.6$ GB.

**出處**　ITU-R Report M.2134 (requirements related to technical performance for IMT-Advanced: spectral efficiency definitions); IEC 80000-13.

</details>

**3.6.5S062**　A report from another region lists an average sector spectral efficiency of 18 bit/s/Hz for the same single-layer configuration. Which bound check rejects it, and how?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　The Shannon bound: 18 bit/s/Hz on one layer needs an SNR of about $2^{18} - 1 \approx 2.6\times10^{5}$, i.e. 54 dB, everywhere in the cell, which no macro cell provides; even 30 dB SINR allows only about 10 bit/s/Hz. A tenfold offset suggests a decimal or bandwidth unit slip.

**詳解**　$10\log_{10}(2^{18} - 1) = 54.2$ dB; $\log_2(1 + 1000) = 9.97$ bit/s/Hz at 30 dB.

**出處**　C. E. Shannon, Bell Syst. Tech. J., 1948.

</details>

**3.6.5S063**　A planner argues that doubling the bandwidth while keeping the same transmit power will double the throughput. Which limiting-case check shows this cannot hold indefinitely?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　With fixed power, a wider bandwidth also admits more noise, so the SNR per hertz falls. As $B \to \infty$ the Shannon capacity tends to the finite limit $P/(N_0 \ln 2)$, so throughput cannot keep doubling; doubling bandwidth only nearly doubles throughput when the link is bandwidth-limited (high SNR).

**詳解**　$\lim_{B\to\infty} B\log_2(1 + P/(N_0 B)) = (P/N_0)\log_2 e$.

**出處**　C. E. Shannon, Bell Syst. Tech. J., 1948; T. M. Cover and J. A. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*.

</details>

**3.6.5S064**　A team member computes the busy-hour volume: Step 1: site throughput $= 108$ Mbit/s. Step 2: $108 \times 3600 = 388\,800$ Mbit. Step 3: volume $= 388.8$ GB. Identify the wrong step and give the correct volume.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 3 is wrong: 388 800 Mbit is 388.8 Gbit, not gigabytes; dividing by 8 bits per byte gives 48.6 GB. The 8× offset is the bit/byte fingerprint.

**詳解**　$388\,800\ \text{Mbit}/8 = 48\,600$ MB $= 48.6$ GB.

**出處**　IEC 80000-13.

</details>
