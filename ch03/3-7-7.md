# 3.7.7 ARQ 與 HARQ：停等、Go-Back-N、選擇性重傳的效率

所屬：[3.7 資訊理論與編碼](3-7.md)

## 內文

#### 為什麼要學

通道編碼不可能把錯誤率壓到零，剩下的錯誤要靠重傳補救。自動重傳請求（automatic repeat request, ARQ）讓接收端用 CRC 偵測錯誤，再用確認（ACK）或否定確認（NACK）要求傳送端重送。行動網路裡有兩層這樣的機制：無線鏈路控制（RLC）層的 ARQ，以及媒體存取控制（MAC）層與實體層合作的混合式 ARQ（hybrid ARQ, HARQ）。HARQ 把重傳和通道編碼結合，接收端不丟掉失敗的那一次，而是和重傳合併後再解碼。這一節要會用三種基本 ARQ 的效率公式估算鏈路利用率，並理解 HARQ 為什麼需要多個平行的行程（process）。

#### 定義與符號

| 符號 | 意思 | 單位 |
|---|---|---|
| $p$ | 一個訊框（封包）傳送失敗的機率，各次獨立 | 無 |
| $T_f$ | 一個訊框的傳送時間（訊框長度 ÷ 位元率） | s |
| $T_p$ | 單程傳播延遲 | s |
| $a$ | 正規化傳播延遲，$a=T_p/T_f$ | 無 |
| $W$ | 滑動視窗大小 | 訊框 |
| $N_r$ | 成功送出一個訊框平均需要傳送的訊框數 | 個 |
| $\eta$ | 效率（正規化吞吐量）：鏈路時間中用於成功送達新訊框的比例 | 無 |
| HARQ 行程數 | 可同時進行的停等行程個數 | 個 |

#### 公式與推導

**模型假設。** 以下公式採用常見的簡化模型：傳送端隨時有資料可送；ACK／NACK 很短，傳送時間與處理時間忽略不計；ACK／NACK 本身不會出錯；每個訊框以機率 $p$ 獨立出錯。不同教科書因假設不同（例如把 ACK 時間或逾時計入）會得到形式不同的式子，使用前要先對照假設。

**停等（stop-and-wait）。** 送出一個訊框後等 ACK 回來才送下一個。一次嘗試佔用 $T_f+2T_p=T_f(1+2a)$。每次成功的機率是 $1-p$，平均要試 $N_r=1/(1-p)$ 次，所以

$$\eta_{SW}=\frac{T_f}{N_r\,T_f(1+2a)}=\frac{1-p}{1+2a}$$

**Go-Back-N。** 傳送端連續送訊框，不等 ACK；一旦某個訊框出錯，就從那個訊框開始全部重送。假設視窗夠大（$W\ge 2a+1$），得知出錯時已經又送出了約 $2a$ 個訊框，所以每次出錯要重送 $2a+1$ 個。平均每成功一個訊框需要傳送

$$N_r=1+\frac{(2a+1)\,p}{1-p}=\frac{1+2ap}{1-p}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \eta_{GBN}=\frac{1-p}{1+2ap}$$

若 $W<2a+1$，視窗先用完，每次出錯重送 $W$ 個，而且無錯時每 $2a+1$ 個訊框時間也只能送 $W$ 個：

$$\eta_{GBN}=\frac{W(1-p)}{(2a+1)(1-p+Wp)}$$

**選擇性重傳（selective repeat）。** 只重送出錯的那一個，$N_r=1/(1-p)$：

$$\eta_{SR}=1-p\ \ (W\ge 2a+1),\qquad \eta_{SR}=\frac{W(1-p)}{2a+1}\ \ (W<2a+1)$$

三者比較：$a$ 很小時差別不大；$a$ 很大（長延遲鏈路）時停等的效率被 $1+2a$ 拖垮，Go-Back-N 的損失則隨 $ap$ 成長。

**HARQ。** HARQ 的每一個行程就是一個停等協定：送出一個傳輸區塊，等回饋，失敗就重傳，接收端把各次收到的軟性資訊合併後再解碼。重傳可以是相同的位元（Chase 合併），也可以是不同的冗餘版本（遞增冗餘）；NR 的 LDPC 速率匹配定義了 0～3 共四個冗餘版本。單一停等行程在等待回饋的期間不能送新資料，所以系統同時開多個行程輪流使用：若從一次傳送到最早可以重傳之間隔了 $T_{RTT}$ 個時槽，至少要有 $T_{RTT}$ 個行程才能每個時槽都排資料。

NR 的行程數由 RRC 設定：下行每個細胞最多 16 個（依 UE 能力可到 32 個），參數 nrofHARQ-ProcessesForPDSCH 可設為 2、4、6、10、12、16，另有擴充參數可設為 32，沒有設定時 UE 假設為 8 個；上行每個細胞 16 個，可用 nrofHARQ-ProcessesForPUSCH 設為 32，沒有設定時為 16 個（TS 38.214 clause 5.1、6.1；TS 38.331 的 PDSCH-ServingCellConfig 與 PUSCH-ServingCellConfig）。

若每次傳送獨立以機率 $p$ 失敗、最多傳 $n_{max}$ 次，平均傳送次數與殘餘錯誤率為

$$\bar{N}=\sum_{i=0}^{n_{max}-1}p^{\,i}=\frac{1-p^{\,n_{max}}}{1-p},\qquad P_{res}=p^{\,n_{max}}$$

實際上合併後的失敗機率會比首次傳送低，這個式子是保守的估計。

#### 算例

**算例 1（三種 ARQ 的效率）** 已知訊框長 1000 bit、位元率 1 Mbit/s、鏈路長 3000 km（傳播速度取 $3\times10^8$ m/s）、$p=0.1$。

1. $T_f=1000/10^6=1$ ms；$T_p=3\times10^6/(3\times10^8)=10$ ms；$a=10$，$2a+1=21$。
2. 停等：$\eta=0.9/21=0.0429$。
3. Go-Back-N（$W\ge21$）：$\eta=0.9/(1+2\times10\times0.1)=0.9/3=0.300$。
4. 選擇性重傳（$W\ge21$）：$\eta=0.900$。
5. 若視窗只有 $W=7$：Go-Back-N 為 $7\times0.9/[21\times(0.9+0.7)]=6.3/33.6=0.1875$；選擇性重傳為 $7\times0.9/21=0.300$。

合理性檢查：效率都不超過 $1-p=0.9$；同樣條件下選擇性重傳 ≥ Go-Back-N ≥ 停等。若鏈路很短使 $a=0.01$，停等為 $0.9/1.02=0.882$，Go-Back-N 為 $0.9/1.002=0.898$，三者幾乎一樣，說明差異主要來自延遲。

**算例 2（HARQ 的次數與行程數）** 假設每次傳送的區塊錯誤率 $p=0.1$ 且各次獨立，最多傳 4 次；假設從傳送到最早可重傳相隔 8 個時槽。

1. 平均傳送次數 $\bar N=1+0.1+0.01+0.001=1.111$ 次。
2. 殘餘錯誤率 $P_{res}=0.1^4=10^{-4}$，這部分留給 RLC 層的 ARQ 處理。
3. 每個時槽都要能排資料，至少需要 8 個行程；NR 下行未設定時的 8 個恰好夠用。若只設定 4 個，最多只有 $4/8=50\%$ 的時槽能排給這個 UE。

合理性檢查：成功送達的比例 $1-10^{-4}$ 除以平均次數 1.111 得 0.900，和 $1-p$ 一致，表示重傳的代價就是那 10% 的資源。

#### 常見錯誤

- 套公式不看假設。$\eta_{GBN}=(1-p)/(1+2ap)$ 只在 $W\ge2a+1$ 時成立；視窗較小要用另一式。
- $a$ 的分子分母寫反，或 $T_f$、$T_p$ 單位不一致（一個用 ms、一個用 s）。
- 把 $p$ 當成位元錯誤率。這裡是訊框錯誤率；若位元錯誤獨立且機率為 $P_b$，$L$ 位元的訊框 $p=1-(1-P_b)^L$。
- 以為 HARQ 行程越多吞吐量就越高。行程數只要蓋住往返時間即可，再多不會增加可用的時槽。

#### 和其他小節的關係

接收端靠 3.7.8 的 CRC 判斷要回 ACK 還是 NACK。被重傳的是 3.7.5 的 LDPC 碼字的不同冗餘版本。$a$ 的計算用到 3.4.5 的傳輸延遲與傳播延遲；重傳造成的有效吞吐量下降會回到 3.2.2 的頻譜效率估算。

#### 情境題：Sliding-window ARQ

**Scenario | Inter-city fibre link with Go-Back-N**

Two data centres 800 km apart are linked by fibre at 1 Gbit/s (propagation speed $2\times10^{8}$ m/s). Use $\eta_{GBN} = (1-p)/(1+2ap)$ for $W \ge 2a+1$ and $\eta_{GBN} = W(1-p)/[(2a+1)(1-p+Wp)]$ otherwise; for selective repeat use $\eta_{SR} = 1-p$ for $W \ge 2a+1$ and $W(1-p)/(2a+1)$ otherwise.

**3.7.7S011**　A planner checks the window:

2. $T_{prop} = 800\times10^{3}/(2\times10^{8}) = 4$ ms.
3. $a = T_{frame}/T_{prop} = 10\,\mu\text{s}/4\,\text{ms} = 0.0025$.

Which step is wrong, and what is the correct result?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 3 is wrong: $a$ is propagation time divided by frame time, not the reverse. $a = 4\,\text{ms}/10\,\mu\text{s} = 400$, so $2a+1 = 801$ frames are needed; the 1023-frame window is sufficient, but a window of 2 is not.

**詳解**　$a = T_{prop}/T_{frame} = 4\times10^{-3}/10^{-5} = 400$. $2a+1 = 801 \le 1023$. The inverted ratio would suggest that the link is short in frame times, while in fact 400 frames are in flight in each direction.

**出處**　W. Stallings, Data and Computer Communications (sliding-window flow control)

</details>

**3.7.7S012**　What is the Go-Back-N efficiency? Give it in percent, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　55.50 %

**詳解**　$a = 400$ and $W = 1023 \ge 801$, so $\eta = (1-p)/(1+2ap)$. $2ap = 2\times400\times0.001 = 0.8$. $\eta = 0.999/1.8 = 0.555 = 55.50\%$.

**3.7.7S013**　A frame error probability of only 0.1 % costs this link almost half of its capacity. Explain why.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Each error makes Go-Back-N resend the failed frame plus every frame sent during the round trip, about $2a+1 = 801$ frames, so the loss grows with the product $ap$ (here $2ap = 0.8$), not with $p$ alone.

**詳解**　By the time the NACK arrives, about $2a = 800$ further frames have been sent and are discarded by the receiver, so one error costs about 801 frame times. On average there are $p/(1-p) \approx 0.001$ errors per good frame, costing $0.8$ frame times per good frame, hence $\eta \approx 1/1.8$. **答案**　Because the larger window removes the idle waiting that the 7-frame window imposes in every cycle, whereas extra retransmissions only occur on the 5 % of frames that fail; the gain from continuous sending outweighs the larger per-error cost.

**詳解**　With $W = 7$ the link is idle $9/16$ of the time in every cycle, error or not. #### 情境題：Frame length and bit errors

**Scenario | Choosing the frame size for a noisy radio link**

A narrowband private radio network runs selective-repeat ARQ at 1 Mbit/s with a window large enough that $\eta_{frame} = 1-p$. Every frame carries a 320-bit header plus $D$ bits of user data. Bit errors are independent with $P_b = 10^{-4}$, so a frame of $L = D + 320$ bits fails with $p = 1-(1-P_b)^{L}$. The fraction of link capacity that delivers user data is $\frac{D}{D+320}(1-P_b)^{D+320}$.

**3.7.7S026**　For $D = 2000$ bits, what is the frame error probability? Give it rounded to four decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　0.2071

**詳解**　$L = 2320$ bit. $(1-10^{-4})^{2320} = \exp[2320\ln(0.9999)] = \exp(-0.232012) = 0.792937$. $p = 1 - 0.792937 = 0.207063$, i.e. 0.2071.

**出處**　W. Stallings, Data and Computer Communications (frame error probability from bit error rate)

</details>

**3.7.7S027**　What fraction of the link capacity delivers user data for $D = 2000$ bits, and how much user data per second does that give? Give the fraction in percent, rounded to two decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　68.36 % (about 684 kbit/s)

**詳解**　Payload fraction $= 2000/2320 = 0.862069$. Success fraction $= 0.792937$. Product $= 0.862069 \times 0.792937 = 0.683566 = 68.36\%$. User data rate $= 0.683566 \times 1000 = 683.6$ kbit/s.

**出處**　W. Stallings, Data and Computer Communications (performance of selective-reject ARQ)

</details>

**3.7.7S028**　With $D = 500$ bits the user-data fraction is about 56 %, and with $D = 8000$ bits about 42 %, both lower than with $D = 2000$ bits. Explain why an optimum frame length exists.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Short frames waste capacity on the fixed 320-bit header; long frames are hit by errors more often and lose more data per error. The product of the two effects has a maximum, near $L \approx H/2 + \sqrt{H^{2}/4 + H/P_b} \approx 1956$ bits here.

**詳解**　The payload fraction $D/(D+H)$ increases with $D$, while the success probability $(1-P_b)^{D+H}$ decreases exponentially with $D$. Using $(1-P_b)^{L} \approx e^{-P_b L}$ and setting the derivative of $(1 - H/L)e^{-P_b L}$ to zero gives $P_b L^{2} - P_b H L - H = 0$, so $L_{opt} = H/2 + \sqrt{H^{2}/4 + H/P_b} = 160 + \sqrt{25600 + 3.2\times10^{6}} = 1956$ bits, i.e. about 1636 data bits. $D = 2000$ is close to this optimum.

**出處**　W. Stallings, Data and Computer Communications (effect of frame size on ARQ performance); Bertsekas & Gallager, Data Networks (optimal packet length)

</details>

**3.7.7S029**　After an antenna upgrade the bit error probability falls to $10^{-6}$. Should the frames get longer or shorter, and roughly by how much?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Longer: the optimum scales roughly as $\sqrt{H/P_b}$, so a 100-fold lower $P_b$ moves the optimum frame length up about 10-fold, to roughly 18 000 bits.

**詳解**　For $H/P_b \gg H^{2}/4$ the optimum is $L_{opt} \approx \sqrt{H/P_b}$. With $P_b = 10^{-6}$: $\sqrt{320/10^{-6}} = \sqrt{3.2\times10^{8}} \approx 17\,900$ bits (adding $H/2 = 160$ gives about 18 050 bits). Fewer errors make long frames cheap to lose, so the header overhead dominates and should be amortized over more data.

**出處**　Bertsekas & Gallager, Data Networks (optimal packet length)

</details>

**3.7.7S053**　Why are the conditional failure probabilities of later transmissions much lower than the 10 % of the first one, even though the radio channel is the same?

**答案**　The independent model ignores the combining gain: it treats each retransmission as a fresh attempt with the same 10 % failure. Here combined attempts fail far less often, so the residual is $4\times10^{-5}$, 2.5 times lower.

**詳解**　Independent model: $P_{res} = 0.1^{4} = 10^{-4}$, $\bar N = 1.111$. Measured: $P_{res} = 4\times10^{-5}$ and $\bar N = 1.122$ (slightly more retransmissions because the conditional probability of the 2nd attempt, 0.2, is above 0.1, but these succeed more reliably later). The model $p^{n_{max}}$ is useful as an upper estimate of the residual when combining gain is unknown.

**出處**　Lin & Costello, Error Control Coding (hybrid ARQ)

</details>

**3.7.7S055**　A planner computes the mean number of transmissions:
1. The 1st transmission is always sent: 1.
2. The 2nd is sent with probability 0.1, the 3rd with probability 0.2, the 4th with probability 0.1.
3. $\bar N = 1 + 0.1 + 0.2 + 0.1 = 1.4$.

Which step is wrong, and what is the correct result?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 2 is wrong: 0.2 and 0.1 are conditional failure probabilities. A 3rd transmission requires both the 1st and the 2nd to fail: $0.1\times0.2 = 0.02$; a 4th requires three failures: $0.002$. $\bar N = 1.122$.

**詳解**　$P(\text{3rd needed}) = 0.1\times0.2 = 0.02$, $P(\text{4th needed}) = 0.1\times0.2\times0.1 = 0.002$. $\bar N = 1 + 0.1 + 0.02 + 0.002 = 1.122$. Adding conditional probabilities directly overstates the retransmission load by about 25 %.

**出處**　Lin & Costello, Error Control Coding (hybrid ARQ)

</details>

**3.7.7S057**　What is the probability that a packet needs at least one RLC retransmission? Give it in percent, rounded to three decimal places.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　0.511 %

**詳解**　Each block passes HARQ with probability $1 - 5.12\times10^{-4} = 0.999488$. All 10 pass with $0.999488^{10} = 0.994892$. At least one fails: $1 - 0.994892 = 0.005108 = 0.511\%$.

</details>

**3.7.7S058**　If HARQ already brings the block error rate down to $5\times10^{-4}$, why does the system still run an ARQ protocol in RLC?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　HARQ gives up after its maximum number of attempts and its 1-bit feedback can be misread (a NACK taken as ACK), so some blocks are silently lost; RLC AM uses sequence numbers and status reports to find and recover these residual losses.

**詳解**　HARQ is fast but not fully reliable: the residual $p^{n_{max}}$ is non-zero, and a NACK-to-ACK feedback error makes the transmitter believe a failed block was delivered. RLC AM tracks sequence numbers end to end across the radio link and requests missing ones, delivering a lossless stream at the cost of tens of milliseconds of delay. The two layers trade speed (HARQ) against reliability (RLC).

**出處**　Lin & Costello, Error Control Coding (hybrid ARQ); Bertsekas & Gallager, Data Networks (ARQ)

</details>

**3.7.7S059**　Why is the HARQ loop designed to be a few milliseconds while the RLC retransmission takes about 20 ms?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　HARQ sits in MAC/PHY with fixed, short feedback timing and a 1-bit ACK/NACK per block; RLC needs status reports built over many PDUs, timers to avoid premature requests and scheduling of the report itself, so its loop is inherently longer.

**詳解**　HARQ feedback is sent at a fixed or signalled offset of a few slots after decoding, so the process can retransmit almost immediately. RLC status reporting is triggered by polling or timers, carried as data, and has to wait for reordering, so its reaction time is an order of magnitude larger. This is why most errors should be fixed by HARQ and only a small residual left to RLC.

**出處**　Lin & Costello, Error Control Coding (hybrid ARQ); Bertsekas & Gallager, Data Networks

</details>

**3.7.7S060**　An engineer computes the HARQ residual error:
1. Each attempt fails with probability 0.08.
2. Up to 3 attempts are made.
3. Residual $= 1 - (1 - 0.08)^{3} = 1 - 0.778688 = 0.2213$.

Which step is wrong, and what is the correct result?

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

**答案**　Step 3 is wrong: $1-(1-p)^{3}$ is the probability that at least one of three attempts fails. The block is lost only if all three fail: $0.08^{3} = 5.12\times10^{-4}$.

**詳解**　HARQ stops at the first success, so the residual error is $P(\text{all fail}) = p^{3} = 5.12\times10^{-4}$, not 22 %.

**出處**　Lin & Costello, Error Control Coding (hybrid ARQ: residual error probability)

</details>
