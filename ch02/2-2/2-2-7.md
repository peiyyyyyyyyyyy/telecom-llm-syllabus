# 2.2.7 規範用語：shall、should、may

所屬：[2.2 規格文件的結構](../2-2.md)

## 正文

In a telecommunications standard, every clause that a product must obey is expressed with a specific modal verb. The three verbs most often encountered are **shall**, **should**, and **may**. Their meaning is not merely linguistic; it determines whether a non‑conforming implementation is a compliance failure or an acceptable design choice. Network operators, equipment vendors, and test laboratories rely on these distinctions to negotiate contracts, certify equipment, and enforce regulatory requirements. If a clause is mis‑interpreted, a product may be deployed that does not meet the mandatory performance level, leading to service outages or regulatory penalties.

### Definitions

| Verb | Intended strength of the provision | Typical interpretation in a 3GPP TS |
|------|------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **shall** | Mandatory requirement | The implementation **must** satisfy the clause to claim compliance. |
| **should** | Recommended (non‑mandatory) | The implementation is **advised** to satisfy the clause, but compliance can still be claimed if a justified deviation is documented. |
| **may** | Optional / discretionary | The implementation **may** include the feature; omission does not affect compliance. |

These definitions are derived from the need to **identify the requirements that are obligatory** and to **distinguish these requirements from other provisions where there is a certain freedom of choice** [TS 21.801 §6.6.1].

### Formal Rule Set (Annex E)

3GPP Annex E specifies a table that maps each kind of provision to a verbal form. The **first column** lists the verb that **shall** be used; the **second column** lists equivalent expressions that may be used **only in exceptional cases** when the primary form cannot be used for linguistic reasons [TS 21.801 §6.6.1].

- **Obligatory provisions** → use **shall** (e.g., “The UE **shall** support LTE band 3.”)  
- **Advisory provisions** → use **should** (e.g., “The network **should** perform handover within 150 ms.”)  
- **Discretionary provisions** → use **may** (e.g., “The UE **may** implement carrier aggregation with up to five component carriers.”)

### Worked Example

Consider a simplified excerpt from a hypothetical TS:

1. The base station **shall** transmit a synchronization signal every 5 ms.  
2. The UE **should** monitor the signal strength every 10 ms.  
3. The UE **may** ignore the signal if the measured RSRP is below –140 dBm.

A test lab wants to verify compliance of a UE implementation.

| Step | Check | Verdict |
|------|-------|---------|
| 1 | Verify that the UE monitors the signal **every 10 ms**. | Since the clause uses **should**, failure to monitor is a deviation but does not automatically constitute non‑compliance; the lab must request a documented justification. |
| 2 | Verify that the UE **ignores** the signal when RSRP < –140 dBm. | The clause uses **may**, so the UE is free to either ignore or process the signal; both behaviours are compliant. |
| 3 | Verify that the base station **transmits** every 5 ms. | The clause uses **shall**; any deviation is a mandatory non‑compliance and the network cannot claim conformance to the TS. |

The lab records the measurement results and produces a compliance report that cites the verb used in each clause to justify the outcome.

### Common Mistakes and Practical Notes

1. **Treating “should” as “shall.”** Engineers sometimes implement a “should” clause as a hard requirement, leading to unnecessary design complexity. Remember that “should” indicates a recommendation, not a mandatory rule.  

2. **Ignoring “may” clauses.** Optional features are often omitted to reduce cost, which is permissible. However, when a downstream contract explicitly requires a “may” feature, the contract imposes the obligation, not the TS itself.  

3. **Using alternative wording without justification.** The standard permits equivalent expressions **only** when the primary verb cannot be used for linguistic reasons; otherwise the alternative is a violation of the specification’s style rules [TS 21.801 §6.6.1].  

4. **Assuming the TS itself imposes legal obligations.** The TS does **not** in itself impose any obligation; obligations arise from legislation, contracts, or certification programmes [TS 21.801 §6.6.1].  

5. **Mixing verbs within a single provision.** A single clause should not contain both “shall” and “may” because it creates ambiguity. If a clause needs both mandatory and optional parts, split it into separate sub‑clauses, each with a single appropriate verb.  

By consistently applying the verb hierarchy defined in Annex E, engineers can produce clear, testable specifications and avoid costly reinterpretations during product integration.

## 練習題

**1.** Which modal verb in a 3GPP TS indicates a mandatory requirement that must be satisfied for compliance?

- A. may
- B. should
- C. shall
- D. could

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。“shall” is defined as the verb used for obligatory provisions that must be met to claim compliance.

</details>

**2.** According to TS 21.801 §6.6.1, why are clear rules for verbal forms essential?

- A. To enforce legal contracts automatically.
- B. To identify obligatory requirements and distinguish them from optional provisions.
- C. To simplify translation into other languages.
- D. To reduce the length of the specification.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The text states that clear rules are needed so users can identify mandatory requirements and separate them from provisions with freedom of choice.

</details>

**3.** Which of the following statements about “may” clauses is correct?

- A. Non‑implementation of a “may” feature always leads to non‑compliance.
- B. “May” indicates a discretionary provision that can be omitted without affecting compliance.
- C. “May” clauses are equivalent to “shall” clauses in legal contracts.
- D. “May” must be replaced by “should” when the clause is ambiguous.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。“may” denotes optional/discretionary provisions; omission does not affect compliance.

</details>

**4.** When is it permissible to use an alternative expression to the primary verbal form prescribed in Annex E?

- A. Whenever the author prefers a shorter sentence.
- B. Only when the primary form cannot be used for linguistic reasons.
- C. When the clause concerns performance metrics.
- D. If the document is being translated into another language.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The standard allows alternative expressions only in exceptional linguistic cases.

</details>

**5.** Which statement best reflects the legal status of a 3GPP TS by itself?

- A. It automatically creates binding obligations for all implementers.
- B. It imposes obligations only when referenced by legislation or contracts.
- C. It nullifies any national regulations that conflict with it.
- D. It grants patent rights to the authors of the specification.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The TS does not impose obligations by itself; obligations arise from external sources such as legislation or contracts.

</details>
