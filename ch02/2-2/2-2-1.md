# 2.2.1 技術規格（TS）與技術報告（TR）

所屬：[2.2 規格文件的結構](../2-2.md)

## 正文

Technical specifications (TS) and technical reports (TR) are the two primary document families used by 3GPP to capture the design, requirements, and analysis of mobile telecommunications systems. In a real network, engineers rely on TS documents to implement protocols that must interoperate bit‑exactly, while TRs provide background, measurement, or performance studies that support those implementations. Correctly identifying whether a document is a TS or a TR, and understanding its numbering, is essential for locating the authoritative source of a rule, avoiding mis‑implementation, and ensuring compliance with regulatory audits.

### Document families and their prefixes
3GPP distinguishes the two families by a simple prefix convention:

* **Technical Specification (TS):** `3GPP TS aa.bbb`
* **Technical Report (TR):** `3GPP TR aa.bbb`

The prefix alone tells the reader whether the document contains normative requirements (TS) or informative material (TR) [TS 21.900 §4.0].

### Numbering scheme – the *aa* and *bbb* fields
The two numeric fields in the identifier are not arbitrary. The first field **aa** indicates the *series* (or “range”) of the specification, while the second field **bbb** pin‑points the exact document within that series. The allowed values for **aa** are defined in Table 1 of the numbering scheme and are divided into several functional categories:

| **aa range** | **Series purpose** | **Remarks** |
|--------------|-------------------|------------|
| 0 – 41       | Requirements specifications | Often transient |
| 0 – 42       | Service aspects | Services, service features, building blocks |
| 0 – 43       | Technical realization | Mainly stage 2 specifications |
| 0 – 44       | Signalling protocols (UE ↔ CN) | Detailed and bit‑exact |
| 0 – 45       | Radio access aspects | Various UTRAN/UTRA layers and interfaces |
| 0 – 46       | Codecs | Speech and other codecs |

These ranges are *strictly enforced* for Table 1, whereas Table 2 (not reproduced here) is only guidance; deviations from Table 2 are permissible for backward compatibility or other reasons [TS 21.900 §4.0].

### Worked example – decoding a specification number
Suppose an engineer encounters the identifier **3GPP TS 25.301**. To understand its scope:

1. The prefix **TS** tells us it is a normative technical specification.  
2. The first numeric field **aa = 25** falls within the **0 – 45** range, which the table labels “Radio access aspects”.  
3. Consequently, the document belongs to the radio‑access series and will describe radio‑access‑related procedures or interfaces, consistent with the remarks for the 0‑45 range.

If the same number had the prefix **TR** (e.g., **3GPP TR 25.301**), the document would still be part of the radio‑access series, but its content would be informative rather than normative [TS 21.900 §4.0].

### Practical notes on using the scheme
* **Always read the prefix first.** Mistaking a TR for a TS can lead to implementing non‑normative recommendations as mandatory requirements.  
* **Check the *aa* range.** A specification numbered in the 0‑41 range is likely to be a requirements document that may become obsolete once a later TS provides a full solution.  
* **Be aware of allowed deviations.** When consulting Table 2 (guidance), a deviation is acceptable if it preserves backward compatibility, but any deviation from Table 1 is a violation of the numbering policy [TS 21.900 §4.0].

### Common mistakes
1. **Confusing series numbers.** Engineers sometimes assume that a higher *aa* value always means a newer document; however, the series purpose, not the magnitude, determines the document’s role.  
2. **Ignoring the “strictly enforced” rule.** Assigning a new specification to an *aa* value outside the defined ranges (e.g., 47) would breach the scheme.  
3. **Overlooking the TR/TS distinction.** Using a TR as a normative reference can cause compliance gaps, especially in certification testing.  

By adhering to the numbering conventions, telecom engineers can reliably locate the correct normative text, trace the evolution of a feature from requirement through service aspect to realization, and avoid costly implementation errors.
