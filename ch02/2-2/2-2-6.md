# 2.2.6 變更請求（CR）與版本號

所屬：[2.2 規格文件的結構](../2-2.md)

## 正文

In the lifecycle of a 3GPP specification, the moment a version *x.0.0* (with *x* ≥ 3) is approved by the Technical Specification Group (TSG) marks the start of **change control**. From that point onward, any technical amendment must be introduced through a **Change Request (CR)** rather than by ad‑hoc edits. This disciplined process guarantees that all network operators, equipment vendors, and test labs work from a single, traceable source of truth, thereby avoiding divergent implementations that could break interoperability in real‑world deployments.  

### Core Definitions

| Symbol | Definition | Unit / Note |
|--------|------------|-------------|
| **CR** | Change Request – a formal proposal to modify a specific version of a specification. | – |
| **CR‑No** | Unique reference number allocated to a CR by the 3GPP portal or Support Team. | – |
| **TSG** | Technical Specification Group – the body that approves specifications and CRs. | – |
| **Version x.y.z** | Version identifier where *x* is the major release number, *y* the minor change level, and *z* the patch level. The reference text explicitly mentions versions of the form *x.0.0* for approved releases. | – |
| **WG Secretary** | Working Group Secretary – collates CRs approved by the WG and forwards them to the parent TSG. | – |
| **Support Team** | The 3GPP team that maintains the CR database and edits specifications after TSG approval. | – |

### The CR Lifecycle

1. **Initiation** – Any individual member may raise a CR and submit it to the responsible Group. The request **must** reference the exact specification version it targets.  

2. **Identification** – The 3GPP portal or Support Team assigns a **unique CR‑No** that is never reused, even if the CR is later rejected. All revisions of the CR are recorded in a central database accessible on the 3GPP file server.  

3. **Evaluation** – The Working Group (WG) reviews the CR. The TSG WG Secretary collects all CRs that the WG has agreed upon and forwards them to the parent TSG. For specifications directly under a TSG, the CR is presented to the TSG without WG mediation.  

4. **Decision** – The TSG may **approve**, **reject**, or **postpone** a CR **in its entirety**. The decision is recorded in the meeting report and reflected in the CR database. The status “rejected” is now synonymous with “not pursued”.  

5. **Implementation** – Upon TSG approval, the Support Team edits the original specification to incorporate the change and publishes a **new version** on the 3GPP file server.  

6. **Interaction Check** – When two or more CRs target the same specification version, the responsible Group must verify that the approved changes do not conflict with each other.  

### CR Forms and Categories

A standardized CR front‑cover form captures essential management information, including:  

- Target specification and its version number.  
- Source of the CR.  
- Reason for the change and impact if not accepted.  
- Category of change (A, B, or C).  

**Category A** links the proposed change to an equivalent functional change in an earlier release.  
**Category B** concerns addition or deletion of a feature (normally tied to a Work Item) and is not used for frozen releases except for alignment CRs.  
**Category C** involves functional modification of an existing feature, again requiring a Work Item and backward compatibility.  

### Worked Example

Assume the specification **TS 21.900** has just been released as version **3.0.0**.  

1. Engineer Lee, a member of the 3GPP community, discovers that the description of a signalling parameter is ambiguous.  
2. Lee creates a CR and submits it to the **RAN Working Group**. The CR form lists:  
   - Target spec: TS 21.900 v3.0.0  
   - Category: **C** (functional modification)  
   - Reason: Ambiguity could lead to mis‑implementation.  
3. The portal assigns **CR‑12345** (unique, never reused). The CR is entered into the database and visible to all participants.  
4. After discussion, the WG agrees that the change is needed. The WG Secretary bundles CR‑12345 with other approved CRs and forwards it to the **TSG**.  
5. The TSG **approves** the CR in its meeting. The decision is logged in the meeting report and marked “approved” in the database.  
6. The Support Team edits TS 21.900, incorporating the clarified wording, and publishes a **new version** (e.g., 3.1.0) on the 3GPP file server.  

All participants can now reference **CR‑12345** when discussing the change, and the version number clearly indicates that the document has been updated since the original 3.0.0 release.  

### Common Mistakes and Practical Tips

| Mistake | Why It Happens | Correct Practice |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Re‑using a CR number** after rejection. | Assumption that a rejected CR can be discarded. | CR numbers are **never reused**, even for rejected (or “not pursued”) requests. |
| **Submitting a CR without specifying the exact version**. | Overlooking that a CR must relate to a specific version. | Always list the target specification **and its version** on the CR form. |
| **Bundling unrelated technical topics in a single CR**. | Desire to reduce paperwork. | A single CR should address **one technical topic only** to allow clean acceptance or rejection. |
| **Confusing “rejected” with “not pursued”**. | Legacy terminology. | Treat “rejected” and “not pursued” as equivalent; the document uses both terms interchangeably. |
| **Neglecting interaction checks when multiple CRs are pending**. | Assuming independent changes. | The responsible Group must **check for potential interaction** among CRs targeting the same version. |

By adhering to the CR regime, engineers ensure that every amendment is transparent, traceable, and compatible with the broader 3GPP ecosystem, which is essential for maintaining global network interoperability.

## 練習題

**1.** Who is allowed to raise a Change Request (CR) for a 3GPP specification?

- A. Only the Working Group Chair.
- B. Any individual member of the 3GPP community.
- C. Only the TSG Secretary.
- D. Only the Support Team.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The reference states that a CR may be raised by any individual member.

</details>

**2.** What must be allocated to each Change Request to ensure uniqueness?

- A. A version number.
- B. A unique reference number (CR‑No).
- C. A priority level.
- D. A deadline date.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The CR is assigned a unique reference number by the portal or Support Team, and numbers are never reused.

</details>

**3.** Which status term has replaced “rejected” in some groups according to the reference?

- A. Deferred.
- B. Not pursued.
- C. Cancelled.
- D. Withdrawn.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The note specifies that “rejected” is replaced by “not pursued”.

</details>

**4.** Who is responsible for collating all approved CRs and sending them to the parent TSG?

- A. The Support Team.
- B. The TSG Chair.
- C. The TSG WG Secretary.
- D. The Specification Owner.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The WG Secretary collects approved CRs and forwards them to the parent TSG.

</details>

**5.** When two or more CRs target the same specification version, what must the responsible Group do?

- A. Approve all of them automatically.
- B. Reject all of them to avoid conflict.
- C. Check for potential interaction amongst those CRs.
- D. Combine them into a single CR.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The reference requires the group to ensure that approved CRs do not contradict each other.

</details>
