# 2.6.1 附上文件、章節、版本

所屬：[2.6 引用規則](../2-6.md)

## 正文

In complex telecom projects a single requirement may be traced through dozens of 3GPP specifications, technical reports, and annexes. An unambiguous citation—identifying the exact document, the precise clause or annex, and the version number—prevents misinterpretation when a document is revised or when multiple editions coexist. The 3GPP referencing rules explicitly require such specificity to guarantee that every engineering decision can be reproduced from the source material, which is essential for both network deployment and for training domain‑specific language models that must learn correct terminology and citation patterns.  

### Document reference elements
A *specific reference* must identify the document by at least three attributes: the date of publication, the edition number, and the version number (x.y.z). This is mandated in clause 6.6.6.5.2 of TS 21.801, which states that “references shall be specific (identified by date of publication, edition number, version number, etc.)”【TS 21.801 §6.6.6.5.2】. The version number follows the nomenclature defined in TS 21.900 §4.0A and consists of three fields:

$$
\text{Version} = x.y.z
$$  

* $x$ – **major** field (draft = 0, release = 1, approval = 2 or higher)  
* $y$ – **technical** field, incremented for each technical change  
* $z$ – **editorial** field, incremented for editorial changes only  

The table in TS 21.900 clarifies the meaning of each field, e.g., “0: draft” for the major field and “Incremented every time a technical change is introduced” for the technical field【TS 21.900 §4.0A】.  

### Clause and annex citations
When referring to a location inside a referenced document, the terms *clause* and *annex* must be used. Clause references take the form “clause 3.1 b)”, “annex B”, or “table 2 in clause 6.6.3” as illustrated in TS 21.801 §6.6.6.3【TS 21.801 §6.6.6.3】. Consistency of capitalisation (lower‑case recommended) is also required. For unnumbered list items the formulation “as specified in TS 21.299 [n] in clause 3.1, second list item” is prescribed.  

### Worked example
Suppose an engineer needs to cite the requirement that a User Equipment shall support carrier aggregation as described in TS 21.299 version 1.2.0, clause 3.1, second list item. The correct citation would be:

> “The UE shall support carrier aggregation as specified in TS 21.299 [1] in clause 3.1, second list item (version 1.2.0).”

Breaking down the citation:

| Element | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Document identifier | TS 21.299 |
| Reference tag | $$1$$ (as used in the bibliography) |
| Version | 1.2.0 (major = 1, technical = 2, editorial = 0) |
| Clause | 3.1 |
| List item qualifier | second list item |

All required elements are present: the document name, a bibliography tag, the exact version number, the clause, and the list‑item qualifier. This complies with the specificity rule (date/edition/version) and uses the mandated term *clause* for the location.  

### Common mistakes and practical notes

* **Omitting the version number** – Leaving out “1.2.0” makes the reference ambiguous once a new revision (e.g., 1.3.0) is published. The standard explicitly requires version identification.  

* **Using “section” or “paragraph” instead of “clause”** – The 3GPP rules state that *clause* and *annex* must be used where applicable; other terms are not acceptable.  

* **Referencing a clause that may change** – The guidance advises avoiding references to specific clauses, figures, or tables of a document still under development, because the structure may change.  

* **Inconsistent capitalisation** – While both “Clause 1” and “clause 1” are allowed, the document must be consistent throughout.  

* **Failing to indicate draft status** – If the major field $x$ is 0, the reference should make clear that the document is a draft; otherwise readers may assume it is an approved release.  

By adhering to these rules, engineers ensure that every requirement can be traced back to a stable, identifiable source, facilitating reliable network design and accurate training data for language models.
