# 2.1.4 ITU 與 IMT 需求

所屬：[2.1 標準體系](../2-1.md)

## 正文

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU‑R) defines the performance envelope for each new generation of mobile systems under the IMT (International Mobile Telecommunications) framework. For IMT‑2020, which corresponds to 5G, the ITU‑R specifications are taken by 3GPP as the top‑level “must‑have” targets for the next‑generation access technologies. These targets drive the design of radio access networks (RAN), device capabilities, and spectrum allocation decisions, ensuring that any commercial deployment can support the envisioned services such as enhanced Mobile Broadband (eMBB), ultra‑reliable low‑latency communications (URLLC), and massive machine‑type communications (mMTC) [TS 38.913 §7].

### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

The ITU‑R defines a set of quantitative KPIs. The most critical for system design are:

| KPI | Definition | Target (downlink / uplink) |
|-----|------------|----------------------------|
| **Peak data rate** | Highest theoretical data rate assignable to a single mobile station when all assignable radio resources are utilised (excluding synchronization, reference signals, guard bands, and guard times) | 20 Gbps / 10 Gbps |
| **Peak spectral efficiency** | Highest theoretical data rate normalised by bandwidth under the same ideal conditions | 30 bps/Hz / 15 bps/Hz |
| **Bandwidth** | Maximal aggregated total system bandwidth, possibly across multiple RF carriers | – |
| **Control‑plane latency** | Time to move from a battery‑efficient state (e.g., IDLE) to the start of continuous data transfer (e.g., ACTIVE) | 10 ms |
| **User‑plane latency** | Time to deliver an application‑layer packet from ingress to egress of the radio protocol stack (no DRX) | URLLC: 0.5 ms / 0.5 ms; eMBB: 4 ms / 4 ms |
| **Mobility interruption time** | Shortest duration during which a terminal cannot exchange user‑plane packets while transitioning between cells | 0 ms |

The definitions of the first two KPIs are reproduced verbatim in the ITU‑R discussion and are quoted directly in the 3GPP technical report [TS 38.913 §7.1‑7.2]. The bandwidth KPI is defined as “the maximal aggregated total system bandwidth” [TS 38.913 §7.3]. Control‑plane and user‑plane latency definitions, together with their numerical targets, are given in §§ 7.4‑7.5 [TS 38.913 §7.4‑7.5]. Mobility interruption time is defined in § 7.7 [TS 38.913 §7.7].

### Frequency Ranges and Spectral Efficiency

3GPP adopts the ITU‑R frequency‑range classification when evaluating spectral efficiency. FR1 covers 410 MHz – 7.125 GHz, while FR2 covers 24.25 GHz – 52.6 GHz [TS 37.910 §5.1]. The ITU‑R definition of peak spectral efficiency (quoted in TS 37.910) matches the KPI in § 7.2 of TS 38.913, reinforcing that the same measurement methodology is used across the two documents [TS 37.910 §5.1.1].

### Formal Relationships

The peak spectral efficiency ($\eta_{\text{peak}}$) and peak data rate ($R_{\text{peak}}$) are linked by the total system bandwidth ($B$):

$$
\eta_{\text{peak}} = \frac{R_{\text{peak}}}{B}\quad\text{[bps/Hz]}
$$

where  

* $R_{\text{peak}}$ – peak data rate (bits s$^{-1}$),  
* $B$ – aggregated bandwidth (Hz),  
* $\eta_{\text{peak}}$ – peak spectral efficiency (bits s$^{-1}$ Hz$^{-1}$).

The ITU‑R targets impose two independent constraints: a minimum $R_{\text{peak}}$ (20 Gbps downlink, 10 Gbps uplink) and a minimum $\eta_{\text{peak}}$ (30 bps/Hz downlink, 15 bps/Hz uplink). Both must be satisfied simultaneously; a system that meets one but not the other does not fulfil the IMT‑2020 requirement.

### Worked Example

Assume a 5G deployment operates in FR1 with an aggregated bandwidth of **100 MHz**. Determine the minimum peak data rate required to meet the downlink spectral‑efficiency target, and verify whether the IMT‑2020 peak data‑rate target is also satisfied.

1. Convert bandwidth: $B = 100\ \text{MHz} = 1.0\times10^{8}\ \text{Hz}$.
2. Apply the spectral‑efficiency target: $\eta_{\text{DL}}^{\text{target}} = 30\ \text{bps/Hz}$.
3. Compute the required peak data rate:  

$$
R_{\text{DL}}^{\text{req}} = \eta_{\text{DL}}^{\text{target}} \times B = 30\ \frac{\text{bits}}{\text{s·Hz}} \times 1.0\times10^{8}\ \text{Hz} = 3.0\times10^{9}\ \text{bits/s} = 3\ \text{Gbps}.
$$

The calculated $R_{\text{DL}}^{\text{req}} = 3\ \text{Gbps}$ is well below the ITU‑R downlink peak‑data‑rate target of **20 Gbps** [TS 38.913 §7.1]. Hence, while the spectral‑efficiency requirement is satisfied, the system would still need additional bandwidth or higher‑order modulation/coding to reach the 20 Gbps absolute target.

### Common Pitfalls and Practical Notes

* **Confusing “peak” with “average” performance** – The ITU‑R KPIs are defined for a single user under ideal, error‑free conditions. Real‑world average throughput will be lower due to interference, scheduling, and protocol overhead.  
* **Ignoring overhead** – The KPI definitions explicitly exclude synchronization signals, reference signals, guard bands, and guard times. When dimensioning a system, engineers must account for these overheads separately; otherwise, the calculated $R_{\text{peak}}$ will be optimistic.  
* **Treating bandwidth and spectral efficiency as interchangeable** – As the definition notes, “higher frequency bands could have higher bandwidth but lower spectral efficiency and lower frequency bands could have lower bandwidth but higher spectral efficiency.” Both dimensions must be evaluated together [TS 38.913 §7.2].  
* **Overlooking satellite‑specific latency allowances** – For non‑terrestrial links, control‑plane latency may be as high as 600 ms for GEO satellites, a special case that does not affect the 10 ms target for terrestrial systems [TS 38.913 §7.4‑7.5].  
* **Misreading frequency‑range labels** – FR1 and FR2 are defined in TS 37.910; using the wrong range can lead to incorrect assumptions about available bandwidth and propagation characteristics [TS 37.910 §5.1].

By adhering to the ITU‑R IMT‑2020 KPI definitions and respecting the dual constraints of peak data rate and spectral efficiency, 3GPP specifications can ensure that future 5G releases remain compliant with global performance expectations.

## 練習題

**1.** What is the ITU‑R target peak data rate for downlink in IMT‑2020?

- A. 10 Gbps
- B. 20 Gbps
- C. 30 Gbps
- D. 40 Gbps

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The ITU‑R specifies 20 Gbps as the downlink peak‑data‑rate target.

</details>

**2.** Which KPI defines the maximal aggregated total system bandwidth?

- A. Peak data rate
- B. Bandwidth
- C. Control‑plane latency
- D. Mobility interruption time

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Bandwidth is explicitly defined as the maximal aggregated total system bandwidth.

</details>

**3.** According to ITU‑R, what is the downlink target for peak spectral efficiency?

- A. 15 bps/Hz
- B. 20 bps/Hz
- C. 30 bps/Hz
- D. 45 bps/Hz

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The downlink target is 30 bps/Hz, as stated in the KPI definition.

</details>

**4.** What is the target control‑plane latency for IMT‑2020 terrestrial deployments?

- A. 5 ms
- B. 10 ms
- C. 20 ms
- D. 100 ms

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The ITU‑R sets a 10 ms target for control‑plane latency.

</details>

**5.** Which frequency range corresponds to FR1 as defined by 3GPP?

- A. 100 MHz – 300 MHz
- B. 410 MHz – 7125 MHz
- C. 10 GHz – 20 GHz
- D. 24.25 GHz – 52.6 GHz

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。FR1 is defined as 410 MHz to 7.125 GHz.

</details>
