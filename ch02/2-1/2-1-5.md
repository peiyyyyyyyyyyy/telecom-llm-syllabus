# 2.1.5 3GPP 組織夥伴（ETSI 等）與引用的外部標準（IETF、IEEE）

所屬：[2.1 標準體系](../2-1.md)

## 正文

In the 3GPP work‑item model, the first step is carried out by **SA1** (the Service and Systems Aspects sub‑group of TSG SA). SA1 is *responsible for defining the features and services required in the 3GPP specifications* and for producing the **stage‑1 descriptions (requirements)** that will be handed over to SA2 [TS 21.900 §6.6]. This early definition is crucial because it anchors the subsequent architectural design and guarantees that the final system will meet the intended service goals.

### Role of SA2 – Architecture and Building Blocks
After SA1 delivers the requirements, **SA2** (the Architecture sub‑group) takes over. SA2 *defines the architecture for the features and the system, and then divides the features into building blocks* based on the architectural decisions [TS 21.900 §6.6]. The building blocks are the modular units that will be implemented by the relevant Technical Specification Groups (TSGs) and their Working Groups (WGs). SA2 also *verifies that all required work for a full system specification will take place within 3GPP without overlap between groups* [TS 21.900 §6.6].

### Interaction with TSGs and Working Groups
SA2 forwards the set of building blocks to the appropriate TSGs (e.g., RAN, SA, CT). The TSGs treat each building block as *one or several dedicated work tasks* and produce outputs such as new specifications, updated specifications, technical reports, or a conclusion that the needed support already exists [TS 21.900 §6.6]. Throughout this detailed work, SA2 *is kept informed about the progress* and may identify **synergy** opportunities where a single building block can serve multiple features [TS 21.900 §6.6].

### Scheduling and Target Setting
The work‑item model also defines a light‑weight scheduling loop. **SA1 sets a target** (e.g., a release deadline), **SA2 performs a first technical review and comments on the target**, then *SA2 indicates a target for the time schedule together with allocation of the defined building blocks* [TS 21.900 §6.6]. The TSGs and their WGs *comment back on these targets*, and SA2 may adjust the schedule to align the involved parties. Both SA1 and SA are *kept informed of the overall schedule* [TS 21.900 §6.6].

### Coordination with Horizontal Tasks (CT, RAN, Conformance)
When a new feature is introduced, SA2 should *invite TSG CT and TSG RAN* to evaluate the impact on **horizontal tasks** such as conformance testing and mobile‑station capabilities [TS 21.900 §6.6]. If additional work on these tasks is required, it *must be included in the overall work plan*.

### External Partners and Standards – Scope Note
The reference work‑item model does **not** describe the formal relationships that 3GPP maintains with external standardisation bodies such as **ETSI**, **IETF**, or **IEEE**, nor does it list the external specifications that 3GPP may cite. Consequently, this subsection can only acknowledge that such partnerships exist but must state that their details are **outside the scope of the provided reference**.

### Worked Example – Allocating Building Blocks for a New Feature
Assume SA1 defines a requirement for a “low‑latency uplink” service (Requirement R1).  

1. **SA1 output** – Stage‑1 description: *“The system shall support uplink latency ≤ 5 ms for 99 % of packets.”*  
2. **SA2 activity** – Architecture design yields two building blocks:  
   - **BB‑PHY** – Physical‑layer procedures to reduce processing delay.  
   - **BB‑MAC** – MAC‑layer scheduling algorithm for fast grant.  
3. **Allocation** – SA2 forwards BB‑PHY to TSG RAN (Working Group RAN‑WG1) and BB‑MAC to TSG SA (Working Group SA‑WG2).  
4. **TSG work** – RAN‑WG1 produces *TS 36.321* amendment adding a new PHY control element; SA‑WG2 updates *TS 23.501* with the latency requirement.  
5. **Schedule** – SA1 targets Release 18 (Q4 2024). SA2 reviews and proposes the same target, adding that BB‑PHY needs 12 months and BB‑MAC 9 months. The TSGs confirm the feasibility, and the overall schedule is locked.

This example follows the flow described in the reference: *SA1 produces requirements, SA2 defines architecture and building blocks, and the TSGs develop specifications* [TS 21.900 §6.6].

### Common Mistakes and Practical Notes

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| **Skipping SA2’s architectural review** | Engineers sometimes pass requirements directly to a TSG, assuming SA2’s role is optional. | Ensure SA2 reviews every stage‑1 requirement and produces building blocks before any WG work starts. |
| **Duplicate work across TSGs** | Without SA2’s coordination, two groups may implement overlapping functions. | Use SA2’s “verify no overlap” responsibility as a checkpoint before finalising work assignments. |
| **Ignoring horizontal tasks** | Conformance testing is sometimes added late, causing schedule slips. | Invite CT and RAN early, as SA2 *should invite those TSGs to evaluate the potential impact of a new feature* [TS 21.900 §6.6]. |
| **Misaligned release targets** | SA1 and SA2 may set different deadlines, leading to integration problems. | Follow the *target‑setting loop* where SA2 *comments on the target* and aligns it with the TSGs [TS 21.900 §6.6]. |
| **Assuming external standards are automatically incorporated** | Teams may believe that citing IETF or IEEE documents suffices without mapping them to building blocks. | Since the reference does not cover external standards, any such incorporation must be explicitly modelled as a building block and reviewed by SA2. |

By adhering to the structured work‑item model, 3GPP ensures that all partners—whether internal TSGs or external standard bodies—contribute coherently to a release, avoiding duplicated effort and meeting the service requirements defined at the outset.

## 練習題

**1.** Which sub‑group of TSG SA is responsible for producing the stage‑1 requirement descriptions?

- A. SA2
- B. SA1
- C. SA3
- D. CT

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。SA1 creates the stage‑1 (requirements) descriptions before handing them to SA2.

</details>

**2.** What is the primary purpose of SA2 in the work‑item model?

- A. To define service pricing models.
- B. To allocate spectrum to operators.
- C. To define the architecture and divide features into building blocks.
- D. To conduct field trials.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。SA2 takes the requirements from SA1 and creates the system architecture and building blocks.

</details>

**3.** Which groups are explicitly mentioned as needing to be invited by SA2 to evaluate the impact of a new feature on horizontal tasks?

- A. TSG SA only
- B. TSG CT and TSG RAN
- C. ETSI and IETF
- D. GSMA and O‑RAN Alliance

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The reference states SA2 should invite TSG CT and TSG RAN for horizontal tasks.

</details>

**4.** According to the model, who keeps SA1 informed about the overall schedule?

- A. Only the external partners.
- B. The Working Groups themselves.
- C. SA2 and the TSGs.
- D. No one; SA1 sets the schedule independently.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The schedule loop involves SA2 setting targets and keeping SA1 informed.

</details>
