# 2.3.2 允許範圍與單位

所屬：[2.3 表格與參數](../2-3.md)

## 正文

In telecommunications specifications, permissible ranges define the limits within which a radio, protocol stack, or network element must operate. Precise unit notation removes ambiguity that could lead to interoperability failures or incorrect test results. The 3GPP Technical Specification 21.801 prescribes a single, document‑wide system for representing numbers, units, and tolerances, ensuring that every engineer reads the same value in the same way. [TR 21.801 §6.6.7] [TR 21.801 §6.6.8] [TR 21.801 §6.6.10]

### Decimal sign and thousands separator
Two mutually exclusive options exist:

* **Option 1** – decimal sign is a comma ( , ) and the thousands separator is a space.  
* **Option 2** – decimal sign is a full‑stop ( . ) and the thousands separator is a comma.  

The chosen option must be used consistently throughout the whole TS or TR; mixing is prohibited. [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]

### Zero‑prefix and non‑breaking spaces
When a value is smaller than 1, a leading zero precedes the decimal sign (e.g., **0,001**, not **,001**). [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]  
Every group of three digits left or right of the decimal sign is separated by a **non‑breaking space** (Ctrl + Shift + space). The same non‑breaking space is used as the thousands separator, before and after binary operators, and between a number and its unit. [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]

### Multiplication notation
The symbol **×** (or a lower‑case **x**) must be used to indicate multiplication; a point or any other symbol is forbidden. Example: write **$1,8 \times 10^{-3}$** rather than **1,8 · 10‑3**. [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]

### Spelling of small counts
When a quantity refers to a count of items (not a physical measurement), the numerals **one** to **nine** shall be spelled out in full (e.g., “five tubes”). [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]

### Units and the non‑breaking space
The unit of any numerical value must always be shown, and a non‑breaking space separates the number from the unit, even for the percent sign. Examples: **2 pages**, **4 seconds**, **15 %**. [TR 21.801 §6.6.8]

### Dimensions, tolerances and range notation
* **Dimensions** are written with each dimension followed by its unit and separated by a non‑breaking space: **$80\ \text{mm} \times 25\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm}$**. [TR 21.801 §6.6.10]  
* **Tolerances** are expressed either as “$80\ \mu\text{F} \pm 2\ \mu\text{F}$” or “$(80 \pm 2)\ \mu\text{F}$”. [TR 21.801 §6.6.10]  
* **Ranges** must include both limits and the unit, e.g., **$16\ \text{kbit/s}$ to **$64\ \text{kbit/s}$**, not “$16$ to $64\ \text{kbit/s}$”. [TR 21.801 §6.6.10]  
* **Percentage ranges** are written as “from 63 % to 67 %”, and a centred value with tolerance as “$(65 \pm 2)\,\%$”. The form “65 ± 2 %” is prohibited. [TR 21.801 §6.6.10]

### Worked example – transmit‑power tolerance
A base‑station specification states: *Allowed E‑UTRA transmit power is 23 dBm ± 1 dB.*  
Correct formatting steps:

1. Use the chosen decimal system (Option 1 in this document, i.e., comma as decimal sign).  
2. Write the tolerance in the “$(\text{value} \pm \text{tolerance})\ \text{unit}$” form.  
3. Insert non‑breaking spaces between the numeric expression and the unit, and between the unary minus and the unit.  

Result: **$(23 \pm 1)\ \text{dBm}$** (displayed as “$(23 ± 1)\,$dBm” with non‑breaking spaces).  

If the tolerance were expressed as a percentage, e.g., 95 % ± 2 %, the correct form would be **$(95 \pm 2)\,\%$**. [TR 21.801 §6.6.10]

### Common mistakes to avoid
* **Mixing decimal conventions** – using a comma in some tables and a period in others leads to ambiguity. [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]  
* **Omitting the leading zero** for values below 1 (e.g., “,001”). [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]  
* **Using a point or dot for multiplication** (e.g., “1,8 · 10‑3”). [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]  
* **Leaving out the non‑breaking space** before units, which can cause line‑breaks that split numbers from their units (e.g., “15%” instead of “15 %”). [TR 21.801 §6.6.8]  
* **Writing ambiguous ranges** such as “$16$ to $64\ \text{kbit/s}$” or “$65 ± 2\%$”. [TR 21.801 §6.6.10]  

### Practical notes for authors and test engineers
* Decide early whether the document follows Option 1 or Option 2 and state the choice in the introductory clause.  
* If any numeric string could be ambiguous (e.g., “234,557”), define the decimal and thousands symbols explicitly in clause 3.3 of the document. [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]  
* Use a **non‑breaking hyphen** (Ctrl + Shift + ‑) for the minus sign in tolerances and negative numbers. [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]  
* Apply non‑breaking spaces around binary operators (+, −, ×, ÷) to keep the expression intact during pagination. [TR 21.801 §6.6.7]  

By adhering strictly to these formatting rules, engineers can read, implement, and verify specifications without misinterpretation, leading to more reliable network deployments. [TR 21.801 §6.6.7] [TR 21.801 §6.6.8] [TR 21.801 §6.6.10]
