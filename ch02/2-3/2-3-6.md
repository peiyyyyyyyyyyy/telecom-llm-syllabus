# 2.3.6 通道模型參數表

所屬：[2.3 表格與參數](../2-3.md)

## 正文

In system‑level simulations and link‑budget calculations the pathloss model is the core element that translates a transmitter‑receiver geometry into a predicted large‑scale attenuation. 3GPP TS 38.901 defines a set of scenario‑specific models (RMa, UMa, UMi, InH, InF) together with the required auxiliary parameters such as shadow‑fading standard deviation and the distance range over which the model is valid. Collecting these values in a single table allows engineers to pick the correct formula quickly, avoid mismatched units, and verify that the deployment scenario satisfies the model’s applicability conditions. All of the information in the table originates from the reference specification [TS 38.901 §7.4.1] [TS 38.901 §7.4.1]​.

### Structure of the table

Table 7.4.1‑1 in the specification is organised by **scenario** (RMa, UMa, …, InF) and by **link condition** (LOS, NLOS). For each cell the table lists  

* the **pathloss expression** (in dB),  
* the **shadow‑fading standard deviation** σ<sub>SF</sub> (in dB),  
* the **applicability range** (typically a distance interval), and  
* any **default antenna‑height values** that the model assumes (e.g., average building height *h* or street width *W*).  

The column headings clarify that the carrier frequency *f*<sub>c</sub> is expressed in GHz and the distance variable *d* in metres (see note 6) [TS 38.901 §7.4.1]​.

### Formal definitions

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $f_c$ | Carrier frequency | GHz |
| $d_{3D}$ | 3‑D Euclidean distance between transmitter and receiver | m |
| $d_{2D}$ | Horizontal (2‑D) distance (used in other scenarios, not shown here) | m |
| $PL$ | Pathloss (average large‑scale attenuation) | dB |
| $\sigma_{\text{SF}}$ | Standard deviation of log‑normal shadow fading | dB |
| $h$, $W$ | Average building height, street width (used for applicability) | m |

The **InF (Indoor‑Free‑Space) LOS** model is explicitly given as  

$$
\text{PL}_{\text{LOS}} = 31.84 \;+\; 21.50\,\log_{10}\!\bigl(d_{3D}\bigr) \;+\; 20\,\log_{10}\!\bigl(f_{c}\bigr)
$$  

where $f_c$ is in GHz and $d_{3D}$ in metres [TS 38.901 §7.4.1]​.  
The corresponding shadow‑fading standard deviation is  

$$
\sigma_{\text{SF}} = 4.3\;\text{dB}
$$  

[TS 38.901 §7.4.1]​.

For **InF NLOS** the specification provides  

$$
\text{PL}_{\text{NLOS}} = 33 \;+\; 25.5\,\log_{10}\!\bigl(d_{3D}\bigr) \;+\; 20\,\log_{10}\!\bigl(f_{c}\bigr)
$$  

and  

$$
\sigma_{\text{SF}} = 5.7\;\text{dB}
$$  

[TS 38.901 §7.4.1]​.

The table also states the **applicability range** for the InF models as  

$$
1 \le d_{3D} \le 600\ \text{m}
$$  

(quoted verbatim as “1 \le q d_{3D}\ \l eq 600 m”) [TS 38.901 §7.4.1]​.

### Worked example

Consider an indoor small‑cell deployment operating at $f_c = 3.5$ GHz with a user equipment (UE) located $d_{3D}=100$ m from the base station. Assuming a LOS condition, the pathloss is computed as follows:

1. Compute the distance term: $\log_{10}(100) = 2$  
2. Compute the frequency term: $\log_{10}(3.5) \approx 0.5441$  

Insert into the LOS formula  

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{PL}_{\text{LOS}} &= 31.84 + 21.50 \times 2 + 20 \times 0.5441 \\
&= 31.84 + 43.00 + 10.882 \\
&\approx 85.72\ \text{dB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The shadow‑fading component is a zero‑mean log‑normal random variable with $\sigma_{\text{SF}} = 4.3$ dB, so a typical realization might add roughly $+3$ dB, yielding an instantaneous loss of about $88.7$ dB. The distance $100$ m satisfies the applicability condition $1 \le d_{3D} \le 600$ m, so the model is valid for this scenario [TS 38.901 §7.4.1]​.

### Practical notes and common pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | Correct practice |
|---------|---------------|------------------|
| **Using 2‑D distance instead of 3‑D** | Some scenarios (e.g., UMi) rely on $d_{2D}$, but InF explicitly requires $d_{3D}$. | Verify the distance variable required by the selected model. |
| **Confusing LOS and NLOS formulas** | The two expressions differ in both the constant term and the distance exponent. | Always check the LOS/NLOS status before selecting the formula. |
| **Applying the model outside its distance range** | The specification limits InF to $1\,$m ≤ $d_{3D}$ ≤ $600\,$m. | Ensure the deployment geometry respects the stated range. |
| **Using frequency in MHz** | The formula assumes $f_c$ in GHz; inserting MHz yields an order‑of‑magnitude error. | Convert MHz to GHz (divide by 1000) before substitution. |
| **Ignoring shadow‑fading variance** | Pathloss alone does not capture real‑world variability; σ<sub>SF</sub> must be added in link‑budget Monte‑Carlo runs. | Sample a log‑normal variable with the given σ<sub>SF</sub> for each link. |

Finally, remember that the table only lists the **average** pathloss; small‑scale fading, antenna patterns, and other link‑level effects are treated in separate sections of the specification.
