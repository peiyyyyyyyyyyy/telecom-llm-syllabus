# 2.4.6 聯絡函（LS）與跨組文件

所屬：[2.4 文件歸類](../2-4.md)

## 正文

In 3GPP, many technical decisions require coordination between different working groups (WGs) or even with external standardisation bodies. The vehicle used for this coordination is the **Liaison Statement (LS)**, a specific type of *TDoc* defined in the 3GPP terminology. Because LS carry information, requests, or decisions that affect more than one WG, correctly identifying an LS and its direction (incoming or outgoing) is essential for traceability, impact analysis, and avoiding duplicated effort in real‑world network deployments.  

### Formal definition of LS types
According to the 3GPP specification, a **Liaison Statement** belongs to the family of *Temporary Documents (TDocs)* and appears in two variants:  

* **LS in** – a liaison statement **received** from another 3GPP group or an external body.  
* **LS out** – a liaison statement **issued** by the current group and **directed** to one or more other 3GPP groups or external bodies.  

Both variants are listed in the permissible TDoc types table (Table 9.1‑1) of TS 21.900 §9.1 [TS 21.900 §9.1].  

### How LS fit into the cross‑group document flow
When a WG (e.g., RAN1) needs input from another WG (e.g., SA2) regarding a radio‑access parameter that impacts system architecture, it drafts an **LS out** and sends it to SA2. SA2 reviews the request and replies with an **LS in** that either provides the needed clarification or raises further questions. This exchange can repeat until consensus is reached. Because LS are *temporary* documents, they are retained electronically but are not intended to become permanent reference documents unless later incorporated into a TS, TR, or a formal Change Request (CR).  

### Distinguishing LS from other TDoc types
It is easy to confuse LS with other collaborative TDocs such as **Change Requests (CR)**, **pseudo‑Change‑Requests (pCR)**, or **Work Item Descriptions (WID)**. The key discriminators are:  

| Feature | LS (in / out) | CR / pCR / WID |
|---------|---------------|----------------|
| Purpose | Communicate or request information across groups | Propose textual changes to an existing specification (CR) or draft new text (pCR) |
| Directionality | Explicit “in” or “out” direction | No direction; originates from the proposing WG |
| Formality | Not a formal change control item | CR is a formal change control item; pCR is informal but still a proposal |

The reference explicitly notes that LS are “received from some other 3GPP group or an external body” and “issued by a group and directed to one or more other 3GPP groups or external bodies” [TS 21.900 §9.1].  

### Worked example
Suppose WG RAN1 is drafting a new feature for carrier aggregation. The feature relies on a timing parameter defined by WG SA2. The steps are:  

1. **RAN1 creates an LS out** titled “Request for clarification on timing parameter X for carrier aggregation”.  
2. The LS out is uploaded to the 3GPP document repository and marked with type “LS”.  
3. **SA2 receives the LS** and replies with an **LS in**: “Clarification: parameter X shall be 5 ms for Release 17”.  
4. RAN1 incorporates the clarified value into its draft TS and later may submit a **CR** if the change requires formal amendment of the existing TS.  

Only steps 1 and 3 involve LS types; steps 2 and 4 involve other TDoc types (document repository handling and CR, respectively).  

### Practical notes and common pitfalls

* **Direction matters** – Mislabeling an LS out as an LS in (or vice‑versa) can break the traceability chain, making it unclear which WG originated the request.  
* **Do not treat LS as permanent** – LS are retained electronically for reference, but they are not “permanent reference documents” unless later incorporated into a TS, TR, or a formal CR.  
* **Avoid mixing LS with CR** – An LS may *lead* to a CR, but the LS itself does not change the specification. Confusing the two can cause unnecessary change‑control procedures.  
* **Cross‑group awareness** – When an LS mentions multiple recipient groups, ensure that each recipient WG logs the LS in its own tracking system; otherwise the statement may be missed during later impact analysis.  

By consistently applying the definitions and recognizing the role of LS in cross‑group coordination, engineers can maintain clear documentation trails and reduce the risk of specification drift across the 3GPP ecosystem.

## 練習題

**1.** In the table of permissible TDoc types, which entry explicitly mentions that LS can be “received from some other 3GPP group or an external body”?

- A. LS in
- B. LS out
- C. pCR
- D. WID new

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。The entry “LS in” is defined as a liaison statement received from another group or external body.

</details>

**2.** Which statement is true regarding the permanence of LS documents?

- A. LS documents are automatically archived as permanent reference documents.
- B. LS documents are retained electronically but are not intended as permanent reference documents.
- C. LS documents become permanent only after a Change Request is filed.
- D. LS documents are never stored after the meeting ends.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The reference notes that TDocs (including LS) are retained electronically and are not intended to be permanent reference documents.

</details>

**3.** When a working group receives an LS in, what is the most appropriate next step according to the cross‑group workflow described?

- A. Immediately file a Change Request.
- B. Incorporate the content into a draft TS without further review.
- C. Review the statement, possibly reply with an LS out, and update internal tracking.
- D. Discard the LS as it is not a permanent document.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The typical workflow involves reviewing the LS in, replying if needed, and tracking it; it does not directly become a CR.

</details>
