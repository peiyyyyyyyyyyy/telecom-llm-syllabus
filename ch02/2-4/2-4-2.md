# 2.4.2 SA 各工作組（SA1～SA6）

所屬：[2.4 文件歸類](../2-4.md)

## 正文

SA1 is the first point of contact for feature definition within the 3GPP System Architecture (SA) Technical Specification Group. Its primary responsibility is to capture *stage 1* descriptions, i.e. the high‑level requirements for any new feature or service that will later be standardized. These requirements are then handed over to SA2 for architectural treatment. SA1 may also forward preliminary thoughts on possible architecture and implementation, but it does **not** own the architectural design work.  
[TS 21.900 §6.6]

### Role of SA2
SA2 receives the stage 1 requirements from SA1 and proceeds to define the overall system architecture that will satisfy those requirements. Based on the architectural decisions, SA2 decomposes the feature set into *building blocks*—coherent functional units that can be assigned to the appropriate TSGs and their Working Groups (WGs). SA2 then forwards these building blocks to the relevant TSGs for detailed specification work. Throughout the detailed design phase, SA2 stays informed of progress and ensures that the building blocks are consistently applied across the project.  
[TS 21.900 §6.6]

### Interaction with SA3 and Other TSGs
While SA1 and SA2 focus on requirements and architecture, SA3 is brought in early to align **security**, **service**, and **architectural** requirements across the entire 3GPP ecosystem. The task of guaranteeing this early involvement rests jointly on the TSG SA, SA1 and SA2. In practice, SA2 also coordinates with the CT (Conformance Testing) and RAN (Radio Access Network) TSGs—inviting them to assess any horizontal impact a new feature may have, such as testing implications or mobile station capability changes. This cross‑TSG interaction is essential to avoid duplicated effort and to capture all necessary conformance activities in the overall work plan.  
[TS 21.900 §6.6]

### Scope of SA4‑SA6 (Outside Reference)
The official reference for the Work Item model does not describe the responsibilities of SA4, SA5, or SA6. Consequently, detailed definitions of these groups are beyond the scope of this subsection and must be consulted in the respective 3GPP documents that cover those groups.  

### Worked Example: Scheduling a New Feature
Consider a hypothetical new feature *X* that SA1 has captured as a stage 1 requirement with a target release date of **Q4 2024**.  

1. **SA1 sets the target** – The target date (Q4 2024) is documented and passed to SA2.  
2. **SA2 performs a first technical review** – SA2 evaluates the architectural impact and proposes a revised target of **Q2 2025** to allow sufficient time for building‑block definition.  
3. **Allocation of building blocks** – SA2 assigns the radio‑access related building block to RAN WG1 and the core‑network block to CT WG3.  
4. **Feedback loop** – The TSGs and WGs comment on the proposed schedule; SA2 may adjust the timeline to **Q3 2025** to accommodate a critical dependency identified by CT.  
5. **Final alignment** – SA1, SA2, and the involved TSGs agree on the final schedule, which is then communicated to all parties.  

This example illustrates the iterative nature of target setting, technical review, building‑block allocation, and cross‑TSG coordination described in the Work Item model.  
[TS 21.900 §6.6]

### Practical Notes & Common Mistakes
- **Mistaking responsibility boundaries** – Engineers sometimes assume SA2 also defines the detailed specifications; however, SA2’s role stops at building‑block definition, after which the detailed work belongs to the respective TSGs/WGs.  
- **Skipping early SA3 involvement** – Overlooking the requirement to involve SA3 early can lead to security gaps that later require costly re‑engineering.  
- **Neglecting horizontal task impact** – Failing to invite CT and RAN to evaluate a new feature may result in missed conformance testing activities, causing delays in the overall schedule.  
- **Assuming static targets** – The target dates are not immutable; they are subject to review and adjustment through the interaction of SA1, SA2, and the TSGs, as demonstrated in the worked example.  

By respecting the defined responsibilities of SA1, SA2, and SA3, and by maintaining active coordination with CT and RAN, engineers can ensure that feature development proceeds smoothly without unnecessary overlap or rework.  
[TS 21.900 §6.6]

## 練習題

**1.** Which SA subgroup is responsible for producing the stage 1 (requirement) descriptions for new features?

- A. SA1
- B. SA2
- C. SA3
- D. SA4

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。SA1 creates the high‑level requirements before any architectural work begins.

</details>

**2.** Which SA subgroup defines the architecture and divides features into building blocks?

- A. SA1
- B. SA2
- C. SA3
- D. SA5

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。SA2 takes the stage 1 requirements and creates the system architecture and building blocks.

</details>

**3.** Early involvement of which SA subgroup ensures alignment of security, service, and architectural requirements?

- A. SA1
- B. SA2
- C. SA3
- D. SA6

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The reference explicitly states that SA3 must be involved early to align these requirements.

</details>

**4.** When a new feature may affect conformance testing, which TSGs does SA2 invite to evaluate the impact?

- A. SA1 and SA2
- B. SA3 and SA4
- C. CT and RAN
- D. SA5 and SA6

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。SA2 invites the CT and RAN TSGs to assess horizontal task impact for testing and capabilities.

</details>

**5.** Which statement correctly describes a core responsibility of SA2?

- A. SA2 works independently of other TSGs.
- B. SA2 allocates building blocks without consulting SA1.
- C. SA2 verifies that required work occurs without overlap between groups.
- D. SA2 only handles security requirements.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。SA2’s task includes ensuring that work is coordinated across groups without duplication.

</details>
