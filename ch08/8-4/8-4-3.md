# 8.4.3 節能與服務品質的取捨

所屬：[8.4 方案與取捨](../8-4.md)

## 正文

Operators of 5G networks are under pressure to reduce operational expenditure (OPEX) by lowering power consumption. The need is amplified by the proliferation of NR small cells with massive MIMO, especially in high‑frequency bands, which increase the total number of radio elements that draw electricity. Energy‑saving management must therefore lower the energy used **while still meeting the operator‑defined limits on coverage, capacity and quality of service**【4.3.1】.  

### Energy‑Saving States and Transitions
Each cell, network element (NE) or network function (NF) can be in one of two logical states:  

| State | Meaning |
|------|----------|
| **notEnergySaving** | Normal operation, full transmit power and processing. |
| **energySaving** | Reduced power mode; the element contributes minimally to radio coverage or processing. |

Two elementary procedures drive the state change:  

* **Energy‑saving activation** – transition **notEnergySaving → energySaving**.  
* **Energy‑saving deactivation** – transition **energySaving → notEnergySaving**.  

When a cell enters the **energySaving** state it must first **drain all its traffic to candidate cells** that can provide the same coverage, otherwise a coverage hole would appear【4.3.2】. The candidate cells must be able to absorb the load without exceeding their own thresholds, ensuring that **no undue load is created on the surrounding cells**【4.3.2】.  

A cell in the **energySaving** state is **not regarded as a fault**; the network shall not raise alarms for the state change itself【4.3.2】.  

### Intra‑RAT vs. Inter‑RAT Energy Saving
Two architectural variants are defined:

* **Intra‑RAT** – the candidate cells are gNBs of the same radio access technology. The gNB capacity‑booster cell is switched off while neighboring gNBs keep coverage【5.1.3.2.2】.  
* **Inter‑RAT** – the candidate cell is an LTE eNB that provides basic coverage; the NR gNB acts as a capacity booster that can be turned off when traffic is low【5.1.3.2.3】.  

Both variants may be applied to **partially** or **fully** overlaid cells. In a fully overlaid scenario the booster cell’s entire service area is covered by one or more candidate cells, allowing deeper energy savings【5.1.3.3】.  

### Compensation for Energy Saving
When a booster cell goes into **energySaving**, the selected candidate cell(s) may be instructed to **enter the state *compensatingForEnergySaving***, which expands their coverage area to fill the gap【5.1.4.2】. The complementary procedure **energy saving compensation deactivation** shrinks the coverage again when the booster cell is re‑activated.  

### Measuring Energy Efficiency – The EE KPI
3GPP defines a network‑level KPI for data‑plane energy efficiency in NG‑RAN:

$$
\text{EE}_{\text{MN,DV}} = \frac{\text{Data Volume (DV)}}{\text{Energy Consumption (EC)}}\quad [\text{bit/J}]
$$

* **DV** – total downlink and uplink PDCP SDU bits transmitted by the considered NEs over the measurement period (kbits).  
* **EC** – electrical energy consumed by the same NEs over the same period (kWh), derived from the PEE.Energy counter.  

The KPI is computed per NG‑RAN node; for split‑gNBs the DV is summed per interface (F1‑U, Xn‑U, X2‑U)【6.7.1.1】.  

### Worked Example – EE Calculation
Assume a non‑split gNB serves **500 Gbit** of user data in a 24‑hour interval and its PEE.Energy counter reports **5 kWh** of consumption.

1. Convert DV to bits: $500\ \text{Gbit} = 500 \times 10^{9}\ \text{bits}$.  
2. Convert EC to joules: $5\ \text{kWh} = 5 \times 3.6 \times 10^{6}\ \text{J} = 18\,000\,000\ \text{J}$.  
3. Compute EE:  

$$
\text{EE} = \frac{5.0 \times 10^{11}\ \text{bits}}{1.8 \times 10^{7}\ \text{J}} \approx 27.8 \times 10^{6}\ \text{bit/J} = 27.8\ \text{Mbit/J}.
$$

The resulting EE of **27.8 Mbit/J** can be compared against a target set by the operator; if the value falls below the target, the operator may decide to activate additional energy‑saving states (e.g., switch off a capacity booster) while ensuring that the QoS impact stays within the allowed limits.  

### Trade‑off Between Energy Saving and QoS
The **operator decides** how much degradation in coverage, capacity or QoS is acceptable when a cell enters **energySaving**【4.3.1】. Typical trade‑offs include:

* **Coverage** – Must be fully maintained by candidate cells; otherwise users experience radio‑access outages.  
* **Capacity** – Reducing active cells lowers aggregate capacity; if traffic exceeds the remaining cells’ thresholds, latency or throughput may suffer.  
* **QoS (eMBB, URLLC)** – Inter‑RAT scenarios often keep LTE for basic eMBB service while NR is turned off; high‑rate eMBB or URLLC traffic must trigger re‑activation of the NR booster【5.1.3.2.3】.  

Effective trade‑off management relies on accurate load monitoring, proper selection of **thresholds**, and timely **handover** of user equipment (UE) before activation/deactivation.  

### Common Mistakes and Practical Notes

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|--------|--------------|
| **Skipping traffic drain** – activating energySaving before all sessions are handed over – leads to coverage holes, violating the “should not cause coverage holes” rule【4.3.2】. |
| **Treating the state as a fault** – generating alarms for a planned energySaving transition – conflicts with the specification that “no alarms should be raised”【4.3.2】. |
| **Ignoring compensation** – not switching candidate cells to *compensatingForEnergySaving* – may overload the candidates, degrading QoS. |
| **Over‑aggressive thresholds** – turning off boosters at too low load levels – reduces capacity below the operator‑defined limits, harming user experience. |
| **Neglecting inter‑RAT differences** – assuming LTE can always carry the same URLLC traffic as NR – inter‑RAT energySaving only applies when “no eMBB services with high data rate or URLLC traffic in Cell B is detected”【5.1.3.2.3】. |

Practical deployment therefore combines **centralized** (via MnS producer) and **distributed** decision logic, uses the **EE KPI** to gauge overall efficiency, and respects the operator‑defined QoS boundaries.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following statements best describes the condition that must be satisfied before a cell can enter the **energySaving** state?

- A. The cell may remain active as long as its transmit power is reduced by at least 30 %.
- B. All of its traffic must be drained to overlaid or umbrella candidate cells.
- C. The cell must report a fault to the network management system.
- D. The cell can enter the state only if a neighboring cell is already in *compensatingForEnergySaving*.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The specification requires that all traffic be offloaded before the cell switches to energySaving, to avoid coverage holes.

</details>

**2.** In an **intra‑RAT** energy‑saving scenario, which entities provide coverage for the switched‑off capacity‑booster cell?

- A. LTE eNBs.
- B. gNB candidate cells of the same RAT.
- C. Fixed wireless access nodes.
- D. Satellite terminals.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Intra‑RAT refers to the same radio access technology, i.e., gNBs providing coverage for the NR booster.

</details>

**3.** What is the purpose of the **energy saving compensation activation** procedure?

- A. To shut down all candidate cells to save additional energy.
- B. To increase the coverage area of the candidate cell(s) that take over traffic.
- C. To generate alarms indicating a cell outage.
- D. To reset the load thresholds of the switched‑off cell.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Compensation activation expands the coverage of the candidate cells to cover the area of the cell that entered energySaving.

</details>

**4.** The EE KPI defined in 3GPP is expressed in which unit?

- A. Watt per bit (W/bit)
- B. Bit per joule (bit/J)
- C. Joule per bit (J/bit)
- D. Kilowatt‑hour per megabit (kWh/Mbit)

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The definition states the KPI unit is bit/J.

</details>

**5.** According to the specifications, how should the network treat a cell that has entered the **energySaving** state?

- A. As a fault condition that triggers an alarm.
- B. As a normal outage that requires manual intervention.
- C. As a non‑fault condition; no alarms should be raised.
- D. As a permanently decommissioned element.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The spec explicitly says a cell in energySaving is not considered a fault and no alarms are raised.

</details>
