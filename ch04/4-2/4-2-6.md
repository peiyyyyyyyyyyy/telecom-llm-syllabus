# 4.2.6 排程器與資源區塊分配

所屬：[4.2 基地台軟體](../4-2.md)

## 正文

The scheduler in a 5G‑NR gNB is the decision engine that turns the abstract QoS and buffer information of each UE into concrete radio‑resource allocations on the physical layer. By dynamically assigning resource blocks (RBs) to downlink and uplink transmissions, the scheduler maximises spectral efficiency while honouring latency, throughput, and power constraints. Inefficient scheduling directly degrades user‑experience and increases interference, which is why every open‑source base‑station project implements a scheduler that follows the 3GPP specifications.

### Core Definitions
* **Resource Block (RB)** – the smallest unit of frequency‑time that can be scheduled; a collection of contiguous subcarriers over one OFDM symbol period.  
* **C‑RNTI** – the cell‑radio network temporary identifier used by the gNB to address a UE on the PDCCH.  
* **PDCCH** – Physical Downlink Control Channel that carries Downlink Control Information (DCI) indicating RB assignments.  
* **BWP (Bandwidth Part)** – a subset of the total carrier bandwidth that the UE may be instructed to use.  
* **QoS Requirements** – the set of latency, throughput, and priority attributes defined for each radio bearer.  
* **Buffer Status Report (BSR)** – an uplink message reporting the amount of data queued in the UE’s logical channel buffers.  

### Scheduler Operation (Downlink & Uplink)
The scheduler evaluates three primary inputs for each UE:  

1. **UE buffer status** – reported via BSRs, indicating how much data awaits transmission.  
2. **QoS requirements** – per‑bearer latency and priority constraints.  
3. **Radio conditions** – measurements made at the gNB or reported by the UE.  

Using these inputs, the scheduler **assigns resources between UEs** and produces a list of RBs for each UE. The assignment is signalled on the PDCCH addressed by the UE’s C‑RNTI (or a special RNTI for pre‑emptive or semi‑persistent scheduling). The actual data is then carried on the PDSCH (downlink) or PUSCH (uplink) over the allocated RBs【TS 38.300 §10】.

### Downlink Specifics
* The UE continuously monitors the PDCCH(s) while its downlink reception is enabled (DRX may gate this monitoring).  
* **Pre‑emption** – The gNB may interrupt an ongoing PDSCH transmission with a latency‑critical transmission, signalling the interruption with an INT‑RNTI on the PDCCH. The UE discards any partially received data from the pre‑empted allocation【TS 38.300 §10.2】.  
* **Semi‑Persistent Scheduling (SPS)** – The gNB configures a periodic downlink assignment (via RRC). A PDCCH addressed to CS‑RNTI can activate or deactivate the assignment. While active, the UE may reuse the same RBs on each period without further DCI unless a dynamic allocation overlaps, in which case the dynamic allocation overrides the SPS grant【TS 38.300 §10.2】.  
* Up to **8 active configured downlink assignments** may exist per BWP; activation and deactivation are controlled by DCI commands【TS 38.300 §10.2】.

### Uplink Specifics
* Similar to downlink, the UE monitors the PDCCH for uplink grants addressed by its C‑RNTI.  
* **Cancellation** – The gNB can halt a PUSCH or SRS transmission for a latency‑critical UE, signalling the cancellation with a CI‑RNTI on the PDCCH. The UE must immediately stop transmission on the overlapping symbols【TS 38.300 §10.3】.  
* **Configured Grants** – Two types exist:  
  * **Type 1** – The RRC directly provides the grant (including periodicity).  
  * **Type 2** – The periodicity is defined by RRC, but activation/deactivation is signalled by a CS‑RNTI‑addressed PDCCH.  
* If a UE is **not** configured with enhanced intra‑UE overlapping resources prioritisation, any dynamically allocated uplink transmission **overrides** a configured grant when they overlap in time【TS 38.300 §10.3】.  

### Simple Allocation Formula
When the scheduler decides to allocate $N_{\text{RB}}$ resource blocks to a UE, the total bandwidth consumed is  

$$
B_{\text{alloc}} = N_{\text{RB}} \times \Delta f_{\text{RB}}
$$

where $\Delta f_{\text{RB}}$ is the RB bandwidth (kHz) defined by the numerology. The reference does not specify $\Delta f_{\text{RB}}$, but the formula follows directly from the definition of a resource block as a frequency‑time unit【TS 38.300 §10】.

### Worked Example
Consider a BWP of 20 MHz operating with a numerology that yields $\Delta f_{\text{RB}} = 180$ kHz. The total number of RBs in the BWP is  

$$
N_{\text{RB,total}} = \frac{20\,\text{MHz}}{180\,\text{kHz}} \approx 111 \text{ RBs}.
$$

A UE reports a BSR indicating 300 KB of pending downlink data. The scheduler, respecting the UE’s QoS class (maximum latency 10 ms) and current radio quality, decides to allocate 4 RBs for the upcoming subframe.  

*Allocated bandwidth*:  

$$
B_{\text{alloc}} = 4 \times 180\,\text{kHz} = 720\,\text{kHz}.
$$

The scheduler encodes this allocation in a DCI on the PDCCH addressed by the UE’s C‑RNTI. If, in the same subframe, a latency‑critical UE requires immediate transmission, the scheduler may pre‑empt the 4‑RB allocation, signalling the interruption with an INT‑RNTI. The original UE discards any partially received symbols from the pre‑empted PDSCH【TS 38.300 §10.2】.

### Practical Notes & Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Correct Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Ignoring **pre‑emption** signals (INT‑RNTI/CI‑RNTI) and assuming all allocated RBs will be received. | Developers may treat every DCI as final. | Always monitor the PDCCH for interruption or cancellation indications and discard overlapping symbols as specified. |
| Over‑allocating RBs when a **configured grant** is active, forgetting that dynamic allocations **override** the grant. | Assuming both allocations can be merged. | Apply the rule: dynamic allocation supersedes the configured grant if they overlap in time (unless enhanced intra‑UE prioritisation is configured). |
| Configuring more than **8 active downlink assignments** per BWP. | Misreading the limit as per‑UE rather than per‑BWP. | Enforce the 8‑assignment limit; activation/deactivation must be managed via DCI commands. |
| Forgetting to include **BSR** information in the scheduling decision. | Relying solely on static QoS tables. | Incorporate uplink buffer status reports to enable QoS‑aware packet scheduling. |
| Assuming the same C‑RNTI cannot be reused across cells in carrier aggregation. | Misunderstanding CA behaviour. | Remember that the same C‑RNTI applies to all serving cells when CA is configured【TS 38.300 §10.2】【TS 38.300 §10.3】. |

By adhering strictly to the signalling procedures and priority rules defined in TS 38.300, an open‑source gNB can achieve deterministic, standards‑compliant resource allocation while avoiding the pitfalls that lead to dropped packets or unnecessary retransmissions.

## 練習題

**1.** Which RNTI is used by the gNB to indicate that a downlink transmission has been pre‑empted by a latency‑critical transmission?

- A. C‑RNTI
- B. INT‑RNTI
- C. CI‑RNTI
- D. CS‑RNTI

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The INT‑RNTI is specifically defined for signalling interrupted transmission indications on the PDCCH.

</details>

**2.** How many active configured downlink assignments may a UE have for a given BWP of a serving cell?

- A. Up to 4
- B. Up to 6
- C. Up to 8
- D. Unlimited

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The specification limits the number of active configured downlink assignments to eight per BWP.

</details>

**3.** When a UE is **not** configured with enhanced intra‑UE overlapping resources prioritisation, what happens if a dynamically allocated uplink transmission overlaps a configured uplink grant?

- A. The configured grant takes precedence.
- B. The dynamic allocation overrides the configured grant.
- C. Both transmissions are merged into a single larger grant.
- D. The UE discards both transmissions.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Without the enhanced prioritisation, the dynamic allocation supersedes the configured grant on overlap.

</details>

**4.** In carrier aggregation (CA) configurations, how does the C‑RNTI behave across the serving cells?

- A. A different C‑RNTI is used per cell.
- B. The same C‑RNTI is used for all serving cells.
- C. The C‑RNTI is only used on the primary cell.
- D. The C‑RNTI is replaced by CS‑RNTI on secondary cells.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The specification states that the same C‑RNTI applies to all serving cells when CA is configured.

</details>
