# 6.3.2 需要工程師批准的處置

所屬：[6.3 處置](../6-3.md)

## 正文

In a live telecom network, many fault‑recovery steps can be executed automatically by the Network Element (NE) or the Element Management (EM) system. However, certain actions have a direct impact on service continuity, involve non‑redundant resources, or require coordination with other network functions. These actions must be initiated only after an engineer has reviewed the fault, evaluated the consequences, and given explicit approval. Requiring engineer sign‑off helps prevent inadvertent service outages, ensures that dependent resources are handled correctly, and provides a documented audit trail for compliance and later analysis.  

### Definition of Engineer‑Approved Disposition
An **engineer‑approved disposition** is a manual fault‑recovery action that:  

1. **Is not performed autonomously** by the NE/EM but is triggered by an operator request.  
2. **Requires an engineer’s assessment** of fault impact, resource redundancy, and service degradation risk.  
3. **Is executed only after explicit approval**, often documented in the EM console or work order system.  

The underlying standards state that the NE shall be able to perform recovery actions **if requested by the operator** [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6]. When the fault involves non‑redundant hardware, the recovery sequence includes isolating and removing the faulty resource, which typically needs engineer validation before the NE proceeds.  

### When Engineer Approval Is Required

| Situation | Reason for Approval |
|-----------|--------------------|
| **Faulty resource has no redundancy** | Isolation and removal may disturb other working resources; the engineer must confirm that the impact is acceptable. |
| **Faulty resource has redundancy but the changeover may affect service** | Even with backup, the specific redundancy‑type sequence can cause temporary loss; the engineer decides whether to proceed now or schedule. |
| **Operator‑initiated proactive maintenance** | The operator may request a recovery action to verify NE capability; an engineer validates the test scope to avoid unintended disruption. |
| **Complex dependency chains** | Removing a faulty unit may require also taking dependent resources out of service; the engineer assesses the propagation effect. |

These situations are directly derived from the fault‑recovery functions described in the specification, which differentiate between automatic actions and those performed **“if requested by the operator”** [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6].  

### Procedure for Engineer‑Approved Disposition

1. **Fault Detection & Identification** – The NE detects a fault and reports the replaceable faulty unit(s).  
2. **Impact Evaluation** – The engineer evaluates the effect on telecommunication services, using the NE’s self‑assessment (“evaluate the effect of the fault”) [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6].  
3. **Redundancy Check** – Determine whether the faulty resource has redundancy. If none, the actions **a) isolate and remove**, **b) remove dependent resources**, **c) state management**, and **d) generate notifications** must be considered [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6].  
4. **Approval Request** – The engineer documents the intended actions and obtains formal approval (e.g., via EM console).  
5. **Command Issuance** – The operator issues the recovery command to the NE.  
6. **Monitoring & Confirmation** – The engineer monitors the NE’s execution, verifies that notifications were generated, and confirms that service degradation is within acceptable limits.  

### Worked Example

A regional router carries 200 Erlang of voice traffic. A line‑card (non‑redundant) fails, and the NE reports the fault.  

1. **Impact Evaluation** – The engineer calculates that removing the line‑card will unload 30 Erlang, leaving 170 Erlang on the remaining cards.  
2. **Redundancy Check** – The card is non‑redundant, so isolation **a)** and removal of dependent resources **b)** are required.  
3. **Approval** – The engineer confirms that the remaining capacity (170 Erlang) exceeds the minimum required threshold of 150 Erlang, and therefore approves the removal.  
4. **Command** – The operator sends the isolation command to the NE.  
5. **Outcome** – The NE isolates the card, updates state management, and forwards notifications to the Operations Support (OS). Service experiences a brief 2‑second degradation, which is within the “temporary and limited loss of service” tolerated for fault‑induced changeovers [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6].  

### Common Mistakes & Practical Notes

- **Assuming Automatic Recovery Is Sufficient** – Not all faults can be healed autonomously; non‑redundant hardware always needs manual isolation.  
- **Neglecting Dependent Resources** – Failing to remove resources that depend on the faulty unit can cause fault propagation; the specification explicitly requires removal of dependent resources **“to prevent the propagation of the fault effects”** [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6].  
- **Overlooking Notification Generation** – Engineers must verify that the NE generated the required notifications; otherwise the Operations Support system may remain unaware of the state change.  
- **Scheduling Changeovers During Peak Load** – Even when redundancy exists, the specific recovery sequence may cause a temporary service loss; schedule such actions during low‑traffic periods unless emergency.  

By following the structured, engineer‑approved workflow, operators ensure that fault recovery balances rapid service restoration with controlled risk, adhering to the management functions defined in the TS 32.111‑1 specification.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following situations *must* involve engineer approval according to the standard?

- A. Automatic hot‑standby switchover after a hardware fault.
- B. Isolation and removal of a non‑redundant faulty resource.
- C. Generation of OS notifications after any recovery action.
- D. Download of a software load on a redundant unit.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Isolation and removal of a non‑redundant faulty resource requires manual assessment of service impact, thus needs engineer approval.

</details>

**2.** What does the specification say about the NE's ability to act on operator requests?

- A. The NE can only act on automatic detections, never on operator requests.
- B. The NE shall be able to perform recovery actions if requested by the operator.
- C. The NE must reject any operator‑initiated recovery to avoid service loss.
- D. The NE performs operator requests only after a software reboot.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The standard explicitly states the NE shall be able to perform recovery actions upon operator request.

</details>

**3.** When a faulty resource has redundancy, which recovery steps are *always* performed regardless of redundancy type?

- A. Activation of a fallback software load.
- B. Isolation of the faulty resource, state management, and notification generation.
- C. Full system reboot.
- D. Manual replacement of the hardware by the operator.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The specification lists actions a), c) and d) (isolate, state management, notifications) as always required, even with redundancy.

</details>

**4.** According to the reference, what is the acceptable condition during a changeover for a resource providing service?

- A. No loss of service is ever acceptable.
- B. A temporary and limited loss of service shall be acceptable.
- C. The service must be completely halted for at least 5 minutes.
- D. Only a permanent service degradation is allowed.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The standard permits a brief, limited loss of service during a changeover.

</details>

**5.** Which of the following is *not* listed as a recovery action for software faults in the specification?

- A. Activation of a backup software load.
- B. System initialization at different levels.
- C. Isolation and removal of a faulty hardware resource.
- D. Download of a software unit.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Isolation and removal of a faulty hardware resource pertains to hardware faults, not software faults.

</details>
