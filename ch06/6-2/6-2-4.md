# 6.2.4 分辨幾種可能

所屬：[6.2 推理](../6-2.md)

## 正文

In a live 5G network the mobility management function must decide whether a hand‑over (HO) failure is caused by a **mobility‑related** issue (e.g., parameters that trigger the HO too early or too late) or by a **coverage‑related** problem (e.g., a deep fading spot).  Mobility Robustness Optimisation (MRO) explicitly provides the means to make this distinction, because corrective actions differ: mobility‑related failures are mitigated by tuning HO parameters, whereas coverage problems require radio‑planning changes [TS 38.300 §15.5.2.1].  Accurately classifying the root cause therefore reduces unnecessary optimisation cycles and improves user‑perceived reliability.

### Key categories defined by the standards
The 3GPP specifications separate HO failures into three mutually exclusive categories [TS 28.627 §6.1.3.1.1]:

| Category | Typical symptom |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Too late HO triggering** | The UE is served by a cell whose signal has already become too weak when the HO is initiated, or the HO never occurs because a Radio Link Failure (RLF) pre‑empts it. |
| **Too early HO triggering** | The UE enters a small “island” of the target cell that is embedded within the serving cell’s coverage; the HO is started, but the UE quickly leaves the island and cannot complete the hand‑over. |
| **HO to a wrong cell** | The UE is directed to a cell that is not on the intended trajectory, often because the source cell sent an incorrect HO command. |

MRO additionally targets four higher‑level problems that are observable from the network side [TS 38.300 §15.5.2.1]:

* Connection failure due to intra‑system or inter‑system mobility  
* Inter‑system **Unnecessary HO** (too early inter‑system HO from NR to E‑UTRAN with no RLF)  
* Inter‑system HO **ping‑pong**  
* PSCell change failure  

MRO also makes it possible to recognise **successful** intra‑NR hand‑overs, which helps to separate truly sub‑optimal events from normal mobility [TS 38.300 §15.5.2.1].

### Worked example: from timeline to classification
Consider the following simplified timeline (all timestamps in seconds) recorded for a single UE:

| Time (s) | Event |
|----------|-------|
| 0.0 | UE is attached to Cell S (serving). RSRP_S = –65 dBm. |
| 1.2 | UE detects a brief rise in RSRP_T (target) to –70 dBm while still inside Cell S’s main coverage. |
| 1.3 | HO command to Cell T is issued. |
| 1.5 | UE exits the small coverage island of Cell T; RSRP_T drops below detection threshold. |
| 1.6 | HO fails; UE remains on Cell S but RSRP_S has already fallen to –85 dBm, leading to an RLF. |

**Classification steps**

1. The HO was started **before** the UE left the serving cell’s strong region (RSRP_S still –65 dBm) → **too early** trigger (the UE entered an unintended island of the target cell) [TS 28.627 §6.1.3.1.1.2].
2. Because the HO failed and the UE subsequently suffered an RLF while still on the original cell, the situation also matches the **too late** description: the serving cell’s signal became “already too low” when the HO finally attempted to complete [TS 28.627 §6.1.3.1.1.1].
3. Since the target cell was the intended one (Cell T) and no wrong command was sent, the **HO to a wrong cell** category does **not** apply.

The network’s MRO logic would therefore flag the event as a **sub‑optimal early HO** and suggest adjusting the HO offset or the measurement reporting thresholds to avoid island‑triggered hand‑overs.

### Practical notes and common mistakes
| Mistake | Why it is wrong | Correct practice |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Treating a coverage hole as a mobility failure** | MRO is designed to *distinguish* mobility‑related problems from NR coverage‑related problems [TS 38.300 §15.5.2.1]. | Verify whether the UE’s signal quality was acceptable in the serving cell before the HO; if not, the issue is likely coverage‑related. |
| **Assuming any early HO is “unnecessary inter‑system HO”** | The term *Unnecessary HO* in the standard refers specifically to *inter‑system* HO from NR to E‑UTRAN with **no** RLF [TS 38.300 §15.5.2.1]. | Check the system boundary (NR ↔ E‑UTRAN) and whether an RLF was observed before classifying it as unnecessary. |
| **Ignoring successful intra‑NR hand‑overs** | MRO also observes successful HO due to intra‑NR mobility, which is useful for baseline performance [TS 38.300 §15.5.2.1]. | Log successful intra‑NR HO events to establish a reference for what constitutes “normal” mobility. |
| **Confusing ping‑pong with “too early” HO** | Ping‑pong describes repeated back‑and‑forth HO between two cells, whereas “too early” HO is a single premature trigger caused by a coverage island [TS 38.300 §15.5.2.1]. | Look for rapid successive HO commands between the same pair of cells to identify ping‑pong. |
| **Applying the same threshold for intra‑ and inter‑system HO** | The standard distinguishes intra‑system, inter‑system, and inter‑NR mobility problems, each potentially requiring different parameter sets [TS 38.300 §15.5.2.1]. | Use separate optimisation loops for intra‑NR, inter‑NR, and inter‑system HO parameters. |

By systematically mapping observed events onto the categories above, engineers can pinpoint the exact root cause and apply the appropriate corrective action—parameter tuning for mobility‑related failures, or radio‑planning measures for coverage‑related issues.  This disciplined approach is the cornerstone of effective **Mobility Robustness Optimisation** in 5G NR deployments.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following problems is *not* listed as a target of Mobility Robustness Optimisation (MRO) in the reference specifications?

- A. Connection failure due to intra‑system mobility
- B. Inter‑system Unnecessary HO
- C. PSCell change failure
- D. Radio Link Failure timeout

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：D。The reference lists connection failure, unnecessary HO, ping‑pong, and PSCell change failure, but not a generic RLF timeout.

</details>

**2.** According to TS 28.627, HO failures are categorized into three groups. Which group is *not* part of this categorization?

- A. Too late HO triggering
- B. Too early HO triggering
- C. HO to a wrong cell
- D. Inter‑system ping‑pong HO

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：D。The three categories are too late, too early, and wrong cell; ping‑pong is mentioned elsewhere but not in the three‑group list.

</details>

**3.** In the “too early HO triggering” scenario, what is the primary cause of the hand‑over failure?

- A. The UE enters an unintended island of coverage of the target cell inside the serving cell’s area.
- B. The UE moves faster than the HO parameters can accommodate.
- C. The source cell sends a wrong HO command.
- D. The target cell’s transmit power is too low.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。The reference describes the island‑coverage situation as the reason for too early failure.

</details>

**4.** Which definition matches “Inter‑system Unnecessary HO” as defined for MRO?

- A. A hand‑over from NR to E‑UTRAN that occurs too early and does not result in a radio link failure.
- B. A hand‑over that repeatedly switches between two NR cells.
- C. A hand‑over that is triggered after the serving cell’s signal has already become too low.
- D. A hand‑over to a cell that is not on the UE’s trajectory.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。The standard defines unnecessary HO as a too‑early inter‑system HO from NR to E‑UTRAN with no RLF.

</details>

**5.** MRO provides observability of successful hand‑overs caused by which type of mobility?

- A. Intra‑NR mobility
- B. Inter‑system mobility
- C. Inter‑frequency mobility
- D. Inter‑RAT mobility

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。The reference explicitly mentions successful HO due to intra‑NR mobility.

</details>
