# 6.1.7 硬體故障

所屬：[6.1 根因的類型](../6-1.md)

## 正文

In a telecom network a **hardware fault** is the malfunction of some physical resource inside a Network Element (NE) — for example a power supply, a radio front‑end, or a back‑plane connector [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.0].  Because the physical resource directly supports the transmission of user data, a failure can instantly degrade or interrupt service, trigger alarms, and force the network management system to take recovery actions.  Prompt detection and correct handling of hardware faults are therefore essential to maintain the availability targets defined in service‑level agreements.

### Key definitions

| Term | Definition (as per the reference) |
|------|-----------------------------------|
| **Hardware fault** | “the malfunction of some physical resource within a NE” [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.0]. |
| **Redundant resource** | An additional (back‑up) hardware unit that can take over the function of a failed unit, enabling fault tolerance [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6]. |
| **Fault recovery** | The set of actions performed after a fault is detected to restore normal operation [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6]. |
| **Isolation** | Removing a faulty resource from service so that it cannot disturb other working resources [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6]. |
| **State management** | Updating the NE’s internal tables to reflect the new status of the faulty and dependent resources [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6]. |
| **Notification** | Generating and forwarding alarms or messages to inform the Operations Support (OS) about the fault and the performed actions [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6]. |

### Fault‑recovery logic for hardware failures

When a hardware fault is detected, the NE follows a defined decision tree:

1. **Determine redundancy** – does the failed resource have a standby counterpart? [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6]  
2. **If no redundancy** – execute the four mandatory steps:  
   a. *Isolate and remove* the faulty resource from service.  
   b. *Remove from service* any physical or functional resources that depend on it.  
   c. Perform *state‑management* updates for all affected elements.  
   d. *Generate and forward* appropriate notifications to the OS. [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6]  
3. **If redundancy exists** – carry out steps a, c, and d, then invoke the redundancy‑specific switch‑over sequence (e.g., hot standby, cold standby, duplex, N+1) [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6].

The recovery sequence starts **immediately** after detection; a brief, limited service loss is acceptable while the change‑over proceeds [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6].

### Worked example (single‑unit failure, no redundancy)

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| **Fault**: 1 × RF power amplifier in a base‑station stops delivering output. | The NE marks the amplifier as *faulty* (alarm raised). |
| **Redundancy check**: No hot‑standby amplifier installed. | The NE follows the *no‑redundancy* path. |
| **Step a – Isolation** | The NE disables the amplifier’s output port, preventing it from affecting downstream filters. |
| **Step b – Dependent removal** | The NE also disables the carrier that used the failed amplifier, because the carrier cannot be transmitted without RF power. |
| **Step c – State management** | The NE updates its resource‑allocation table: the amplifier state = *offline*, the carrier state = *blocked*. |
| **Step d – Notification** | An alarm “RF AMP FAILURE” together with “CARRIER BLOCKED” is sent to the OS for operator awareness. |

The example illustrates the exact sequence prescribed for a non‑redundant hardware fault, using only the logical steps defined in the specification.

### Common mistakes and practical notes

* **Assuming automatic switchover** – If a resource has no redundant counterpart, the NE **cannot** perform a seamless switch‑over; the service will experience at least a brief interruption [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6].  
* **Skipping dependent removal** – Leaving dependent logical entities (e.g., carriers, bearers) active after a hardware failure can cause error propagation and further alarms; step b explicitly requires their removal [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6].  
* **Neglecting state‑management updates** – Failure to reflect the new status in the NE’s tables may lead to inconsistent alarm correlation and hinder subsequent troubleshooting.  
* **Improper alarm handling** – Operators must treat the generated notifications as the authoritative source of truth; manual “guesswork” can mask the real fault and delay recovery.  

In practice, engineers should verify the redundancy design (hot vs. cold standby, N+1, etc.) during the planning phase, because the recovery actions and service impact differ drastically depending on the redundancy type [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.6].

## 練習題

**1.** Which fault category explicitly refers to “the malfunction of some physical resource within a NE”?

- A. Software problems
- B. Functional faults
- C. Hardware failures
- D. Communication failures

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The definition of hardware failures mentions the malfunction of a physical resource.

</details>

**2.** When a faulty hardware resource has **no** redundancy, which of the following actions is required first?

- A. Activate a backup software load.
- B. Isolate and remove from service the faulty resource.
- C. Perform a system initialization.
- D. Download a new software unit.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The specification lists isolation and removal as the initial recovery step for non‑redundant hardware faults.

</details>

**3.** In the case of a hardware fault, the recovery actions depend primarily on what factor?

- A. The severity of the fault.
- B. The existence and type of redundant (i.e. back‑up) resources.
- C. The operator’s manual commands.
- D. The number of ongoing calls.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The text states that hardware‑fault recovery is based on whether redundant resources exist and their type.

</details>

**4.** After isolating a non‑redundant faulty hardware resource, which additional activity must be performed according to the specification?

- A. Generate and forward appropriate notifications to inform the OS.
- B. State management related activities for the faulty resource and other affected/dependent resources.
- C. Activate a fallback software load.
- D. Perform a system reboot.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The specification requires state‑management activities for the faulty resource after isolation.

</details>
