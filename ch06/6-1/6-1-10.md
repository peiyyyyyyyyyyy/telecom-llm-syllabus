# 6.1.10 上下行涵蓋不平衡

所屬：[6.1 根因的類型](../6-1.md)

## 正文

Uplink‑downlink (UL/DL) coverage imbalance, also called a **DL and UL channel coverage mismatch**, occurs when the quality of the downlink radio link differs significantly from that of the uplink link in the same geographical area. In a balanced cell the same UE can both receive (DL) and transmit (UL) with sufficient signal strength and quality; when the balance is broken the UE may experience successful downlink reception yet fail to complete uplink procedures such as random access, RRC connection establishment, or handover. The 3GPP specification lists this condition explicitly as one of the coverage‑related problems that can degrade user experience [TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1].

### Why does it matter?

A mismatch can lead to several observable symptoms:

* **Out‑of‑service (OOS) conditions** – the UE can see a cell (strong DL) but cannot be granted access because the UL is too weak.  
* **Random‑access failures** – the UE’s preamble does not reach the gNB with enough SINR, causing retransmissions and increased latency.  
* **Abnormal RRC releases** – the network may drop the UE after detecting poor UL quality, even if the DL remains acceptable.  

These effects are cited as examples of performance degradation caused by RAN coverage problems, which directly impact QoE and may trigger KPI alarms in the operations support system [TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1].

### Root causes of UL/DL imbalance

The specification groups the causes of coverage problems into two broad categories:

| Category | Typical contributors |
|----------|----------------------|
| **Insufficient or weak transmission power** | Low eNodeB/gNB DL power, low UE UL transmit power, antenna gain or tilt errors |
| **Physical obstructions** | Buildings, terrain, foliage that attenuate either the DL or UL path differently |

Both categories are mentioned as “different sorts of reasons, such as insufficient or weak transmission power, blocked by constructions and/or restricted by terrain” [TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1].

Because the DL and UL use the same carrier frequency (in TDD) or adjacent frequencies (in FDD), the propagation loss is often similar, but asymmetries arise from **UE power limitations** (the UE cannot transmit as strongly as the base station) and from **environmental asymmetries** (e.g., a wall that blocks the UE’s uplink more than the downlink due to antenna placement).

### Detecting the imbalance

The MDA (Measurement Data Analytics) function must **correlate multifold data** – performance measurements, MDT reports, RLF reports, RCEF reports, UE location reports, and configuration data – to pinpoint where and when the mismatch occurs [TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1]. In practice, analysts look for:

* **DL‑centric metrics** (e.g., high RSRP, good DL SINR) that contrast with **UL‑centric metrics** (e.g., low UL SINR, high UL transmission power, high RRC connection failure rate).  
* Spatial patterns on a **Radio Environment Map (REM)** that display RSRP or SINR per cell. The REM helps visualise that a certain area enjoys strong DL coverage while the UL metric surface shows a “hole” or “dip” [TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1].

### Worked example

Consider a macro cell operating in FDD mode. A UE located 800 m from the gNB reports the following measurements (averaged over 10 s):

| Metric | Measured value |
|--------|----------------|
| DL RSRP | –78 dBm (good) |
| DL SINR | 12 dB (acceptable) |
| UL TX power (max) | 23 dBm (UE limit) |
| UL SINR | –3 dB (poor) |
| Random‑access success rate | 45 % |

The DL indicators suggest the UE is well within the cell’s coverage area, yet the UL SINR is below the typical threshold for successful random access (≈ 0 dB). Because the UE already transmits at its maximum power, the poor UL SINR points to **excessive path loss** on the uplink, possibly due to a building that shields the UE’s antenna from the gNB. Using the REM, the analyst spots a “dip” in UL SINR exactly at the UE’s coordinates, confirming an UL/DL imbalance.

Remedial actions suggested by MDA could include **adding a low‑power small cell** or **re‑tilting the macro antenna** to improve UL gain toward the affected area, both of which are typical recommendations for coverage‑imbalance problems [TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1].

### Practical notes and common pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it happens | How to avoid |
|--------|----------------|--------------|
| **Assuming DL coverage guarantees UL coverage** | UE transmit power is limited; the spec explicitly separates DL and UL mismatch as a distinct problem. | Always examine UL‑specific KPIs (UL SINR, UL RLF) in addition to DL metrics. |
| **Relying on a single measurement source** | A single MDT report may miss transient UL failures. | Correlate multiple sources (MDT, RLF, RCEF) as required by the analysis capability. |
| **Ignoring terrain and building data** | Physical obstructions are listed as causes of coverage problems. | Incorporate GIS data when building the REM to identify likely blockage zones. |
| **Over‑optimising DL power to mask UL issues** | Increasing DL power does not improve UL performance and may worsen interference. | Adjust UL‑related parameters (e.g., antenna tilt, add UL‑focused cells) rather than only boosting DL. |

By following the analysis workflow prescribed in the specification—identifying when/where the problem occurs, correlating the appropriate measurements, and visualising the result on a REM—engineers can efficiently diagnose UL/DL coverage imbalance and apply targeted remedies, thereby restoring balanced service and improving overall QoE [TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1].
