# 6.1.1 涵蓋問題：涵蓋不足、過度涵蓋、重疊涵蓋

所屬：[6.1 根因的類型](../6-1.md)

## 正文

In a mobile network the radio access must be able to deliver the required signaling (random access, paging, RRC connection establishment, handover) and user‑plane data. When the RAN coverage is insufficient, UEs may be **out of service** or experience a **downgrade of network performance** such as low throughput or abnormal RRC releases. Conversely, too much coverage (overshoot) can generate interference to neighbouring cells. Both conditions directly affect the Quality of Experience (QoE) and therefore belong to the first line of root‑cause analysis in any fault‑diagnosis workflow [[TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1.2]].

### Types of coverage problems

The specification enumerates the following canonical coverage anomalies:  

* **Weak coverage / coverage hole** – the received signal level (e.g., RSRP) is below the threshold needed for reliable access, producing a “hole” in the service map.  
* **Pilot pollution** – excessive pilot (reference signal) power from many cells creates a high interference floor, reducing the usable SINR despite strong absolute power.  
* **Overshoot coverage** – a cell’s radiation pattern extends far beyond the intended service area, causing overlap with neighbouring cells and potential interference.  
* **Downlink / Uplink (DL‑UL) mismatch** – the DL and UL coverage contours differ, so a UE can receive a DL signal but cannot transmit on the UL (or vice‑versa).  

All of these are explicitly listed as coverage problem types in the MDA capability description [[TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1.1]].

### Data sources for analysis

To pinpoint when, where and why a coverage problem occurs, the MDA consumer must **correlate** a variety of measurements:  

* Performance metrics (RSRP, SINR, throughput)  
* MDT (Minimisation of Drive Tests) reports  
* RLF (Radio Link Failure) and RCEF (Radio Connection Establishment Failure) logs  
* UE location reports  
* Geographic, terrain and RAN configuration data (transmit power, antenna tilt, gain)  

Only by fusing these sources can the system **detect and describe** the problem with sufficient detail for remediation [[TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1.2]].

### Radio Environment Map (REM)

A practical way to visualise coverage quality is a **Radio Environment Map**. The REM plots a chosen metric—commonly **RSRP** or **SINR**—for a set of cells, derived either from observed UE performance or from the cells’ radio parameters (power, antenna gain, tilt). The map is used both for **post‑event analysis** and for **proactive planning** when a new node is added, enabling the optimisation of initial radio settings [[TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1.2]].

### Overlapping coverage and load balancing

When two cells cover the same geographical area, the network may apply **load‑balancing** to distribute UEs more evenly. The 3GPP specifications distinguish three overlapping scenarios:  

* **Overlapping coverage** – two same‑size cells (Cell A, Cell B) fully overlap; load can be balanced regardless of the UE’s exact location.  
* **Hierarchical coverage** – a small cell (Cell B) is completely inside a larger cell (Cell A); balancing may flow from the small to the large cell, but only UEs in the overlap are considered for the reverse direction.  
* **Neighbouring coverage** – partial overlap between adjacent cells; balancing is limited to UEs located in the shared region.  

These scenarios are defined in the load‑balancing section of TS 28.627 and are the basis for many automated optimisation algorithms [[TS 28.627 §5.4.1]].

### Optimisation objectives

The **Coverage and Capacity Optimisation (CCO)** function must operate with **minimal human intervention**, allowing the operator to set area‑specific objectives (e.g., eliminate coverage holes, shrink overshoot, respect trade‑offs with capacity). Input data collection is expected to be highly automated, and the optimisation loop may suggest actions such as re‑configuring transmit power, adjusting antenna tilt, or adding new cells/beams. The standard also mandates that the optimisation consider the scenarios listed above (coverage holes, isolated island cells, cells with too large coverage) [[TS 28.627 §5.1.5]].

### Worked example

Assume a macrocell (Cell X) is configured with a down‑link transmit power of 46 dBm, antenna gain of 15 dBi and a mechanical tilt of 2°. UE measurements in a suburban area show an average RSRP of –105 dBm, which is below the typical –95 dBm threshold for reliable RRC connection.  

1. **Identify the problem** – weak coverage (coverage hole).  
2. **Correlate data** – MDT reports confirm a high RLF rate in the same area; terrain data indicate a hill blocking the line‑of‑sight.  
3. **Remedy** – the MDA suggests either (a) increase transmit power by 2 dB, (b) add a small cell on the hill, or (c) tilt the antenna downward by 3° to concentrate energy towards the affected region.  

The suggested actions follow directly from the requirement that MDA provide **recommended remedy actions** such as “reconfigure or add cells, beams, antennas” [[TS 28.104 §7.2.1.1.2]].

### Common pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it happens | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Confusing weak coverage with pilot pollution** | Both manifest as low SINR, but the root cause differs (insufficient power vs. excessive interference). | Use MDT and RLF reports together with neighbour‑cell power levels to distinguish. |
| **Ignoring DL‑UL mismatch** | Operators often focus on DL RSRP only; UL power limits may still cause failures. | Verify UL reference signal power (e.g., SRS) and compare DL/UL coverage maps. |
| **Over‑relying on a single metric** | RSRP alone does not capture interference; SINR is needed for pilot pollution detection. | Build REMs for both RSRP and SINR. |
| **Static load‑balancing rules** | Fixed handover offsets may not adapt to dynamic traffic, leading to persistent overload in overlapping zones. | Enable dynamic CCO that can adjust coverage configurations and notify neighbours via NG‑RAN NODE CONFIGURATION UPDATE messages [[TS 38.300 §15.5.5.3]]. |
| **Neglecting hierarchical overlap** | Treating all overlaps as equal can cause unnecessary handovers from small to large cells. | Apply the hierarchical‑coverage rule: balance from the small cell to the large one only for UEs in the overlap. |

By systematically applying the analysis steps, visualising the REM, and respecting the overlapping‑coverage scenarios, engineers can efficiently resolve coverage‑related root causes and maintain the intended service quality.

## 練習題

**1.** 哪一項術語在規格中用來描述「涵蓋不足」的情況？

- A. weak coverage
- B. coverage hole
- C. overshoot coverage
- D. pilot pollution

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。「coverage hole」直接指示了覆蓋缺失的情形，即用戶設備可能無法取得服務。

</details>

**2.** 以下哪個描述對應「過度涵蓋」的概念？

- A. pilot pollution
- B. weak coverage
- C. overshoot coverage
- D. coverage hole

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。「overshoot coverage」指的是信號覆蓋範圍超過需求，造成過度覆蓋的情況。

</details>

**3.** 在 TS 28.627 §5.5 所列的情境中，哪一項直接關聯到「過度涵蓋」？

- A. E-UTRAN Coverage holes with 2G/3G coverage
- B. E-UTRAN cells with too large coverage
- C. E-UTRAN Coverage holes without any other radio coverage
- D. E-UTRAN Coverage holes with isolated island cell coverage

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。「too large coverage」即表示電槽的覆蓋範圍過大，屬於過度涵蓋。

</details>

**4.** NR Coverage and Capacity Optimization (CCO) 功能的主要目標是什麼？（參考 TS 38.300 §15.5.5.1）

- A. 減少 UE 電池消耗
- B. 解決 pilot pollution 問題
- C. 偵測並緩解 coverage and cell edge interference issues
- D. 提升核心網路吞吐量

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。規格指出 CCO 旨在偵測與緩解「coverage and cell edge interference issues」。

</details>
