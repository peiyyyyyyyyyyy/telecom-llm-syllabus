# 6.4.2 通報與升級

所屬：[6.4 流程](../6-4.md)

## 正文

In a 3GPP‑managed network, **alarm reporting** is the mechanism that delivers a notification to the operating personnel as soon as a failure is detected.  The purpose is to “alert the operating personnel when failures appear in the networks” and to do so “as fast as possible” so that the impact on user‑perceived Quality of Service (QoS) can be minimised [TS 32.111-1 §4.4].  Because the network consists of many heterogeneous elements, the alarm surveillance function is regarded as “the first line Network Management Assurance Activity and is often maintained in near real time” [TS 32.111-1 §4.4].

### Escalation Criteria
Escalation is triggered only when an alarm **requires an operator response**.  The specification states that “the key criterion is that alarms must require an operator response – that is, an action” [TS 32.111-1 §4.4].  If an alarm does not lead to a concrete action (e.g., a purely informational event), it is filtered out or suppressed to avoid flooding the operator console.  Suppression methods are explicitly mentioned as a way to “handle alarm flooding so that saturation of the alarm management systems will not happen and control of the network is never lost” [TS 32.111-1 §4.4].

### Operator Workflow
When a qualifying alarm reaches the operator, the following sequence—derived directly from the standard’s list of possible responses—is expected:

1. **Recognize** the alarm (identify its source and type).  
2. **Acknowledge** the alarm (confirm it has been seen).  
3. **Verify** that the alarm is valid and not a malfunction of the alarm system itself.  
4. **Obtain enhanced information** related to the alarm (e.g., performance counters, error logs).  
5. **Analyse** the situation to estimate the root cause, possible service impact, and to decide whether to involve second‑line support.  
6. **Take corrective actions** such as resetting equipment, replacing hardware, or creating a trouble report.  
7. **Continue surveillance** of the affected element(s) to confirm that the fault has been cleared.  

These steps are enumerated in the reference as “Operator response to an alarm may consist of many different steps such as:” followed by the bullet list [TS 32.111-1 §4.4].

### Worked Example (Illustrative Numbers)
Assume a radio base station (eNodeB) detects a hardware fault and generates an alarm with severity *Critical*.  

| Step | Action | Example Value |
|------|--------|---------------|
| 1 – Recognize | Operator sees alarm ID **RAN‑HW‑001** on the console. | – |
| 2 – Acknowledge | Operator clicks “Acknowledge” at **09:15:03**. | – |
| 3 – Verify | Operator checks that the alarm is not a duplicate by consulting the last 5 minutes of logs; no duplicate found. | – |
| 4 – Enhanced Info | System provides **temperature = 85 °C**, **voltage = ‑5 V**. | – |
| 5 – Analyse | High temperature suggests cooling fan failure; potential service outage for 150 users. | – |
| 6 – Action | Operator issues a **remote reset** at **09:16:10**; if no recovery, a field engineer is dispatched (escalation). | – |
| 7 – Surveillance | After reset, alarm state changes to **Cleared** at **09:18:45**; KPI monitoring shows normal traffic. | – |

Even though the reference does not provide numeric thresholds, the example demonstrates the **flow** defined by the standard: from detection to verification, analysis, action, and post‑action monitoring.  Each step is a direct instantiation of the bullet‑point actions listed in the specification [TS 32.111-1 §4.4].

### Practical Notes and Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Remedy (per standard) |
|---------|----------------|-----------------------|
| **Treating every alarm as actionable** | Operators may lack filtering, leading to “alarm flooding”. | Apply suppression methods so that “only monitor the necessary alarms at the right time” [TS 32.111-1 §4.4]. |
| **Missing the “never miss” expectation** | Over‑reliance on automated scripts can hide critical alarms. | Remember that “operators should never miss alarms requiring an operation action” [TS 32.111-1 §4.4]. |
| **Skipping verification** | Assuming the alarm is genuine can waste time on false positives. | Follow the step “Verifying that the alarm is valid and not a malfunction” [TS 32.111-1 §4.4]. |
| **Not escalating when needed** | Keeping the fault at first‑line may delay resolution. | If analysis indicates a need for “reporting/activating other people from the second line support”, do so promptly [TS 32.111-1 §4.4]. |
| **Neglecting post‑action surveillance** | The fault may re‑appear unnoticed. | Continue monitoring “to ensure the fault correction” [TS 32.111-1 §4.4]. |

In summary, **alarm reporting and escalation** in 3GPP networks are tightly bound to the principle that *only alarms demanding an operator action should be presented*, and that *operators must follow a disciplined workflow* to recognise, verify, analyse, act, and re‑monitor.  Adhering to these practices yields the “few alarms” and “clear prioritisation” expectations that keep the management system responsive and the network QoS stable [TS 32.111-1 §4.4].

## 練習題

**1.** What is the key criterion that determines whether an alarm should be presented to the operator?

- A. The alarm originates from a core network element.
- B. The alarm has a severity level of "Critical".
- C. The alarm must require an operator response.
- D. The alarm is generated at least once per hour.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The specification explicitly states that alarms must require an operator response to be considered relevant.

</details>

**2.** According to the reference, what is the expected quantity of alarms presented to operators?

- A. Hundreds per minute
- B. A few alarms
- C. One alarm per network element per day
- D. Unlimited, as long as they are logged

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The expectation of alarm handling includes “Few alarms”.

</details>

**3.** What is the primary purpose of alarm suppression methods as described in the specification?

- A. To permanently disable low‑priority alarms
- B. To reduce the number of alarms so that the operator never sees any
- C. To aid the operator in handling alarm flooding and prevent saturation of the alarm management system
- D. To delay alarm notifications until a scheduled maintenance window

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Suppression is meant to avoid flooding and loss of control, not to hide alarms permanently.

</details>

**4.** The reference describes alarm notifications as a human‑machine interface. What common expectation is associated with this interface?

- A. Operators can ignore alarms that occur during peak traffic.
- B. Operators should never miss alarms requiring an operation action.
- C. Operators must manually log every alarm in a spreadsheet.
- D. Operators only need to acknowledge alarms, not act on them.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The standard explicitly states that operators should never miss actionable alarms.

</details>
