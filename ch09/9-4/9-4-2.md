# 9.4.2 和現有設定的衝突

所屬：[9.4 變更前檢查](../9-4.md)

## 正文

When a change proposal is applied to a network element (NE), the new values must not clash with the configuration that already exists in the managed information base (MIB). A conflict can cause service degradation, alarm storms, or even a loss of control over the NE. Because the Network Manager (NM) and the Element Manager (EM) rely on a shared Network Resource Model (NRM) to exchange object abstractions, any inconsistency between the proposed change and the current MIB jeopardises the integrity of the whole managed network [TS 32.600 §4.2.1.3].

### Core concepts

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit / Type |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **MOI** | Managed Object Instance – a concrete representation of a physical or logical resource in the MIB | – |
| **NE‑local MIB** | MIB that exists only on the originating NE; consistency must be kept between NEs | – |
| **Mirrored MIB** | A copy of the NE‑local MIB is also stored in the NM/EM; consistency must be kept across all copies | – |
| **Δ** | Detected deviation (conflict) between two views of the same MOI | – |

No algebraic formula is required; conflict detection is a logical comparison:

$$\Delta = \begin{cases}
\text{true}, & \text{if } \text{value}_{\text{NM/EM}} \neq \text{value}_{\text{NE}} \\
\text{false}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}$$

where $\text{value}_{\text{NM/EM}}$ and $\text{value}_{\text{NE}}$ are the attribute values of the same MOI as seen by the NM/EM and the NE respectively.

### Conflict detection workflow

1. **Retrieve current information** – The NM requests all relevant MOIs from the EM and the NE via the Itf‑N interface (periodically or on demand).  
2. **Compare views** – For each MOI, the NM compares the NE‑local value with the NM/EM copy (mirrored MIB) or with the values from peer NEs (NE‑local MIB).  
3. **Report deviations** – Any $\Delta = \text{true}$ is logged and presented to the operator as a conflict.  
4. **Resolution** – Depending on the master‑ship rule (manufacturer‑defined), the NM either updates the NE, updates its own copy, or prompts the operator for manual correction.  

These steps are explicitly recommended in the operational procedures of TS 32.600 §4.2.1.3 [TS 32.600 §4.2.1.3].

### Worked example (mirrored MIB)

Assume a 4G LTE eNodeB (NE) hosts a MOI *TxPower* with attribute *value* expressed in dBm.  

| Source | Reported value |
|--------|----------------|
| NE‑local MIB | 23 dBm |
| NM/EM mirrored copy | 20 dBm |

**Step 1 – Retrieval:** NM issues a Get request over Itf‑N and receives 23 dBm from the NE and 20 dBm from its own store.  

**Step 2 – Comparison:**  
$$\Delta = (23\ \text{dBm} \neq 20\ \text{dBm}) = \text{true}$$  

**Step 3 – Reporting:** NM generates an alarm “TxPower conflict: NE reports 23 dBm, NM expects 20 dBm.”  

**Step 4 – Resolution:** If the operator has defined the NM as master for *TxPower*, the NM automatically writes 23 dBm back to the NE, bringing both sides into agreement.  

The example follows the “mirrored MIB” actions: retrieve, compare, report, and correct [TS 32.600 §4.2.1.3].

### Conflict detection for NE‑local MIB

When the network uses the NE‑local MIB approach, the NM does not keep a copy of the MOIs. Instead, it compares the values reported by *all* connected NEs. A conflict is flagged if two NEs present different values for a resource that should be identical (e.g., a shared carrier frequency). The resolution always targets the NE side, because the NM holds no authoritative copy [TS 32.600 §4.2.1.3].

### Practical notes and common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it happens | How to avoid |
|---------|----------------|--------------|
| **Assuming automatic consistency** | Believing that peer‑to‑peer consistency guarantees network‑wide consistency. The specification clarifies that peer‑to‑peer consistency *does not* guarantee overall data consistency [TS 32.600 §4.2.1.3]. | Implement NM‑level audits that compare information across all NEs. |
| **Skipping the retrieval step** | Directly applying a change without first reading the current values can overwrite unknown settings. | Always perform step 1 of the workflow before any modification. |
| **Modifying NE‑local data without notification** | Local OS terminals can change the NE‑local MIB without the NM’s knowledge, breaking the “mirrored” view. | Use a remote OS terminal and ensure that any local change is notified to the managing OS(s) [TS 32.600 §4.2.1.3]. |
| **Confusing master‑ship** | Applying a correction to the wrong side (NE instead of NM) leads to persistent conflicts. | Follow the manufacturer‑defined master rule; document it in the change plan. |
| **Neglecting autonomous re‑configuration reports** | Autonomous NE re‑configuration (e.g., self‑healing) may silently alter MOIs. | Enable reporting of all autonomous re‑configurations as recommended [TS 32.600 §4.2.1.3]. |

By adhering to the prescribed retrieval‑compare‑report‑resolve cycle, engineers can reliably detect and resolve configuration conflicts before they impact service quality.

## 練習題

**1.** In the conflict detection workflow, what is the first mandatory step according to TS 32.600 §4.2.1.3?

- A. Resolve any detected deviations automatically.
- B. Retrieve all management information from the EM and NE via the Itf‑N.
- C. Update the NM's copy of the MIB before comparing values.
- D. Notify the operator before any comparison is made.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The specification states that the NM shall be able to retrieve all management information first.

</details>

**2.** Which of the following is *not* a recommended action when using the NE‑local MIB approach?

- A. The NM compares retrieved information between connected NEs.
- B. The NM reports deviations between related NEs' views to the operator.
- C. The NM automatically corrects deviating information in the NM's own MIB.
- D. The NM retrieves all management information from the EM and NE.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。In the NE‑local MIB approach, correction is performed in the NEs, not in the NM’s own MIB.

</details>

**3.** According to the reference, peer‑to‑peer data consistency between NM‑EM and EM‑NE guarantees overall network‑level data consistency.

- A. True
- B. False
- C. Only when a mirrored MIB is used
- D. Only when autonomous re‑configuration is disabled

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The specification explicitly says peer‑to‑peer consistency does *not* guarantee overall consistency.

</details>

**4.** When an autonomous NE re‑configuration occurs, what does the specification recommend?

- A. Ignore it because it is outside the NM's control.
- B. Report it to the NM/EM.
- C. Automatically revert the change.
- D. Store it only in the NE‑local MIB.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Autonomous re‑configurations should always be reported to maintain data consistency.

</details>
