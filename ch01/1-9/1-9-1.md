## 正文

Modern wireless networks are increasingly heterogeneous and spectrum‑constrained. To meet the ever‑growing demand for higher data rates, massive connectivity, and low latency, engineers exploit three complementary dimensions: (i) **spatial multiplexing** through large antenna arrays (massive MIMO), (ii) **non‑orthogonal multiple access** (NOMA) to serve multiple users on the same time‑frequency resource, (iii) **reconfigurable intelligent surfaces** (RIS) that shape the radio environment, and (iv) **network‑controlled relays** that extend coverage and improve link reliability. Together, these techniques form a toolkit for 6G‑era networks where the traditional “cell‑centric” design is being replaced by a “cell‑free” and “environment‑aware” paradigm.

---

### 1. Massive MIMO (Multiple‑Input Multiple‑Output)

**Concept.** Massive MIMO equips a base station (BS) with $M \gg 1$ antennas (typically $M = 64$–$256$) and serves $K$ single‑antenna users simultaneously in the same time‑frequency resource. By exploiting the law of large numbers, the channel vectors become nearly orthogonal, enabling simple linear precoding/combining with near‑optimal performance.

**Key definitions.**  
- $ \mathbf{h}_k \in \mathbb{C}^{M} $: downlink channel vector from the BS to user $k$, modeled as $\mathbf{h}_k \sim \mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\beta_k \mathbf{I}_M)$ where $\beta_k$ is the large‑scale fading coefficient (unitless, often expressed in dB).  
- $ \mathbf{w}_k \in \mathbb{C}^{M}$: precoding vector for user $k$.  
- $P_\text{tot}$: total transmit power of the BS (W).  
- $ \rho = P_\text{tot} / \sigma^2$: transmit SNR, where $\sigma^2$ is the noise variance (W).  

**Downlink received signal** for user $k$:
$$
y_k = \mathbf{h}_k^{\mathrm H}\mathbf{w}_k s_k + \sum_{i\neq k}\mathbf{h}_k^{\mathrm H}\mathbf{w}_i s_i + n_k,
$$
where $s_i$ is the data symbol for user $i$ (unit power) and $n_k\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^2)$.

**Maximum Ratio Transmission (MRT)** (a simple linear precoder):
$$
\mathbf{w}_k = \sqrt{\frac{P_\text{tot}}{K}} \frac{\mathbf{h}_k}{\|\mathbf{h}_k\|}.
$$
With MRT and $M\to\infty$, the inter‑user interference term vanishes, yielding an **asymptotic SINR**:
$$
\text{SINR}_k \xrightarrow[M\to\infty]{} \frac{M\beta_k P_\text{tot}/K}{\sigma^2}.
$$
Thus, the array gain is linear in $M$.

### 2. NOMA (Non‑Orthogonal Multiple Access)

**Concept.** NOMA superposes users’ signals in the power domain and relies on **successive interference cancellation** (SIC) at the receivers. Users with good channel conditions are allocated lower power, while cell‑edge users receive higher power.

**Key definitions.**  
- $P_k$: transmit power allocated to user $k$ (W), with $\sum_{k=1}^{K}P_k = P_\text{tot}$.  
- $|h_k|^2$: instantaneous channel power gain for user $k$ (unitless).  
- Ordering: assume $|h_1|^2 \le |h_2|^2 \le \dots \le |h_K|^2$ (user 1 is the weakest).  

**Received signal** at user $k$:
$$
y_k = h_k\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sqrt{P_i}s_i + n_k.
$$
User $k$ first decodes the signals of users $i<k$ (weaker users) and subtracts them, then decodes its own. The **SINR** for decoding user $j$ at user $k\ge j$ is:
$$
\text{SINR}_{k\to j}= \frac{|h_k|^2 P_j}{|h_k|^2\sum_{i=j+1}^{K}P_i + \sigma^2}.
$$
The achievable rate for user $j$ (bits/s/Hz) is:
$$
R_j = \log_2\bigl(1+\text{SINR}_{j\to j}\bigr).
$$

### 3. RIS (Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface)

**Concept.** An RIS is a planar array of $N$ passive reflecting elements whose reflection coefficients $\theta_n = \beta_n e^{j\phi_n}$ can be tuned electronically. By adjusting the phase shifts $\phi_n$, the RIS creates constructive interference at intended receivers and destructive interference elsewhere.

**Key definitions.**  
- $\mathbf{h}_{\text{BS-RIS}} \in \mathbb{C}^{N}$: channel from BS to RIS.  
- $\mathbf{h}_{\text{RIS-UE}} \in \mathbb{C}^{N}$: channel from RIS to the user equipment (UE).  
- $\mathbf{\Theta}= \operatorname{diag}\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_N\}$: diagonal matrix of element coefficients.  
- $P_\text{BS}$: BS transmit power (W).  

**Effective channel** with RIS assistance:
$$
\mathbf{h}_{\text{eff}} = \mathbf{h}_{\text{direct}} + \mathbf{h}_{\text{RIS-UE}}^{\mathrm H}\mathbf{\Theta}\,\mathbf{h}_{\text{BS-RIS}},
$$
where $\mathbf{h}_{\text{direct}}$ is the direct BS‑UE channel. The received power scales approximately as $N^2$ when phases are aligned, i.e.,
$$
| \mathbf{h}_{\text{RIS-UE}}^{\mathrm H}\mathbf{\Theta}\,\mathbf{h}_{\text{BS-RIS}} |^2 \approx N^2 \,\bar\beta_{\text{BS-RIS}}\bar\beta_{\text{RIS-UE}},
$$
with $\bar\beta$ denoting average small‑scale gain.

### 4. Network‑controlled Relays

**Concept.** A relay node (RN) operates under the control of the network (rather than as an autonomous device) and can be configured for **decode‑and‑forward** (DF) or **amplify‑and‑forward** (AF). In 5G NR, the term *relay node* appears in 3GPP TS 38.300, but future 6G work extends the idea to **intelligent relays** that may incorporate RIS or edge‑computing functions.

**Key definitions (AF case).**  
- $h_{SR}$: channel from source (e.g., BS) to relay (unitless).  
- $h_{RD}$: channel from relay to destination UE.  
- $G$: relay amplification gain (dimensionless).  
- $P_S$: source transmit power (W).  

**End‑to‑end SNR** for AF relay:
$$
\text{SNR}_{\text{AF}} = \frac{P_S |h_{SR}|^2 |h_{RD}|^2 G^2}{G^2 |h_{RD}|^2 \sigma_R^2 + \sigma_D^2},
$$
where $\sigma_R^2$ and $\sigma_D^2$ are noise variances at the relay and destination, respectively. Choosing $G = \sqrt{\frac{P_R}{P_S|h_{SR}|^2+\sigma_R^2}}$ (with $P_R$ the relay’s transmit power) maximizes the SNR under a power constraint.

## Worked Example: Joint Massive MIMO + NOMA in a Small Cell

Consider a BS with $M=128$ antennas serving $K=2$ users in the same time‑frequency slot using NOMA. Large‑scale fading coefficients are $\beta_1 = -80\,$dB (cell‑edge) and $\beta_2 = -60\,$dB (near‑center). Total transmit power $P_\text{tot}=10$ W, noise power $\sigma^2 = -94$ dBm ($=3.98\times10^{-13}$ W). Power allocation follows the NOMA rule: $P_1 = 0.7P_\text{tot}=7$ W, $P_2 = 0.3P_\text{tot}=3$ W.

1. **Massive MIMO array gain** for each user (MRT):
   $$
   G_{\text{array}} = M = 128.
   $$
2. **Effective SNR for user 1** (weak user decodes directly, no SIC):
   $$
   \text{SNR}_1 = \frac{G_{\text{array}} \beta_1 P_1}{\sigma^2}
               = \frac{128 \times 10^{-8} \times 7}{3.98\times10^{-13}}
               \approx 2.25\times10^{4} \;(= 43.5\ \text{dB}).
   $$
   (Here $\beta_1 = 10^{-8}$ because $-80$ dB = $10^{-8}$.)
3. **User 2** first decodes user 1’s signal (SIC). Its SINR for decoding user 1:
   $$
   \text{SINR}_{2\to1}= \frac{G_{\text{array}}\beta_2 P_1}{G_{\text{array}}\beta_2 P_2+\sigma^2}
                     = \frac{128\times10^{-6}\times7}{128\times10^{-6}\times3+3.98\times10^{-13}}
                     \approx \frac{0.896}{0.384}\approx2.33\;(=3.7\ \text{dB}).
   $$
   After successful SIC, user 2’s own SINR:
   $$
   \text{SINR}_{2\to2}= \frac{G_{\text{array}}\beta_2 P_2}{\sigma^2}
                     = \frac{128\times10^{-6}\times3}{3.98\times10^{-13}}
                     \approx 9.66\times10^{5}\;(=59.8\ \text{dB}).
   $$

4. **Achievable rates** (using $R=\log_2(1+\text{SINR})$):
   - $R_1 = \log_2(1+2.25\times10^{4}) \approx 14.5$ bits/s/Hz.
   - $R_2 = \log_2(1+9.66\times10^{5}) \approx 19.9$ bits/s/Hz.

The example shows how massive MIMO’s array gain dramatically improves the SNR of both NOMA users, while the power‑domain multiplexing enables the weak user to achieve a respectable rate without sacrificing the strong user’s performance.

### Common Mistakes & Practical Notes

| Issue | Why it Happens | Remedy |
|------|----------------|--------|
| **Assuming perfect CSI** for massive MIMO precoding | In practice, channel estimation error grows with $M$ and pilot contamination limits performance. | Employ pilot decontamination, covariance‑based precoders, or robust designs that account for estimation error. |
| **Neglecting SIC error propagation** in NOMA | Decoding errors for the weak user’s signal degrade the strong user’s SINR. | Design power allocation with a safety margin; use adaptive modulation and error‑detecting codes before SIC. |
| **Treating RIS elements as lossless** | Real metasurfaces have finite reflection amplitude $\beta_n<1$ and phase quantization (typically 2‑4 bits). | Include an amplitude factor (e.g., $\beta_n=0.8$) and quantization loss in link‑budget calculations. |
| **Overlooking relay latency** in DF relays | Decode‑and‑forward introduces processing delay, which can violate ultra‑low‑latency requirements. | Use AF relays for latency‑critical links or place edge‑computing resources at the relay to parallelize decoding. |
| **Forgetting hardware power consumption** of massive antenna arrays | The radiated power $P_\text{tot}$ is only a part of total energy consumption; RF chains dominate. | Adopt hybrid analog‑digital beamforming or low‑resolution ADCs to reduce RF chain count. |

**Practical design tip:** When integrating these techniques, start from the most restrictive requirement (e.g., latency, coverage, or energy) and select the combination that satisfies it with the lowest added complexity. For instance, in a dense urban micro‑cell, an RIS can replace a costly relay, while massive MIMO already provides sufficient array gain; NOMA may be unnecessary unless the scheduler must serve many low‑rate IoT devices simultaneously.
