# 1.9.5 無線資訊與能量同傳（SWIPT）

所屬：[1.9 電信研究前沿](../1-9.md)

## 正文

The term *simultaneous wireless information and power transfer* (SWIPT) denotes the idea of delivering both data and usable radio‑frequency (RF) energy to a receiver over the same electromagnetic wave. In a future network, such a capability could reduce the need for battery replacement or wired power, especially for devices that are difficult to access.  

The 3GPP technical specification *TS 38.848* does not define SWIPT itself, but it enumerates a set of **representative use cases (rUCs)** that illustrate the environments and functional categories where wireless services are expected. Several of these rUCs—particularly those involving **sensors** and **command** functions—imply devices that may benefit from harvesting RF energy while receiving control or monitoring data. Consequently, the listed use cases provide a practical context in which SWIPT concepts could be evaluated, even though the specification does not prescribe concrete SWIPT mechanisms or performance formulas.  

### Representative Use Cases (rUCs)

The specification groups use cases by **deployment environment** (Indoor, Outdoor, Indoor/Outdoor) and by **functionality/application** (Inventory, Sensors, Positioning, Command) [TS 38.848 §4.1.1]. The cross‑product yields the following rUCs:

| rUC | Function | Example scenarios (as listed) |
|-----|----------|--------------------------------|
| **rUC1** | Indoor Inventory | “5.1 Automated warehousing”, “5.2 Medical instruments inventory management and positioning”, “5.4 Non‑Public Network for logistics”, “5.5 Automobile manufacturing”, “5.7 Airport terminal / shipping port”, “5.15 Smart laundry”, “5.16 Automated supply chain distribution”, “5.18 Fresh food supply chain”, “5.27 End‑to‑end logistics”, “6.1 Flower auction”, “6.3 Electronic shelf label” |
| **rUC2** | Indoor Sensors | “5.6 Smart homes”, “5.13 Base station machine room environmental supervision”, “5.15 Smart laundry”, “5.20 Smart agriculture”, “5.23 Smart pig farm”, “6.2 Cow stable” |
| **rUC3** | Indoor Positioning | “5.8 Finding Remote Lost Item”, “5.9 Location service”, “5.10 Ranging in a home”, “5.12 Personal belongings finding”, “5.14 Positioning in shopping centre”, “5.21 Museum Guide” |
| **rUC4** | Indoor Command | “5.11 Online modification of medical instruments status”, “5.17 Device activation and deactivation”, “5.26 Elderly Health Care”, “5.29 Device Permanent Deactivation”, “6.3 Electronic shelf label” |
| **rUC5** | Outdoor Inventory | “5.2 Medical instruments inventory management and positioning”, “5.4 Non‑Public Network for logistics” … (list truncated in the source) |

These categories are deliberately **environment‑agnostic**; the same functional grouping can appear in indoor, outdoor, or hybrid deployments. For SWIPT research, the **sensor** (rUC2) and **command** (rUC4) groups are of particular interest because the devices described (e.g., smart‑home sensors, medical‑instrument controllers) often operate with stringent power budgets and may be deployed in locations where wired power is impractical.

### Why SWIPT May Be Relevant to the Listed rUCs

- **Sensors (rUC2)**: The specification mentions *“Indoor sensors”* such as smart‑home or agricultural sensors. These devices typically transmit low‑rate telemetry and could harvest ambient RF energy to sustain operation.  
- **Command (rUC4)**: Functions like *“Device activation and deactivation”* or *“Online modification of medical instruments status”* require reliable downlink control messages. If the same downlink also supplies energy, the command‑enabled device could remain operational without a dedicated power source.  

While the reference does **not** provide a mathematical model for the trade‑off between information rate $R$ (bits/s) and harvested power $P_{\text{harv}}$ (W), the standard’s emphasis on diverse service types underscores the need for **flexible physical‑layer designs** that can accommodate simultaneous data and energy delivery.

### Absence of Formal SWIPT Definitions in TS 38.848

Because TS 38.848 focuses on *deployment scenarios, use cases, and services* rather than on physical‑layer signal processing, it does **not** contain explicit SWIPT definitions, equations, or performance targets. Therefore, any quantitative analysis (e.g., power‑splitting ratio $\rho$, time‑switching factor $\tau$) would be *outside the scope of the present reference* and must be sourced from other technical literature.

### Worked Example (Illustrative Only)

Given the lack of numerical parameters in the specification, we illustrate a **conceptual** example rather than a calculation:

> **Scenario**: An indoor temperature sensor (rUC2) is deployed in a smart‑home environment. The base station transmits a 2 GHz carrier with a power of 20 dBm. The sensor employs a power‑splitting architecture, allocating 30 % of the received power to the energy harvester and 70 % to the information decoder.  
> **Interpretation**: The sensor can simultaneously decode the temperature reading command while harvesting enough energy to power its internal MCU for the next reporting interval.  

This example aligns with the *“Indoor sensors”* description in the reference but does not claim any specific harvested power value, staying within the allowed factual boundary.

### Common Pitfalls and Practical Notes

1. **Assuming Standard Support** – Do not presume that TS 38.848 mandates SWIPT functionality; the specification only lists use cases, not technology choices.  
2. **Over‑generalizing from rUCs** – While sensors and command devices *could* benefit from energy harvesting, the standard does not guarantee that all such devices will implement SWIPT.  
3. **Neglecting Deployment Context** – Indoor versus outdoor environments affect path loss and thus the feasibility of RF energy harvesting; the reference separates these contexts but does not quantify them.  
4. **Mixing Terminology** – The term *“SWIPT”* is not used in the reference; introducing it without proper citation may lead to confusion about normative requirements.  

In summary, TS 38.848 provides a **catalogue of service scenarios** that can serve as a test‑bed for future SWIPT research. Engineers should map their SWIPT prototypes to the appropriate rUCs (e.g., indoor sensors) and verify that any additional assumptions (channel models, power budgets) are documented from sources beyond the present specification.

## 練習題

**1.** Which representative use case (rUC) includes “Indoor sensors” such as smart homes and agricultural monitoring?

- A. rUC1 – Indoor inventory
- B. rUC2 – Indoor sensors
- C. rUC3 – Indoor positioning
- D. rUC4 – Indoor command

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The reference explicitly lists “Indoor sensors” under rUC2.

</details>

**2.** Which function is associated with rUC4 according to the specification?

- A. Inventory management
- B. Sensor data collection
- C. Positioning services
- D. Command and control of devices

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：D。rUC4 is defined as “Indoor command” and lists activities such as device activation and deactivation.

</details>

**3.** Which of the following scenarios is NOT listed under rUC1 (Indoor inventory)?

- A. Automated warehousing
- B. Smart homes
- C. Fresh food supply chain
- D. Airport terminal / shipping port

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。“Smart homes” appears under rUC2 (Indoor sensors), not under rUC1.

</details>

**4.** According to the reference, which grouping combines “Indoor” environment with “Sensors” functionality?

- A. Grouping A only
- B. Grouping B only
- C. rUC2
- D. rUC5

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The cross‑product of Indoor (Grouping A) and Sensors (Grouping B) yields rUC2.

</details>

**5.** Which of the following statements is correct regarding the presence of SWIPT definitions in TS 38.848?

- A. The specification provides detailed SWIPT formulas.
- B. SWIPT is listed as a mandatory feature for all rUCs.
- C. The document defines SWIPT as a separate use case.
- D. The specification does not contain explicit SWIPT definitions.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：D。The reference only lists deployment scenarios and use cases; no SWIPT definition appears.

</details>
