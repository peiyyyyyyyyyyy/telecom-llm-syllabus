## 正文

In next‑generation wireless systems, the radio spectrum is a scarce resource that must serve both traditional data traffic and emerging sensing services such as automotive radar, environmental monitoring, and indoor localization. Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) is the paradigm that deliberately designs the physical‑layer waveform, transceiver architecture, and network protocols so that a single radio interface simultaneously delivers high‑rate information transfer **and** extracts useful scene information (range, velocity, angle) from the same electromagnetic (EM) signal.  

Why ISAC matters  

* **Spectrum efficiency** – By reusing the communication waveform for sensing, ISAC eliminates the need for separate radar bands, reducing overall spectrum occupancy.  
* **Hardware consolidation** – A shared antenna array and RF front‑end lower cost, size, and power consumption, which is crucial for mobile devices, UAVs, and vehicular platforms.  
* **Latency and coordination** – Co‑designed waveforms enable real‑time feedback from the sensed environment (e.g., blockage prediction) to the communication scheduler, improving reliability in dynamic scenarios such as mmWave/THz links.  

### 1. Key Definitions  

| Symbol | Definition | Unit |
|--------|------------|------|
| $f_c$ | Carrier frequency | Hz |
| $B$ | Signal bandwidth | Hz |
| $T_p$ | Pulse (or symbol) duration | s |
| $P_t$ | Transmit power (average) | W |
| $G_t$, $G_r$ | Transmit/receive antenna gains | dimensionless (linear) |
| $L$ | Path loss factor (linear) | dimensionless |
| $N_0$ | Noise power spectral density | W/Hz |
| $SNR$ | Signal‑to‑Noise Ratio = $\frac{P_t G_t G_r}{L N_0 B}$ | dimensionless (linear) |
| $R$ | Communication data rate | bit/s |
| $R_{\text{max}} = B \log_2(1+SNR)$ | Shannon capacity (upper bound) | bit/s |
| $\lambda = c/f_c$ | Wavelength (with $c\approx 3\times10^8$ m/s) | m |
| $N$ | Number of antenna elements in a uniform linear array (ULA) | – |
| $d = \lambda/2$ | Inter‑element spacing (commonly) | m |
| $\theta$ | Direction of arrival/departure angle | rad |
| $\tau$ | Propagation delay (time of flight) | s |
| $v$ | Radial velocity of target | m/s |
| $f_D = 2 v / \lambda$ | Doppler shift (monostatic) | Hz |
| $S_{\text{NR}}$ | Sensing SNR (also called radar SNR) | dimensionless |
| $P_{\text{FA}}$, $P_{\text{D}}$ | Probability of false alarm / detection | – |

A monostatic ISAC node (transmitter and receiver co‑located) transmits a known probing waveform $x(t)$ while simultaneously embedding communication symbols $c_k$ (e.g., via phase modulation). The received baseband signal after propagation over a target at range $R$ and velocity $v$ can be modeled as  

$$
y(t) = \alpha \, x(t-\tau) e^{j2\pi f_D t} + n(t),
$$  

where $\alpha = \sqrt{P_t G_t G_r / L}$ is the complex channel gain, $\tau = 2R/c$ the round‑trip delay, $f_D$ the Doppler shift, and $n(t)$ additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) with PSD $N_0$.  

The **sensing SNR** for a coherent integration of $M$ pulses is  

$$
SNR_{\text{radar}} = \frac{|\alpha|^2 P_t G_t G_r}{L N_0 B} \, M .
$$  

The same $SNR$ appears in the communication link, but the **performance trade‑off** arises because waveform parameters that improve radar resolution (large $B$, long $T_p$, high $M$) may reduce spectral efficiency or increase latency for data bits.

### 2. Fundamental Trade‑off Formulas  

1. **Range resolution** (minimum distinguishable distance)  

   $$\Delta R = \frac{c}{2B}.$$  

2. **Velocity resolution** (minimum distinguishable radial speed)  

   $$\Delta v = \frac{\lambda}{2 T_{\text{int}}},$$  

   where $T_{\text{int}} = M T_p$ is the total integration time.  

3. **Angular resolution** for a ULA  

   $$\Delta \theta \approx \frac{2}{N}\quad\text{(in radians for half‑wave spacing)}.$$  

4. **Achievable communication rate under ISAC constraints**  

   If a fraction $\beta\in[0,1]$ of the total power $P_t$ is allocated to data modulation (the remainder devoted to waveform shaping for sensing), the effective SNR for communication becomes  

   $$SNR_{\text{com}} = \beta \frac{P_t G_t G_r}{L N_0 B}.$$

   The corresponding rate is  

   $$R = B \log_2\!\bigl(1 + SNR_{\text{com}}\bigr).$$  

   Simultaneously, the radar SNR scales with $(1-\beta)$ because the same total energy contributes to target illumination.  

### 3. Worked Example  

Consider a vehicular ISAC unit operating at $f_c = 77$ GHz (typical automotive radar) with bandwidth $B = 200$ MHz, transmit power $P_t = 0.5$ W, and a 8‑element ULA ($N=8$, $d=\lambda/2$). The vehicle wishes to simultaneously transmit data at $R\approx 1$ Gbps to a roadside unit (RSU) while detecting a pedestrian at range $R_{\text{ped}} = 30$ m.

**Step 1: Compute wavelength and range resolution**  

$$
\lambda = \frac{c}{f_c}= \frac{3\times10^8}{77\times10^9}\approx 3.90\text{ mm}.
$$

$$
\Delta R = \frac{c}{2B}= \frac{3\times10^8}{2\times 200\times10^6}=0.75\text{ m}.
$$

Thus the system can resolve objects spaced at least 0.75 m apart.

**Step 2: Path loss to pedestrian** (free‑space, $L = (4\pi R_{\text{ped}} f_c /c)^2$)

$$
L = \left(\frac{4\pi \times 30 \times 77\times10^9}{3\times10^8}\right)^2 \approx 1.5\times10^{5}.
$$

Assume isotropic antennas $G_t=G_r=1$ and $N_0 = -174$ dBm/Hz $= 3.98\times10^{-21}$ W/Hz.

**Step 3: Radar SNR for $M=64$ pulses**  

$$
SNR_{\text{radar}} = \frac{P_t}{L N_0 B} M = \frac{0.5}{1.5\times10^{5}\times 3.98\times10^{-21}\times 200\times10^{6}} \times 64 \approx 2.1\times10^{3}\;(=33\text{ dB}).
$$

A 33 dB radar SNR comfortably yields $P_{\text{D}}>0.99$ for $P_{\text{FA}}=10^{-6}$.

**Step 4: Allocate power to communication**  

Let $\beta = 0.4$ (40 % of power for data). Effective communication SNR:

$$
SNR_{\text{com}} = \beta \frac{P_t}{L N_0 B}=0.4 \times \frac{0.5}{1.5\times10^{5}\times 3.98\times10^{-21}\times 200\times10^{6}} \approx 13.2\;(=11.2\text{ dB}).
$$

**Step 5: Compute achievable rate**  

$$
R = B\log_2(1+SNR_{\text{com}})=200\times10^{6}\times \log_2(1+13.2) \approx 200\times10^{6}\times 3.73 \approx 746\text{ Mbps}.
$$

By slightly increasing $B$ or using higher‑order modulation, the target 1 Gbps can be reached while still maintaining >30 dB radar SNR.

### 4. Common Mistakes and Practical Notes  

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Correct Practice |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Treating sensing and communication SNR as independent** | Overlooking that both share the same transmit power, antenna gain, and bandwidth. | Use the power‑allocation factor $\beta$ and ensure the total energy budget satisfies both $SNR_{\text{radar}}$ and $SNR_{\text{com}}$ requirements. |
| **Assuming any modulation is compatible with radar processing** | Some high‑order constellations (e.g., 256‑QAM) introduce rapid phase changes that degrade coherent integration. | Choose modulation schemes with slowly varying envelopes (e.g., PSK, OFDM subcarrier mapping) or embed sensing pilots that are orthogonal to data symbols. |
| **Neglecting mutual interference in multi‑node ISAC networks** | Each ISAC node may act as both a radar illuminator and a communication interferer to others. | Adopt coordinated beamforming or time‑division multiplexing across neighboring ISAC nodes; consider interference‑aware waveform design. |
| **Using overly narrow bandwidth for high‑resolution radar** | Bandwidth directly determines $\Delta R$; a narrow $B$ cannot meet automotive radar standards (~0.1 m). | Verify that $B \ge c/(2\Delta R_{\text{req}})$ before finalizing the waveform. |
| **Ignoring antenna array calibration** | Beamforming errors increase $\Delta\theta$ and reduce $SNR_{\text{radar}}$. | Perform regular array calibration and apply digital beamforming weights that are jointly optimized for sensing and communication. |

**Implementation tip:** Many ISAC prototypes employ OFDM because subcarriers can be assigned distinct roles (some dedicated to radar’s high‑peak‑to‑average‑power requirements, others to data). The cyclic prefix length must be at least the maximum expected round‑trip delay $\tau_{\max}=2R_{\max}/c$ to avoid inter‑symbol interference for the radar echo.
