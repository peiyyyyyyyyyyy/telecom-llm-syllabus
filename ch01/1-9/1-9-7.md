# 1.9.7 6G 願景與研究方向

所屬：[1.9 電信研究前沿](../1-9.md)

## 正文

*Why a new generation?*  
The relentless growth of mobile traffic, the emergence of immersive applications (e.g., holographic telepresence, tactile internet, extended reality), and the convergence of communication, sensing, and computation demand performance levels far beyond 5G. The sixth‑generation (6G) mobile system is envisioned as a *holistic, intelligent, and ultra‑reliable* platform that can deliver **Terabit‑per‑second (Tbps) data rates**, **sub‑millisecond latency**, **$10^{-5}$‑level reliability**, **massive connectivity (10⁷ devices/km²)**, and **energy‑efficient operation** while integrating non‑terrestrial platforms, terahertz (THz) spectrum, and AI‑driven network automation. 

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU‑R) has already published *Recommendation ITU‑R M.2600-0* “Key Technology Areas for 6G” outlining these targets. Translating the vision into engineering practice requires concrete performance indicators, physical‑layer models, and system‑level design frameworks, which are the focus of current 6G research.

### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) and Formal Definitions

| KPI | Symbol | Definition | Target (typical 6G scenario) |
|-----|--------|------------|------------------------------|
| Peak Data Rate | $R_{\text{peak}}$ | Maximum achievable user‑plane throughput (bit/s) | $1$ Tbps per user |
| Bandwidth | $B$ | Allocated spectral width (Hz) | $>100$ GHz (THz bands) |
| Spectral Efficiency | $\eta_{\text{SE}}$ | $ \displaystyle \eta_{\text{SE}} = \frac{R}{B}\;\;[\text{bit/s/Hz}]$ | $>10$ bit/s/Hz (with AI‑assisted coding) |
| Latency | $L$ | End‑to‑end delay from packet generation to successful reception (seconds) | $<0.1$ ms for tactile internet |
| Reliability | $P_{\text{succ}}$ | Probability of successful packet delivery; equivalently $P_{\text{err}} = 1-P_{\text{succ}}$ | $P_{\text{err}} < 10^{-5}$ |
| Energy Efficiency | $\eta_{\text{EE}}$ | $ \displaystyle \eta_{\text{EE}} = \frac{R}{P_{\text{tot}}}\;\;[\text{bit/J}]$ | $>10^{5}$ bit/J (ultra‑low‑power IoT) |
| Area Capacity | $C_{\text{area}}$ | Aggregate throughput per unit area $C_{\text{area}} = \frac{\sum_i R_i}{\text{Area}}$ | $>10$ Gbps/m² |

All symbols are expressed in the International System of Units (SI): $R$ in bit s⁻¹, $B$ in Hz, $L$ in s, $P_{\text{tot}}$ in W, and area in m².

### Fundamental Theoretical Limits

1. **Shannon Capacity in Wideband Regime**  
   For a single‑input single‑output (SISO) AWGN channel with bandwidth $B$ and received signal‑to‑noise ratio $\gamma$, the maximum achievable rate is  

   $$
   C = B \log_2(1+\gamma)\;\;[\text{bit/s}].
   $$  

   In the THz regime, $\gamma$ is limited by molecular absorption; the *effective* SNR becomes  

   $$
   \gamma_{\text{eff}} = \frac{P_{\text{tx}} G_t G_r}{N_0 B} \, e^{-\kappa(f) d},
   $$  

   where $P_{\text{tx}}$ (W) is transmit power, $G_t$, $G_r$ are antenna gains (dimensionless), $N_0$ (W/Hz) is noise PSD, $\kappa(f)$ (m⁻¹) is frequency‑dependent absorption coefficient, and $d$ (m) is link distance.

2. **Massive MIMO Scaling Law**  
   For a base station equipped with $M$ antennas serving $K$ single‑antenna users (with $M\gg K$), the *asymptotic* spectral efficiency per user under zero‑forcing (ZF) precoding is  

   $$
   \eta_{\text{SE}}^{\text{ZF}} \approx \log_2\!\left(1 + \frac{P_{\text{tx}} (M-K)}{K \sigma^2}\right),
   $$  

   where $\sigma^2$ is the noise variance per antenna. This shows a linear increase with $M$ up to the point where hardware impairments dominate.

3. **Cell‑Free Distributed MIMO**  
   When $L$ access points (APs) cooperate without cell boundaries, the received signal for user $k$ is  

   $$
   y_k = \sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \mathbf{h}_{k,\ell}^{\mathrm{H}} \mathbf{w}_{\ell} s_k + n_k,
   $$  

   with $\mathbf{h}_{k,\ell}$ the channel vector (dimension $N_\ell$), $\mathbf{w}_{\ell}$ the precoder, $s_k$ the transmitted symbol, and $n_k\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^2)$. The *macro‑diversity* gain reduces the required transmit power dramatically, a key enabler for ultra‑low‑energy 6G nodes.

### Representative Research Directions

| Area | Core Idea | Representative Challenges |
|------|-----------|----------------------------|
| **Terahertz (0.1–10 THz) Radio** | Exploit ultra‑wide bandwidth for Tbps links. | Severe molecular absorption, hardware (sources/detectors), beamforming at sub‑mm wavelengths. |
| **Extreme Massive MIMO (eMIMO)** | Deploy thousands of antenna elements on planar or conformal arrays. | Calibration, mutual coupling, scalable signal processing, power consumption. |
| **Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS)** | Programmable metasurfaces reshape propagation environment. | Real‑time phase‑shift control, channel estimation with passive elements, integration with AI. |
| **AI‑Native Network Architecture** | End‑to‑end learning loops for resource allocation, topology control, and fault diagnosis. | Data privacy (federated learning), model generalization across heterogeneous nodes, inference latency. |
| **Cell‑Free & Distributed MIMO** | Eliminate cell edges by joint processing of many distributed APs. | Fronthaul capacity, synchronization, scalable cooperation protocols. |
| **Orbital Angular Momentum (OAM) Multiplexing** | Use orthogonal OAM modes to increase spatial streams. | Mode purity over turbulence, mode detection hardware. |
| **Quantum‑Assisted Communications** | Leverage quantum key distribution (QKD) and quantum repeaters for ultimate security. | Integration with classical THz links, decoherence, cost. |
| **Integrated Sensing‑Communication (ISAC)** | Simultaneously use the same waveform for radar‑like sensing and data transmission. | Trade‑off between sensing resolution and data rate, waveform design. |
| **Energy‑Harvesting & SWIPT Extensions** | Combine information and power transfer at THz frequencies. | Rectenna efficiency, safety regulations, coexistence with data traffic. |

These topics are not isolated; e.g., AI‑driven RIS control, cell‑free massive MIMO combined with THz front‑ends, and ISAC waveforms that embed OAM modes are active interdisciplinary research fronts.

### Worked Example

**Goal:** Determine the minimum bandwidth required to support a single‑user peak data rate of $R_{\text{peak}} = 2$ Tbps assuming an achievable spectral efficiency of $\eta_{\text{SE}} = 12$ bit/s/Hz (realistic with AI‑optimized coding and eMIMO).

**Solution:**  

The relationship $R = \eta_{\text{SE}} B$ gives  

$$
B = \frac{R_{\text{peak}}}{\eta_{\text{SE}}}
   = \frac{2 \times 10^{12}\ \text{bit/s}}{12\ \text{bit/s/Hz}}
   \approx 1.67 \times 10^{11}\ \text{Hz}
   = 167\ \text{GHz}.
$$

Thus, a **bandwidth of at least 167 GHz** is needed, which lies well within the lower THz spectrum (0.3–1 THz).  

If the transmitter can deliver $P_{\text{tx}} = 0.5$ W, antenna gains $G_t = G_r = 30$ dBi (linear factor $10^{3}$), noise PSD $N_0 = 10^{-21}$ W/Hz, link distance $d = 20$ m, and absorption coefficient $\kappa(0.5\text{ THz}) \approx 0.2$ m⁻¹, the effective SNR becomes  

$$
\gamma_{\text{eff}} = \frac{0.5 \times 10^{3} \times 10^{3}}{10^{-21} \times 1.67\times10^{11}} e^{-0.2\times20}
                 \approx \frac{5\times10^{5}}{1.67\times10^{-10}} e^{-4}
                 \approx 3.0\times10^{15} \times 0.018
                 \approx 5.4\times10^{13},
$$

or about $137$ dB, far exceeding the Shannon requirement for 12 bit/s/Hz. In practice, hardware nonlinearities and beam misalignment will dominate, illustrating why *hardware‑aware* modeling is a central 6G research theme.

### Common Mistakes and Practical Notes

| Pitfall | Why it Happens | Correct Approach |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Assuming ideal Shannon capacity** | Neglects molecular absorption, phase noise, and hardware impairments at THz. | Incorporate frequency‑dependent loss $\exp[-\kappa(f)d]$ and realistic transceiver noise figures. |
| **Over‑estimating spectral efficiency** | Uses 5G‑level coding gains for THz links without considering limited modulation bandwidth and channel sparsity. | Perform link‑level simulations with AI‑generated constellations and account for channel sparsity. |
| **Ignoring synchronization overhead in AI‑driven control** | Treats learning latency as negligible. | Model the control loop delay $L_{\text{ctrl}}$ and ensure $L_{\text{ctrl}} \ll$ target $L$ (e.g., $<10\,\mu$s). |
| **Treating RIS as passive mirrors only** | Omits the need for channel estimation through the surface. | Design joint training protocols that estimate the *cascaded* BS–RIS–UE channel. |
| **Scaling massive MIMO without power budgeting** | More antennas automatically imply higher EE. | Use the EE formula $\eta_{\text{EE}} = R/P_{\text{tot}}$ and include RF chain power $P_{\text{RF}} = M\cdot P_{\text{chain}}$. |

Finally, **system integration** is the decisive factor for 6G success. Engineers must co‑design RF front‑ends, digital baseband, AI algorithms, and network protocols under realistic constraints (hardware, regulation, and economics). The vision of a fully immersive, AI‑native, and sustainable 6G society will be realized only through such multidisciplinary, rigorously validated research.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following expressions correctly captures the Shannon capacity for a wideband THz link that includes molecular absorption loss?

- A. $C = B \log_2(1+\gamma)$
- B. $C = B \log_2\!\bigl(1+\frac{P_{\text{tx}}}{N_0 B}\bigr)$
- C. $C = B \log_2\!\bigl(1+\frac{P_{\text{tx}} G_t G_r}{N_0 B} e^{-\kappa(f)d}\bigr)$
- D. $C = \frac{B}{\ln 2}\bigl(\gamma - \ln(1+\gamma)\bigr)$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Option C augments the classic SNR term with the exponential absorption factor $e^{-\kappa(f)d}$, which is required for THz propagation.

</details>

**2.** In a cell‑free massive MIMO system with $L$ distributed APs, the received signal for user $k$ is $y_k = \sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \mathbf{h}_{k,\ell}^{\mathrm{H}} \mathbf{w}_{\ell} s_k + n_k$. Which term represents the *macro‑diversity* gain?

- A. $\mathbf{h}_{k,\ell}^{\mathrm{H}}$
- B. $\mathbf{w}_{\ell}$
- C. The summation over $\ell$
- D. $n_k$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Summing contributions from many APs provides macro‑diversity, reducing required transmit power and improving reliability.

</details>

**3.** A 6G link aims for a peak data rate of 2 Tbps with an achievable spectral efficiency of 12 bit/s/Hz. What minimum bandwidth is required?

- A. 24 GHz
- B. 166.7 GHz
- C. 2 THz
- D. 12 GHz

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Using $B = R/\eta_{\text{SE}} = 2\times10^{12}/12 \approx 1.67\times10^{11}$ Hz = 166.7 GHz.

</details>

**4.** Which KPI directly quantifies the energy cost per transmitted bit?

- A. Latency $L$
- B. Spectral efficiency $\eta_{\text{SE}}$
- C. Energy efficiency $\eta_{\text{EE}} = R/P_{\text{tot}}$
- D. Area capacity $C_{\text{area}}$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Energy efficiency is defined as bits transmitted per joule; its inverse gives energy per bit.

</details>

**5.** A common mistake in early 6G studies is to assume that increasing the number of antenna elements $M$ linearly improves energy efficiency without limit. Why is this assumption flawed?

- A. Because spectral efficiency saturates at $M=K$ regardless of hardware.
- B. Because each antenna adds a fixed RF‑chain power consumption, reducing overall EE.
- C. Because molecular absorption eliminates the benefit of many antennas.
- D. Because AI algorithms cannot process more than 100 antennas.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Adding antennas increases total power $P_{\text{tot}}$ (e.g., $M\cdot P_{\text{chain}}$), so EE eventually degrades despite higher $R$.

</details>
