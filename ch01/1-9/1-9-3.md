# 1.9.3 毫米波（FR2）與高頻通道；太赫茲

所屬：[1.9 電信研究前沿](../1-9.md)

## 正文

Modern 5G and prospective 6G systems push carrier frequencies into the millimeter‑wave (mmWave) range (FR2, 24 GHz – 52 GHz) and even towards the terahertz (THz) band (> 100 GHz). At such high frequencies the wavelength λ becomes very small (e.g., λ ≈ 5 mm at 60 GHz), which enables the deployment of dense antenna arrays on a compact physical aperture. Large‑scale antenna arrays provide the narrow beams required to overcome the severe free‑space path loss that dominates mmWave/THz links, and they are the cornerstone of beamforming, massive MIMO, and reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) techniques.  

The 3GPP specification **TS 36.897 §5.2.1** formalises the antenna‑array model that is used for performance evaluation of FR2 and higher‑frequency deployments. The model is a **2‑D planar uniformly spaced array** described by the tuple **(M, N, P)**:

* $M$ – number of antenna elements that share the same polarization **in each column**.  
* $N$ – **number of columns** of the planar array.  
* $P$ – **polarization dimensions** (e.g., $P=2$ for cross‑polarisation, $P=1$ for co‑polarisation).  

The element spacing is defined separately in the horizontal and vertical directions:

* Horizontal spacing $d_{\mathrm H}$ = **0.5 λ** (half‑wavelength) – this spacing is the same as in the reference [TS 36.897 §5.2.1].  
* Vertical spacing $d_{\mathrm V}$ and the corresponding $M$ are given as pairs: **(0.8 λ, 8)** or **(0.5 λ, 4)**, the latter being used for 3‑D urban macro (UMa) and micro (UMi) scenarios at 3.5 GHz.  

The **maximum directional gain** of a single antenna element is specified as **$G_{\mathrm{E,max}} = 8\,$dBi**.  

A practical design constraint imposed by the specification is  

$$
\max(M \times N) = 32,
$$

and when the number of columns reaches $N=16$, the vertical dimension is limited to $M=2$.

---

### Key Definitions and Simple Formulas

* **Total number of radiating ports** (including polarization)  

$$
N_{\text{ports}} = M \times N \times P.
$$

* **Array aperture size** (assuming uniform spacing)  

$$
L_{\text{H}} = (N-1) \, d_{\mathrm H},\qquad
L_{\text{V}} = (M-1) \, d_{\mathrm V}.
$$

* **Half‑power beamwidth (approximate)** for a uniform linear array in one dimension  

$$
\theta_{3\text{dB}} \approx \frac{2\lambda}{L},
$$

where $L$ is the physical length of the array in the considered dimension. This expression follows directly from the standard array factor theory and is compatible with the half‑wavelength spacing defined above.

All symbols are dimensionless unless otherwise noted; $λ$ is the wavelength (metre), $d_{\mathrm H}$ and $d_{\mathrm V}$ are expressed in metres (or as a multiple of $λ$), and $G_{\mathrm{E,max}}$ is in decibels‑isotropic (dBi).

---

### Worked Example

**Problem:**  
A base‑station operating at 28 GHz (λ ≈ 10.7 mm) uses a planar array with $N=4$ columns, $M=4$ elements per column, and cross‑polarisation ($P=2$). Compute:

1. The total number of antenna ports.  
2. The horizontal aperture $L_{\text{H}}$ in millimetres.  
3. The approximate half‑power beamwidth in the horizontal plane.

**Solution:**  

1. $N_{\text{ports}} = M \times N \times P = 4 \times 4 \times 2 = 32$ ports, which respects the $\max(M\! \times\! N)=32$ limit.  

2. Horizontal spacing $d_{\mathrm H}=0.5λ = 0.5 \times 10.7\text{ mm}=5.35\text{ mm}$.  
   $L_{\text{H}} = (N-1)d_{\mathrm H}= (4-1)\times5.35\text{ mm}=16.05\text{ mm}$.  

3. Using the approximate beamwidth formula  

$$
\theta_{3\text{dB}} \approx \frac{2λ}{L_{\text{H}}}
= \frac{2\times10.7\text{ mm}}{16.05\text{ mm}}
\approx 1.33\ \text{rad} \approx 76^{\circ}.
$$

Thus the array provides a relatively wide horizontal beam, which could be narrowed further by increasing $N$ or reducing $d_{\mathrm H}$ (subject to the half‑wavelength constraint).

---

### Practical Notes and Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Correct Approach |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Confusing $N$ with the number of rows** | The 2‑D model uses *columns* as the horizontal dimension; many textbooks describe rows first. | Remember that $N$ is explicitly defined as “the number of columns” in the specification. |
| **Omitting the polarization factor $P$** | $P$ is optional (co‑pol vs. cross‑pol) and sometimes ignored in early calculations. | Multiply by $P$ when counting ports; $P=2$ doubles the number of RF chains required for full cross‑polarisation. |
| **Using spacing other than $0.5λ$ horizontally** | Designers may think finer spacing yields better performance. | The standard mandates $d_{\mathrm H}=0.5λ$ for the evaluated scenarios; altering it invalidates comparison with the reference results. |
| **Exceeding the $M\times N$ limit** | Adding more elements to improve gain without checking the product constraint. | Verify that $M\times N\le 32$; if $N=16$, enforce $M=2$ as required. |
| **Assuming $G_{\mathrm{E,max}}$ scales with array size** | Believing the element gain increases with the number of elements. | $G_{\mathrm{E,max}}$ is a property of a *single* antenna element (8 dBi) and remains constant; array gain comes from beamforming, not from changing $G_{\mathrm{E,max}}$. |

Finally, the reference text does **not** provide propagation models, hardware impairments, or regulatory limits for the THz band. Those topics lie outside the scope of this subsection and should be consulted in dedicated standards or research papers.
