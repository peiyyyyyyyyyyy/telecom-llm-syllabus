# 1.6.5 資源使用率：PRB、處理器、傳輸

## 正文

In a modern cellular system the operator must constantly balance three “resource planes”: the radio‑access plane (how many physical resource blocks – PRBs – are occupied), the compute plane (how much base‑station processing power is consumed), and the transport plane (how much back‑haul or fronthaul capacity is used).  Resource utilization metrics translate raw measurements into percentages that can be compared across cells, vendors, and time‑scales, enabling capacity planning, energy‑saving algorithms, and SLA verification.  An overload in any one plane will manifest as reduced throughput, higher latency, or increased call drops, even if the other planes appear healthy.

Below we define the three most common utilization indicators, give the standard formulas, and illustrate a typical calculation.

---

### 1. PRB Utilization (Radio‑Access Plane)

A **Physical Resource Block (PRB)** is the smallest unit of time‑frequency that can be scheduled in LTE and NR.  
- In LTE, one PRB = 12 sub‑carriers × 0.5 ms (one OFDM symbol duration) → 180 kHz × 0.5 ms.  
- In NR, the PRB bandwidth depends on the sub‑carrier spacing (SCS) but the definition (12 sub‑carriers) remains.

The **PRB utilization** for a given measurement interval (e.g., one 1‑ms sub‑frame) is

$$
U_{\text{PRB}} = \frac{N_{\text{used}}}{N_{\text{total}}}\times 100\;[\%],
$$

where  

* $N_{\text{used}}$ = number of PRBs actually assigned to traffic (including control overhead) during the interval,  
* $N_{\text{total}}$ = total number of PRBs available on the carrier (depends on bandwidth and SCS).

Averages over longer periods (e.g., 1 s, 1 min) are obtained by time‑averaging $U_{\text{PRB}}$.

---

### 2. Processor (CPU) Utilization (Compute Plane)

Base‑station processing includes PHY‑layer baseband operations, MAC scheduling, RRC signaling, and higher‑layer functions.  It is usually measured in **CPU cycles per second** (or MIPS/GPIPS) and expressed as a percentage of the installed processing capacity.

$$
U_{\text{CPU}} = \frac{C_{\text{used}}}{C_{\text{max}}}\times 100\;[\%],
$$

where  

* $C_{\text{used}}$ = CPU cycles actually consumed during the measurement window (e.g., 1 s),  
* $C_{\text{max}}$ = total cycles the processor can deliver in the same window (core frequency × number of cores).

If the processor is a multi‑core DSP or a general‑purpose CPU, $C_{\text{max}}$ is the sum of all cores’ capacities.  In practice, vendors expose $U_{\text{CPU}}$ via OAM interfaces (e.g., 3GPP TS 32.401 performance management).

---

### 3. Transport Utilization (Back‑haul / Fronthaul Plane)

The transport link carries the aggregated user‑plane data from the gNB/eNB to the core network.  Utilization is defined as the ratio of the **effective traffic bit rate** to the **nominal link capacity**.

$$
U_{\text{TX}} = \frac{R_{\text{traffic}}}{R_{\text{link}}}\times 100\;[\%],
$$

where  

* $R_{\text{traffic}}$ = measured aggregate payload rate (including protocol overhead) over the interval,  
* $R_{\text{link}}$ = advertised raw capacity of the transport interface (e.g., 100 Mbps Ethernet, 10 Gbps fiber).  

Transport utilization is often monitored per direction (UL/DL) because asymmetry is common in mobile traffic.

---

### Worked Example

Consider a 20 MHz LTE carrier (bandwidth 20 MHz, 15 kHz sub‑carrier spacing).  
*Number of PRBs*: $N_{\text{total}} = \frac{20\ \text{MHz}}{180\ \text{kHz}} \approx 111$, rounded to the standard 100 PRBs for 20 MHz.  

During a 1‑second observation:

| Metric | Measured Value | Capacity / Reference | Formula | Result |
|--------|----------------|----------------------|---------|--------|
| PRBs used (average) | $N_{\text{used}} = 30$ PRBs per sub‑frame | $N_{\text{total}} = 100$ PRBs | $U_{\text{PRB}} = \frac{30}{100}\times100$ | **30 %** |
| CPU cycles consumed | $C_{\text{used}} = 1.5\times10^{9}$ cycles/s | $C_{\text{max}} = 10\times10^{9}$ cycles/s (10 GHz core) | $U_{\text{CPU}} = \frac{1.5}{10}\times100$ | **15 %** |
| Traffic rate | $R_{\text{traffic}} = 25$ Mbps (including IP/PDCP overhead) | $R_{\text{link}} = 100$ Mbps Ethernet | $U_{\text{TX}} = \frac{25}{100}\times100$ | **25 %** |

Interpretation: The radio plane is the bottleneck (30 % PRB usage) relative to the compute (15 %) and transport (25 %).  If traffic grows, the PRB utilization will reach the 80–90 % threshold where scheduling latency spikes, even though the processor and back‑haul still have headroom.

---

### Practical Notes & Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it Happens | Remedy |
|---------|----------------|--------|
| **Counting only data PRBs** | Control channels (PDCCH, PHICH) also consume PRBs; omitting them under‑estimates $U_{\text{PRB}}$. | Include all scheduled PRBs, or add a fixed control‑overhead factor (≈ 10 %). |
| **Using peak CPU frequency as $C_{\text{max}}$** | Turbo‑boost or dynamic scaling means the core may not sustain the peak continuously. | Use the *sustained* rated frequency or the vendor‑specified maximum MIPS for the measurement interval. |
| **Neglecting protocol overhead in $R_{\text{traffic}}$** | Ethernet framing, IP headers, and PDCP/ROHC compression add up to 10–20 % extra traffic. | Measure the *actual* line rate on the transport interface (e.g., via SNMP) rather than the payload alone. |
| **Averaging over too long a window** | Short spikes (e.g., bursty video) can be hidden, leading to an overly optimistic utilization view. | Complement long‑term averages with 1‑s or sub‑second histograms to capture peak loads. |
| **Assuming PRB utilization directly maps to user throughput** | Scheduling efficiency, MCS, and retransmissions affect the bits per PRB. | Correlate $U_{\text{PRB}}$ with CQI/SINR statistics to assess spectral efficiency. |

By consistently measuring and correlating these three utilization dimensions, engineers can pinpoint the true limiting factor, apply targeted upgrades (e.g., more spectrum, higher‑performance DSPs, or larger back‑haul links), and maintain the network within the design thresholds defined in 3GPP specifications (e.g., TS 36.214 for LTE PRB allocation and TS 38.214 for NR).
