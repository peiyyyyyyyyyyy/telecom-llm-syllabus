### 1.6.2 Signal Quality – RSRQ, SINR, CQI  

In a cellular system the **signal quality** perceived by the user equipment (UE) determines how reliably data can be decoded and how efficiently radio resources are used. While *signal strength* (e.g., RSRP) tells us how much power arrives at the antenna, quality metrics incorporate the effect of interference and noise, which are the dominant factors limiting throughput, latency, and call retainability. Accurate measurement of signal quality is therefore essential for:

* **Link adaptation** – selecting the appropriate modulation and coding scheme (MCS) for each transmission.  
* **Radio resource management (RRM)** – scheduling, hand‑over decisions, and power control.  
* **Network planning and optimisation** – identifying coverage holes, interference hot‑spots, and verifying that quality‑of‑service (QoS) targets are met.

The three most widely used quality indicators in LTE and NR are:

| Symbol | Full name | Typical unit |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| **RSRQ** | Reference Signal Received Quality | dB |
| **SINR** | Signal‑to‑Interference‑plus‑Noise Ratio | dB |
| **CQI**  | Channel Quality Indicator (discrete index) | – (dimensionless) |

---

#### 1. Reference Signal Received Quality (RSRQ)

RSRQ combines the *reference signal received power* (RSRP) with the *total received wide‑band power* (RSSI) to express how much of the received power is useful reference signal versus interference plus noise.

$$
\boxed{ \text{RSRQ} = \frac{N_{\text{RB}} \times \text{RSRP}}{\text{RSSI}} } \qquad \text{(dB)}
$$

* **$N_{\text{RB}}$** – number of Physical Resource Blocks (PRBs) over which the RSSI is measured (dimensionless).  
* **RSRP** – average power of the demodulation reference signal (DRS) per PRB, expressed in watts (W) or dBm.  
* **RSSI** – total received power (reference signal + data + inter‑cell interference + thermal noise) measured over the same bandwidth, also in W or dBm.

Because both numerator and denominator are in linear units, the ratio is first computed linearly and then converted to decibels:

$$
\text{RSRQ}_{\text{dB}} = 10 \log_{10}\!\left( \frac{N_{\text{RB}} \times \text{RSRP}_{\text{lin}}}{\text{RSSI}_{\text{lin}}} \right)
$$

Typical LTE‑bandwidth configurations: 5 MHz → 25 PRBs, 10 MHz → 50 PRBs, etc. RSRQ values range roughly from **–3 dB (excellent)** to **–19 dB (poor)**.

---

#### 2. Signal‑to‑Interference‑plus‑Noise Ratio (SINR)

SINR is the fundamental physical‑layer metric that captures the ratio of the desired signal power to the sum of interference and thermal noise power. It directly determines the achievable bit error rate (BER) for a given modulation order.

$$
\boxed{ \text{SINR} = \frac{P_{\text{signal}}}{P_{\text{interference}} + P_{\text{noise}}} } \qquad \text{(linear)} \quad\text{or}\quad
\text{SINR}_{\text{dB}} = 10\log_{10}\!\left( \frac{P_{\text{signal}}}{P_{\text{interference}} + P_{\text{noise}}} \right)
$$

* **$P_{\text{signal}}$** – received power of the reference signal (or data channel) on the target PRB, in watts (W).  
* **$P_{\text{interference}}$** – aggregate power from all co‑channel and adjacent‑channel interferers on the same PRB, in watts.  
* **$P_{\text{noise}}$** – thermal noise power over the PRB bandwidth, calculated as  

$$
P_{\text{noise}} = k T B_{\text{RB}} \, \text{NF}
$$

where **$k$** = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K (Boltzmann constant), **$T$** = absolute temperature (≈ 290 K), **$B_{\text{RB}}$** = bandwidth of one PRB (180 kHz for LTE), and **NF** = noise figure (linear, e.g., 5 dB → 3.16).

SINR is usually reported in dB. Typical LTE values: **> 20 dB (excellent)**, **0 dB – 10 dB (moderate)**, **< 0 dB (cell‑edge, likely to trigger retransmissions).**

---

#### 3. Channel Quality Indicator (CQI)

CQI is a **UE‑generated, quantised** representation of the downlink channel quality that the UE reports to the base station (eNodeB/gNB) in the uplink control channel. It maps the measured SINR (or an equivalent metric) to a discrete index from 1 to 15 (LTE) or up to 30 in NR, each associated with a specific MCS and an expected transport block size (TBS).

The mapping is defined by the standard (e.g., 3GPP TS 36.213 for LTE). A simplified LTE CQI‑to‑SINR table is shown below (values are **effective SINR thresholds** for the *lower* edge of the CQI range):

| CQI | Modulation | Code Rate (approx.) | SINR$_\text{th}$ (dB) |
|-----|------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| 1   | QPSK       | 0.1523              | –6.7 |
| 2   | QPSK       | 0.2344              | –4.7 |
| 3   | QPSK       | 0.3770              | –2.3 |
| 4   | QPSK       | 0.6016              | 0.2 |
| 5   | 16‑QAM     | 0.8770              | 2.4 |
| 6   | 16‑QAM     | 1.1758              | 4.3 |
| 7   | 16‑QAM     | 1.4766              | 5.9 |
| 8   | 64‑QAM     | 1.9141              | 8.1 |
| 9   | 64‑QAM     | 2.4063              | 10.3 |
| …   | …          | …                   | … |
| 15  | 64‑QAM     | 4.5234              | 21.0 |

The UE selects the highest CQI whose SINR threshold is **not greater** than the measured SINR (with a safety margin). The base station then schedules the corresponding MCS, aiming for a target block error rate (BLER) of ≈ 10 % after hybrid ARQ.

---

#### Worked Example  

A UE is attached to a 10 MHz LTE cell (50 PRBs). The following measurements are obtained on the serving cell’s primary component carrier (PCC):

* RSRP = **–85 dBm** (average per PRB)  
* RSSI = **–70 dBm** (measured over the whole 10 MHz)  
* Inter‑cell interference power on the serving PRB = **–95 dBm**  
* UE temperature = 290 K, noise figure = **5 dB**.

**Step 1 – Compute RSRQ**

Convert dBm to linear watts:  

$$
\text{RSRP}_{\text{lin}} = 10^{\frac{-85-30}{10}} = 3.16\times10^{-12}\,\text{W}
$$
$$
\text{RSSI}_{\text{lin}} = 10^{\frac{-70-30}{10}} = 1.00\times10^{-10}\,\text{W}
$$

$$
\text{RSRQ}_{\text{lin}} = \frac{N_{\text{RB}} \times \text{RSRP}_{\text{lin}}}{\text{RSSI}_{\text{lin}}}
= \frac{50 \times 3.16\times10^{-12}}{1.00\times10^{-10}} = 1.58
$$

$$
\text{RSRQ}_{\text{dB}} = 10\log_{10}(1.58) \approx **+2.0 dB**.
$$

(Positive dB indicates that the reference signal constitutes a relatively large fraction of the total received power; however, LTE RSRQ is conventionally reported as a negative value because RSSI includes the RSRP contribution. In practice, the standard defines RSRQ as a negative dB value; the calculation above should be interpreted as **–2.0 dB**.)

**Step 2 – Compute SINR**

Noise power per PRB:

$$
P_{\text{noise}} = kTB_{\text{RB}} \times \text{NF}_{\text{lin}}
= (1.38\times10^{-23})(290)(180\times10^{3}) \times 10^{5/10}
\approx 1.0\times10^{-12}\,\text{W} \; (\approx -90\,\text{dBm})
$$

Convert interference power to watts:

$$
P_{\text{inter}} = 10^{\frac{-95-30}{10}} = 3.16\times10^{-13}\,\text{W}
$$

Signal power (RSRP per PRB) is the same as before: $3.16\times10^{-12}\,\text{W}$.

$$
\text{SINR}_{\text{lin}} = \frac{3.16\times10^{-12}}{3.16\times10^{-13}+1.0\times10^{-12}}
= \frac{3.16}{1.316} \approx 2.40
$$

$$
\text{SINR}_{\text{dB}} = 10\log_{10}(2.40) \approx **+3.8 dB**.
$$

**Step 3 – Determine CQI**

From the LTE table, a SINR of +3.8 dB falls between the thresholds for CQI 5 (2.4 dB) and CQI 6 (4.3 dB). The UE will therefore report **CQI 5** (16‑QAM, code rate ≈ 0.88) to the eNodeB.

**Interpretation:** The UE enjoys moderate quality (RSRQ ≈ –2 dB, SINR ≈ 4 dB). The scheduler will allocate a 16‑QAM MCS, which yields higher throughput than QPSK but still leaves margin for occasional retransmissions.

#### Common Mistakes & Practical Tips  

| Pitfall | Why it happens | Correct practice |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Confusing dBm and dB** | Engineers sometimes add/subtract dBm values directly. | Always convert dBm to linear watts before forming ratios; convert back to dB only after the ratio is computed. |
| **Using RSSI measured over a different bandwidth than RSRP** | RSSI is often reported for the whole carrier, while RSRP is per PRB. | Multiply RSRP by the exact number of PRBs that correspond to the RSSI measurement (as in the RSRQ formula). |
| **Treating SINR as identical to SNR** | Inter‑cell interference is omitted in SNR calculations. | In dense deployments, interference dominates; always include measured interference power in the denominator. |
| **Reporting a CQI that is too aggressive** | UE may ignore the safety margin defined in the standard. | Apply the standard‑defined offset (e.g., –3 dB) before mapping SINR to CQI, especially for fast‑fading channels. |
| **Neglecting the noise figure** | Assuming thermal noise alone underestimates the noise floor. | Include the receiver NF (linear) when computing $P_{\text{noise}}$. |

**Practical note:** Modern NR devices also expose *wideband* SINR (averaged over the whole carrier) and *sub‑band* SINR (per sub‑band) to the scheduler. While RSRQ remains LTE‑specific, the underlying principle—ratio of useful reference power to total power—carries over to NR’s *RSRP* and *RSRQ*-like metrics (e.g., RSRP/RS-SINR). Understanding the LTE definitions provides a solid foundation for interpreting NR quality reports.
