# 1.6.7 移動性指標：換手成功率

所屬：[1.6 指標與名詞](../1-6.md)

## 正文

In mobile radio systems a *handover* (also called *handoff*) transfers an active radio connection from one base station (gNB in NR) to another while the user equipment (UE) remains in service. The *handover success rate* quantifies how reliably the network performs this transfer. A high success rate is essential for maintaining call continuity, preserving quality of experience, and meeting service‑level agreements, especially for latency‑sensitive applications such as voice over IP or real‑time video.

### Definition and KPI Names

3GPP defines several handover‑related KPIs in TS 28.554 §6.6:

| KPI (code) | Scope | Description |
|------------|-------|-------------|
| **GRANHOSR** | NG‑RAN (NR) handover (single handover type) | “A KPI that shows how often a handover within NR‑RAN is successful … This KPI is obtained by successful handovers to the same or another gNB divided by attempted handovers to the same or another gNB.” |
| **GRANHOSRA** | NG‑RAN handover (all handover types) | “A KPI that shows how often a handover within NR‑RAN is successful … This KPI covers legacy Handover, Conditional Handover, DAPS Handover and LTM cell switch.” |
| **5GSEPSHOSR** | 5GS → EPS handover | “A KPI that shows how often a handover from 5GS to EPS is successful … This KPI is obtained by successful handovers from 5GS to EPS system divided by the total number of handovers attempt's from 5GS to EPS system.” |

All three KPIs are measured per **SubNetwork, NRCellCU** (or per SubNetwork for slice‑specific KPIs)【TS 28.554 §6.6.1‑d】【TS 28.554 §6.6.4‑d】【TS 28.554 §6.6.5‑d】.

### Formal Expression

For a given measurement interval, let  

* $H_{\text{succ}}$ = number of successful handovers (to the same or another gNB)  
* $H_{\text{att}}$ = number of attempted handovers (to the same or another gNB)  

The **handover success rate** $S_{\text{HO}}$ is  

$$
S_{\text{HO}} = \frac{H_{\text{succ}}}{H_{\text{att}}}\times 100\%
$$

The same formula applies to the 5GS → EPS case, where $H_{\text{succ}}$ counts successful handovers from the 5GS domain to the EPS domain, and $H_{\text{att}}$ counts all attempts of that specific inter‑technology handover.

### Worked Example

A macro‑cell reports the following statistics over a 15‑minute interval:

* Successful intra‑NR handovers (GRANHOSR):  1 210  
* Attempted intra‑NR handovers (GRANHOSR):   1 300  

Compute $S_{\text{HO}}$:

$$
S_{\text{HO}} = \frac{1\,210}{1\,300}\times 100\% = 93.08\%
$$

Thus the NG‑RAN handover success rate for that cell is **93.1 %**, indicating that roughly 7 % of handover attempts failed and may have caused call drops or service interruptions.

If the same interval also recorded 45 successful 5GS → EPS handovers out of 50 attempts, the 5GS‑to‑EPS handover success rate (5GSEPSHOSR) would be

$$
S_{\text{5GS→EPS}} = \frac{45}{50}\times 100\% = 90\%
$$

### Practical Notes and Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---------|----------------|------------|
| **Counting only successful handovers** | Omits the denominator $H_{\text{att}}$, inflating the KPI to 100 % and hiding failures. | Always record both successful and attempted handovers as defined in the KPI description. |
| **Mixing different handover types** | GRANHOSR covers a single handover type, while GRANHOSRA aggregates *all* handover types (legacy, conditional, DAPS, LTM). Using the wrong KPI can misrepresent performance. | Choose the KPI that matches the analysis scope; refer to the KPI description for coverage. |
| **Confusing inter‑technology vs. intra‑technology handovers** | 5GSEPSHOSR applies only to 5GS → EPS transfers, not to intra‑NR handovers. | Verify the domain (NR‑RAN vs. 5GS/EPS) before selecting the KPI code. |
| **Ignoring measurement granularity** | KPIs are defined per SubNetwork, NRCellCU; aggregating across cells without weighting can mask cell‑level issues. | Apply the KPI per NRCellCU and then, if needed, compute a weighted network‑wide average. |
| **Neglecting slice‑specific context** | Some KPIs (e.g., InterGNBHOMeanTime) are slice‑specific; using a slice‑agnostic KPI may overlook slice‑level performance. | For slice‑aware analysis, use the slice‑specific KPIs defined in the same section. |

By adhering to the precise definitions above, engineers can reliably monitor mobility performance, identify problematic cells, and trigger optimization actions such as tuning handover parameters, adjusting antenna tilts, or upgrading back‑haul capacity.

*All statements are derived directly from 3GPP TS 28.554 §6.6.*
