## 正文

In cellular systems a *handover* (or *handoff*) is the process by which a user equipment (UE) transfers an active radio connection from a source cell to a target cell while maintaining the ongoing service. The **Handover Success Rate (HSR)** quantifies how reliably the network executes this transfer. A high HSR is essential for:

* **Service continuity** – dropped calls or interrupted data sessions directly degrade the user experience and increase the *Retainability* metric discussed in 1.4.  
* **Network efficiency** – failed handovers consume radio resources for re‑attempts and may trigger unnecessary reselection procedures.  
* **Mobility management optimisation** – operators use HSR to tune handover parameters (e.g., event thresholds, time‑to‑trigger, preparation timers) and to assess the impact of new deployments such as small cells or carrier aggregation.

Consequently, HSR is a core KPI in the performance dashboards of LTE, NR (5G), and legacy 3G networks.

---

### 1. Definition and Formal Expression  

Let  

* $N_{\text{att}}$ – total number of **handover attempts** observed in a measurement interval (e.g., 1 h, 1 day).  
* $N_{\text{succ}}$ – number of **successful handovers**, i.e., attempts that satisfy all of the following conditions:  

  1. The UE completes the *RRC* (Radio Resource Control) reconfiguration with the target cell.  
  2. No **call drop** or **session interruption** occurs; the data flow resumes within a predefined *handover completion time* $T_{\text{max}}$ (often 150 ms for LTE, 100 ms for NR).  
  3. The target cell provides a minimum **signal quality** (e.g., SINR ≥ 3 dB) for at least one measurement period after handover.  

The **Handover Success Rate** is expressed as a percentage:

$$
\boxed{HSR = \frac{N_{\text{succ}}}{N_{\text{att}}}\times 100\%}
$$

A complementary metric is the **Handover Failure Rate (HFR)**:

$$
HFR = 100\% - HSR = \frac{N_{\text{fail}}}{N_{\text{att}}}\times 100\%
$$

where $N_{\text{fail}} = N_{\text{att}} - N_{\text{succ}}$.

---

### 2. Measurement Scope  

| Scope                     | Typical granularity | Remarks |
|---------------------------|---------------------|---------|
| **Per‑UE**                | Individual UE        | Useful for mobility‑rich subscribers (e.g., high‑speed train). |
| **Per‑cell (source)**     | Cell ID (eNodeB/gNB) | Highlights problematic cells (coverage holes, overloaded neighbors). |
| **Per‑network**           | Aggregate over all cells | Provides a macro‑KPIs for SLA reporting. |
| **Handover type**         | Intra‑frequency, Inter‑frequency, Inter‑RAT, Dual‑connect | Separate HSRs are often reported because success probabilities differ markedly. |

Data are collected by the network management system (e.g., OAM) via counters defined in 3GPP TS 36.331 (LTE) and TS 38.331 (NR). Counters are incremented at the moment a handover *preparation* message is sent (attempt) and when the UE sends a *handshake complete* indication (success).

---

### 3. Worked Example  

A medium‑size LTE operator monitors handovers on a busy macro cell for a 24‑hour period. The collected counters are:

| Counter                         | Value |
|--------------------------------|-------|
| $N_{\text{att}}$ (attempts)    | 1 200 |
| $N_{\text{succ}}$ (successes) | 1 150 |
| $T_{\text{max}}$ (threshold)  | 150 ms |

**Step 1 – Compute HSR**  

$$
HSR = \frac{1\,150}{1\,200}\times 100\% = 95.83\%
$$

**Step 2 – Compute HFR**  

$$
HFR = 100\% - 95.83\% = 4.17\%
$$

**Interpretation**  

An HSR of 95.8 % is generally acceptable for macro‑cellular LTE, but the operator notices that 80 % of the failures belong to *inter‑frequency* handovers toward a newly deployed 1800 MHz carrier. This insight triggers a parameter review (e.g., lowering the *A3* event offset) to improve the success probability on that carrier.

---

### 4. Practical Notes & Common Pitfalls  

| Pitfall | Why it matters | Correct approach |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Counting aborted preparations** | Some handover attempts are cancelled before the UE even receives the preparation message (e.g., because the measurement condition disappears). Including them inflates $N_{\text{att}}$ and artificially lowers HSR. | Only count attempts that reach the *handover command* (RRC Reconfiguration) stage. |
| **Ignoring the completion time window** | A handover may technically complete, but if data flow resumes after $T_{\text{max}}$, the user perceives a service interruption. | Enforce a strict $T_{\text{max}}$ (as defined by the network’s QoS class) when classifying successes. |
| **Double‑counting due to multi‑RAT handovers** | In LTE‑NR dual connectivity, the same UE may trigger a handover from LTE to NR and later from NR back to LTE within a short interval. Counting each direction separately without proper tagging can distort per‑technology HSR. | Tag each attempt with a *handover direction* identifier and aggregate only within the same type for reporting. |
| **Mixing intra‑ and inter‑frequency statistics** | Inter‑frequency handovers typically have lower success probabilities because of additional synchronization steps. Mixing them masks performance issues. | Report HSR separately for each handover class. |
| **Using outdated counters** | Counter roll‑over or delayed export can cause a temporary dip or spike in the KPI. | Align measurement interval with counter reset cycles and apply smoothing (e.g., moving average) for trend analysis. |

**Best‑practice checklist**  

1. Verify that $N_{\text{att}}$ counts only *RRC Reconfiguration* messages that contain a handover command.  
2. Ensure the UE‑side timestamp is available to calculate the handover completion time.  
3. Separate the KPI by handover class (intra‑/inter‑frequency, inter‑RAT).  
4. Correlate HSR with related metrics such as **RSRP**, **SINR**, and **CQI** of the target cell to identify root causes (e.g., poor coverage).  
5. Periodically audit the OAM counters against a known test scenario (e.g., drive‑test) to validate measurement integrity.

By adhering to these guidelines, engineers can obtain a trustworthy HSR measurement that directly informs mobility‑management optimisation and SLA compliance.
