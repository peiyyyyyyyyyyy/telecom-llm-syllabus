# 1.6.1 訊號強度：RSRP、RSSI

所屬：[1.6 指標與名詞](../1-6.md)

## 正文

In a cellular system the radio link is established over a shared wireless medium. The *signal strength* that a user equipment (UE) perceives determines whether a cell can be selected, how much transmit power the UE must use, and whether a hand‑over is needed. Two fundamental measurements are used throughout 3GPP specifications:

* **Reference‑signal received power (RSRP)** – the average power of the *reference* signals that are defined for cell identification and channel estimation.  
* **Received signal strength indicator (RSSI)** – the average total received power (including the reference signal, all co‑channel transmissions, adjacent‑channel interference, and thermal noise) measured over a configured bandwidth.

Both quantities are defined as *linear* averages of power contributions (in W) over a set of resource elements (REs) and are reported by the UE to higher‑layer functions. The definitions in the 3GPP Technical Specification 38.215 are reproduced below, together with the symbols used in the textbook.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.6.1.1 Reference‑signal received power (RSRP)

In NR the term *RSRP* can refer to several reference‑signal types. The specification distinguishes **SS‑RSRP** (secondary‑synchronization signal) and **CSI‑RSRP** (channel‑state‑information reference signal). Their common definition is:

$$
P_{\text{RSRP}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{RE}}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{RE}}} P_k\;,
$$

where  

* $N_{\text{RE}}$ – number of resource elements that carry the considered reference signal within the measurement period (implementation‑specific, subject to accuracy requirements),  
* $P_k$ – instantaneous power (in W) received on the $k$‑th resource element, measured during the useful part of the OFDM symbol (excluding the cyclic prefix).

*SS‑RSRP* is the linear average of the power contributions of the REs that carry **secondary synchronization signals** (and optionally PBCH and CSI‑RS) within the *SS/PBCH block measurement time configuration* window. The reference point is the UE antenna connector for frequency range 1, and the combined signal of the antenna elements of a receiver branch for frequency range 2. When receiver diversity is active, the reported SS‑RSRP shall not be lower than any individual branch value【TS 38.215 §5.1.1】.

*CSI‑RSRP* is defined analogously for **CSI‑RS** transmitted on antenna port 3000 (and optionally 3001). The reference point follows the same rule as for SS‑RSRP (antenna connector for FR1, combined branch for FR2) and the same diversity constraint applies【TS 38.215 §5.1.2】.

For LTE the corresponding measurement is called **E‑UTRA RSRP**. It averages the power of the cell‑specific reference signals (R0, optionally R1) over the considered measurement bandwidth, using the UE antenna connector as reference point, and respects the same diversity rule【TS 38.215 §5.1.15】.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.6.1.2 Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI)

RSSI is defined as the linear average of the *total* received power observed on a **configured OFDM symbol** and within the **measurement bandwidth** indicated by higher layers (or the channel bandwidth defined in TS 37.213). The measurement includes:

* power from the serving cell’s reference signal,  
* power from non‑serving cells on the same carrier,  
* adjacent‑channel interference, and  
* thermal noise.

Formally,

$$
P_{\text{RSSI}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{RE}}^{\text{tot}}}\sum_{m=1}^{N_{\text{RE}}^{\text{tot}}} P^{\text{tot}}_m\;,
$$

where $N_{\text{RE}}^{\text{tot}}$ is the number of REs in the configured measurement bandwidth and $P^{\text{tot}}_m$ is the total received power on RE $m$. The reference point is the antenna connector for frequency range 1 and the combined signal of the antenna elements for each receiver branch in frequency range 2; the reported RSSI shall not be lower than any individual branch value【TS 38.215 §5.1.21】.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.6.1.3 Worked Example

Assume a UE operating in frequency range 1 measures the following powers on ten resource elements that belong to a secondary‑synchronization block:

| RE | Measured power (W) |
|----|--------------------|
| 1  | $1.0\times10^{-6}$ |
| 2  | $1.1\times10^{-6}$ |
| …  | …                  |
|10  | $0.9\times10^{-6}$ |

For simplicity let the average power be $1.0\times10^{-6}\,$W (the exact values cancel in the average). The SS‑RSRP is

$$
P_{\text{SS‑RSRP}} = \frac{1}{10}\sum_{k=1}^{10} 1.0\times10^{-6} = 1.0\times10^{-6}\ \text{W}.
$$

If the same UE also measures total received power (including interference and noise) on the same ten REs and obtains an average of $5.0\times10^{-6}\,$W, the RSSI is

$$
P_{\text{RSSI}} = 5.0\times10^{-6}\ \text{W}.
$$

The ratio of RSSI to RSRP indicates the level of interference and noise present on the carrier. In this example the interference contribution is $4.0\times10^{-6}\,$W, i.e. four times the reference‑signal power.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.6.1.4 Practical Notes & Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it matters | Reference guidance |
|---------|----------------|--------------------|
| **Confusing RSRP with RSSI** – treating the two as interchangeable. | RSRP reflects only the reference‑signal power and is used for cell‑selection and hand‑over decisions; RSSI includes all interference and noise and is used for absolute power budgeting. | RSSI “comprises the linear average of the total received power … from all sources, including co‑channel serving and non‑serving cells, adjacent channel interference, thermal noise etc.”【TS 38.215 §5.1.21】 |
| **Ignoring the measurement window** – using REs outside the SS/PBCH block for SS‑RSRP. | The specification confines SS‑RSRP measurements to the SMTC window; using other REs violates the definition. | “The measurement time resource(s) for SS‑RSRP are confined within SS/PBCH Block Measurement Time Configuration (SMTC) window duration.”【TS 38.215 §5.1.1】 |
| **Using the UE transmit power in RSSI** – adding the UE’s own Tx power to the RSSI calculation. | RSSI is defined only for *received* power; the UE’s transmit power does not appear in the measurement. | RSSI definition explicitly mentions “total received power … observed … from all sources”, without reference to UE transmit power. |
| **Reporting a lower value when diversity is active** – averaging across branches and ending up lower than one branch. | When receiver diversity is employed, the reported value must be at least as high as the best individual branch. | “If receiver diversity is in use by the UE, the reported … value shall not be lower than the corresponding … of any of the individual receiver branches.”【TS 38.215 §5.1.1 & §5.1.2 & §5.1.21】 |
| **Assuming a fixed number of REs** – the UE may choose any number as long as accuracy requirements are met. | The specification leaves the exact RE count to the UE implementation; imposing a fixed count would be non‑compliant. | “The number of resource elements … is left up to the UE implementation with the limitation that corresponding measurement accuracy requirements have to be fulfilled.”【TS 38.215 §5.1.1, §5.1.2, §5.1.15】 |

Understanding the precise definitions of RSRP and RSSI is essential for interpreting measurement reports, designing link‑budget calculations, and troubleshooting radio‑access problems.
