# 1.6.4 接通率（Accessibility）、掉話率（Retainability）與可用率（Availability）

所屬：[1.6 指標與名詞](../1-6.md)

## 正文

In a live mobile network the three most frequently quoted “quality‑of‑service” indicators are **Accessibility (接通率)**, **Retainability (掉話率)**, and **Availability (可用率)**.  
They answer three distinct questions that operators must answer daily:

* **Accessibility** – *Can a user establish a call or data session when he/she tries?*  
* **Retainability** – *Once the session is established, does it stay alive for the required duration?*  
* **Availability** – *Is the network element (cell, carrier, core node) actually up and capable of providing service?*

Together they form the backbone of the “service reliability” pillar in 3GPP performance specifications (e.g., TS 22.133). A network that is up 99 % of the time but repeatedly fails to let users attach is useless; conversely, a network that lets users connect but drops them after a few seconds also fails the operator’s service‑level agreement (SLA). Understanding, measuring, and improving these metrics is therefore essential for capacity planning, maintenance scheduling, and SLA compliance.

---

### 1.6.4.1 Formal Definitions

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit / Dimension |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| $N_{\text{att}}$ | Number of **attempted** call or session setups (including both successful and failed attempts) | count |
| $N_{\text{succ}}$ | Number of **successful** call or session establishments | count |
| $N_{\text{drop}}$ | Number of **drops** that occur after a session has been established (excluding normal call termination by the user) | count |
| $N_{\text{conn}}$ | Number of **established** sessions (identical to $N_{\text{succ}}$ for a given measurement interval) | count |
| $T_{\text{up}}$ | Cumulative **uptime** of the network element during the observation window | seconds (s) |
| $T_{\text{down}}$ | Cumulative **downtime** (planned or unplanned outages) | seconds (s) |
| $T_{\text{obs}}$ | Total observation time, $T_{\text{obs}} = T_{\text{up}} + T_{\text{down}}$ | seconds (s) |

#### Accessibility ($\mathcal{A}$)
$$
\boxed{\mathcal{A} = \frac{N_{\text{succ}}}{N_{\text{att}}}}
$$
$\mathcal{A}$ is dimension‑less; it is usually expressed as a **percentage**. A higher value means that a larger fraction of users can *initially* reach the network.

#### Retainability ($\mathcal{R}$)
Two equivalent forms are used:

*Drop‑count form*  
$$
\boxed{\mathcal{R} = 1 - \frac{N_{\text{drop}}}{N_{\text{conn}}}}
$$

*Drop‑time form* (preferred when call‑duration data are available)  
$$
\boxed{\mathcal{R} = \frac{T_{\text{connected}} - T_{\text{drop}}}{T_{\text{connected}}}}
$$
where $T_{\text{connected}}$ is the sum of all session durations and $T_{\text{drop}}$ is the sum of the portions of those sessions that ended prematurely. Again, $\mathcal{R}$ is a percentage; values above 99 % are typical targets for LTE/5G voice over IP services.

#### Availability ($\mathcal{V}$)
$$
\boxed{\mathcal{V} = \frac{T_{\text{up}}}{T_{\text{obs}}} = \frac{T_{\text{up}}}{T_{\text{up}}+T_{\text{down}}}}
$$
$\mathcal{V}$ measures the **operational time** of the element, irrespective of traffic load. It is also expressed as a percentage; mission‑critical infrastructures often require $\mathcal{V}>99.999$ % (“five‑nines”).

---

### 1.6.4.2 Worked Example

A regional LTE operator collects KPI data over a 24‑hour period for a single macro cell:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Call attempts $N_{\text{att}}$ | 10 000 |
| Successful calls $N_{\text{succ}}$ | 9 500 |
| Dropped calls $N_{\text{drop}}$ | 200 |
| Total connected time $T_{\text{connected}}$ | 1 800 000 s (≈ 500 h) |
| Dropped‑time $T_{\text{drop}}$ | 12 000 s (≈ 3.33 h) |
| Uptime $T_{\text{up}}$ | 23.5 h = 84 600 s |
| Downtime $T_{\text{down}}$ | 0.5 h = 1 800 s |

Compute the three KPIs:

1. **Accessibility**  
   $$
   \mathcal{A}= \frac{9\,500}{10\,000}=0.95\;\Rightarrow\;95\%
   $$

2. **Retainability (drop‑count form)**  
   $$
   \mathcal{R}=1-\frac{200}{9\,500}=1-0.02105=0.97895\;\Rightarrow\;97.9\%
   $$

   *or* using the drop‑time form:  
   $$
   \mathcal{R}= \frac{1\,800\,000-12\,000}{1\,800\,000}=0.9933\;\Rightarrow\;99.33\%
   $$
   The two results differ because the drop‑count form treats each dropped call equally, while the drop‑time form weights longer calls more heavily. Operators choose the version that matches their SLA definition.

3. **Availability**  
   $$
   \mathcal{V}= \frac{84\,600}{84\,600+1\,800}= \frac{84\,600}{86\,400}=0.9792\;\Rightarrow\;97.9\%
   $$

Interpretation: The cell is reachable 95 % of the time, once a call is set up it stays alive roughly 98 % of the time, and the cell itself is up 97.9 % of the day. If the operator’s SLA requires ≥ 99 % accessibility, the cell must be optimized (e.g., antenna tilt, power increase) or the coverage hole must be mitigated.

### 1.6.4.3 Practical Notes & Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it Happens | Correct Approach |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Confusing Accessibility with Availability** | Both are percentages; engineers sometimes quote “availability = 99 %” when they actually measured call‑setup success. | Keep the definitions distinct: Availability concerns *hardware/software uptime*; Accessibility concerns *user‑initiated setup success* under normal operating conditions. |
| **Counting normal call termination as a drop** | User hangs up → counted as $N_{\text{drop}}$ if the logging system does not differentiate. | Use detailed call‑event logs (e.g., “NORMAL_RELEASE” vs “ABNORMAL_RELEASE”) and exclude intentional releases from $N_{\text{drop}}$. |
| **Mixing planned maintenance with unplanned outage** | Planned cell re‑configurations artificially lower $\mathcal{V}$ if not excluded. | Subtract scheduled maintenance windows from $T_{\text{obs}}$ or report a separate “planned‑availability” figure. |
| **Using raw attempt counts without filtering “busy” or “blocked” states** | In high‑load cells many attempts are rejected due to lack of resources; counting them as failures can unfairly penalize accessibility. | Follow 3GPP TS 22.133: only *radio‑access* failures (e.g., RRC connection reject due to poor radio) count toward $N_{\text{att}}$, while *resource‑blocking* is reported under a separate KPI (e.g., Blocking Probability). |
| **Applying the drop‑count form to very short sessions** | A brief 2‑second call that drops counts the same as a 20‑minute call, inflating the perceived problem. | Prefer the drop‑time form when detailed session‑duration data are available, especially for data‑session retainability. |

**Measurement windows** – KPI values are time‑dependent. A 5‑minute snapshot may show poor accessibility during a temporary rainstorm, while the 24‑hour average looks acceptable. Operators typically publish both short‑term (e.g., 5 min, 15 min) and long‑term (daily, weekly) figures.

**Impact of handovers** – In LTE/5G the majority of drops occur during inter‑cell handovers. A cell may have high accessibility but low retainability if its handover parameters are mis‑tuned. Monitoring *Handover Success Rate* (Section 1.6.7) in parallel helps isolate the root cause.

**Target values** – The exact thresholds depend on service type. Voice over LTE (VoLTE) often targets $\mathcal{A} \ge 99.5\%$, $\mathcal{R} \ge 99.9\%$, $\mathcal{V} \ge 99.9\%$, whereas best‑effort data services may accept lower retainability because users can reconnect.

By keeping these definitions rigorous, measuring them consistently, and avoiding the common traps above, telecom engineers can provide reliable, quantifiable evidence of network performance and drive focused optimisation efforts.
