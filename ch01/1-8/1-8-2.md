# 1.8.2 障礙管理流程：受理、派工、修復、結案

所屬：[1.8 維運組織](../1-8.md)

## 正文

In a live telecommunications network, faults in the physical or logical resources (e.g., an optical line failure, a malfunctioning base‑band unit, or a software crash) inevitably occur. The *trouble management process* is the set of coordinated activities that turn a raw failure event into a restored service and a documented lesson‑learned. It matters because any prolonged resource outage degrades the quality of service perceived by customers and can trigger contractual penalties. Moreover, a disciplined process ensures that repair work is traceable, repeatable, and aligned with higher‑level service‑oriented problem handling.

### Key Definitions (derived from TS 32.101)

| Term | Definition (as stated or implied in the standard) |
|------|---------------------------------------------------|
| **Resource Failure Event** | An occurrence indicating that a network resource has stopped performing its intended function. |
| **Resource Trouble Management (RTM)** | The collection of processes that *detect, analyse and report Resource Failure Events*; perform *fault localization analysis*; *correct resource faults*; *report troubles* to other management functions; and *administer* repair activities. |
| **Service Problem Management (SPM)** | The processes whose *purpose* is *to respond immediately to customer‑affecting service problems or failures* and to restore or provide an alternate service. |
| **Problem Handling (PH)** | The processes *responsible for receiving trouble reports from customers* and for keeping the customer informed about repair status. |
| **Acceptance (受理)** | The initial capture of a Resource Failure Event, typically by the network operations centre (NOC) or an automated collector. |
| **Dispatch (派工)** | The assignment of a repair task to a field or engineering team, governed by *resource trouble administration to ensure repair activities are assigned and tracked efficiently*. |
| **Repair (修復)** | The execution of *correcting resource faults* on the identified asset. |
| **Closure (結案)** | The final step where the fault is declared resolved, the *resource trouble reporting* is sent to SPM, and the incident is documented for future analysis. |

No mathematical formula is required for the process itself; however, a simple performance metric often used is **Mean Time to Repair (MTTR)**:

$$
\text{MTTR} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} T_{\text{repair},i}}{N}
$$

where $T_{\text{repair},i}$ is the elapsed time from *acceptance* to *closure* for incident $i$, and $N$ is the total number of incidents considered. The unit is **seconds** (or minutes/hours as appropriate).

### Process Flow Mapped to Standard Responsibilities

1. **Acceptance** – The NOC detects a failure (e.g., via alarms from Resource Data Collection) and *detects, analyses and reports Resource Failure Events* [TS 32.101 §6.7.3].  
2. **Dispatch** – A trouble ticket is created and *resource trouble administration* assigns it to the appropriate crew, ensuring the work is tracked efficiently [TS 32.101 §6.7.3].  
3. **Repair** – The assigned crew performs *correcting resource faults* on the affected element [TS 32.101 §6.7.3].  
4. **Closure** – After verification, the RTM process *reports the trouble* to Service Problem Management, which can then confirm that the *customer‑affecting service problem* has been resolved [TS 32.101 §6.6.3; §6.7.3].

The *Service Problem Management* function may also trigger a *temporary fix or work‑around* while the root cause is being eliminated, as described in its purpose statement.

### Worked Example

A metropolitan area network (MAN) operator receives an alarm that a 40 km fiber segment has lost signal.

| Step | Action | Time (minutes) |
|------|--------|----------------|
| Acceptance | NOC logs the alarm as Trouble Ticket #2026‑001. | 5 |
| Dispatch | Ticket is assigned to the field crew; the crew acknowledges receipt. | 10 |
| Repair | Crew replaces the faulty splice and performs optical‑power testing. | 45 |
| Closure | NOC verifies restored signal, closes the ticket, and sends a trouble report to SPM. | 5 |

**MTTR calculation** for this single incident:

$$
\text{MTTR} = \frac{5 + 10 + 45 + 5}{1} = 65\ \text{minutes}
$$

The MTTR of 65 minutes can be compared against service‑level agreements (SLAs) to evaluate performance.

### Common Mistakes & Practical Notes

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Correct Approach |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Skipping fault localization** and jumping straight to repair. | Pressure to restore service quickly. | Perform *fault localization analysis* first; it reduces unnecessary work and prevents repeat failures [TS 32.101 §6.7.3]. |
| **Treating RTM and SPM as isolated**. | Organizational silos. | Ensure that *resource trouble reporting* is sent to SPM so that the *service impact* view can be incorporated [TS 32.101 §6.7.3]. |
| **Not informing the customer** after acceptance. | Assumption that only internal teams need updates. | *Problem Handling* mandates *customer contact and support* throughout the incident lifecycle [TS 32.101 §6.5.6]. |
| **Closing a ticket before verification**. | Misinterpretation of “repair completed”. | Verify the restored functionality (e.g., optical power levels) before issuing the closure record. |
| **Ignoring the “administration” bullet** and failing to track assignments. | Reliance on informal communication. | Use a ticketing system that enforces *resource trouble administration* to keep assignments visible and auditable [TS 32.101 §6.7.3]. |

By adhering to the responsibilities defined in TS 32.101, operators can ensure that each incident follows a disciplined path from detection to closure, minimizing service disruption and maintaining compliance with contractual obligations.

## 練習題

**1.** Which process is explicitly tasked with detecting, analysing and reporting resource failure events?

- A. Service Problem Management
- B. Problem Handling
- C. Resource Trouble Management
- D. Network Configuration Management

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The standard lists this as a core responsibility of Resource Trouble Management.

</details>

**2.** What is the primary purpose of Service Problem Management as defined in the reference?

- A. To generate performance statistics for network resources.
- B. To respond immediately to customer‑affecting service problems or failures.
- C. To allocate bandwidth for new services.
- D. To perform routine maintenance on network elements.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The purpose statement directly mentions immediate response to customer‑affecting problems.

</details>

**3.** Which set of processes is responsible for receiving trouble reports from customers?

- A. Resource Trouble Management
- B. Service Problem Management
- C. Problem Handling
- D. Fault Management

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The reference assigns receipt of customer trouble reports to Problem Handling.

</details>

**4.** Which activity ensures that repair tasks are assigned and tracked efficiently?

- A. Fault localization analysis
- B. Resource trouble administration
- C. Service level monitoring
- D. Capacity planning

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The standard cites resource trouble administration as the function that guarantees efficient assignment and tracking.

</details>

**5.** Why must Resource Trouble Management interact with Service Problem Management?

- A. Because SPM provides the technical specifications for hardware.
- B. Because SPM has a view on service impact.
- C. Because SPM handles billing for repaired resources.
- D. Because SPM defines security policies for the network.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Interaction is needed so that the service impact perspective can be incorporated.

</details>
