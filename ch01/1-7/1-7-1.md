# 1.7.1 涵蓋與涵蓋容量優化（CCO）

## 正文

In a cellular system the *coverage* of a base station (BS) is the geographical area where a user equipment (UE) can achieve the required quality of service (QoS) – typically expressed as a minimum downlink (DL) signal‑to‑interference‑plus‑noise ratio (SINR) or block error rate (BLER). *Coverage‑capacity optimization* (CCO) is the systematic process of adjusting network parameters so that the coverage area is maximised **without** sacrificing the capacity needed to serve the expected traffic load. In practice, operators must strike a balance: expanding coverage reduces the number of sites (lower CAPEX/OPEX) but may increase interference and lower the per‑cell throughput.  

---

### 1.7.1.1 Key definitions  

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $P_{\text{TX}}$ | Transmit power of the BS (including antenna gain) | dBm |
| $L_{\text{path}}(d)$ | Large‑scale path‑loss at distance $d$ | dB |
| $G_{\text{BS}}$, $G_{\text{UE}}$ | Antenna gains of BS and UE | dBi |
| $N_0$ | Thermal noise power spectral density | $-174$ dBm/Hz |
| $B$ | System bandwidth | Hz |
| $I$ | Aggregate inter‑cell interference power | dBm |
| $\sigma^2 = N_0 + 10\log_{10}B$ | Noise power over $B$ | dBm |
| $\text{SINR}_{\text{DL}}$ | Downlink SINR | dB |
| $\gamma_{\text{min}}$ | Minimum SINR required for the target BLER | dB |
| $C(d)$ | Cell‑edge capacity (bits/s) at distance $d$ | bps |
| $R_{\text{UE}}$ | Average traffic demand per UE | bps |
| $N_{\text{UE}}$ | Number of active UEs in the cell | dimensionless |
| $\eta$ | Spectral efficiency (bits/s/Hz) | bps/Hz |

The basic *link budget* for a UE at distance $d$ is  

$$
\text{Received power } P_{\text{RX}}(d) = P_{\text{TX}} + G_{\text{BS}} + G_{\text{UE}} - L_{\text{path}}(d) \quad \text{[dBm]} .
$$

The downlink SINR is then  

$$
\text{SINR}_{\text{DL}}(d) = P_{\text{RX}}(d) - \bigl(\sigma^2 + I\bigr) .
$$

A UE is considered *covered* if  

$$
\text{SINR}_{\text{DL}}(d) \ge \gamma_{\text{min}} .
$$

Coverage capacity is linked to the Shannon‑like approximation  

$$
\eta(d) \approx \log_2\bigl(1 + \text{SINR}_{\text{DL}}(d)\bigr) \quad \text{[bps/Hz]} .
$$

The *cell‑edge capacity* is  

$$
C(d) = \eta(d) \times B \quad \text{[bps]} .
$$

A cell can support the traffic demand if  

$$
C(d) \ge \frac{N_{\text{UE}} \, R_{\text{UE}}}{\alpha} ,
$$

where $\alpha\;(0<\alpha\le 1)$ is the load factor accounting for scheduling inefficiencies (typical $\alpha=0.7$).

---

### 1.7.1.2 CCO workflow  

1. **Initial planning** – Use a propagation model (e.g., COST‑231 Hata, 3GPP TR 36.814) to generate a *coverage map* for a nominal $P_{\text{TX}}$ and antenna tilt.  
2. **Coverage verification** – Identify *coverage holes* where $\text{SINR}_{\text{DL}} < \gamma_{\text{min}}$ and *coverage excess* where SINR is far above the threshold (wasted power).  
3. **Capacity assessment** – For each location, compute $\eta(d)$ and compare $C(d)$ with the required load $N_{\text{UE}}R_{\text{UE}}/\alpha$.  
4. **Parameter tuning** – Adjust a set of controllable knobs:  
   * **Transmit power $P_{\text{TX}}$** (per carrier/component).  
   * **Antenna mechanical/electrical tilt** (degrees).  
   * **Antenna azimuth** (direction).  
   * **Cell‑specific reference signal (CRS) power offset** (dB).  
   * **Inter‑cell interference coordination (ICIC) patterns** (e.g., almost‑blank subframes).  
5. **Iterative simulation** – Re‑run the link‑budget and capacity calculations after each change; stop when the *coverage probability* $P_{\text{cov}} = \Pr\{\text{SINR}_{\text{DL}} \ge \gamma_{\text{min}}\}$ meets the operator’s target (often $>95\%$) **and** the *cell‑edge capacity* exceeds the traffic requirement with the chosen load factor.  
6. **Field validation** – Perform drive‑test or remote‑minimization‑of‑drive‑test (MDT) measurements to confirm the simulated results; fine‑tune parameters if necessary.

### 1.7.1.3 Worked example  

*Scenario*: A single LTE macro cell operates at 1800 MHz with $B=10$ MHz. The BS transmit power is $P_{\text{TX}}=46$ dBm, $G_{\text{BS}}=14$ dBi, UE antenna gain $G_{\text{UE}}=0$ dBi. The target SINR for 16‑QAM (MCS 9) is $\gamma_{\text{min}}=5$ dB. Noise density $N_0=-174$ dBm/Hz, thus $\sigma^2 = -174 + 10\log_{10}(10\text{ MHz}) = -104$ dBm. Inter‑cell interference is approximated as $I=-95$ dBm.  

Assume the COST‑231 Hata model gives $L_{\text{path}}(d)=128.1+37.6\log_{10}d$ (d in km).  

**Step 1 – Compute received power at $d=1$ km**  

$$
P_{\text{RX}}(1) = 46 + 14 + 0 - \bigl[128.1 + 37.6\log_{10}1\bigr] = 60 - 128.1 = -68.1\ \text{dBm}.
$$

**Step 2 – SINR**  

$$
\text{SINR}_{\text{DL}}(1) = -68.1 - (-104 + (-95)) = -68.1 - (-199) = 130.9\ \text{dB}.
$$

The above value is unrealistic because interference and noise are summed in linear scale; we must convert to linear first:

$$
\sigma^2_{\text{lin}} = 10^{\frac{-104}{10}} = 3.98\times10^{-11}\ \text{W},
\qquad
I_{\text{lin}} = 10^{\frac{-95}{10}} = 3.16\times10^{-10}\ \text{W},
$$
$$
P_{\text{RX,lin}} = 10^{\frac{-68.1}{10}} = 1.55\times10^{-7}\ \text{W}.
$$

$$
\text{SINR}_{\text{lin}} = \frac{P_{\text{RX,lin}}}{\sigma^2_{\text{lin}} + I_{\text{lin}}}
= \frac{1.55\times10^{-7}}{3.98\times10^{-11}+3.16\times10^{-10}}
\approx 426.
$$

$$
\text{SINR}_{\text{DL}} = 10\log_{10}(426) \approx 26.3\ \text{dB}.
$$

Since $26.3\ \text{dB} > \gamma_{\text{min}}=5\ \text{dB}$, the UE at 1 km is covered.

**Step 3 – Spectral efficiency and capacity**  

$$
\eta = \log_2(1+426) \approx \log_2(427) \approx 8.73\ \text{bps/Hz}.
$$

$$
C = \eta \times B = 8.73 \times 10\ \text{MHz} = 87.3\ \text{Mbps}.
$$

Assume $N_{\text{UE}}=30$ active UEs each demanding $R_{\text{UE}}=2$ Mbps. Required load:

$$
\frac{N_{\text{UE}}R_{\text{UE}}}{\alpha}= \frac{30 \times 2}{0.7} \approx 85.7\ \text{Mbps}.
$$

Because $C=87.3$ Mbps > 85.7 Mbps, the cell can support the traffic at the 1 km edge.

**Step 4 – Extending coverage**  

If we want to increase the edge distance to 1.5 km, recompute $L_{\text{path}}(1.5)=128.1+37.6\log_{10}1.5=128.1+5.1=133.2$ dB.  

$$
P_{\text{RX}}(1.5) = 60 - 133.2 = -73.2\ \text{dBm},
$$
$$
P_{\text{RX,lin}} = 4.78\times10^{-8}\ \text{W},
$$
$$
\text{SINR}_{\text{lin}} = \frac{4.78\times10^{-8}}{3.98\times10^{-11}+3.16\times10^{-10}} \approx 132,
$$
$$
\text{SINR}_{\text{DL}} = 10\log_{10}(132) \approx 21.2\ \text{dB}.
$$

Spectral efficiency $\eta \approx \log_2(1+132) \approx 7.04$ bps/Hz, giving $C \approx 70.4$ Mbps, which is insufficient for the 85.7 Mbps load.  

A practical CCO action is to increase $P_{\text{TX}}$ by 2 dB (to 48 dBm) or reduce the antenna tilt to increase the down‑tilt gain toward the edge. Re‑evaluating with $P_{\text{TX}}=48$ dBm yields $P_{\text{RX}}(1.5) = -71.2$ dBm, SINR ≈ 23.2 dB, $\eta \approx 7.53$ bps/Hz, $C \approx 75.3$ Mbps – still short. A combined solution (2 dB power increase **plus** a 2° mechanical tilt reduction) typically provides enough margin to meet the capacity requirement, illustrating the iterative nature of CCO.

### 1.7.1.4 Common pitfalls and practical notes  

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | Remedy |
|---------|---------------|--------|
| **Using only path‑loss for coverage** | Ignores interference, leading to overly optimistic coverage maps. | Include realistic $I$ from neighbouring cells; use system‑level simulators or field‑measured interference statistics. |
| **Relying on a single SINR threshold** | Different services (voice, video, IoT) have distinct $\gamma_{\text{min}}$ values. | Perform *service‑aware* CCO: compute separate thresholds per QoS class and adopt the most stringent as the design target. |
| **Changing transmit power without considering UE battery** | Higher $P_{\text{TX}}$ improves downlink but may increase UE uplink power (due to power control) and reduce battery life. | Evaluate uplink impact; consider *downlink‑only* power boosts (e.g., CRS offset) or antenna pattern adjustments. |
| **Neglecting antenna downtilt limits** | Mechanical tilt may be bounded by pole height or civil regulations. | Use electrical tilting (beamforming) where mechanical tilt is constrained; verify compliance with local regulations. |
| **Assuming uniform UE distribution** | Real traffic is often hotspot‑centric, causing local overload even when average load is satisfied. | Apply *load‑balancing* or *small‑cell* densification in identified hotspots before increasing macro coverage. |

Finally, remember that CCO is not a one‑time design step; it is an *ongoing* optimisation loop that must be revisited after major events such as spectrum refarming, addition of new carriers, or significant changes in traffic patterns. Continuous monitoring (via MDT, KPI dashboards) and automated SON functions can greatly accelerate the CCO cycle.
