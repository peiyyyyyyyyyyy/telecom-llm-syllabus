# 1.7.7 自我組態與自我優化（SON）

所屬：[1.7 網路優化與終端](../1-7.md)

## 正文

Modern cellular systems (LTE, NR, and beyond) are deployed with thousands of cells, each equipped with multiple radio parameters that must be tuned continuously. Manual planning and periodic manual retuning become impractical as networks densify, traffic patterns become more volatile, and new services (e.g., massive IoT) appear. Self‑Organizing Network (SON) techniques automate three complementary functions:

* **Self‑configuration** – automatic provisioning of new cells (e.g., initial neighbour list, antenna tilt, transmit power) without human intervention.  
* **Self‑optimization** – ongoing adjustment of radio and mobility parameters to improve key performance indicators (KPIs) such as coverage, throughput, and handover success.  
* **Self‑healing** – detection of faults (e.g., a failed eNodeB) and automatic mitigation (e.g., load redistribution).

SON is defined in 3GPP Release 10 and later (TS 36.902, TS 38.902) and is a mandatory feature for most commercial deployments because it reduces OPEX, shortens deployment time, and sustains QoS under dynamic conditions.

### Key Definitions

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $\mathbf{p}$ | Vector of configurable radio parameters (e.g., antenna tilt $\theta$, transmit power $P_{\text{Tx}}$, handover offset $O_{\text{HO}}$) | varies (° for tilt, dBm for power, dB for offset) |
| $\mathbf{k}$ | Vector of observed KPI values (e.g., coverage probability $C$, average user throughput $T$, handover success rate $H$) | dimensionless or bps/Hz |
| $f(\mathbf{k})$ | Objective (cost) function to be minimized (or maximized) | depends on KPI units |
| $\mathbf{C}$ | Constraint matrix defining allowable ranges for $\mathbf{p}$ | – |
| $\mathbf{b}$ | Constraint bounds (e.g., $p_i^{\min} \le p_i \le p_i^{\max}$) | – |
| $w_i$ | Weight assigned to KPI $k_i$ in the composite objective | dimensionless |

A generic SON optimisation problem can be expressed as  

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf{p}} \; & f(\mathbf{k}(\mathbf{p})) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \, g_i\big(k_i(\mathbf{p})\big) \\
\text{s.t. } & \mathbf{C}\mathbf{p} \le \mathbf{b},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $g_i(\cdot)$ is a monotonic transformation (e.g., $g_i(k)= -k$ for KPIs that should be maximized). The mapping $\mathbf{k}(\mathbf{p})$ is obtained from measurements (RSRP, RSRQ, handover logs) and statistical models (e.g., path‑loss, interference).

### Representative SON Algorithms

1. **Automatic Neighbor Relation (ANR)** – builds the neighbour list $\mathcal{N}$ for a cell by analysing measurement reports $M_{u}$ from UEs $u$. The algorithm adds a neighbour $j$ if  

   $$
   \frac{1}{|U|}\sum_{u\in U}\mathbf{1}\big(RSRP_{u,j} > \tau_{\text{ANR}}\big) \ge \alpha,
   $$

   where $\tau_{\text{ANR}}$ is a threshold (e.g., –95 dBm) and $\alpha$ a confidence factor (e.g., 0.2).

2. **Load‑Balancing (LB) via Cell‑Specific Offset (CSO)** – adjusts $O_{\text{HO}}$ to steer users from an overloaded cell $c$ to a neighbour $n$. The revised handover condition for UE $u$ becomes  

   $$
   RSRP_{u,n} + O_{\text{HO},n} > RSRP_{u,c} + O_{\text{HO},c} + \Delta_{\text{HO}},
   $$

   where $\Delta_{\text{HO}}$ is a hysteresis margin (e.g., 3 dB). The offset update follows a proportional‑integral (PI) rule  

   $$
   O_{\text{HO},n}^{(t+1)} = O_{\text{HO},n}^{(t)} + K_P \, e^{(t)} + K_I \sum_{k=0}^{t} e^{(k)},
   $$

   with error $e^{(t)} = \frac{L_c^{(t)} - L_{\text{target}}}{L_{\text{target}}}$, $L$ being cell load (percentage).

3. **Antenna Tilt Optimization** – aims to meet a target coverage probability $C^{\star}$ (e.g., 95 %). The coverage probability for a given tilt $\theta$ can be approximated by  

   $$
   C(\theta) = \Pr\big(RSRP \ge \tau_{\text{cov}}\big) \approx \Phi\!\left(\frac{\mu_{\text{RSRP}}(\theta)-\tau_{\text{cov}}}{\sigma_{\text{RSRP}}}\right),
   $$

   where $\Phi(\cdot)$ is the standard normal CDF, $\mu_{\text{RSRP}}(\theta)$ and $\sigma_{\text{RSRP}}$ are the mean and standard deviation of RSRP derived from a path‑loss model. The optimal tilt $\theta^{\star}$ solves  

   $$
   \theta^{\star} = \arg\min_{\theta_{\min}\le\theta\le\theta_{\max}} \big|C(\theta)-C^{\star}\big|.
   $$

### Worked Example – Tilt Adjustment

A macro cell currently uses a mechanical downtilt $\theta = 4^{\circ}$. The operator targets $C^{\star}=95\%$ coverage with $\tau_{\text{cov}}=-95\,$dBm. Based on drive‑test data, the RSRP distribution for a given tilt follows a normal law with  

* $\mu_{\text{RSRP}}(\theta) = -85 - 0.8\,\theta$ dBm,  
* $\sigma_{\text{RSRP}} = 6$ dB (independent of tilt).

1. Compute $C(4^{\circ})$:  

   $$
   \mu_{\text{RSRP}}(4) = -85 - 0.8\times4 = -88.2\text{ dBm}.
   $$  

   $$
   z = \frac{\mu_{\text{RSRP}}(4)-\tau_{\text{cov}}}{\sigma_{\text{RSRP}}}
       = \frac{-88.2 - (-95)}{6}= \frac{6.8}{6}=1.13.
   $$  

   $$
   C(4^{\circ}) \approx \Phi(1.13) \approx 0.87 \;(87\%).
   $$

   Coverage is below the target.

2. Find $\theta$ that yields $C=0.95$. The required $z$ is $\Phi^{-1}(0.95)=1.645$. Solve for $\mu_{\text{RSRP}}$:

   $$
   1.645 = \frac{\mu_{\text{RSRP}}(\theta)-(-95)}{6}
   \;\;\Rightarrow\;\;
   \mu_{\text{RSRP}}(\theta) = -95 + 1.645\times6 = -85.13\text{ dBm}.
   $$

   Set $\mu_{\text{RSRP}}(\theta) = -85 - 0.8\theta = -85.13$:

   $$
   -85 -0.8\theta = -85.13 \;\Rightarrow\; -0.8\theta = -0.13 \;\Rightarrow\; \theta = 0.1625^{\circ}.
   $$

   The optimal downtilt is approximately $0.2^{\circ}$ (i.e., almost flat). Since mechanical limits often restrict tilt to $[0^{\circ}, 15^{\circ}]$, the SON controller would command a reduction from $4^{\circ}$ to $0^{\circ}$, then monitor for interference increase.

3. Verify after adjustment (assume $\theta=0^{\circ}$):

   $$
   \mu_{\text{RSRP}}(0) = -85\text{ dBm},\quad
   z = \frac{-85 - (-95)}{6}=1.667,\quad
   C \approx \Phi(1.667)=0.952\;(95.2\%).
   $$

   The target is met.

### Common Mistakes & Practical Notes

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | Corrective practice |
|---------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Over‑aggressive parameter changes** (e.g., large tilt jumps) | SON loops often use a fixed step size; large steps can cause oscillations or degrade neighbour cells. | Apply a damping factor or limit the per‑iteration change (e.g., $|\Delta\theta| \le 1^{\circ}$). |
| **Ignoring inter‑cell coupling** | Optimizing a KPI for a single cell without considering neighbour interference may improve local metrics but hurt network‑wide performance. | Use a coordinated SON framework where the objective includes neighbour KPI terms or apply hierarchical optimisation (cluster‑level first). |
| **Insufficient measurement granularity** | KPI estimation based on too few UE reports leads to noisy $\mathbf{k}(\mathbf{p})$, causing unstable updates. | Enforce a minimum sample count (e.g., $N_{\text{UE}} \ge 100$) before triggering an optimisation cycle. |
| **Hard‑coded thresholds** | Fixed $\tau_{\text{ANR}}$ or $\Delta_{\text{HO}}$ may become sub‑optimal as traffic evolves. | Implement adaptive thresholds that scale with average RSRP or load. |
| **Neglecting self‑healing** | When a cell crashes, self‑optimisation may keep steering traffic to it, worsening dropped calls. | Integrate fault detection (e.g., heartbeat loss) with immediate neighbour load‑balancing before the optimisation loop runs. |

In practice, SON controllers are implemented as closed‑loop automation agents that run on a network management server (e.g., O-RAN near‑real‑time RIC). They ingest KPI streams via the O‑DL (O‑Data Lake) or 3GPP PM counters, compute the gradient or PI updates, and push parameter changes through standardized interfaces (e.g., NETCONF/YANG or O‑RAN E2). Properly tuned SON reduces manual OPEX by 30–40 % and enables rapid rollout of new sites (including small cells and indoor DAS) while preserving the QoS promised to subscribers.

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following best describes the objective function $f(\mathbf{k}(\mathbf{p}))$ used in a generic SON optimisation problem?

- A. The sum of all radio parameters $\mathbf{p}$.
- B. A weighted combination of KPI transformations that the network aims to minimise or maximise.
- C. The total number of neighbours added by ANR.
- D. The mechanical tilt angle of the antenna.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The objective function aggregates KPI metrics (through weights $w_i$ and functions $g_i$) to be optimised; it is not a simple sum of parameters or a single metric.

</details>

**2.** In the ANR rule $\displaystyle \frac{1}{|U|}\sum_{u\in U}\mathbf{1}(RSRP_{u,j} > \tau_{\text{ANR}}) \ge \alpha$, what does the parameter $\alpha$ represent?

- A. The maximum allowed transmit power.
- B. The confidence factor indicating the minimum fraction of UEs that must report a neighbour above the threshold.
- C. The hysteresis margin for handover decisions.
- D. The target coverage probability.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。$\alpha$ is the minimum proportion of users that must see the neighbour cell with sufficient signal strength for it to be added.

</details>

**3.** When adjusting antenna tilt to meet a target coverage probability $C^{\star}=95\%$, which statistical function is used to map the mean RSRP to coverage probability?

- A. Exponential decay function.
- B. Standard normal cumulative distribution function $\Phi(\cdot)$.
- C. Log‑normal probability density function.
- D. Linear regression coefficient.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Coverage probability is approximated by $\Phi\!\big((\mu_{\text{RSRP}}-\tau_{\text{cov}})/\sigma_{\text{RSRP}}\big)$, the CDF of a normal distribution.

</details>

**4.** In the PI update rule for cell‑specific handover offset $O_{\text{HO}}$, the error term $e^{(t)}$ is computed as $\displaystyle \frac{L_c^{(t)} - L_{\text{target}}}{L_{\text{target}}}$. What does $L_c^{(t)}$ denote?

- A. The latency of the control plane at time $t$.
- B. The load (percentage of resource utilisation) of the overloaded cell at iteration $t$.
- C. The number of neighbouring cells discovered by ANR.
- D. The average user throughput in bps.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。$L_c^{(t)}$ is the measured load of the cell being balanced; the error quantifies deviation from the desired load level.

</details>

**5.** Which practical mistake can cause SON optimisation to degrade network‑wide performance despite improving a single cell’s KPI?

- A. Using a damping factor that limits parameter changes.
- B. Ignoring inter‑cell coupling and optimizing only local KPIs.
- C. Enforcing a minimum number of UE measurements before updates.
- D. Integrating self‑healing with fault detection.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Optimising a KPI in isolation may increase interference to neighbours; considering inter‑cell coupling avoids this pitfall.

</details>
