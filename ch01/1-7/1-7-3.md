## 正文

In a cellular system a user equipment (UE) constantly moves relative to the fixed infrastructure. To preserve an active session the network must transfer the radio link from the serving cell to a neighboring cell without perceptible interruption. This transfer is called **handover** (or hand‑off). Handover is a cornerstone of mobility management; without it, users would experience dropped calls, interrupted data sessions, and severe degradation of quality‑of‑service (QoS). Efficient handover also influences network capacity because unnecessary or delayed handovers increase radio resource waste and generate extra signalling load.

---

### 1. Key Definitions

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $R_{\text{serv}}$ | Measured reference signal received power (RSRP) from the serving cell | dBm |
| $R_{\text{tgt}}$ | Measured RSRP from a candidate target cell | dBm |
| $H$ | Hysteresis margin applied to avoid ping‑pong effects | dB |
| $T_{\text{TTT}}$ | **Time‑to‑Trigger**: the minimum time the handover condition must be satisfied before a handover is initiated | ms |
| $M$ | Handover decision metric (positive → trigger) | dB |
| $P_{\text{HF}}$ | Probability of handover failure (e.g., radio link failure after handover) | – |
| $\lambda$ | Exponential decay constant of the signal‑strength process (related to UE speed) | s$^{-1}$ |
| $v$ | UE speed | km/h |
| $D_{\text{HO}}$ | Handover distance (distance travelled during $T_{\text{TTT}}$) | m |
| $TA$ | Tracking Area – a set of cells used for mobility management in LTE/NR |
| $TAU$ | Tracking Area Update – signalling message sent when UE moves to a new TA |
| $P_{\text{paging}}$ | Paging probability for a given UE within a TA | – |

---

### 2. Handover Decision Metric

A common LTE/NR handover trigger is based on the **event A3** condition: the target cell becomes offset better than the serving cell. The metric is  

$$
M = R_{\text{tgt}} - R_{\text{serv}} - H .
$$

If $M \ge 0$ for a continuous period of at least $T_{\text{TTT}}$, the UE reports the condition to the Radio Resource Control (RRC) layer, which then initiates the handover procedure.  

- **Hysteresis ($H$)** prevents rapid toggling when the two cells have similar signal levels.  
- **Time‑to‑Trigger ($T_{\text{TTT}}$)** filters out short‑term fluctuations caused by fast fading.

---

### 3. Handover Types

| Type | Description | Typical Use |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Hard handover** (break‑before‑make) | UE disconnects from the serving cell before establishing the new link. | Most LTE/NR deployments (single‑carrier). |
| **Soft handover** (make‑before‑break) | UE simultaneously connects to both cells; the connection to the serving cell is released after the target link is confirmed. | CDMA/UMTS legacy, some dual‑connect scenarios. |
| **Conditional handover** | Target cell parameters are pre‑negotiated; UE executes handover automatically when the condition is met, reducing latency. | 5G NR (Release 15+). |

---

### 4. Mobility Management Procedures

1. **Tracking Area Update (TAU)** – When a UE detects that it has entered a new TA, it sends a TAU request to the Mobility Management Entity (MME) or Access and Mobility Management Function (AMF). This updates the subscriber’s location context.  

2. **Paging** – If the network needs to reach a UE, it pages the entire TA. Efficient TA design (size vs. paging load) is a key optimisation problem.  

3. **RRC Measurement Reporting** – UE periodically measures $R_{\text{serv}}$ and $R_{\text{tgt}}$ for a configured set of neighbor cells and reports when the handover metric exceeds the threshold.

---

### 5. Worked Example

A UE travels at $v = 60\ \text{km/h}$ (≈ 16.7 m/s). The serving cell reports $R_{\text{serv}} = -85\ \text{dBm}$, while a neighboring cell measures $R_{\text{tgt}} = -78\ \text{dBm}$. The network configures $H = 3\ \text{dB}$ and $T_{\text{TTT}} = 200\ \text{ms}$.

1. **Compute the metric**  

$$
M = (-78) - (-85) - 3 = 4\ \text{dB}.
$$

Since $M > 0$, the condition is satisfied.

2. **Check the time condition** – Assume the UE’s measurement reports the condition continuously for $250\ \text{ms}$ (greater than $T_{\text{TTT}}$). The handover is therefore triggered.

3. **Handover distance**  

$$
D_{\text{HO}} = v \times T_{\text{TTT}} = 16.7\ \text{m/s} \times 0.2\ \text{s} = 3.34\ \text{m}.
$$

Thus the UE will travel only about 3 m after the condition is first met before the handover command is executed—a typical value that balances responsiveness and robustness.

4. **Probability of failure (simplified model)**  

If the signal‑strength process can be approximated by an exponential decay with $\lambda = 0.05\ \text{s}^{-1}$,  

$$
P_{\text{HF}} = e^{-\lambda T_{\text{TTT}}}=e^{-0.05 \times 0.2}=e^{-0.01}\approx 0.99.
$$

In this simplistic model the failure probability is low (≈ 1 %). Real‑world $P_{\text{HF}}$ also depends on radio link quality, load, and timing of the handover command.

---

### 6. Common Mistakes and Practical Notes

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | Mitigation |
|---------|---------------|------------|
| **Too small hysteresis** | Leads to “ping‑pong” handovers when two cells have similar coverage. | Use $H$ values of 3–6 dB for macro‑macro handovers; increase for dense small‑cell scenarios. |
| **Excessively large $T_{\text{TTT}}$** | Delays handover, causing the UE to drop below a usable signal before the command arrives. | Tune $T_{\text{TTT}}$ according to UE speed: e.g., 100 ms for high‑speed (≥ 120 km/h), 300 ms for pedestrian. |
| **Ignoring UE speed in TA design** | Large TAs reduce TAU signalling but increase paging latency for fast movers. | Partition high‑speed corridors (e.g., highways) into smaller TAs. |
| **Relying solely on RSRP** | RSRP does not capture interference; a cell may have strong signal but poor SINR. | Combine RSRP with Reference Signal Received Quality (RSRQ) or SINR thresholds. |
| **Assuming hard handover is always optimal** | In ultra‑dense deployments, simultaneous connections can reduce interruption. | Evaluate conditional or make‑before‑break handovers for critical services (e.g., URLLC). |

Finally, remember that handover is not only a radio‑level decision but also tightly coupled with core‑network procedures (TAU, paging, bearer re‑establishment). A holistic view—covering measurement configuration, decision thresholds, and signalling flow—is essential for a robust mobility management design.
