## 正文

In a cellular system the *capacity* of a cell (or a sector) is the maximum amount of traffic that can be carried while still satisfying the operator‑defined quality‑of‑service (QoS) targets, such as blocking probability for voice or packet loss rate for data. *Load balancing* is the set of mechanisms that redistribute traffic among neighboring cells/sectors so that no single element becomes a bottleneck while others are under‑utilised. Proper capacity planning and dynamic load balancing are essential for:

* Maintaining acceptable call‑setup success and data‑throughput levels.
* Reducing the frequency of radio‑resource failures (e.g., excessive retransmissions, high latency).
* Extending the useful lifetime of a network before costly hardware upgrades are required.
* Enabling efficient use of spectrum, which is a scarce and expensive resource.

---

### 1. Key Definitions

| Symbol | Definition | Unit |
|--------|------------|------|
| $A$ | **Offered traffic** (Erlangs). The product of call arrival rate $\lambda$ (calls s⁻¹) and average call holding time $T$ (s). | Erl (dimensionless) |
| $\lambda$ | Call (or session) arrival rate. | calls s⁻¹ |
| $T$ | Mean holding time per call/session. | s |
| $N$ | Number of **available channels** (e.g., voice circuits, PRBs, resource blocks). | dimensionless |
| $B$ | **Blocking probability** (probability that a new call is denied service). | dimensionless |
| $E_B(A,N)$ | Erlang‑B formula (loss system). | dimensionless |
| $E_C(A,N)$ | Erlang‑C formula (delay system). | dimensionless |
| $\rho$ | **Load factor** (traffic per channel). $\rho = \frac{A}{N}$. | dimensionless |
| $C_{\text{cell}}$ | **Cell capacity** (maximum sustainable traffic). | Erl |
| $L_i$ | Load of cell $i$ (e.g., measured as PRB utilisation ratio). | dimensionless (0–1) |
| $LB_{ij}$ | **Load‑balancing factor** from cell $i$ to neighbour $j$. | dimensionless |
| $S$ | Spectral efficiency (bits s⁻¹ Hz⁻¹). | bits s⁻¹ Hz⁻¹ |
| $BW$ | Allocated bandwidth. | Hz |
| $SINR$ | Signal‑to‑interference‑plus‑noise ratio. | dB (linear used in formulas) |

---

### 2. Capacity Modeling

#### 2.1 Voice/Circuit‑Switched Capacity (Erlang B)

For a pure loss system (no queuing), the blocking probability is given by the Erlang‑B formula:

$$
B = E_B(A,N) = \frac{\displaystyle \frac{A^{N}}{N!}}{\displaystyle \sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{A^{k}}{k!}} .
$$

The operator chooses a target $B_{\text{target}}$ (e.g., 2 %). Solving the equation for $N$ yields the **minimum number of channels** required to support an offered load $A$ while meeting the target.

#### 2.2 Data/Packet‑Switched Capacity (Shannon Approximation)

For broadband services, capacity is often expressed in bits per second (bps). Using the Shannon‑Hartley bound as a first‑order estimate:

$$
C_{\text{cell}} = BW \times S = BW \times \log_2\bigl(1 + \text{SINR}_{\text{lin}}\bigr) .
$$

Here $\text{SINR}_{\text{lin}}$ is the linear (non‑dB) SINR value. The spectral efficiency $S$ captures modulation and coding scheme (MCS) selection.

---

### 3. Load Balancing Metrics

A common real‑time load metric for LTE/NR is the **PRB utilisation ratio**:

$$
L_i = \frac{\text{Number of PRBs used in cell }i}{\text{Total PRBs in cell }i}.
$$

Load balancing aims to keep $L_i$ within a predefined window, e.g., $0.6 \le L_i \le 0.8$. The *load‑balancing factor* $LB_{ij}$ controls the probability that a handover request from cell $i$ is directed to neighbour $j$:

$$
LB_{ij} = \frac{L_{\max} - L_j}{\sum_{k\in \mathcal{N}_i}(L_{\max} - L_k)},
$$

where $L_{\max}$ is the upper load threshold and $\mathcal{N}_i$ is the set of neighbours of cell $i$. $LB_{ij}$ is recomputed periodically (typically every 5 s to 1 min) by the self‑optimising network (SON) function.

### 4. Worked Example

**Scenario:** A 3‑sector macrocell operates in the 1800 MHz band with 50 Physical Resource Blocks (PRBs) per sector. The operator targets a downlink PRB utilisation of at most 75 % and a blocking probability of 2 % for voice calls. The average voice call uses 1 PRB for its entire duration.

1. **Voice capacity using Erlang‑B**  
   Offered traffic per sector: $A = 30$ Erl (observed from traffic measurements).  
   We need $N$ such that $E_B(30,N) \le 0.02$.

   Using a lookup table or calculator, $N = 38$ channels yields $B \approx 0.018$, satisfying the target. Therefore each sector must reserve **38 PRBs** for voice.

2. **Data capacity after reserving voice**  
   Remaining PRBs per sector: $50 - 38 = 12$ PRBs.  
   Assume an average downlink SINR of 10 dB ($\text{SINR}_{\text{lin}} = 10$).  

   Spectral efficiency:  
   $$
   S = \log_2(1+10) \approx \log_2(11) \approx 3.46\ \text{bits/s/Hz}.
   $$

   LTE sub‑carrier spacing is 15 kHz, so one PRB occupies $12 \times 15\text{kHz}=180\text{kHz}$.  

   Data rate per sector:  
   $$
   C_{\text{data}} = 12\ \text{PRBs} \times 180\text{kHz} \times 3.46\ \frac{\text{bits}}{\text{s·Hz}} \approx 7.5\ \text{Mbps}.
   $$

3. **Load balancing check**  
   Current PRB utilisation (voice + data) = $\frac{38 + 4}{50}=0.84$, exceeding the 75 % threshold. The SON algorithm will increase $LB_{ij}$ toward less loaded neighbours, triggering handovers of some low‑priority data sessions to balance the load.

### 5. Common Mistakes & Practical Notes

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Correct Practice |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Using Erlang‑B for data traffic** | Erlang‑B assumes pure loss, no queuing, which is unrealistic for packet‑switched services. | Apply Shannon‑based or empirical throughput models for data; use Erlang‑C only for voice with queuing (e.g., call‑center). |
| **Confusing offered traffic $A$ with carried traffic** | $A$ is the demand before blocking; carried traffic is $A(1-B)$. | Always compute carried traffic when evaluating actual utilisation. |
| **Ignoring neighbour load when configuring $LB_{ij}$** | Static handover offsets may overload a neighbour that is already near $L_{\max}$. | Re‑evaluate $LB_{ij}$ periodically and incorporate neighbour $L_j$ in the denominator. |
| **Assuming SINR is constant across the cell** | Propagation, shadowing, and load‑induced interference cause spatial SINR variation. | Use sector‑averaged SINR or, better, a map‑based SINR distribution for capacity estimation. |
| **Over‑provisioning voice channels without considering traffic growth** | Fixed $N$ may become insufficient as $A$ rises, leading to higher blocking. | Perform dimensioning for peak‑hour traffic and include a growth factor (e.g., 10 % per year). |

**Practical tip:** In LTE/5G, the *eNodeB/gNB* exposes the **RAN Load Indicator (RLI)** as a 0–100 % value derived from PRB utilisation, CQI distribution, and scheduling backlog. Operators should monitor RLI alongside $L_i$ to detect hidden overloads caused by high‑order MCS failures.
