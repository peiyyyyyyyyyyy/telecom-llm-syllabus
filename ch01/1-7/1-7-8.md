# 1.7.8 最小化路測（MDT）

所屬：[1.7 網路優化與終端](../1-7.md)

## 正文

In a mature cellular network the traditional “drive test” – a vehicle equipped with a calibrated radio scanner that traverses the coverage area – is no longer the only source of radio‑performance data.  While drive tests still provide high‑resolution, ground‑truth measurements, they are costly, time‑consuming, and cannot keep up with rapid configuration changes in dense 4G/5G deployments.  The **Minimisation of Drive Tests (MDT)** concept addresses this limitation by leveraging measurements that are already collected by user equipment (UE) during normal traffic.  By configuring the network to request, aggregate, and analyse these **UE‑generated measurement reports**, operators can dramatically reduce the number of dedicated drive‑test runs while still obtaining statistically reliable coverage, interference, and hand‑over performance indicators.

MDT is defined in 3GPP TS 36.133 (LTE) and TS 38.133 (NR) as a set of procedures that (i) specify *what* measurements the UE shall collect, (ii) *when* and *how* the UE reports them (periodic, event‑triggered, or on‑demand), and (iii) how the network processes the collected data to infer radio‑environment metrics.  The key benefits are:

* **Cost reduction** – fewer vehicle deployments, lower labour.
* **Timeliness** – measurements are available as soon as the UE experiences a condition.
* **Granularity** – data is gathered from a large, diverse UE population, covering indoor, underground, and high‑mobility scenarios that are hard to reach with a drive‑test vehicle.

### Core Definitions

| Symbol | Definition | Unit |
|--------|------------|------|
| $M$ | Number of UE measurement reports collected for a given cell or area | – |
| $p$ | Probability that a single UE report detects a specific radio‑condition (e.g., RSRP < ‑110 dBm) | – |
| $P_{\text{det}}$ | Overall probability that the condition is detected by at least one report in the sample | – |
| $N_{\text{UE}}$ | Number of distinct UEs contributing reports in the observation period | – |
| $T_{\text{obs}}$ | Observation window length | seconds (s) |
| $R_{\text{rep}}$ | Reporting rate per UE (reports per second) | s$^{-1}$ |
| $L_{\text{lat}}$ | Latency between measurement occurrence and its availability at the network analytics platform | seconds (s) |
| $C_{\text{agg}}$ | Aggregation factor (e.g., number of reports combined before forwarding) | – |

**Detection probability** – Assuming independence of reports, the probability that at least one of the $M$ reports captures the condition is  

$$
P_{\text{det}} = 1-(1-p)^{M}.
\tag{1}
$$

If the desired detection confidence is $P_{\text{det}}^{\star}$, solving (1) for $M$ gives  

$$
M = \frac{\ln\!\bigl(1-P_{\text{det}}^{\star}\bigr)}{\ln\!\bigl(1-p\bigr)}.
\tag{2}
$$

**Reporting load** – The total number of reports arriving at the network in the observation window is  

$$
M = N_{\text{UE}} \times R_{\text{rep}} \times T_{\text{obs}}.
\tag{3}
$$

Combining (2) and (3) allows dimensioning of $N_{\text{UE}}$ or $R_{\text{rep}}$ to satisfy a target detection confidence.

### Measurement Types

| Type | Trigger | Typical Parameter(s) | Use Cases |
|------|---------|----------------------|-----------|
| **Periodic MDT** | Time‑based (e.g., every 10 s) | RSRP, RSRQ, SINR, PCI | Long‑term coverage maps |
| **Event‑based MDT** | Condition‑based (e.g., RSRP < ‑110 dBm) | Same as periodic + event ID | Detecting coverage holes, hand‑over failures |
| **On‑Demand MDT** | Network‑initiated request (e.g., after a complaint) | Subset of above | Targeted troubleshooting |

The UE may also include **location information** (GNSS, cell‑ID based) and **UE capability class** to enable weighted analysis (e.g., indoor vs outdoor devices).

### Worked Example

**Problem:** An operator wants at least a 95 % confidence ($P_{\text{det}}^{\star}=0.95$) of detecting any cell‑edge coverage hole where the probability that a single UE report falls below the RSRP threshold is $p=0.02$. The observation window is $T_{\text{obs}}=3600$ s (1 hour). Each UE is configured to send an event‑based MDT report on average every $R_{\text{rep}} = 0.001$ reports / s (i.e., one report per 1000 s).  

**Step 1 – Required number of reports $M$**  

$$
M = \frac{\ln(1-0.95)}{\ln(1-0.02)} = \frac{\ln(0.05)}{\ln(0.98)} \approx \frac{-2.996}{-0.0202} \approx 148.3.
$$

Thus at least **149 reports** are needed in the hour.

**Step 2 – Required number of distinct UEs**  

From (3):  

$$
M = N_{\text{UE}} \times R_{\text{rep}} \times T_{\text{obs}} \;\Rightarrow\;
N_{\text{UE}} = \frac{M}{R_{\text{rep}} \times T_{\text{obs}}}
= \frac{149}{0.001 \times 3600}
= \frac{149}{3.6}
\approx 41.4.
$$

Therefore the operator must ensure **at least 42 active UEs** in the target area during the hour. If the area normally hosts 200 UEs, the requirement is comfortably met; otherwise the operator may increase $R_{\text{rep}}$ (e.g., by lowering the event‑trigger threshold) or schedule a supplemental drive test.

**Interpretation:** By collecting the MDT reports from a modest UE population, the operator can detect the coverage hole with the same confidence that would traditionally require a dedicated drive test covering hundreds of kilometres.

### Practical Deployment Guidelines

1. **Balance reporting rate vs. signalling load.**  
   High $R_{\text{rep}}$ improves detection speed but can overload the Radio Access Network (RAN) control plane. Use aggregation ($C_{\text{agg}}$) or selective UE groups (e.g., high‑mobility UEs) to mitigate.

2. **Ensure measurement independence.**  
   Formula (1) assumes independent reports. Correlated measurements (e.g., many UEs on the same bus) reduce the effective $p$. Introduce spatial decorrelation by weighting reports with location variance.

3. **Latency awareness.**  
   $L_{\text{lat}}$ can be several seconds to minutes, depending on back‑haul and processing pipelines. Real‑time optimisation (e.g., hand‑over parameter tuning) must account for this delay.

4. **Privacy compliance.**  
   MDT may contain precise UE location. Operators must anonymise or aggregate data per regulatory requirements (e.g., GDPR in the EU).

5. **Hybrid validation.**  
   Periodically validate MDT‑derived maps against a limited drive test to calibrate $p$ and detect systematic biases (e.g., UE hardware differences).

### Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Impact |
|---------|----------------|--------|
| **Over‑estimating $p$** – assuming every UE report will detect the condition. | Ignoring that many UEs are deep inside buildings or on the far side of a street. | Leads to under‑provisioned $N_{\text{UE}}$, causing missed coverage holes. |
| **Setting $R_{\text{rep}}$ too high** without capacity analysis. | Desire for faster detection. | Increases signalling overhead, may trigger RRC congestion and degrade user experience. |
| **Neglecting UE capability diversity** (e.g., mixing LTE‑Category 1 and Category 20 devices). | Assuming all UEs have identical measurement accuracy. | Skews statistical confidence; low‑capability UEs may miss fast fading events. |
| **Using only periodic MDT** for rare events (e.g., deep fades). | Simplicity of periodic configuration. | Rare but critical events may never be captured, defeating the purpose of MDT. |
| **Failing to filter out outliers** (e.g., erroneous GPS coordinates). | Trusting raw UE data. | Produces artefacts in coverage maps, leading to misguided optimisation actions. |

By adhering to the formulas, properly configuring reporting triggers, and continuously validating the collected data, MDT becomes a powerful tool for **minimising drive‑test effort while preserving, or even enhancing, network optimisation insight**.

## 練習題

**1.** In the detection probability formula $P_{\text{det}} = 1-(1-p)^{M}$, which assumption is critical for its validity?

- A. All UE reports are collected from the same physical location.
- B. Each report is statistically independent of the others.
- C. The reporting interval $R_{\text{rep}}$ is constant for all UEs.
- D. The UE measurement hardware has identical sensitivity.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The formula derives from the complement of the probability that none of the $M$ independent reports detect the condition; independence is essential.

</details>

**2.** An operator configures event‑based MDT with a per‑UE reporting rate of $0.002$ reports/s. If 150 UEs are active and the observation window is 30 minutes, how many reports are expected?

- A. 540
- B. 270
- C. 1800
- D. 900

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：D。$M = N_{\text{UE}} \times R_{\text{rep}} \times T_{\text{obs}} = 150 \times 0.002 \times 1800 = 540$ reports; however, 30 minutes = 1800 s, so the correct calculation yields $150 \times 0.002 \times 1800 = 540$ reports. The correct answer is thus A. (Correction: the correct answer is A.)

</details>

**3.** Which 3GPP Technical Specification defines the MDT procedures for LTE?

- A. TS 36.133
- B. TS 38.133
- C. TS 23.501
- D. TS 24.301

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。TS 36.133 (E-UTRA) contains the MDT definition for LTE; TS 38.133 is for NR.

</details>

**4.** If the single‑report detection probability $p$ is 0.01 and the operator requires $P_{\text{det}}^{\star}=0.99$, approximately how many reports $M$ are needed?

- A. 46
- B. 100
- C. 459
- D. 230

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Using $M = \ln(1-0.99)/\ln(1-0.01) \approx \ln(0.01)/\ln(0.99) \approx -4.605/-0.01005 \approx 459$.

</details>

**5.** Which of the following is NOT a typical risk when configuring a very high MDT reporting rate?

- A. Increased RRC signalling load
- B. Higher latency in data processing
- C. Potential violation of UE battery life constraints
- D. Over‑estimation of coverage hole probability

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：D。A high reporting rate creates signalling and battery issues, but it does not directly cause over‑estimation of coverage hole probability; that stems from modelling errors.

</details>
