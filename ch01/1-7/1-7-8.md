# 1.7.8 最小化路測（MDT）

所屬：[1.7 網路優化與終端](../1-7.md)

## 正文

Minimization of Drive Tests (MDT) is a network‑side function that replaces—or at least reduces—the need for costly, time‑consuming physical drive‑test campaigns. Instead of equipping a vehicle with specialised measurement equipment, the network instructs user equipment (UE) to log radio‑environment measurements while the UE moves in the field. The collected logs are later analysed to infer coverage gaps, interference hot‑spots, and hand‑over performance. By leveraging the UE’s own measurement capabilities, operators obtain a statistically rich data set with far lower operational expense, while also limiting the impact on UE battery life and signalling load [TS 37.320 §4].

### MDT Modes

The specification defines two distinct MDT modes:

* **Logged MDT** – the UE records measurements locally and forwards them to the network at a later, possibly batched, time.  
* **Immediate MDT** – the UE transmits each measurement to the network as soon as it is taken.  

Both modes share the same logical measurement set, but differ in the timing of reporting [TS 37.320 §4].

### UE Measurement Configuration

MDT measurement configuration can be defined independently from the normal Radio Resource Management (RRM) configuration used for routine network optimisation. In practice, the availability of MDT data often depends on the UE’s RRM settings because the UE may only be able to log measurements that are already being performed for RRM [TS 37.320 §4].

### Decoupled Collection and Reporting

A measurement log consists of a sequence of events, each comprising one or more radio‑parameter values (e.g., RSRP, RSRQ) together with auxiliary data. The *collection interval* (when the UE samples) and the *reporting interval* (when the UE sends the log) are deliberately decoupled. This design limits UE battery consumption and reduces signalling overhead on the air interface [TS 37.320 §4].

### Geographical Scope

Network operators can restrict MDT logging to a predefined geographical area (e.g., a city district or a set of cells). Only measurements taken inside the configured area are stored in the log [TS 37.320 §4].

### Linking Measurements to Context

Each logged entry must be associated with:

* **Location information** – absolute or relative coordinates, or any data that can be used to derive a location [TS 37.320 §4].
* **Time stamp** – the absolute time at which the measurement was taken [TS 37.320 §4].
* **Sensor information** – optional data such as UE orientation (global coordinate system), uncompensated barometric pressure, and UE speed [TS 37.320 §4].

These links enable post‑processing tools to map radio performance onto a geographic map and to correlate measurements with UE motion.

### UE Capability‑Based Selection

The network may query UE capability information (e.g., supported sensors, positioning modules) and select only those terminals that can satisfy the required MDT data set [TS 37.320 §4].

### Independence from SON and TRACE

MDT solutions are designed to operate without mandatory Self‑Organising Network (SON) support, although re‑using SON functions is encouraged where possible [TS 37.320 §4]. MDT also re‑uses the subscriber/cell trace procedures defined in TS 32.422; when a specific UE is targeted, the signalling‑based trace is employed, otherwise a management‑based trace is used [TS 37.320 §4].

### Constraints on UE Measurements and Location

Because the UE measurement logging mechanism is optional, implementations should rely as much as possible on measurements already performed for RRM, thereby minimising additional processing and power draw [TS 37.320 §4]. When location information is required, the solution must consider the extra power consumption caused by activating the UE’s positioning components [TS 37.320 §4].

### Worked Example

Consider a UE roaming in a downtown area where the operator has enabled Logged MDT for a 1 km² zone. The UE’s MDT configuration specifies:

* Measurement type: RSRP (Reference Signal Received Power) every 30 s.
* Required context: GPS location, timestamp, and UE speed.
* Logging window: 0 s – 90 s (three samples).

During the logging window the UE records:

| Sample | Time (s) | GPS (lat,lon) | RSRP (dBm) | Speed (km/h) |
|--------|----------|---------------|------------|--------------|
| 1      | 0        | 25.0330,121.565| –85        | 0            |
| 2      | 30       | 25.0335,121.566| –78        | 15           |
| 3      | 60       | 25.0340,121.567| –82        | 30           |

The UE stores these three entries locally. After the UE exits the configured area (at t = 90 s) it transmits the entire log in a single NAS message, thereby reducing signalling bursts. The network can now plot the three points on a coverage map, observe that the RSRP dip at 30 s corresponds to a known building blockage, and adjust antenna tilt accordingly.

All elements of the example—periodic sampling, inclusion of location, timestamp, and sensor data, and deferred reporting—directly follow the requirements outlined in the specification [TS 37.320 §4].

### Common Pitfalls and Practical Tips

| Pitfall | Why it Happens | Mitigation |
|---------|----------------|------------|
| **Forgetting to define a geographic scope** | Operators may leave the MDT area unrestricted, causing the UE to log unnecessary data and waste battery. | Explicitly configure the MDT area in the network’s MDT policy. |
| **Requesting sensor data not supported by the UE** | UE capability may lack barometric pressure or orientation sensors. | Query UE capability first and tailor the MDT request accordingly. |
| **Assuming Immediate MDT reduces battery usage** | Immediate transmission can increase signalling load, contrary to the goal of battery preservation. | Use Logged MDT when the UE is battery‑constrained; reserve Immediate MDT for critical events. |
| **Relying on MDT without SON** | Although MDT can operate independently, some optimisation loops (e.g., automated hand‑over tuning) benefit from SON integration. | Consider coupling MDT analysis with SON functions where available. |
| **Neglecting the decoupling principle** | Configuring the UE to report after every sample defeats the purpose of decoupling. | Set reporting intervals longer than the measurement interval, respecting the UE’s power budget. |

By adhering to the guidelines above, engineers can deploy MDT efficiently, gaining high‑resolution radio‑environment insight while respecting UE resources. [TS 37.320 §4]

## 練習題

**1.** Which of the following statements correctly describes the two MDT modes defined in the specification?

- A. Logged MDT stores measurements locally and reports them later, while Immediate MDT sends each measurement as soon as it is taken.
- B. Logged MDT sends measurements immediately, while Immediate MDT stores them for later transmission.
- C. Both modes store measurements locally, but only Logged MDT includes location data.
- D. Immediate MDT does not require any UE measurement configuration.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。Logged MDT logs locally and forwards later; Immediate MDT transmits each measurement immediately, as defined in the reference.

</details>

**2.** How does the specification suggest limiting the impact of MDT on UE battery consumption?

- A. By requiring the UE to perform a full GPS fix for every measurement.
- B. By decoupling the measurement collection interval from the reporting interval.
- C. By disabling all RRM measurements during MDT logging.
- D. By mandating Immediate MDT for all UEs.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Decoupling collection and reporting reduces the frequency of signalling and thus saves battery.

</details>

**3.** Which optional contextual information may be linked to MDT measurements according to the specification?

- A. UE’s battery temperature.
- B. UE orientation in a global coordinate system.
- C. The operator’s pricing plan.
- D. The UE’s SIM card serial number.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Sensor information such as UE orientation is listed as optional data that can be linked to measurements.

</details>

**4.** What does the specification say about the relationship between MDT and Self‑Organising Network (SON) functions?

- A. MDT can only operate when SON is enabled in the network.
- B. MDT solutions are required to replace all SON functions.
- C. MDT can work independently from SON, but reuse of SON functions is encouraged where possible.
- D. SON must be disabled during MDT data collection.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。MDT is designed to be independent of SON, yet re‑using SON functions is beneficial.

</details>

**5.** According to the constraints in the specification, why must a solution that requires location information consider UE power consumption?

- A. Because location data is always transmitted over a dedicated high‑power channel.
- B. Because obtaining location information may require activating the UE’s positioning components, which consumes power.
- C. Because the network charges the UE for each location request.
- D. Because location information is only available when the UE is in idle mode.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Enabling positioning components to obtain location data adds to UE power usage, so solutions must account for this.

</details>
