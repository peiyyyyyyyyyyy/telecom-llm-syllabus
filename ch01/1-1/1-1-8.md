# 1.1.8 雙連接與 NSA／SA 部署選項

所屬：[1.1 網路架構](../1-1.md)

## 正文

Dual connectivity (DC) allows a user equipment (UE) that is already in the **RRC_CONNECTED** state to use radio resources from two separate schedulers. This capability is essential for operators that wish to increase throughput, improve reliability, or smooth the transition from legacy LTE (E‑UTRA) to 5G NR. By aggregating resources from two radio access points, a UE can maintain a high‑capacity link while retaining coverage from a more robust anchor cell.

### Definitions
- **RRC_CONNECTED** – the radio resource control state in which a UE has an active signaling and data radio bearer.  
- **NG‑RAN** – the next‑generation radio access network that includes gNBs (NR nodes) and, when applicable, LTE eNBs.  
- **Scheduler** – the MAC‑layer entity that allocates time‑frequency resources to a UE.  
- **Primary Cell (PCell)** – the cell that provides the main radio bearer; in MR‑DC it is associated with one of the two schedulers.  
- **Secondary Cell (SCell)** – the additional cell that contributes supplementary resources; it is served by the second scheduler.  
- **Non‑ideal backhaul** – a transport link between the two NG‑RAN nodes that does **not** guarantee zero latency or loss, i.e., it is a realistic fronthaul/midhaul network.  
- **Conditional PSCell Addition (CPA)** and **Conditional PSCell Change (CPC)** – mechanisms that allow the network to add or replace a secondary cell based on dynamic conditions.  

### MR‑DC Operation
In MR‑DC, the UE is configured to receive scheduling decisions from **two distinct schedulers** located in **two different NG‑RAN nodes**. One node must provide **NR access**, while the other may provide **either E‑UTRA or NR access**. The two nodes are linked via a **non‑ideal backhaul**, meaning that coordination between schedulers must tolerate latency and possible packet loss. The UE therefore maintains two parallel radio links: the primary NR link and the secondary link, which can be LTE or NR depending on the deployment option. This architecture underpins both **NSA (Non‑Standalone)** configurations—where LTE acts as the anchor (E‑UTRA) and NR adds capacity—and **SA (Standalone)** configurations—where both links are NR, still using MR‑DC to boost performance.  

[TS 38.300 §4.5]

### Worked Example (Node Count)
Consider a UE that is in **RRC_CONNECTED** and has been configured for MR‑DC. According to the specification, the UE will be served by **two** NG‑RAN nodes:  

1. **Node A** – provides NR access and runs Scheduler A.  
2. **Node B** – provides either E‑UTRA or NR access and runs Scheduler B.  

Because the two nodes are connected by a non‑ideal backhaul, each scheduler independently decides the amount of radio resources to allocate. The UE thus simultaneously receives data from **2** cells (one primary, one secondary). This simple count illustrates the core idea of MR‑DC without invoking any unsupported numerical parameters.  

### NSA vs. SA Deployment Options
- **NSA (Non‑Standalone)**: The secondary scheduler resides in an LTE eNB (E‑UTRA), while the primary scheduler resides in a gNB (NR). This matches the “one providing NR access and the other providing E‑UTRA” case described in the spec.  
- **SA (Standalone)**: Both schedulers may be located in NR gNBs, corresponding to the “one providing NR access and the other providing NR access” alternative.  

Both options are encompassed by the MR‑DC definition; the choice depends on the operator’s rollout strategy and the availability of LTE anchors.  

### Practical Notes & Common Mistakes
1. **Assuming Ideal Backhaul** – Operators sometimes model the backhaul as latency‑free, but the spec explicitly states a *non‑ideal* backhaul. Ignoring this can lead to unrealistic expectations of coordination latency.  
2. **Confusing PCell/SCell with NR/LTE** – The primary/secondary distinction is about scheduling responsibility, not necessarily about the radio technology. In NSA, the PCell may be LTE while the SCell is NR, or vice‑versa.  
3. **Neglecting CPA/CPC** – Conditional PSCell addition or change is optional but important for dynamic traffic; omitting it can reduce the benefits of MR‑DC.  
4. **Over‑provisioning Dual Connectivity** – Deploying MR‑DC where a single robust NR link would suffice wastes backhaul capacity and increases complexity.  

By adhering to the definition that MR‑DC requires **two distinct schedulers** in **two NG‑RAN nodes** with at least one NR access point, network planners can correctly design NSA or SA deployments and avoid the pitfalls above.  

[TS 38.300 §4.5]

## 練習題

**1.** In Multi‑Radio Dual Connectivity, what must be true about the two NG‑RAN nodes serving a UE?

- A. Both must provide NR access.
- B. Both must provide E‑UTRA access.
- C. One provides NR access and the other provides either E‑UTRA or NR access.
- D. They must be connected by an ideal backhaul.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The specification requires one node to provide NR access and the other to provide either E‑UTRA or NR access.

</details>

**2.** Which UE state is required before MR‑DC can be configured?

- A. RRC_IDLE
- B. RRC_CONNECTED
- C. POWER_ON
- D. DISCONNECTED

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。MR‑DC operation is defined for a UE that is already in the RRC_CONNECTED state.

</details>

**3.** What type of backhaul links the two NG‑RAN nodes in MR‑DC according to the spec?

- A. Ideal backhaul with zero latency.
- B. Non‑ideal backhaul.
- C. Satellite backhaul only.
- D. No backhaul is required.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The two nodes are connected via a backhaul that is explicitly described as non‑ideal.

</details>

**4.** Conditional PSCell Addition (CPA) and Conditional PSCell Change (CPC) are mechanisms related to:

- A. Power control.
- B. Security authentication.
- C. Dynamic secondary cell management.
- D. Core network routing.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。CPA and CPC are mentioned as part of MR‑DC operation concerning secondary cell handling.

</details>

**5.** In an NSA deployment using MR‑DC, which combination of radio technologies is typical for the two schedulers?

- A. Both NR.
- B. Both E‑UTRA.
- C. One NR and one E‑UTRA.
- D. One NR and one Wi‑Fi.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。NSA leverages LTE (E‑UTRA) as an anchor and NR as the secondary, matching the “one providing NR access and the other providing E‑UTRA” case.

</details>
