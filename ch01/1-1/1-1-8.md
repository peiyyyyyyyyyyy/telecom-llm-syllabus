# 1.1.8 雙連接與 NSA／SA 部署選項

所屬：[1.1 網路架構](../1-1.md)

## 正文

In 4G‑LTE and 5G‑NR networks the term *dual connectivity* (DC) refers to the simultaneous use of two distinct radio access points (RAPs) for the transmission of user‑plane data. In practice a device (UE) maintains a *master node* (MN) that controls the radio resource management (RRM) and a *secondary node* (SN) that provides additional data bearers. Dual connectivity is the cornerstone of two deployment philosophies for 5G:

* **NSA (Non‑Standalone)** – the 5G NR gNB is added to an existing LTE EPC, and the LTE eNodeB (eNB) remains the MN.  
* **SA (Standalone)** – the 5G core (5GC) and the NR gNB both act as MN and SN, with no LTE control plane involvement.

Understanding DC and the NSA/SA split is essential for network planners because it determines:

1. **Coverage strategy** – NSA can reuse LTE coverage while NR radios are rolled out, reducing the amount of new site construction.  
2. **Capacity scaling** – DC enables a UE to aggregate the throughput of two carriers (e.g., 20 MHz LTE + 100 MHz NR) without waiting for a full SA migration.  
3. **Service continuity** – During a hand‑over from LTE to NR, DC allows a “make‑before‑break” transition, minimizing session interruption.

---

### 1.1.8.1 Key Definitions

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| **MN** | Master Node (eNB in NSA, gNB in SA) that holds the PDCP, RRC, and mobility control. | – |
| **SN** | Secondary Node (gNB in NSA, another gNB in SA) that provides additional PDCP bearers. | – |
| **PDCP** | Packet Data Convergence Protocol – layer where data are split/combined for DC. | – |
| **DRB** | Data Radio Bearer – logical channel carrying user data. | – |
| **NR‑CA** | Carrier Aggregation within a single gNB (intra‑gNB). | – |
| **DC‑CA** | Carrier Aggregation across MN and SN (inter‑gNB). | – |
| **$R_{\text{total}}$** | Aggregate user‑plane throughput (bits per second). | bps |
| **$R_{\text{MN}}$, $R_{\text{SN}}$** | Throughput contributed by MN and SN respectively. | bps |

When DC is active, the total downlink throughput can be approximated as the sum of the two independent links, assuming no scheduling overlap:

$$
R_{\text{total}} = R_{\text{MN}} + R_{\text{SN}} \tag{1}
$$

If the MN uses LTE and the SN uses NR, the spectral efficiencies ($\eta$) differ:

$$
R_{\text{MN}} = \eta_{\text{LTE}} \times B_{\text{LTE}} \quad ; \quad
R_{\text{SN}} = \eta_{\text{NR}} \times B_{\text{NR}} \tag{2}
$$

where $B$ denotes the allocated bandwidth (MHz) and $\eta$ the achieved spectral efficiency (bits/s/Hz).  

---

### 1.1.8.2 NSA vs. SA – Architectural Differences

| Aspect | NSA (LTE‑anchored) | SA (NR‑only) |
|--------|-------------------|--------------|
| **Control Plane** | LTE‑RRC, EPC (MME, SGW, PGW) | NR‑RRC, 5GC (AMF, SMF, UPF) |
| **User Plane** | Split: LTE PDCP → LTE MAC → LTE PHY; NR PDCP → NR MAC → NR PHY | Single 5GC UPF; PDCP split may still exist between gNBs |
| **Reference Points** | X2 (LTE‑LTE), Xn (LTE‑NR) for DC | Xn (NR‑NR) for DC |
| **Initial Access** | UE connects first to LTE eNB (PCell) | UE can attach directly to NR gNB (PCell) if coverage permits |
| **Deployment Path** | Add gNBs to existing LTE sites → fast rollout | Deploy gNBs with full 5GC support → higher CAPEX/OPEX but future‑proof |

The **NSA option** is defined in 3GPP TS 38.300 clause 5.2.2 and TS 36.300 for LTE. The **SA option** follows the same series but with the NR‑only configuration (TS 38.300 clause 5.2.1). Both specifications require the Xn interface to support the *DC data split* at the PDCP layer.

---

### 1.1.8.3 Worked Example

A carrier wishes to evaluate the capacity gain of adding an NR gNB (SN) to an existing LTE eNB (MN) in an NSA deployment.

* LTE eNB: allocated bandwidth $B_{\text{LTE}} = 20\,$MHz, measured spectral efficiency $\eta_{\text{LTE}} = 1.8\,$bits/s/Hz.  
* NR gNB: allocated bandwidth $B_{\text{NR}} = 100\,$MHz, measured spectral efficiency $\eta_{\text{NR}} = 4.5\,$bits/s/Hz.

Compute the expected aggregate downlink throughput $R_{\text{total}}$.

**Solution**

1. Compute LTE contribution using (2):  
   $$
   R_{\text{MN}} = 1.8\;\frac{\text{bits}}{\text{s·Hz}} \times 20\;\text{MHz}
   = 1.8 \times 20 \times 10^{6}
   = 36 \times 10^{6}\ \text{bps} = 36\ \text{Mbps}.
   $$

2. Compute NR contribution:  
   $$
   R_{\text{SN}} = 4.5\;\frac{\text{bits}}{\text{s·Hz}} \times 100\;\text{MHz}
   = 4.5 \times 100 \times 10^{6}
   = 450 \times 10^{6}\ \text{bps} = 450\ \text{Mbps}.
   $$

3. Aggregate throughput from (1):  
   $$
   R_{\text{total}} = 36\ \text{Mbps} + 450\ \text{Mbps} = 486\ \text{Mbps}.
   $$

Thus, dual connectivity yields roughly a **13.5‑fold** increase over LTE‑only (36 Mbps → 486 Mbps) without requiring the UE to switch cells.

---

### 1.1.8.4 Practical Notes & Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it Happens | Mitigation |
|---------|----------------|------------|
| **Assuming linear throughput addition** | Overlaps in scheduling, back‑haul bottlenecks, or PDCP re‑ordering can reduce the effective sum. | Verify Xn back‑haul capacity ≥ $R_{\text{total}}$ and enable *split bearer* coordination. |
| **Neglecting UE capability** | Not all devices support NR‑LTE DC; some only support intra‑band CA. | Query UE capability via RRC Capability Information before offering DC. |
| **Mixing SA and NSA in the same cell** | Control‑plane confusion if a UE is attached to an SA gNB but receives a DC anchor from an LTE eNB. | Enforce a consistent deployment mode per PLMN; use *mode‑selection* flags in the AMF. |
| **Improper Xn configuration** | Incorrect QoS mapping between MN and SN leads to packet loss. | Align QoS Class Identifier (QCI) and 5QI mappings across the Xn interface; test with traffic generators. |
| **Under‑estimating power consumption** | Dual active radios double the UE’s RF power draw, affecting battery life. | Implement *dynamic DC* where the SN is activated only for high‑throughput sessions. |

Finally, remember that DC is a *radio‑layer* feature; the core network (EPC or 5GC) sees only the aggregated PDCP flows. Consequently, capacity planning for the core must consider the *effective* number of bearers rather than the raw number of physical carriers.

--- 

**Key Takeaway:** Dual connectivity offers a flexible bridge between LTE and 5G NR, enabling operators to reap capacity gains early (NSA) while preserving a clean migration path to a fully 5G‑standalone network. Correct dimensioning of the Xn interface, awareness of UE capabilities, and careful QoS alignment are essential to avoid over‑promising performance.

## 練習題

**1.** In an NSA deployment, which node typically serves as the Master Node (MN) for dual connectivity?

- A. The 5G NR gNB
- B. The LTE eNB
- C. The 5GC AMF
- D. The UPF

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。In NSA, LTE remains the control‑plane anchor, so the LTE eNB is the MN.

</details>

**2.** Which formula correctly expresses the aggregate downlink throughput for a UE using dual connectivity?

- A. $R_{\text{total}} = \max(R_{\text{MN}}, R_{\text{SN}})$
- B. $R_{\text{total}} = R_{\text{MN}} \times R_{\text{SN}}$
- C. $R_{\text{total}} = R_{\text{MN}} + R_{\text{SN}}$
- D. $R_{\text{total}} = \frac{R_{\text{MN}} + R_{\text{SN}}}{2}$

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Dual connectivity adds the separate data rates from MN and SN, assuming no overlap.

</details>

**3.** Which 3GPP technical specification defines the NSA dual connectivity architecture?

- A. TS 38.300
- B. TS 36.300
- C. TS 23.501
- D. TS 38.331

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：A。TS 38.300 (NR) includes clause 5.2.2 describing NSA deployment with LTE‑anchored DC.

</details>

**4.** A UE that supports only intra‑band carrier aggregation but not inter‑RAT dual connectivity will:

- A. Be able to use LTE‑NR DC.
- B. Fail to attach to any 5G NR cell.
- C. Be limited to LTE‑only service in NSA.
- D. Automatically switch to SA mode.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Without inter‑RAT DC capability, the UE cannot combine LTE and NR; it remains LTE‑only in NSA.

</details>

**5.** Which of the following is NOT a common cause of reduced throughput when dual connectivity is deployed?

- A. Insufficient Xn back‑haul capacity
- B. UE battery saving mode disabling the secondary radio
- C. Perfectly synchronized scheduling between MN and SN
- D. Mismatched QoS mapping across the Xn interface

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。Perfect synchronization would *increase* throughput; the other options can degrade performance.

</details>
