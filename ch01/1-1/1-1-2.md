## 正文

In a mobile system the **Wireless Access Network (RAN)** is the bridge between user equipment (UE) and the core network. Its performance directly determines coverage, capacity, latency, and ultimately the quality of service perceived by subscribers. The RAN is composed of **base stations (BS)**, the **antenna systems** that radiate and receive radio waves, and the **frequency bands** allocated for transmission. Understanding how these three elements interact is essential for designing, dimensioning, and troubleshooting 4G (LTE) and 5G NR deployments.

---

### 1.1 Base Station (BS)

A base station is a physical node that houses radio transceiver hardware, digital baseband processing, and, in modern 5G, a split architecture (CU/DU/RU). For the purpose of this subsection we treat the BS as the **gNB** (5G) or **eNB** (LTE) that controls one or more **cells**.

Key parameters:

| Symbol | Meaning | Typical Unit |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| $P_{\text{TX}}$ | Transmit power (output of the PA) | dBm |
| $G_{\text{TX}}$ | Antenna gain (Tx) | dBi |
| $G_{\text{RX}}$ | Antenna gain (Rx) | dBi |
| $L_{\text{cable}}$ | Cable and connector loss | dB |
| $E_{\text{IRP}}$ | Effective Isotropic Radiated Power | dBm |
| $f$ | Carrier frequency | MHz or GHz |
| $\lambda$ | Wavelength = $c/f$ | m |
| $B$ | System bandwidth | MHz |
| $N_{\text{PRB}}$ | Number of Physical Resource Blocks | – |
| $T_{\text{slot}}$ | Slot duration (5G) | ms |

The **Effective Isotropic Radiated Power (EIRP)**, the quantity that is regulated by spectrum authorities, is

$$
E_{\text{IRP}} = P_{\text{TX}} + G_{\text{TX}} - L_{\text{cable}} \quad \text{[dBm]}. \tag{1}
$$

---

### 1.2 Antenna Systems

Antenna design determines both **gain** and **radiation pattern**. In macro‑cell deployments the typical configuration is a **panel array** (e.g., 8 × 2 elements) with a downtilt angle to shape the coverage footprint. For higher frequencies (mmWave, $>$24 GHz) **phased‑array** or **lens** antennas are used to achieve narrow beams and high gains (30 dBi or more).

Two important antenna metrics:

* **Half‑power beamwidth (HPBW)** – angular width where gain falls to –3 dB.
* **Electrical tilt** – the angle by which the main lobe is tilted downward (positive values) to limit interference and control cell radius.

Antenna gain for an array can be approximated by

$$
G_{\text{array}} \approx 10 \log_{10}(N_{\text{elem}}) + G_{\text{elem}} \quad \text{[dBi]}, \tag{2}
$$

where $N_{\text{elem}}$ is the number of radiating elements and $G_{\text{elem}}$ is the gain of a single element.

---

### 1.3 Frequency Bands

The **frequency band** determines propagation characteristics, available bandwidth, and regulatory constraints. 4G LTE uses bands from 600 MHz up to 3.8 GHz, while 5G NR adds **FR1** (410 MHz–7.125 GHz) and **FR2** (24.25 GHz–52.6 GHz). The **free‑space path loss (FSPL)** is

$$
\text{FSPL}(d) = 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi d}{\lambda}\right) \quad \text{[dB]}, \tag{3}
$$

where $d$ is the link distance (m) and $\lambda = c/f$ is the wavelength.

Higher frequencies suffer larger FSPL (∝ $f^{2}$) and have poorer diffraction, leading to smaller cell radii but larger available bandwidth.

---

### 1.4 Worked Example – Estimating Cell Radius for a 3.5 GHz 5G gNB

**Given**

* Transmit power $P_{\text{TX}} = 30$ dBm (1 W)
* Antenna array gain $G_{\text{TX}} = 18$ dBi
* Cable loss $L_{\text{cable}} = 2$ dB
* Receiver sensitivity $S_{\text{RX}} = -95$ dBm (minimum $E_{\text{IRP}}$ required at UE)
* Carrier frequency $f = 3.5$ GHz
* Propagation exponent $\alpha = 3.5$ (urban macro)
* No additional shadow fading margin

**Step 1 – Compute EIRP**

$$
E_{\text{IRP}} = 30\ \text{dBm} + 18\ \text{dBi} - 2\ \text{dB} = 46\ \text{dBm}.
$$

**Step 2 – Convert to linear units for link budget**

$$
E_{\text{IRP}}^{\text{lin}} = 10^{(46/10)}\ \text{mW} = 39{,}810\ \text{mW}.
$$

**Step 3 – Determine the maximum path loss that can be tolerated**

$$
L_{\max} = E_{\text{IRP}} - S_{\text{RX}} = 46\ \text{dBm} - (-95\ \text{dBm}) = 141\ \text{dB}.
$$

**Step 4 – Solve for distance using the log‑distance path loss model**

Log‑distance model:

$$
L(d) = \text{FSPL}(d_0) + 10\alpha \log_{10}\!\left(\frac{d}{d_0}\right),
$$

choose reference distance $d_0 = 1$ m. FSPL at 1 m:

$$
\text{FSPL}(1\text{ m}) = 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi}{\lambda}\right) = 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi \cdot 3.5\times10^{9}}{3\times10^{8}}\right) \approx 31.8\ \text{dB}.
$$

Set $L(d) = L_{\max}$:

$$
141 = 31.8 + 10\alpha \log_{10}(d) \\
\Rightarrow 10\alpha \log_{10}(d) = 109.2 \\
\Rightarrow \log_{10}(d) = \frac{109.2}{10\alpha} = \frac{109.2}{35} \approx 3.12 \\
\Rightarrow d \approx 10^{3.12} \approx 1{,}320\ \text{m}.
$$

**Result:** Under the stated assumptions the gNB can reliably serve UEs out to roughly **1.3 km**. In practice a margin for shadowing (e.g., 8 dB) and for hand‑over overlap reduces the planning cell radius to about **1 km**.

### 1.5 Common Mistakes & Practical Notes

| Mistake | Why it matters | Practical tip |
|---------|----------------|---------------|
| **Ignoring cable loss** in (1) – assuming $L_{\text{cable}}=0$ | Over‑estimates EIRP, may cause regulatory violations | Always include measured insertion loss (typically 1–3 dB for macro panels) |
| **Using antenna gain in dBi as if it were isotropic** | Gains are directional; UE may see lower gain off‑boresight | Apply the antenna pattern (HPBW, downtilt) to compute effective gain for edge users |
| **Applying free‑space loss at sub‑6 GHz for urban macro** | FSPL ignores reflections, diffraction, and shadowing, leading to optimistic range | Use the log‑distance model with an appropriate exponent (3–4) and add a shadow‑fading margin (6–10 dB) |
| **Selecting a band solely on bandwidth** without considering penetration loss | Higher bands (mmWave) cannot provide indoor coverage | Pair FR2 with a lower‑frequency overlay (e.g., 3.5 GHz) for indoor service |
| **Assuming all 5G NR carriers have the same slot length** | Slot duration varies with numerology ($\mu$) | Remember $T_{\text{slot}} = 1\ \text{ms} / 2^{\mu}$; for $\mu=2$, $T_{\text{slot}}=0.25$ ms. |

Finally, remember that **regulatory limits** on EIRP differ per band and per region (e.g., 3 GPP TS 38.104 defines maximum EIRP for each NR band). Always verify compliance before finalizing BS configuration.
