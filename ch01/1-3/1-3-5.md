## 正文

Network slicing is a core tenet of 5G and beyond, enabling a single physical infrastructure to host multiple logical networks—*slices*—each tailored to a distinct service class (e.g., eMBB, URLLC, mMTC). A slice is defined by a set of end‑to‑end resource guarantees (bandwidth, latency, reliability, security, etc.) that are enforced by the radio access network (RAN), transport network, and core. By partitioning resources in a programmable way, operators can simultaneously support high‑throughput video streaming, ultra‑low‑latency industrial control, and massive sensor deployments without over‑provisioning the whole system.

Why it matters  
* **Economics:** A single shared radio site can serve multiple business models, reducing capex and opex.  
* **Performance isolation:** Guarantees for one slice are not jeopardized by traffic spikes in another, which is essential for safety‑critical URLLC services.  
* **Flexibility:** Slices can be instantiated, scaled, or terminated on demand through software‑defined networking (SDN) and network function virtualization (NFV) orchestrators.

### 1.3.5.1 Key Definitions  

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $S$ | Set of all slices on a given network | – |
| $s \in S$ | A particular slice (e.g., $s_{\text{eMBB}}$, $s_{\text{URLLC}}$) | – |
| $R_s$ | Aggregate data rate guaranteed for slice $s$ | bit/s (bps) |
| $L_s$ | Maximum one‑way latency budget for slice $s$ | seconds (s) |
| $P_s$ | Packet loss/reliability target for slice $s$ (e.g., $10^{-5}$) | – |
| $B_{\text{tot}}$ | Total spectrum bandwidth allocated to the gNB | Hz |
| $N_{\text{PRB}}^{\text{tot}}$ | Total number of Physical Resource Blocks (PRBs) per subframe | – |
| $N_{\text{PRB}}^{s}$ | PRBs assigned to slice $s$ | – |
| $\Delta f$ | Subcarrier spacing (e.g., 30 kHz for FR1, 120 kHz for FR2) | Hz |
| $T_{\text{sub}}$ | Subframe duration (e.g., 1 ms) | s |
| $\eta$ | Spectral efficiency of the modulation‑coding scheme (MCS) used for slice $s$ | bit/s/Hz |
| $C_{\text{Shannon}}$ | Theoretical channel capacity per PRB | bit/s/PRB |

A **slice type** (or *service category*) is a pre‑defined template (eMBB, URLLC, mMTC) that specifies $R_s$, $L_s$, $P_s$, and optional security or connectivity requirements. A **slice instance** is a concrete realization of a slice type, identified by a unique Slice ID (S‑ID) and bound to a set of network functions (e.g., AMF, SMF, UPF) that are logically isolated.

### 1.3.5.2 Fundamental Formulas  

1. **PRB allocation per slice**  
   The number of PRBs required to meet the rate $R_s$ under a given spectral efficiency $\eta_s$ is:

   $$
   N_{\text{PRB}}^{s}= \left\lceil \frac{R_s}{\eta_s \, \Delta f} \right\rceil .
   $$

   The denominator $\eta_s \, \Delta f$ yields the achievable bit rate per PRB per subframe (ignoring overhead).  

2. **Latency feasibility**  
   For a slice that traverses the RAN, transport, and core, the end‑to‑end latency $L_s$ must satisfy:

   $$
   L_s \geq T_{\text{proc}}^{\text{RAN}} + T_{\text{proc}}^{\text{TRAN}} + T_{\text{proc}}^{\text{CORE}} + N_{\text{sub}} \, T_{\text{sub}},
   $$

   where $T_{\text{proc}}^{\cdot}$ are processing delays of each domain and $N_{\text{sub}}$ is the number of subframes needed to transmit the data payload.  

3. **Isolation metric**  
   A simple isolation index $I_{s_1,s_2}$ between two slices $s_1$ and $s_2$ can be expressed as the ratio of shared PRBs to total PRBs:

   $$
   I_{s_1,s_2}=1-\frac{N_{\text{PRB}}^{s_1}\cap N_{\text{PRB}}^{s_2}}{\min\!\left(N_{\text{PRB}}^{s_1},N_{\text{PRB}}^{s_2}\right)} .
   $$

   $I=1$ indicates perfect isolation (no overlap), $I=0$ indicates full sharing.

4. **Resource budget constraint**  

   $$
   \sum_{s\in S} N_{\text{PRB}}^{s} \le N_{\text{PRB}}^{\text{tot}} .
   $$

   This ensures that the sum of allocated PRBs does not exceed the physical limit.

### 1.3.5.3 Worked Example  

Consider a 5G gNB operating at 28 GHz (FR2) with a total carrier bandwidth $B_{\text{tot}} = 100\,$MHz and subcarrier spacing $\Delta f = 120\,$kHz. The subframe duration is $T_{\text{sub}} = 0.125\,$ms (i.e., 8 slots per subframe). The total number of PRBs per subframe is:

$$
N_{\text{PRB}}^{\text{tot}} = \frac{B_{\text{tot}}}{\Delta f}= \frac{100 \times 10^{6}}{120 \times 10^{3}} \approx 833 \text{ PRBs}.
$$

Three slices must be instantiated:

| Slice | Service | $R_s$ (Mbps) | Target $\eta_s$ (bit/s/Hz) |
|-------|---------|--------------|----------------------------|
| $s_{\text{eMBB}}$ | High‑throughput video | 1 000 | 4 |
| $s_{\text{URLLC}}$| Factory automation | 200 | 2 |
| $s_{\text{mMTC}}$ | Sensor aggregation | 50 | 0.5 |

Compute $N_{\text{PRB}}^{s}$ for each slice:

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\text{PRB}}^{\text{eMBB}} &= \left\lceil \frac{1\,000 \times 10^{6}}{4 \times 120 \times 10^{3}} \right\rceil
 = \left\lceil \frac{1\,000\,000\,000}{480\,000} \right\rceil \approx 2085,\\[4pt]
N_{\text{PRB}}^{\text{URLLC}} &= \left\lceil \frac{200 \times 10^{6}}{2 \times 120 \times 10^{3}} \right\rceil
 = \left\lceil \frac{200\,000\,000}{240\,000} \right\rceil \approx 834,\\[4pt]
N_{\text{PRB}}^{\text{mMTC}} &= \left\lceil \frac{50 \times 10^{6}}{0.5 \times 120 \times 10^{3}} \right\rceil
 = \left\lceil \frac{50\,000\,000}{60\,000} \right\rceil \approx 834.
\end{aligned}
$$

The sum $2085+834+834 = 3753$ PRBs exceeds the available $833$, indicating that the initial guarantees are infeasible. A common practical approach is **dynamic slicing**: allocate a baseline of $N_{\text{PRB}}^{\text{eMBB}}=400$, $N_{\text{PRB}}^{\text{URLLC}}=200$, $N_{\text{PRB}}^{\text{mMTC}}=233$ (total 833) and allow on‑demand scaling for eMBB when URLLC and mMTC traffic are below their peak. The URLLC slice can further reduce latency by using mini‑slots (e.g., 2‑symbol slots) which effectively halves $T_{\text{sub}}$ for its traffic, satisfying a $L_{\text{URLLC}} = 5$ ms budget.

### 1.3.5.4 Practical Notes & Common Mistakes  

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | Remedy |
|---------|---------------|--------|
| **Ignoring control‑plane resources** | Slicing discussions often focus on user‑plane PRBs, but the AMF/SMF signaling also consumes resources. | Include a separate control‑plane budget (e.g., $R_s^{\text{CP}}$) when dimensioning slices. |
| **Static PRB allocation** | Fixed partitions cannot adapt to traffic bursts, leading to under‑utilization or QoS violations. | Deploy *elastic* slices with a guaranteed minimum and a shared pool that can be borrowed under policy control. |
| **Over‑looking isolation** | Sharing PRBs without proper scheduling may cause a heavy eMBB load to increase URLLC latency. | Enforce isolation at the scheduler level (e.g., dedicated PRBs or priority queuing) and monitor $I_{s_1,s_2}$ periodically. |
| **Mismatched latency budgeting** | Using only radio‑link latency while neglecting transport/core delays yields optimistic $L_s$ estimates. | Apply the end‑to‑end latency formula and measure each domain’s processing time. |
| **Assuming a single spectral efficiency** | Different slices may use different MCS; using a common $\eta$ leads to mis‑allocation. | Compute $N_{\text{PRB}}^{s}$ per slice with its own $\eta_s$, reflecting channel conditions and QoS. |

**Standard reference:** 3GPP TS 23.501, *System Architecture for the 5G System*, defines the slice architecture, slice types, and management procedures.
