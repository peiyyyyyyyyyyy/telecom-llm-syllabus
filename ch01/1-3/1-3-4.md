## 正文

*What is roaming and why does it matter?*  
Roaming is the capability of a mobile subscriber to obtain **continuity of service** while moving outside the coverage area of its **Home Public Land Mobile Network (H‑PLMN)** and into a **Visited Public Land Mobile Network (V‑PLMN)**. In commercial terms, roaming enables users to keep making voice calls, sending SMS/MMS, and using data services when they travel internationally or across domestic operator boundaries. For operators, roaming is a major source of revenue (inter‑operator settlement) and a critical component of network‑level quality of experience (QoE); for regulators, it is a lever to ensure non‑discriminatory access and to protect consumers from excessive fees.

---

### 1. Core concepts and definitions  

| Symbol | Definition | Unit |
|--------|------------|------|
| **H‑PLMN** | Home network that owns the subscriber’s SIM/USIM | – |
| **V‑PLMN** | Visited network that provides radio access to the subscriber | – |
| **HLR** | Home Location Register – database of subscriber profiles in H‑PLMN | – |
| **VLR** | Visited Location Register – temporary copy of subscriber data in V‑PLMN | – |
| **MSC / MME / AMF** | Core network nodes (circuit‑switched, LTE‑EPC, 5G‑core) handling mobility | – |
| **Roaming Agreement** | Bilateral contract defining technical, billing, and QoS parameters | – |
| **$C_{\text{roam}}$** | Total roaming charge for a billing period | [currency] |
| **$V_i$** | Volume of traffic for service *i* (e.g., data, voice) | MB, min |
| **$p_i$** | Unit price of service *i* in the roaming tariff | [currency]/MB or [currency]/min |
| **$S$** | Fixed signaling or administrative surcharge per billing cycle | [currency] |
| **$L_{\text{roam}}$** | End‑to‑end latency incurred by roaming procedures | ms |
| **$T_{\text{attach}}$**, **$T_{\text{auth}}$**, **$T_{\text{PDN}}$** | Times for attach, authentication, and PDN (or PDU) session establishment | ms |

A typical roaming cost model is expressed as  

$$
C_{\text{roam}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} V_i \, p_i \;+\; S
\tag{1}
$$

where *N* is the number of distinct services (voice, SMS, data, etc.) used while roaming.

The **roaming latency** can be approximated by  

$$
L_{\text{roam}} = T_{\text{attach}} + T_{\text{auth}} + T_{\text{PDN}}
\tag{2}
$$

These timestamps are measured from the moment the UE (User Equipment) first receives a broadcast from the V‑PLMN until the data session is ready for user traffic.

---

### 2. Roaming procedure (high‑level)

1. **Network selection** – The UE scans for suitable PLMNs, applies the **Preferred PLMN list** stored in the SIM, and selects a V‑PLMN.  
2. **Attach / Registration** – The UE sends an *Attach Request* (LTE) or *Registration Request* (5G) to the V‑PLMN’s **MME/AMF**.  
3. **Location information exchange** – The V‑PLMN contacts the H‑PLMN’s **HLR** (or **HSS/UDM** in LTE/5G) via the **Diameter** (or legacy SS7) interface to retrieve the subscriber profile.  
4. **Authentication & Security** – The V‑PLMN performs mutual authentication using the **Authentication Center (AUC)** data retrieved from the H‑PLMN.  
5. **Bearer / PDU session setup** – After successful authentication, the V‑PLMN establishes a default bearer (LTE) or a default PDU session (5G) with the appropriate QoS attributes negotiated in the roaming agreement.  
6. **Service delivery** – Voice may be carried over **VoLTE/VoNR** (IMS) or fallback to circuit‑switched (CSFB) depending on the agreement; SMS uses **SMS over IP (SMS‑over‑SIP)** or traditional MAP; data follows the negotiated QoS class.  
7. **Accounting & Settlement** – The V‑PLMN records usage in **Charging Data Records (CDR)**, applies the tariff of Eq. (1), and periodically sends the records to the H‑PLMN for settlement (usually via **Diameter Roaming‑Exchange (REx)**).  

The entire flow is specified in **3GPP TS 23.402** (Network architecture; Service aspects; Mobility management; Roaming).

### 3. Worked example  

*Scenario*: A subscriber from **Home PLMN A** travels to **Visited PLMN B** for 3 days. During the visit the subscriber uses:

* 200 MB of LTE data  
* 5 minutes of VoLTE voice  

The roaming tariff negotiated between A and B is:

* Data: $p_{\text{data}} = \$0.10$ / MB  
* Voice: $p_{\text{voice}} = \$.05$ / min  
* Fixed signaling surcharge: $S = \$1.00$ per billing cycle  

**Step 1 – Compute service charges**  

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\text{data}} &= V_{\text{data}} \, p_{\text{data}} = 200\;\text{MB} \times 0.10\;\$/\text{MB} = \$20.00 \\
C_{\text{voice}} &= V_{\text{voice}} \, p_{\text{voice}} = 5\;\text{min} \times 0.05\;\$/\text{min} = \$0.25
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 – Apply fixed surcharge**  

$$
C_{\text{roam}} = C_{\text{data}} + C_{\text{voice}} + S = 20.00 + 0.25 + 1.00 = \boxed{\$21.25}
$$

Thus the subscriber’s roaming bill for the trip is **\$21.25**.

If the operator wants to estimate the **average roaming latency**, assume measured values:

* $T_{\text{attach}} = 250\,$ms  
* $T_{\text{auth}} = 180\,$ms  
* $T_{\text{PDN}} = 320\,$ms  

$$
L_{\text{roam}} = 250 + 180 + 320 = 750\;\text{ms}
$$

A latency of 750 ms is acceptable for data but may be noticeable for real‑time voice hand‑overs.

### 4. Common pitfalls and practical notes  

| Pitfall | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---------|----------------|------------|
| **Assuming identical QoS** | V‑PLMN may only support a subset of the subscriber’s subscribed QoS classes; data sessions can be downgraded, causing user‑perceived slowdown. | Verify QoS mapping tables in the roaming agreement; implement **QoS negotiation** (TS 23.401). |
| **Neglecting intra‑PLMN roaming** | In large countries a subscriber may move between regional operators that share the same PLMN ID; signaling still traverses different core nodes. | Treat intra‑PLMN roaming as a separate case; ensure VLR/MME hand‑over procedures are correctly configured. |
| **Forgetting to purge VLR entries** | Stale VLR entries cause failed authentication when the UE returns to the home network, leading to “attach errors”. | Implement timely **VLR cleanup** (e.g., timer T‑VLR) and monitor abnormal detach rates. |
| **Overlooking regulatory caps** | Some jurisdictions impose maximum per‑MB roaming fees; exceeding them can trigger fines. | Integrate **regulatory tariff tables** into the billing engine and validate against the latest ITU‑R or local regulator publications. |
| **Relying on legacy SS7 for all roaming** | Modern LTE/5G networks use Diameter; mixed SS7/Diameter environments can cause mismatched CDRs and settlement delays. | Deploy **Diameter Edge Agents (DEA)** and ensure proper mapping of SS7 MAP to Diameter interfaces. |

Finally, remember that **roaming is a contract‑driven service**. Technical interoperability alone does not guarantee billing correctness; both parties must keep the **roaming agreement**, **policy tables**, and **charging rules** synchronized whenever a new service (e.g., e‑SIM, network slicing for roaming) is introduced.
