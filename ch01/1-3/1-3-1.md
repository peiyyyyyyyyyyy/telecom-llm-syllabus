## 正文

Voice has been the original service that drove the development of mobile networks. Even after data traffic dominates, voice still accounts for a large share of signalling load and quality‑of‑service (QoS) requirements, especially for emergency calls and mission‑critical communications. Modern networks therefore support several parallel delivery mechanisms:

| Mechanism | Core concept | Typical deployment |
|-----------|--------------|--------------------|
| **Circuit‑Switched (CS) voice** | A dedicated time‑slot (or carrier) is allocated for the entire duration of a call. | 2G GSM, 3G UMTS (circuit‑switched domain). |
| **CS‑Fallback (CSFB)** | When a 4G/5G device is on a packet‑switched (PS) radio but needs to place a voice call, the UE is instructed to move to a legacy CS radio (e.g., LTE‑to‑GSM/UMTS). | LTE‑Advanced networks without native VoLTE. |
| **VoLTE / VoNR (IMS)** | Voice is carried as IP packets over the LTE (4G) or NR (5G) radio, using the IP Multimedia Subsystem (IMS) for session control and media handling. | 4G LTE (VoLTE, 3GPP TS 23.261) and 5G NR (VoNR, 3GPP TS 23.468). |
| **Single Radio Voice Call Continuity (SRVCC)** | During a CS‑to‑PS hand‑over (or the reverse), the ongoing call is transferred from the IMS domain to a CS domain (or vice‑versa) without dropping the call. | Enables seamless mobility between LTE/NR and legacy networks (3GPP TS 23.216, TS 23.251). |

---

### 1. Circuit‑Switched Voice

In a CS network a **time‑division multiplex (TDM)** or **frequency‑division multiplex (FDM)** channel is reserved for the call. The key performance indicator is the **voice capacity** $C_{\text{CS}}$ (simultaneous calls per cell):

$$
C_{\text{CS}} = \frac{B_{\text{cell}}}{R_{\text{codec}} + R_{\text{overhead}}}
$$

* $B_{\text{cell}}$ – total usable bandwidth of the cell (kHz for GSM, MHz for UMTS).  
* $R_{\text{codec}}$ – codec bit rate (e.g., 13 kbps for GSM‑FR, 12.2 kbps for AMR‑NB).  
* $R_{\text{overhead}}$ – signalling and framing overhead (typically 2–3 kbps).

Because the channel is exclusive, the capacity is limited by the number of physical timeslots (GSM: 8 per carrier) or codes (UMTS: limited by the spreading factor).

---

### 2. CS‑Fallback (CSFB)

When a UE attached to LTE wishes to place/receive a voice call, the **CSFB procedure** is triggered:

1. The **Mobility Management Entity (MME)** receives a “CS fallback” request from the UE.  
2. The MME selects a suitable **target cell** on a legacy radio (GSM/UMTS) and sends a **CSFB command** to the UE via the **eNodeB**.  
3. The UE performs a **hard handover** to the target cell, establishing a CS radio link.  
4. The call proceeds in the CS domain; after call termination the UE returns to LTE.

The CSFB latency is dominated by the radio re‑selection time $T_{\text{reselect}}$ and the core‑network signalling delay $T_{\text{sg}}$:

$$
T_{\text{CSFB}} = T_{\text{reselect}} + T_{\text{sg}}
$$

Typical values: $T_{\text{reselect}} \approx 150\text{ ms}$, $T_{\text{sg}} \approx 100\text{ ms}$, giving $T_{\text{CSFB}} \approx 250\text{ ms}$, which is perceptible to users.

---

### 3. VoLTE / VoNR (IMS)

The **IP Multimedia Subsystem (IMS)** abstracts the media plane from the access technology. Voice packets are encoded with an **Adaptive Multi‑Rate (AMR)** codec, encapsulated in **RTP** and transported over the **Evolved Packet System (EPS)** bearer with QoS Class Identifier (QCI) 1 (for conversational voice).

Key parameters:

* **Codec bit rate** $R_{\text{codec}}$ (e.g., 12.65 kbps for AMR‑NB, 23.85 kbps for AMR‑WB).  
* **IP/UDP/RTP overhead** $R_{\text{IP}} = 40\text{ bits per packet}$ (IP + UDP + RTP headers).  
* **Packetization interval** $T_{\text{pkt}}$ (typically 20 ms).  

The **effective bandwidth per call** $B_{\text{call}}$ (in kbps) is:

$$
B_{\text{call}} = R_{\text{codec}} + \frac{R_{\text{IP}}}{T_{\text{pkt}}}
$$

For $R_{\text{codec}} = 12.65\text{ kbps}$, $R_{\text{IP}} = 40\text{ bits} = 0.04\text{ kb}$, $T_{\text{pkt}} = 20\text{ ms}=0.02\text{ s}$:

$$
B_{\text{call}} = 12.65 + \frac{0.04}{0.02}=12.65+2 = 14.65\text{ kbps}
$$

The **maximum simultaneous VoLTE calls** $C_{\text{VoLTE}}$ on a cell with total downlink capacity $C_{\text{DL}}$ (kbps) is:

$$
C_{\text{VoLTE}} = \frac{C_{\text{DL}} \cdot \rho_{\text{voice}}}{B_{\text{call}}}
$$

where $\rho_{\text{voice}}$ is the fraction of radio resources reserved for voice (commonly 10–15 %).  

VoNR follows the same principle but runs over the 5G NR numerology (subcarrier spacing, slot length). The underlying IMS procedures are identical; only the radio interface differs (3GPP TS 23.468).

---

### 4. Single Radio Voice Call Continuity (SRVCC)

SRVCC enables a **seamless hand‑over** of an ongoing voice session from the IMS (PS) domain to a CS domain (or vice‑versa). The hand‑over is initiated by the **Policy and Charging Control (PCC) function** when the UE detects that radio conditions for the target technology fall below a threshold.

The **handover delay** $T_{\text{SRVCC}}$ can be approximated as:

$$
T_{\text{SRVCC}} = T_{\text{detect}} + T_{\text{prepare}} + T_{\text{execute}}
$$

* $T_{\text{detect}}$ – measurement reporting delay (≈ 50 ms).  
* $T_{\text{prepare}}$ – context transfer between IMS and MSC (≈ 150 ms).  
* $T_{\text{execute}}$ – radio link re‑establishment (≈ 100 ms).

Thus $T_{\text{SRVCC}}$ ≈ 300 ms, which is acceptable for most voice services but may affect latency‑sensitive applications like mission‑critical push‑to‑talk.

### Worked Example: Capacity Planning for VoLTE on a 10 MHz LTE Carrier

**Given**  
* Carrier bandwidth $B_{\text{cell}} = 10\text{ MHz} = 10\,000\text{ kHz}$.  
* Downlink spectral efficiency for voice‑optimized QCI 1: $\eta = 0.5\ \text{bits/s/Hz}$ (typical for low‑order modulation).  
* AMR‑NB codec $R_{\text{codec}} = 12.65\text{ kbps}$.  
* Packetization interval $T_{\text{pkt}} = 20\text{ ms}$.  
* Voice resource reservation $\rho_{\text{voice}} = 15\%$.

**Step 1 – Compute downlink capacity for voice**  

$$
C_{\text{DL}} = B_{\text{cell}} \times \eta = 10\,000\text{ kHz} \times 0.5\frac{\text{bits}}{\text{s·kHz}} = 5\,000\text{ kbps}
$$

**Step 2 – Compute per‑call bandwidth**  

$$
B_{\text{call}} = 12.65\text{ kbps} + \frac{0.04\text{ kb}}{0.02\text{ s}} = 12.65 + 2 = 14.65\text{ kbps}
$$

**Step 3 – Apply voice reservation factor**  

$$
C_{\text{voice}} = C_{\text{DL}} \times \rho_{\text{voice}} = 5\,000 \times 0.15 = 750\text{ kbps}
$$

**Step 4 – Compute simultaneous calls**  

$$
C_{\text{VoLTE}} = \frac{750}{14.65} \approx 51\text{ calls}
$$

Thus a single 10 MHz LTE cell can support roughly **50 simultaneous VoLTE calls** under the assumed conditions.

### Common Mistakes and Practical Notes

1. **Ignoring Over‑head:** Designers often count only the codec bit rate and forget the fixed IP/UDP/RTP headers, leading to optimistic capacity estimates.  
2. **Mismatched QCI and Scheduler:** Assigning QCI 1 to a bearer that the scheduler treats as best‑effort eliminates the guaranteed latency, causing voice jitter.  
3. **Incomplete SRVCC Triggering:** Relying solely on signal strength for SRVCC can cause premature hand‑overs; a combined metric of **RRC quality**, **QoS degradation**, and **UE speed** yields more stable transitions.  
4. **CSFB Latency Under‑estimation:** The hard hand‑over to a legacy radio blocks data temporarily; applications that cannot tolerate a 250 ms pause (e.g., real‑time video) should be steered to VoLTE/VoNR when possible.  
5. **Codec Compatibility:** The network must support the same AMR mode on both CS and IMS sides for SRVCC; otherwise a transcoding step is required, adding processing delay and potential quality loss.

By carefully balancing the resource allocation between CS and PS domains, and by correctly configuring IMS, CSFB, and SRVCC procedures, operators can deliver a seamless voice experience across 2G‑5G generations.
