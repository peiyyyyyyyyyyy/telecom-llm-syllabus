## 正文

Modern mobile networks are expected to carry a vast amount of heterogeneous traffic: web browsing, video streaming, IoT telemetry, cloud gaming, and enterprise VPNs. Unlike traditional voice services, which are tolerant to small jitter but require strict latency, data services have diverse performance requirements. **Quality of Service (QoS)** is the set of mechanisms that the radio access network (RAN) and the core network use to guarantee that each data flow receives the resources it needs, while still maximizing overall spectral efficiency.

### 1.3.3.1 Key QoS concepts  

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit / Range |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| **QCI** (QoS Class Identifier) | Pre‑defined QoS profile (e.g., conversational, streaming, best‑effort) | Integer 1‑255 |
| **ARP** (Allocation‑Retention‑Priority) | Priority for resource allocation and pre‑emption | 1 (highest) – 15 (lowest) |
| **GBR** (Guaranteed Bit Rate) | Minimum sustained throughput guaranteed for a bearer | bits/s (bps) |
| **MBR** (Maximum Bit Rate) | Upper bound on throughput for a bearer | bits/s (bps) |
| **Delay** | Maximum tolerated one‑way packet delay | milliseconds (ms) |
| **Jitter** | Maximum tolerated variation in packet inter‑arrival time | ms |
| **Packet Loss Rate (PLR)** | Acceptable probability of packet discard | dimension‑less (e.g., 10⁻⁴) |

In 5G NR, the QoS framework is defined in **3GPP TS 23.501** and **TS 23.402**. A **QoS flow** (the smallest QoS unit) is attached to a **PDU session**; each flow carries the above parameters. The network maps QCI to concrete values of Delay, PLR, and priority (see Table 23.301 in the spec).

### 1.3.3.2 QoS enforcement in the RAN  

The radio scheduler uses the following fundamental formula to compute the **resource allocation** for a bearer:

$$
R_i = \frac{GBR_i}{\eta_i} + \alpha \cdot \frac{(MBR_i - GBR_i)}{N_{\text{non‑GBR}}}
$$

where  

* $R_i$ – allocated radio resources for bearer *i* (e.g., number of PRBs per TTI).  
* $GBR_i$ – guaranteed bit rate of bearer *i* (bps).  
* $MBR_i$ – maximum bit rate of bearer *i$ (bps).  
* $\eta_i$ – spectral efficiency of the UE (bits/PRB) determined by the current MCS (Modulation and Coding Scheme).  
* $N_{\text{non‑GBR}}$ – number of active non‑GBR bearers sharing the remaining resources.  
* $\alpha$ – fairness factor (0 ≤ α ≤ 1) that balances between strict GBR enforcement and opportunistic use of leftovers.

The scheduler first satisfies all GBR bearers (ensuring $R_i \ge GBR_i/\eta_i$). Remaining PRBs are distributed among non‑GBR bearers proportionally to their requested rates, controlled by $\alpha$.

### 1.3.3.3 End‑to‑end QoS handling  

When a UE initiates a data session, the **Policy Control Function (PCF)** and **Session Management Function (SMF)** negotiate the QoS parameters with the UE. The resulting QoS flow description is encoded in the **QoS Flow Description (QFD)** IE and sent to the RAN via the **NG‑RAN** interface. The network must enforce:

* **Latency bound**: $T_{\text{total}} = T_{\text{prop}} + T_{\text{queue}} + T_{\text{proc}} \le T_{\text{max}}$  
* **Packet loss**: $PLR_{\text{observed}} \le PLR_{\text{allowed}}$

If the measured $T_{\text{total}}$ or $PLR_{\text{observed}}$ exceeds the allowed limits, the PCF may trigger **QoS adaptation** (e.g., downgrade to a lower QCI) or **pre‑emption** of lower‑priority bearers (using ARP).

### 1.3.3.4 Worked example  

A user equipment (UE) requests a video streaming session with the following QoS requirements:

* QCI = 2 (low‑latency streaming) → Delay ≤ 100 ms, PLR ≤ 10⁻⁴.  
* GBR = 2 Mbps, MBR = 5 Mbps.  
* The UE reports a current spectral efficiency $\eta = 2.5$ bits/PRB.  
* The cell has 100 PRBs available per TTI.  
* There are three other non‑GBR bearers (N=3) with total requested rate 9 Mbps.  
* Fairness factor $\alpha = 0.5$.

**Step 1 – Allocate GBR resources**

$$
R_{\text{GBR}} = \frac{GBR}{\eta} = \frac{2 \times 10^{6}\ \text{bps}}{2.5\ \text{bits/PRB}} = 800{,}000\ \text{PRBs}
$$

Since the cell provides only 100 PRBs per TTI, we interpret $R_{\text{GBR}}$ as the *average* number of PRBs needed per second. Assuming a 1 ms TTI, the required PRBs per TTI are $800{,}000 \times 10^{-3} = 800$ PRBs, which exceeds the cell capacity. The scheduler therefore **prioritizes** the GBR bearer using ARP = 1 and will pre‑empt non‑GBR traffic until enough PRBs are freed. After pre‑emption, the GBR bearer receives the full 100 PRBs, delivering an instantaneous rate:

$$
R_{\text{inst}} = 100\ \text{PRBs} \times \eta = 100 \times 2.5 = 250\ \text{Mbps}
$$

which far exceeds the GBR, satisfying the latency bound.

**Step 2 – Allocate remaining resources to non‑GBR bearers**

After reserving the necessary PRBs for the GBR bearer, suppose the scheduler leaves 20 PRBs for the three non‑GBR bearers (the rest are used for control channels). The share per bearer:

$$
R_{\text{non‑GBR}} = \alpha \cdot \frac{(MBR-GBR)}{N} = 0.5 \times \frac{5-2}{3}\ \text{Mbps} = 0.5 \times 1\ \text{Mbps} = 0.5\ \text{Mbps}
$$

Converted to PRBs:

$$
\text{PRBs per bearer} = \frac{0.5 \times 10^{6}}{\eta} = \frac{500{,}000}{2.5}=200\ \text{PRBs}
$$

Because only 20 PRBs are available, each non‑GBR bearer receives $20/3 \approx 7$ PRBs, yielding a real throughput of $7 \times 2.5 = 17.5$ Mbps per bearer, which is below the requested 0.5 Mbps. The PCF may decide to **downgrade** the video QoS (e.g., switch to QCI = 4) or reduce the video resolution to keep the latency target.

**Result:** The GBR bearer meets its delay and loss targets; non‑GBR bearers experience reduced throughput, illustrating the trade‑off managed by ARP and the fairness factor.

### 1.3.3.5 Common pitfalls and practical notes  

1. **Confusing QCI with ARP** – QCI defines the *type* of service (delay, loss), while ARP determines *priority* for resource contention. Both must be set correctly; a low‑delay QCI with a low ARP will still be pre‑empted by higher‑priority traffic.  

2. **Assuming static spectral efficiency** – $\eta$ varies with UE speed, channel quality, and selected MCS. Over‑estimating $\eta$ leads to under‑allocation and QoS violations.  

3. **Neglecting control‑channel overhead** – The scheduler’s resource budget must account for PDCCH, PHICH, and SRS symbols; otherwise the calculated PRBs may be unavailable for data.  

4. **Ignoring end‑to‑end latency** – Radio latency is only part of $T_{\text{total}}$. Core‑network processing and backhaul delays can dominate for cloud‑native services; QoS negotiation must consider the full path.  

5. **Misusing the fairness factor $\alpha$** – Setting $\alpha = 1$ forces strict proportional sharing, which can starve GBR bearers if the cell is overloaded. Conversely, $\alpha = 0$ wastes any leftover capacity. Operators typically tune $\alpha$ per traffic profile.  

By carefully mapping service requirements to QCI, ARP, GBR/MBR, and by dynamically monitoring $\eta$ and $T_{\text{total}}$, a 5G network can deliver reliable data services ranging from ultra‑reliable low‑latency communication (URLLC) to massive machine‑type communication (mMTC).
