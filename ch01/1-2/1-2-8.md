# 1.2.8 Wi-Fi 與 IEEE 802 區域網路

所屬：[1.2 通訊協定](../1-2.md)

## 正文

Wi‑Fi (IEEE 802.11) is the most common non‑3GPP access technology that can be used to attach a user equipment (UE) to the 5G Core (5GC). In 5G terminology the Wi‑Fi link is referred to as a *Non‑3GPP access* and is handled by the registration and connection management procedures defined in TS 23.501 §5.5. Because Wi‑Fi networks are typically operated by operators other than the mobile network operator, they are considered *untrusted* unless a trusted WLAN is explicitly deployed (see TS 23.501 §8.2.7). Proper handling of registration state, timers, and the signalling connection (NWu, NWt, etc.) is essential for seamless mobility and for preserving the UE’s ability to keep user‑plane (UP) sessions alive across different accesses.

---

### 2.1 Registration Management over Wi‑Fi

When a UE attaches to a Wi‑Fi access network it performs a **registration** with the Access and Mobility Management Function (AMF). The registration state machine contains three relevant states:

* **RM‑REGISTERED** – the UE is known to the AMF on the non‑3GPP access.  
* **RM‑DEREGISTERED** – the UE is no longer registered on that access.  
* **CM‑IDLE / CM‑CONNECTED** – control‑plane (CM) connectivity status (see §5.5.2).

A UE **enters the RM‑DEREGISTERED state** and the AMF does the same **(i) after an explicit deregistration procedure, (ii) after the Network non‑3GPP Implicit Deregistration timer expires, or (iii) after the UE non‑3GPP Deregistration timer expires**【TS 23.501 §5.5】.  

When the UE moves to **CM‑IDLE** on the non‑3GPP access, it starts the *UE non‑3GPP Deregistration timer* with a value received from the AMF during registration【TS 23.501 §5.5】. The AMF simultaneously starts the *Network non‑3GPP Implicit Deregistration timer* with a **value longer than the UE timer**【TS 23.501 §5.5】. This inequality guarantees that the UE has a grace period to reactivate UP connections before the network forces deregistration.

$$
T_{\text{Network}} \;>\; T_{\text{UE}}
$$

*where* $T_{\text{Network}}$ *is the Network non‑3GPP Implicit Deregistration timer and* $T_{\text{UE}}$ *is the UE non‑3GPP Deregistration timer (both expressed in seconds).*

**Worked example**  
Assume the AMF configures $T_{\text{UE}} = 5\,$s and $T_{\text{Network}} = 8\,$s.  

1. UE enters CM‑IDLE → starts a 5 s timer.  
2. If the UE re‑establishes a Non‑3GPP Access Connection before 5 s, the timer stops and the UE returns to CM‑CONNECTED.  
3. If the UE does not reconnect, after 5 s the UE timer expires, causing the UE to transition to RM‑DEREGISTERED.  
4. The network timer still runs for another 3 s; however, because the UE is already deregistered, the network timer’s expiry has no further effect.

This simple timing relationship is the core of reliable Wi‑Fi attachment in 5G.

---

### 2.2 Connection Management over Wi‑Fi

After successful registration, a UE **establishes a Non‑3GPP Access Connection** to the 5GC. The connection type depends on the trust level of the WLAN:

| Trust level | Connection identifier |
|-------------|-----------------------|
| Untrusted   | **NWu** (Non‑3GPP‑U)  |
| Trusted     | **NWt** (Non‑3GPP‑T)  |
| N5CW device (trusted WLAN) | **Yt’** |

The UE **transitions to the CM‑CONNECTED state** for that access【TS 23.501 §5.5.2】. Only **one** Non‑3GPP Access Connection can exist at a time【TS 23.501 §5.5.2】. The connection is released either by an **explicit deregistration** or by an **AN Release** procedure initiated by the access node (e.g., N3IWF, TNGF, TWIF, or W‑AGF).

Release detection mechanisms differ per connection type:

* For **NWu/NWt** the release may be detected by the *dead‑peer detection* mechanism in IKEv2 (RFC 7296)【TS 23.501 §5.5.2】.  
* For **Y4/Y5** (wireline) releases may be inferred from loss of physical link or PPPoE session【TS 23.501 §5.5.2】.  

When the Non‑3GPP Access Connection is released, the UE **transitions to CM‑IDLE** while remaining in RM‑REGISTERED unless the release is part of a deregistration procedure, in which case it moves to RM‑DEREGISTERED【TS 23.501 §5.5.2】. The UE then restarts its non‑3GPP deregistration timer.

---

### 2.3 RSSI Measurement in IEEE 802.11

The 5G RRC layer uses the **IEEE 802.11 RSSI** value as defined in the IEEE 802.11 specification. RSSI is **measured from Beacon, DMG Beacon, or FILS discovery frames in passive scanning mode, or from probe‑response frames in active scanning mode**【TS 38.215 §5.1.12】. This metric is used, for example, when the UE decides whether a Wi‑Fi cell is suitable for registration or hand‑over.

---

### 2.4 Practical notes and common pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | Correct practice |
|---------|---------------|------------------|
| Assuming the UE can keep several simultaneous Wi‑Fi connections to the 5GC. | The specification explicitly limits the UE to **one** Non‑3GPP Access Connection【TS 23.501 §5.5.2】. | Design the UE’s connection manager to tear down the existing connection before establishing a new one. |
| Configuring the Network implicit deregistration timer shorter than the UE timer. | The spec requires the network timer to be **longer** than the UE timer【TS 23.501 §5.5】. | Verify the timer values during network planning; the inequality must hold to avoid premature deregistration. |
| Treating RSSI as a generic “signal strength” without regard to frame type. | RSSI is defined only for specific frame types (Beacon, DMG Beacon, FILS discovery, probe response)【TS 38.215 §5.1.12】. | When measuring RSSI for RRC decisions, ensure the UE captures the correct frames. |
| Assuming a change of Wi‑Fi access point (AP) forces a new registration. | The UE **does not** need to re‑register when moving to a different WLAN AP within the same PLMN【TS 23.501 §5.5】. | Use the Mobility Registration Update procedure only when the point of attachment changes to a *new* non‑3GPP AN node (e.g., a different N3IWF). |
| Believing that traffic can be switched from Wi‑Fi to a wired access in this Release. | The specification states that **traffic switching from an old non‑3GPP access to a wireline access is not supported**【TS 23.501 §5.5】. | Limit mobility scenarios to Wi‑Fi ↔ Wi‑Fi or Wi‑Fi ↔ 3GPP radio. |

Understanding these mechanisms ensures that Wi‑Fi‑based access integrates smoothly with the 5GC, preserving session continuity and respecting the security model of trusted vs. untrusted WLANs.

## 練習題

**1.** Which timer must have a longer value according to TS 23.501 §5.5?

- A. UE non‑3GPP Deregistration timer
- B. Network non‑3GPP Implicit Deregistration timer
- C. Both timers must be equal
- D. No timer relationship is specified

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The network timer is required to be longer than the UE timer to give the UE a grace period.

</details>

**2.** After a UE successfully establishes a Non‑3GPP Access Connection over Wi‑Fi, which state does it transition to?

- A. CM‑IDLE
- B. CM‑CONNECTED
- C. RM‑DEREGISTERED
- D. CM‑SUSPENDED

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Successful establishment moves the UE to the CM‑CONNECTED state.

</details>

**3.** Which statement about multiple simultaneous Non‑3GPP Access Connections is correct?

- A. The UE may maintain two connections, one for uplink and one for downlink.
- B. The UE may maintain connections to both trusted and untrusted WLANs simultaneously.
- C. The UE does not establish multiple simultaneous Non‑3GPP Access Connections to the 5GC.
- D. The UE can have multiple connections if they use different IP addresses.

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：C。The specification explicitly limits the UE to a single Non‑3GPP Access Connection.

</details>

**4.** What mechanism may be used to detect failure of an NWu signalling connection?

- A. Loss of PPPoE session
- B. Dead peer detection in IKEv2 (RFC 7296)
- C. Physical link loss detection
- D. TCP keep‑alive timeout

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。For NWu/NWt connections, the release may be determined by the dead‑peer detection mechanism defined in IKEv2.

</details>
