## 正文

In any commercial telecommunication network the operator must translate raw usage data into monetary charges. The **billing architecture** defines where, when and how this translation happens, and consequently determines latency, scalability, and the richness of services that can be offered (e.g., real‑time balance alerts, prepaid throttling, or post‑paid invoice generation). Three archetypal models coexist in modern networks:

| Model | Execution point | Typical latency | Primary use‑case |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Offline Billing (OB)** | Charging Data Function (CDF) collects usage records after the service session ends. | Minutes to hours (batch processing). | Post‑paid contracts, large‑volume data analytics. |
| **Online Billing (OB)** | Online Charging System (OCS) intercepts each request in real time. | Sub‑second (typically < 100 ms). | Prepaid balance enforcement, real‑time QoS throttling. |
| **Converged Billing (CHF – Charging and Handling Function)** | Hybrid: the CHF receives both real‑time charging requests and offline usage records, correlates them, and produces a unified charge. | Mixed: real‑time for control, batch for settlement. | Hybrid prepaid/post‑paid services, unified customer billing across voice, data, OTT, IoT. |

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) and 3GPP define the functional split. In 3GPP TS 32.295 the **Charging Architecture** is described, while TS 32.400, TS 32.401 and TS 32.402 detail offline, online and converged charging respectively. The following sections present the core concepts, the key mathematical model used for rating, a worked example, and practical pitfalls.

---

### 1.4.3.1 Core Definitions  

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $U$ | **Usage record** – a tuple describing a single chargeable event (e.g., a voice call, a data flow). | – |
| $t_s$, $t_e$ | Start and end timestamps of the event. | seconds (s) since epoch |
| $q$ | Quantity of the measured resource (e.g., bytes, minutes, SMS count). | depends on service (B, min, msg) |
| $p$ | **Tariff parameter** – price per unit of $q$ (or a piece‑wise function). | $/unit |
| $c$ | **Charge** – monetary amount derived from $q$ and $p$. | $ |
| $R$ | **Rating function** – maps $U$ to $c$, possibly with discounts, caps, or promotions. | $/record |
| $B$ | **Balance** – prepaid amount remaining for a subscriber. | $ |
| $L$ | **Latency** – time between event occurrence and charge availability to the control plane. | ms or s |

A **rating function** can be expressed generically as  

$$
c = R(U) = f(q, p, \theta)
$$

where $\theta$ denotes additional context (time‑of‑day, subscriber tier, roaming flag). For linear tariffs $f(q, p, \theta) = q \times p$; for tiered tariffs a piece‑wise definition is required.

---

### 1.4.3.2 Offline Billing Workflow  

1. **Event Generation** – Network elements (e.g., MSC, SGW) emit **Call Detail Records (CDRs)** or **Usage Data Records (UDRs)** after session termination.  
2. **Collection** – A **Charging Data Function (CDF)** aggregates records, applies filtering, and stores them in a **Rating Database (RDB)**.  
3. **Rating** – The **Rating Engine** processes each record:  
   $$
   c_i = R(U_i) \quad \forall i \in \{1,\dots,N\}
   $$  
4. **Settlement** – Rated charges are posted to the **Billing System (BSS)**, which generates invoices, applies taxes, and updates the **Account Management System (AMS)**.  

Because rating occurs after the fact, the system can afford complex tariff calculations (e.g., volume discounts, roaming surcharges) without impacting user experience.

---

### 1.4.3.3 Online Billing Workflow  

1. **Real‑time Intercept** – When a subscriber initiates a service, the **Policy and Charging Control (PCC)** function sends a **Credit Control Request (CCR)** to the **Online Charging System (OCS)**.  
2. **Quota Allocation** – OCS evaluates the subscriber’s balance $B$ and returns a **Credit Control Answer (CCA)** containing an allowed quota $Q$ (e.g., 5 MB).  
3. **Usage Monitoring** – The network element decrements $Q$ as traffic proceeds; when $Q$ is exhausted, a new CCR is sent.  
4. **Immediate Balance Update** – Each CCR/CCA exchange updates $B$ as  
   $$
   B_{\text{new}} = B_{\text{old}} - c_{\text{session}}
   $$  
   where $c_{\text{session}}$ is the charge for the consumed quota.  

Online charging enables **prepaid** services and instantaneous throttling but limits tariff complexity due to latency constraints.

---

### 1.4.3.4 Converged Billing (CHF)  

The **Charging and Handling Function (CHF)** introduced in 3GPP TS 32.402 unifies the two paradigms:

* **Hybrid Rating Engine** – Accepts both **real‑time CCRs** (for immediate quota enforcement) and **offline CDRs** (for detailed settlement).  
* **Correlation Layer** – Matches online quota consumption with the corresponding offline record to avoid double‑charging.  
* **Unified Customer View** – BSS receives a single stream of rated charges, regardless of origin, simplifying revenue assurance.

Mathematically, the CHF computes a **net charge**  

$$
c_{\text{net}} = \sum_{k=1}^{K_{\text{online}}} c^{\text{online}}_k + \sum_{j=1}^{J_{\text{offline}}} c^{\text{offline}}_j - \Delta_{\text{dup}}
$$

where $\Delta_{\text{dup}}$ subtracts any duplicated amount identified by the correlation logic.

---

### 1.4.3.5 Worked Example  

A mobile subscriber with a prepaid balance $B = \$10.00$ initiates a 3 GB video streaming session. The operator’s tariff is:

* First 2 GB @ \$0.02/MB  
* Subsequent usage @ \$0.015/MB  

The network uses **online charging** to enforce a quota of 500 MB, then falls back to **offline charging** for the remainder.

1. **Online Phase (first 500 MB)**  
   $$
   q_1 = 500\ \text{MB},\quad p_1 = 0.02\ \$/\text{MB}
   $$
   $$
   c_1 = q_1 \times p_1 = 500 \times 0.02 = \$10.00
   $$
   The OCS deducts $c_1$ from $B$, leaving $B_{\text{new}} = 0$; the session is throttled after 500 MB.

2. **Offline Phase (remaining 2.5 GB)**  
   The CDR records $q_2 = 2500\ \text{MB}$. Since the first 2 GB have already been charged, the rating engine applies the second‑tier price to the excess:  

   * Tier‑1 remaining: $2000\ \text{MB}$ at \$0.02/MB → $c_{2a}=2000 \times 0.02 = \$40.00$  
   * Tier‑2: $500\ \text{MB}$ at \$0.015/MB → $c_{2b}=500 \times 0.015 = \$7.50$  

   Total offline charge $c_2 = c_{2a}+c_{2b}= \$47.50$.

3. **Converged Net Charge**  

   The CHF detects that $c_1$ already covered the first 500 MB of Tier‑1, so it subtracts the overlapping amount:  

   $$
   \Delta_{\text{dup}} = 500\ \text{MB} \times 0.02 = \$10.00
   $$

   Hence  

   $$
   c_{\text{net}} = c_1 + c_2 - \Delta_{\text{dup}} = 10.00 + 47.50 - 10.00 = \$47.50
   $$

   The subscriber’s final bill is \$47.50, and the prepaid balance remains zero (the system prevented negative balances by throttling after the online quota).

### 1.4.3.6 Common Mistakes and Practical Notes  

| Pitfall | Why it occurs | Mitigation |
|---------|---------------|------------|
| **Double‑counting** – charging the same usage both online and offline. | Correlation logic omitted or mis‑configured. | Implement a unique session identifier (e.g., IMSI + start‑time) and enforce deduplication in CHF. |
| **Latency‑induced quota exhaustion** – OCS response > 100 ms causing packet loss. | Network congestion or overloaded OCS. | Deploy OCS in a high‑availability cluster; use local caching of recent tariffs. |
| **Tariff drift** – offline rating uses an outdated price table. | Asynchronous distribution of tariff updates. | Push tariff version numbers with each CDR/CCR and reject mismatched versions. |
| **Balance underflow** – prepaid balance becomes negative due to race conditions. | Parallel CCRs for the same subscriber. | Serialize CCR handling per IMSI or apply atomic balance updates in the OCS database. |
| **Insufficient granularity** – rating at coarse intervals hides micro‑transactions. | Aggregating records before rating. | Preserve per‑event granularity in the CHF; aggregate only for reporting. |

When designing a billing architecture, engineers must balance **real‑time control** against **tariff flexibility**. Offline billing excels at complex pricing but cannot protect prepaid balances in real time. Online billing provides instant enforcement but limits the richness of promotions. Converged billing (CHF) is the industry’s answer to hybrid services such as “pay‑as‑you‑go data bundles combined with post‑paid voice plans,” but it requires disciplined session correlation and robust, low‑latency OCS components.
