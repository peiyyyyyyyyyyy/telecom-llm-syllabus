# 1.4.3 計費架構：離線、線上與融合計費（CHF）

所屬：[1.4 網管、計費、安全與密碼學](../1-4.md)

## 正文

*Why billing matters* – In any commercial telecommunication network the operator must translate the consumption of network resources into monetary charges. The billing system therefore needs a reliable view of how much service a user (or a tenant) has actually used. Although the detailed mechanisms of **offline**, **online**, and **converged** charging are defined in 3GPP specifications that are not part of the present reference, the *performance indicators* that feed those charging functions are described in **TS 28.552 §4.1**. This subsection therefore concentrates on the performance‑indicator framework that underlies all three billing models, and it points out where the reference stops providing detail.

### Key definitions (supported by TS 28.552 §4.1)

| Symbol | Definition | Unit / comment |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| $N$ | Number of Network Functions (NFs) belonging to a group | – |
| $L_i$ | Measured latency (or any other metric) of NF *i* | ms, µs, … |
| $PI$ | **Performance Indicator** – the aggregated value over the group of NFs | same unit as the underlying metric |
| $S\!-\!NSSAI$ | Slice/Service type identifier associated with a tenant | – |
| $sNSSAIList$ | List of $S\!-\!NSSAI$ values belonging to a tenant | – |

*Performance Indicator* is defined as **the performance data aggregated over a group of NFs**【TS 28.552 §4.1】. The aggregation method (e.g., arithmetic mean, maximum, sum) is **identified in the performance indicator definition**【TS 28.552 §4.1】. A common example is the *average latency* of a network slice.

Performance indicators exist at three hierarchical levels:

1. **NF level** – raw measurements collected directly at each NF.  
2. **Network‑slice‑subnet level** – derived from the NF‑level measurements of the NFs that belong to the subnet **or to the constituent sub‑nets**【TS 28.552 §4.1】.  
3. **Network‑slice level** – derived from the subnet‑level indicators of the constituent sub‑nets **and/or NFs**【TS 28.552 §4.1】.

Each level can be exposed through a **performance‑management service** that makes the indicator “available” to consumers (e.g., OSS/BSS, billing functions)【TS 28.552 §4.1】.

When a **tenant** receives a communication service, the operator may associate the tenant with one or more $S\!-\!NSSAI$ values. In that case the performance indicators are **split into sub‑counters per $S\!-\!NSSAI$ for the individual tenant**【TS 28.552 §4.1】. Consequently, the billing system can charge per slice type rather than per raw NF usage.

### Simple aggregation formula

For a group of $N$ NFs, if the chosen aggregation method is the arithmetic mean, the performance indicator is

$$
PI = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} L_i .
$$

All symbols are defined above; the unit of $PI$ is the same as that of $L_i$ (e.g., milliseconds for latency). The reference states that *average latency* is a valid example of a performance indicator, confirming the suitability of the mean formula【TS 28.552 §4.1】.

### Worked example

Assume a network slice subnet *Subnet‑A* consists of three NFs that report the following latencies during a 5‑minute interval:

| NF | Measured latency $L_i$ (ms) |
|----|----------------------------|
| NF‑1 | 12 |
| NF‑2 | 18 |
| NF‑3 | 15 |

1. **NF‑level measurements** are the three raw values above.  
2. **Subnet‑level aggregation** (average latency) is  

$$
PI_{Subnet‑A}= \frac{12+18+15}{3}= \frac{45}{3}=15\;\text{ms}.
$$

The operator’s performance‑management service now makes $PI_{Subnet‑A}=15$ ms available to the billing function.

If a tenant *T1* is associated with two $S\!-\!NSSAI$ values, *S1* and *S2*, and the subnet’s traffic is split 60 % to *S1* and 40 % to *S2*, the performance indicator can be **split into sub‑counters**:

$$
PI_{T1,S1}=0.6 \times 15\;\text{ms}=9\;\text{ms},\qquad
PI_{T1,S2}=0.4 \times 15\;\text{ms}=6\;\text{ms}.
$$

These sub‑counters are the basis for *converged* charging, where a single billing run consumes both offline (post‑hoc) and online (real‑time) indicator values. The reference confirms that such splitting “per $S\!-\!NSSAI$ for individual tenant” is defined【TS 28.552 §4.1】.

### Practical notes and common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it matters |
|---------|----------------|
| **Using the wrong aggregation method** – e.g., summing latencies instead of averaging – leads to inflated or meaningless $PI$ values because the reference explicitly ties the method to the indicator definition【TS 28.552 §4.1】. |
| **Skipping the subnet‑level step** – aggregating directly from NF measurements to the slice level without intermediate subnet indicators can violate the hierarchical derivation described in the reference【TS 28.552 §4.1】. |
| **Ignoring $S\!-\!NSSAI$ splitting** – charging a tenant on a single aggregate $PI$ while the tenant actually owns multiple slices leads to unfair billing; the reference requires sub‑counters per $S\!-\!NSSAI$【TS 28.552 §4.1】. |
| **Not exposing the indicator via a performance‑management service** – billing functions that pull data directly from NFs bypass the standardized service layer, breaking the “made available via the corresponding performance management service” rule【TS 28.552 §4.1】. |

Finally, it is important to recognise that **the reference does not describe the internal logic of offline, online, or converged charging**. Those mechanisms are defined elsewhere; the present text only establishes the performance‑indicator foundation that any of those billing architectures must consume.
