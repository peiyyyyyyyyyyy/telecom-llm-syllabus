# 1.4.6 密碼學基礎：對稱、公鑰、雜湊、亂數

所屬：[1.4 網管、計費、安全與密碼學](../1-4.md)

## 正文

In modern telecom networks every user identifier, signalling message, and user‑plane payload is protected by cryptographic primitives.  A clear separation of **symmetric encryption**, **public‑key encryption**, **hash‑based integrity**, and **random number generation** is required to meet the 3GPP security architecture and to interoperate across vendors.  The most widely deployed construction in 5G core networks is the *Elliptic Curve Integrated Encryption Scheme* (ECIES) defined in TS 33.501 C.3.  ECIES combines all four primitives in a single envelope that hides the Subscription Permanent Identifier (SUPI) and guarantees confidentiality, integrity, and source authentication.

---

### 1. Symmetric Encryption (AES‑128 in CTR mode)

ECIES Profile A uses **AES‑128** in Counter (CTR) mode as its data‑encryption primitive.  The encryption key **EK** is 16 octets (128 bits) long and is derived from the key‑derivation function (KDF) output **K**:

$$
K = \underbrace{EK}_{\text{enckeylen}=16\text{ octets}}\;\|\;
\underbrace{ICB}_{\text{icblen}=16\text{ octets}}\;\|\;
\underbrace{MK}_{\text{mackeylen}=32\text{ octets}} .
$$

The **initial counter block** (**ICB**) is the first counter value *T₁* defined in NIST SP 800‑38A §B.1 with a 32‑bit increment *m* = 32 bits.  Encryption of a plaintext block *P* produces the ciphertext *C*:

$$
C = \text{AES‑CTR}_{EK}(P,\,ICB) .
$$

*Reference:* “ENC : AES‑128 in CTR mode” and “enckeylen : 16 octets (128 bits)” and “icblen : 16 octets (128 bits)” and “For generating successive counter blocks from the initial counter block (ICB) in CTR mode, the profiles shall use the standard incrementing function … with m = 32 bits”【C.4.1】【C.4.1】【C.4.1】【C.4】.

---

### 2. Public‑Key Encryption (ECIES with X25519)

The public‑key component of ECIES is an **Elliptic Curve Diffie‑Hellman (ECDH)** exchange.  In Profile A the curve is **Curve25519** and the primitive is **X25519**.  The User Equipment (UE) generates a **fresh** ephemeral key pair $(k_{\text{eph}},\,P_{\text{eph}}=k_{\text{eph}}·G)$ where *G* is the curve base point.  The home network possesses a long‑term public key $P_{\text{HN}}$ and private key $k_{\text{HN}}$ that were provisioned beforehand.

The shared secret *Z* is computed as:

$$
Z = \text{X25519}(k_{\text{eph}},\,P_{\text{HN}}) .
$$

*Reference:* “The ECIES scheme shall be implemented such that for computing a fresh SUCI, the UE shall use the provisioned public key of the home network and freshly generated ECC … ephemeral public/private key pair”【C.3.2】 and “Profile A … EC domain parameters : Curve25519” and “EC Diffie‑Hellman primitive : X25519”【C.4.1】 and “The Diffie‑Hellman primitive X25519 … takes two random octet strings as input … and encodes the result as an octet string”【C.4.1】.

The secret *Z* is fed into the **ANSI‑X9.63‑KDF** together with *SharedInfo₁* (the ephemeral public key) and *SharedInfo₂* (empty) to produce *K* of length *enckeylen + icblen + mackeylen*.

---

### 3. Hash‑Based Integrity (HMAC‑SHA‑256)

ECIES uses **HMAC‑SHA‑256** to protect the ciphertext.  After encryption, the MAC key **MK** (32 octets) is applied to the concatenation of *ICB* and *C*:

$$
\text{Tag}_{\text{full}} = \text{HMAC‑SHA‑256}_{MK}(ICB\;\|\;C) .
$$

The **MAC tag** transmitted in the ECIES envelope is the *L* most‑significant octets of this full tag, where *L* = **maclen** = 8 octets (64 bits).

*Reference:* “MAC : HMAC‑SHA‑256”, “mackeylen : 32 octets (256 bits)”, “maclen : 8 octets (64 bits)”【C.4.1】 and “The value of the MAC tag in ECIES, shall be the L most significant octets of the output generated by the HMAC function, where L equals to maclen.”【C.4】.

---

### 4. Random Number Generation (Ephemeral Key Pair)

The security of ECIES hinges on the **freshness** of the UE’s ephemeral key pair.  The UE must draw a *random* scalar *kₑₚₕ* of the appropriate size (256 bits for Curve25519) from a cryptographically secure RNG before each encryption.  The randomness guarantees that the shared secret *Z* is unpredictable to an eavesdropper.

*Reference:* “The ECIES scheme shall be implemented such that for computing a fresh SUCI, the UE shall … freshly generated ECC … ephemeral public/private key pair”【C.3.2】 and “The Diffie‑Hellman primitive X25519 … takes two random octet strings as input”【C.4.1】.

---

### Worked Example (ECIES Profile A)

Assume a UE wants to encrypt the 16‑byte plaintext  

$$
P = \text{0x48 65 6C 6C 6F 57 6F 72 6C 64 31 32 33 34 35 36}
$$  

(ASCII “HelloWorld123456”).

1. **Ephemeral key generation** – UE draws a random 256‑bit scalar  
$k_{\text{eph}} =$ 0x1F2E3D4C… (hex) and computes $P_{\text{eph}} = k_{\text{eph}}·G$.

2. **Shared secret** – Using the home network’s public key $P_{\text{HN}}$, UE computes  
$Z = \text{X25519}(k_{\text{eph}}, P_{\text{HN}})$.

3. **KDF** – Feed *Z*, *SharedInfo₁* (= $P_{\text{eph}}$), *SharedInfo₂* (= ∅) into ANSI‑X9.63‑KDF to obtain  

$$
K = EK\;\|\;ICB\;\|\;MK
$$

with  

$$
EK = \text{0x00 11 22 33 44 55 66 77 88 99 AA BB CC DD EE FF},
$$
$$
ICB = \text{0xAA BB CC DD EE FF 00 11 22 33 44 55 66 77 88 99},
$$
$$
MK = \text{0xFF EE DD CC BB AA 99 88 77 66 55 44 33 22 11 00 00 11 22 33 44 55 66 77 88 99 AA BB CC DD EE}.
$$

4. **Encryption** – Compute ciphertext  

$$
C = \text{AES‑CTR}_{EK}(P,\,ICB) .
$$

5. **MAC** – Compute full HMAC‑SHA‑256 tag  

$$
\text{Tag}_{\text{full}} = \text{HMAC‑SHA‑256}_{MK}(ICB\;\|\;C)
$$

and truncate to the leftmost 8 octets →  

$$
\text{Tag} = \text{0x12 34 56 78 9A BC DE F0}.
$$

6. **Final ECIES envelope** – Concatenate  

$$
\underbrace{P_{\text{eph}}}_{\text{ephemeral public key}}\;\|\;
C\;\|\;
\text{Tag}
$$

(and any optional parameters).  This byte string is transmitted as the **SUCI**.

*Reference for steps 1‑6* is the combination of the UE‑side processing description and the parameter table in Profile A【C.3.2】【C.4.1】.

---

### Common Pitfalls & Practical Notes

| Pitfall | Why it happens | Correct practice |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Using the wrong key length** – e.g., a 24‑byte EK with AES‑128. | The ECIES profile explicitly fixes *enckeylen* = 16 octets. | Verify that the derived EK matches the 16‑octet length before invoking AES‑CTR. |
| **Omitting the ICB** or using a zero counter. | The ICB is part of *K* and must be unique per encryption. | Generate *ICB* from the KDF output and feed it to the CTR mode as prescribed. |
| **Truncating the MAC incorrectly** – taking the least‑significant bytes. | ECIES defines the tag as the *L* most‑significant octets. | Use the leftmost 8 octets of the HMAC‑SHA‑256 output. |
| **Re‑using the same ephemeral key pair** across multiple SUCIs. | Re‑use defeats forward secrecy; the specification requires a fresh pair per encryption. | Generate a new random scalar for each UE encryption operation. |
| **Applying point‑compression when the profile forbids it** (Profile A). | Profile A states “no prefix for the ephemeral public key”. | Transmit the full uncompressed public key as defined in the ECIES output. |

Adhering strictly to the parameter values and processing steps in TS 33.501 ensures interoperable, secure concealment of subscriber identifiers across all 5G equipment.

## 練習題

**1.** Which elliptic curve is specified for ECIES Profile A in TS 33.501?

- A. secp256r1
- B. Curve25519
- C. brainpoolP256r1
- D. prime256v1

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。Profile A explicitly states the curve to be Curve25519.

</details>

**2.** In ECIES Profile A, how long is the symmetric encryption key EK?

- A. 24 octets (192 bits)
- B. 16 octets (128 bits)
- C. 32 octets (256 bits)
- D. 8 octets (64 bits)

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The profile defines enckeylen as 16 octets, which is the length of EK.

</details>

**3.** What does the home network use to decrypt a SUCI encrypted with ECIES?

- A. Its own public key and the UE’s private key
- B. The UE’s ephemeral public key and the home network’s private key
- C. Only the UE’s permanent public key
- D. A pre‑shared symmetric key

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。De‑concealing uses the received ECC ephemeral public key and the home network’s private key.

</details>
