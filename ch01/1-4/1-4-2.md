## 正文

Performance Management (PM) is the systematic process of collecting, processing, and delivering network‑operation data so that operators can assess whether a telecommunications system meets its Service Level Agreements (SLAs) and internal targets. In live mobile and fixed‑line networks, PM drives capacity planning, fault isolation, and commercial billing. Without accurate and timely performance data, operators cannot guarantee voice quality, data throughput, or reliability, leading to revenue loss and customer churn.

---

### 1.2 Key Concepts and Definitions  

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| $M$ | Number of measurement intervals (samples) | – |
| $T_i$ | Duration of the *i*‑th interval | seconds (s) |
| $C_i$ | Counter value reported at the end of interval *i* (e.g., bytes transmitted) | bytes (B) |
| $\Delta C_i = C_i - C_{i-1}$ | Incremental counter for interval *i* | bytes (B) |
| $R_i = \dfrac{\Delta C_i}{T_i}$ | Instantaneous rate (throughput) for interval *i* | bits per second (bps) |
| $\overline{R} = \dfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M} R_i$ | Average throughput over $M$ intervals | bps |
| $L$ | Number of lost packets observed in an interval | packets |
| $S$ | Number of transmitted packets in the same interval | packets |
| $PLR = \dfrac{L}{S}$ | Packet‑Loss Ratio (dimensionless, often expressed as % ) |
| $J$ | Jitter, i.e., variance of inter‑arrival time | milliseconds (ms) |
| $U$ | Uptime (seconds the element was operational) | s |
| $A = \dfrac{U}{U+D}$ | Availability, where $D$ is downtime | dimensionless (0–1) |
| $K$ | Key performance indicator (KPI) – any measurable attribute defined by the operator | – |
| $C_{PM}$ | PM data collection object (e.g., SNMP OID, NETCONF/YANG node) | – |

**Performance Measurement Cycle**  
1. **Collection** – Sensors (e.g., counters, probes) sample raw data at a predefined granularity $T_i$.  
2. **Aggregation** – Incremental values $\Delta C_i$ are computed and transformed into rates $R_i$ or ratios (PLR, jitter).  
3. **Analysis** – KPIs such as $\overline{R}$, $PLR$, $A$ are compared against thresholds.  
4. **Transport** – Processed results are encoded (TLV, JSON, XML) and sent to the OSS/BSS via protocols such as SNMP, NETCONF/RESTCONF, or streaming telemetry (gNMI).  

The 3GPP specification **TS 32.401** defines the PM framework for LTE/5G networks, including the set of mandatory counters, reporting intervals, and transport methods (e.g., OMA‑DM, HTTP‑based).

---

### 2. Formulas Commonly Used in PM  

1. **Average Throughput**  

$$
\overline{R} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\Delta C_i}{T_i}\times 8 \quad\text{(bits/s)}
$$

The factor 8 converts bytes to bits.

2. **Packet‑Loss Ratio (PLR)**  

$$
PLR = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{M} L_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{M} S_i}
$$

3. **Jitter (RFC 3550 definition)**  

$$
J = \frac{1}{M-1}\sum_{i=2}^{M}\big| (D_i - D_{i-1}) - (R_i - R_{i-1}) \big|
$$

where $D_i$ is the arrival time of packet $i$ and $R_i$ its timestamp at the source.

4. **Availability**  

$$
A = \frac{U}{U + D}
$$

If $A$ is expressed as a percentage, multiply by 100.

### 3. Worked Example  

A 4G eNodeB reports the following cumulative downlink byte counters every 60 s:

| Interval (s) | Counter $C_i$ (bytes) |
|--------------|-----------------------|
| 0            | 0                     |
| 60           | 150 000 000           |
| 120          | 310 000 000           |
| 180          | 470 000 000           |

The node also reports packet statistics for the same intervals:

| Interval | Sent packets $S_i$ | Lost packets $L_i$ |
|----------|-------------------|--------------------|
| 0‑60 s   | 1 200 000         | 12 000             |
| 60‑120 s | 1 250 000         | 10 000             |
| 120‑180 s| 1 300 000         | 13 000             |

**Step 1 – Compute incremental bytes**  

$$
\Delta C_1 = 150\,000\,000 - 0 = 150\,000\,000 \text{ B}
$$  
$$
\Delta C_2 = 310\,000\,000 - 150\,000\,000 = 160\,000\,000 \text{ B}
$$  
$$
\Delta C_3 = 470\,000\,000 - 310\,000\,000 = 160\,000\,000 \text{ B}
$$

**Step 2 – Instantaneous throughputs**  

$$
R_1 = \frac{150\,000\,000}{60}\times8 = 20\,000\,000 \text{ bps}
$$  
$$
R_2 = \frac{160\,000\,000}{60}\times8 = 21\,333\,333 \text{ bps}
$$  
$$
R_3 = \frac{160\,000\,000}{60}\times8 = 21\,333\,333 \text{ bps}
$$

**Step 3 – Average throughput**  

$$
\overline{R} = \frac{20\,000\,000 + 21\,333\,333 + 21\,333\,333}{3}
               \approx 20\,888\,889 \text{ bps} \; (\approx 20.9 \text{ Mbps})
$$

**Step 4 – Packet‑Loss Ratio**  

Total sent $S = 1\,200\,000 + 1\,250\,000 + 1\,300\,000 = 3\,750\,000$  

Total lost $L = 12\,000 + 10\,000 + 13\,000 = 35\,000$  

$$
PLR = \frac{35\,000}{3\,750\,000}=0.00933 \; (\text{0.933 %})
$$

**Step 5 – Availability** (assume the node reported 5 min of downtime over the 3‑hour observation period)  

$$
U = 3\,\text{h} - 5\,\text{min} = 10\,800\,\text{s} - 300\,\text{s}=10\,500\,\text{s}
$$  

$$
D = 300\,\text{s}
$$  

$$
A = \frac{10\,500}{10\,800} \approx 0.9722 \; (\text{97.22 %})
$$

The processed KPIs (≈20.9 Mbps average throughput, 0.933 % PLR, 97.22 % availability) are then encapsulated in a JSON payload and streamed to the OSS via gNMI over a TLS‑protected channel, as prescribed by TS 32.401.

### 4. Practical Notes & Common Pitfalls  

| Pitfall | Why it Happens | Remedy |
|---------|----------------|--------|
| **Mismatched measurement intervals** – mixing counters with 1‑minute granularity and probes with 5‑second granularity leads to inaccurate aggregation. | Operators often reuse default SNMP poll periods without aligning them to the KPI definition. | Define a **common reporting period** for each KPI in the PM policy; use bulk retrieval to keep counters synchronized. |
| **Counter roll‑over not handled** – 32‑bit byte counters wrap after $2^{32}$ ≈ 4.29 GB, causing negative $\Delta C_i$. | High‑capacity links exceed the limit within a reporting interval. | Use 64‑bit counters where available, or apply modulo arithmetic: $\Delta C_i = (C_i - C_{i-1}) \bmod 2^{32}$. |
| **Time‑synchronisation errors** – timestamps from different network elements are not aligned, inflating jitter or latency calculations. | NTP or PTP mis‑configuration. | Enforce strict clock discipline (e.g., PTP Grandmaster) and include time‑offset fields in telemetry. |
| **Double‑counting of retransmissions** – treating both MAC‑layer and IP‑layer loss statistics as independent PLR. | Both layers report dropped packets for the same event. | Consolidate loss counts at a single layer or apply correlation rules before KPI derivation. |
| **Unsecured transport of PM data** – sending raw counters over plain SNMP v2c exposes network topology. | Legacy equipment still uses community strings. | Upgrade to SNMPv3, NETCONF over SSH, or gNMI over TLS; encrypt and authenticate all PM streams. |

Finally, remember that **PM is not a one‑off activity**. Continuous improvement cycles require periodic review of KPI thresholds, adaptation to new services (e.g., URLLC), and alignment with evolving regulatory reporting obligations. By adhering to a disciplined measurement‑aggregation‑transport workflow, operators can guarantee that the performance data feeding capacity planning, fault management, and billing is both trustworthy and actionable.
