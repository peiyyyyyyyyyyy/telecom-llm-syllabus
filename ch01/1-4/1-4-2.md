# 1.4.2 績效管理：量測工作、結果產生與傳送

所屬：[1.4 網管、計費、安全與密碼學](../1-4.md)

## 正文

In a 5G‑core environment the operator must be able to demonstrate that a network slice (or a slice subnet) complies with the service‑level agreement (SLA) promised to a tenant.  Performance management provides the quantitative evidence for such guarantees by converting raw measurements taken inside network functions (NFs) into *performance indicators* (PIs) that can be queried by management entities or directly by the tenant.  Without this systematic aggregation and delivery of performance data, operators cannot reliably detect violations, optimise resources, or charge tenants based on actual usage.  Consequently, performance management is a core pillar of the FCAPS (Fault, Configuration, Accounting, Performance, Security) model and is exposed through the 3GPP *performance management service* defined for network slices and slice subnets [TS 28.552 §4.1].

---

### Key Definitions  

| Symbol / Term | Definition | Unit / Type |
|---------------|------------|-------------|
| **NF** | A network function that belongs to a network slice or slice subnet (e.g., AMF, SMF). | – |
| **Network Slice (NS)** | A logical end‑to‑end partition of the physical infrastructure that delivers a set of services. | – |
| **Network Slice Subnet (NSS)** | A subset of a network slice, typically confined to a specific domain (e.g., radio, transport). | – |
| **Performance Measurement (PM)** | Raw data collected at an NF (e.g., latency, throughput). | Depends on metric (ms, Mbps, …) |
| **Performance Indicator (PI)** | Aggregated performance data over a *group* of NFs, defined by an aggregation method. | Same as underlying measurement |
| **Aggregation Method** | The mathematical rule (average, max, sum, …) identified in the PI definition that combines the set of measurements $\{m_i\}$ from the NFs. | – |
| **Performance Management Service (PMS)** | The interface through which PIs are made available for a slice, a slice subnet, or a tenant. | – |
| **Tenant** | An external party that consumes a communication service on a particular slice. | – |
| **S‑NSSAI** | Single Network Slice Selection Assistance Information; used to identify a tenant’s slice(s). | – |
| **Sub‑counter** | A per‑S‑NSSAI breakdown of a PI when a tenant is associated with one or more S‑NSSAIs. | – |

A **performance indicator** is therefore a function  

$$
PI = f\!\left(\{m_i\}_{i=1}^{N}\right)
$$  

where $f$ is the *aggregation method* specified in the PI definition and $\{m_i\}$ are the *performance measurements* reported by the $N$ NFs belonging to the considered group [TS 28.552 §4.1].

---

### Derivation Hierarchy  

1. **NF‑level measurements** are gathered locally at each NF.  
2. **Slice‑subnet PIs** are derived by applying the aggregation method to the NF‑level measurements belonging to that subnet.  The resulting PI can be queried via the *performance management service for network slice subnet* [TS 28.552 §4.1].  
3. **Slice‑level PIs** are obtained by aggregating the slice‑subnet PIs (and optionally additional NF measurements) belonging to the constituent subnets.  These are exposed through the *performance management service for network slice* [TS 28.552 §4.1].  

When a tenant consumes a service, the operator may expose the slice‑level (or slice‑subnet‑level) PIs to the tenant through the same PMS.  If the tenant is linked to one or more S‑NSSAIs, the PI is *split into sub‑counters per S‑NSSAI* so that the tenant can see performance per logical slice [TS 28.552 §4.1].

---

### Worked Example  

Assume a network slice subnet that comprises three NFs (NF‑A, NF‑B, NF‑C).  Each NF reports the **one‑way latency** for a set of user plane packets during a 5‑minute interval:

| NF | Measured latency (ms) |
|----|----------------------|
| A  | 12 |
| B  | 15 |
| C  | 9 |

The PI definition for “average latency” specifies **arithmetic mean** as the aggregation method.  The slice‑subnet PI is therefore  

$$
\text{AverageLatency}_{\text{NSS}} = \frac{12 + 15 + 9}{3} = 12\ \text{ms}.
$$

The PMS for the slice subnet makes this value available to any authorised consumer.  Suppose the slice consists of two subnets (NSS‑1 and NSS‑2) with average latencies 12 ms and 18 ms respectively.  The slice‑level PI “overall average latency” uses the same arithmetic mean across the two subnets:

$$
\text{AverageLatency}_{\text{Slice}} = \frac{12 + 18}{2} = 15\ \text{ms}.
$$

If a tenant is associated with two S‑NSSAIs (e.g., **S‑NSSAI‑1** and **S‑NSSAI‑2**) the operator splits the slice‑level PI into sub‑counters, reporting 15 ms for each S‑NSSAI (or a different value if the underlying subnets differ per S‑NSSAI).  The tenant can retrieve these values via the PMS for the network slice [TS 28.552 §4.1].

### Practical Notes & Common Mistakes  

| Mistake | Why it Happens | Correct Approach |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Confusing raw measurements with PIs** | The term “performance measurement” is sometimes used loosely to refer to the final indicator. | Remember that *measurements* are collected at individual NFs; *indicators* are the aggregated results defined by a PI. |
| **Applying an aggregation method that is not defined in the PI** | Operators may be tempted to compute a median when the PI definition requires a maximum. | Use only the aggregation method *identified in the performance indicator definition* [TS 28.552 §4.1]. |
| **Ignoring the hierarchy (NF → subnet → slice)** | Directly aggregating NF data to a slice without respecting subnet‑level PIs can lead to double‑counting. | Follow the prescribed derivation steps: first compute subnet PIs, then slice PIs. |
| **Not exposing sub‑counters for multi‑S‑NSSAI tenants** | A tenant may receive a single aggregated value, losing per‑slice visibility. | When a tenant is associated with an S‑NSSAI list, split the PI into sub‑counters per S‑NSSAI [TS 28.552 §4.1]. |
| **Assuming the PMS automatically pushes data** | The reference only states that PIs *can be made available* via the service; it does not mandate a push model. | Design the integration (pull or push) according to the operator’s architecture, but do not assume automatic delivery. |

By adhering strictly to the definitions, aggregation methods, and service exposure rules outlined in the 3GPP TS 28.552, engineers can implement a performance‑management pipeline that is both standards‑compliant and reliable for tenant monitoring.  

---  

**Summary**  
Performance management transforms per‑NF measurements into hierarchical performance indicators, first at the slice‑subnet level and then at the slice level, using explicitly defined aggregation methods.  These indicators are made available through dedicated performance‑management services and, when tenants are involved, are split into sub‑counters per S‑NSSAI.  Proper handling of the measurement‑to‑indicator chain prevents common errors and ensures transparent, SLA‑driven operation of network slices. [TS 28.552 §4.1]
