# 1.5.3 O-RAN 介面：A1、E2、O1、O2、開放前傳

## 正文

The Open Radio Access Network (O‑RAN) initiative defines a family of standardized, open, and programmable interfaces that replace the proprietary “black‑box’’ connections of legacy RANs. By exposing the control, management, and user‑plane functions through well‑specified APIs, operators can mix and match components from different vendors, automate network optimisation, and dynamically allocate compute resources in the O‑Cloud. The five primary O‑RAN interfaces covered in this subsection are:

| Interface | Primary endpoints | Typical protocol stack | Main purpose |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------------|--------------|
| **A1**    | SMO ↔ Near‑RT RIC   | REST/JSON or gRPC over HTTP/2 | Transfer of *policy* and *service models* (e.g., xApp deployment, AI/ML model updates). |
| **E2**    | Near‑RT RIC ↔ O‑CU/O‑DU | Protobuf‑encoded messages over SCTP (or TCP) | Real‑time RAN control (KPM, RC) and analytics (KPI streaming). |
| **O1**    | O‑CU/O‑DU ↔ O‑Cloud (or OSS/BSS) | NETCONF/YANG, RESTCONF, or gRPC | Configuration, fault, and performance management of O‑CU/O‑DU (NFV‑MANO integration). |
| **O2**    | O‑CU/O‑DU ↔ O‑RU   | O‑RAN Transport Interface (OTI) – typically eCPRI or F1‑based | *Control‑plane* configuration (carrier aggregation, beamforming) and *user‑plane* data transport for split‑7.x functional splits. |
| **Open Fronthaul** (often referred to as the *F1* or *eCPRI* interface) | O‑DU ↔ O‑RU | eCPRI (UDP/IP) or F1 (HTTP/2, gRPC) | Low‑latency transport of IQ samples or precoded symbols, depending on the functional split. |

---

### 1. A1 Interface – Policy Exchange

The A1 interface carries **service models** (SM) and **policy models** (PM) from the Service Management and Orchestration (SMO) to the Near‑RT RIC. The SM defines *what* the RIC may control (e.g., power‑control, handover), while the PM defines *how* (e.g., thresholds, timers). Messages are expressed as JSON objects or protobuf structures; the schema is versioned in the **O‑RAN Alliance A1‑SPEC**.

A typical A1 **policy request** contains:
$$
\text{PolicyRequest} = \{ \text{policy\_id},\; \text{target\_entity},\; \text{parameters} \}
$$
where *parameters* are key‑value pairs whose units follow the corresponding SM (e.g., dBm for transmit power). The SMO may also push a **service model descriptor**:
$$
\text{ServiceModelDescriptor} = \{ \text{model\_id},\; \text{version},\; \text{capabilities} \}
$$

**Why it matters:** A1 enables *closed‑loop* optimisation: the SMO can instantly adjust RIC policies in response to network‑wide analytics (e.g., congestion, energy saving).

---

### 2. E2 Interface – Near‑RT RIC ↔ O‑CU/O‑DU

E2 is the **real‑time control** conduit. It transports three families of messages:

| Message family | Direction | Typical use |
|----------------|-----------|-------------|
| **KPM (Key Performance Measurement)** | DU → RIC (report) | Stream KPI such as PRB utilisation, CQI, RRC state. |
| **RC (Radio Control)** | RIC → DU (command) | Immediate actions: handover trigger, scheduler weight change. |
| **RIC Service Model (RSM) messages** | Both | Negotiation of supported actions. |

E2 uses **Protocol Buffers** for compact binary encoding. A KPM report is defined as:
$$
\text{KPMReport} = \{ \text{measurement\_id},\; \text{timestamp},\; \text{value},\; \text{unit} \}
$$
*timestamp* is expressed in **5‑GTP epoch time** (seconds since 1970‑01‑01 00:00:00 UTC). The *unit* follows the 3GPP TS 32.300 style (e.g., dB, %).

**Latency requirement:** For split 7.2, the E2 control loop must complete within **≤ 10 ms** to guarantee stable scheduling decisions.

---

### 3. O1 Interface – Management Plane

O1 is the **north‑bound management** interface between the O‑CU/O‑DU (as NFV Virtual Network Functions) and the O‑Cloud orchestrator (e.g., ONAP). It adopts **NETCONF** with YANG data models defined in the **O‑RAN O1‑SPEC**. A typical *configuration* transaction:

$$
\text{<edit-config>} \rightarrow \text{<target>running</target>} \rightarrow \text{<config>}\; \text{<du>}\dots\text{</du>}\; \text{</config>}
$$

Key YANG leafs include:
- `du:transport-delay-budget` (unit: **µs**)
- `du:cpu-allocation` (unit: **percentage**)

**Practical note:** O1 also carries **fault** and **performance** notifications (NETCONF `<notification>`), enabling OSS/BSS systems to trigger alarms without polling.

---

### 4. O2 Interface – Control & User‑Plane between DU and RU

O2 implements the **O‑RAN Transport Interface (OTI)**. For functional split 7.x, O2 carries *control* messages (configuration, beamforming weights) and *user‑plane* data (IQ or precoded symbols). The transport can be:

* **eCPRI** – UDP/IP packets with a minimal header (12 bytes) and a **payload** that may be raw IQ or compressed.
* **F1** – HTTP/2 or gRPC streams, used predominantly in 5G‑New Radio (NR) deployments.

A simplified **eCPRI payload size** for IQ transport:
$$
B_{\text{IQ}} = 2 \times N_{\text{ant}} \times N_{\text{sc}} \times f_{s} \times Q \quad \text{[bits/s]}
$$
where  
$N_{\text{ant}}$ = number of antenna elements,  
$N_{\text{sc}}$ = number of active sub‑carriers per antenna,  
$f_{s}$ = sampling rate (samples/s),  
$Q$ = quantisation bits per I/Q sample (typically 12–16 bits).  

The factor **2** accounts for I and Q components.

---

### 5. Open Fronthaul – Low‑Latency Transport

Open Fronthaul is the *physical* layer realization of O2 (or F1) and must satisfy stringent **latency** and **throughput** budgets. For split 7.2 (high‑layer split), the **one‑way latency budget** is **≤ 250 µs** (including processing, serialization, and propagation). The required **bandwidth** is derived from the IQ formula above, but when using *precoded* symbols (split 7.1), the bandwidth reduces roughly by a factor of the number of users served simultaneously.

#### Worked Example

*Scenario:* A massive MIMO gNodeB with **64** antenna elements (dual‑polarised, i.e., 128 RF chains). The system uses **30.72 Msps** sampling (15 kHz sub‑carrier spacing, 2048‑FFT). Quantisation is **16 bits** per I/Q sample. The functional split is **7.2** (IQ transport).

Compute the required fronthaul data rate.

1. Determine $N_{\text{ant}}$: 128 RF chains (since dual‑polarised).  
2. For NR, each antenna carries the full OFDM symbol; we approximate $N_{\text{sc}} = 1200$ active sub‑carriers (for 20 MHz).  
3. Sampling rate $f_{s}=30.72 \times 10^{6}$ samples/s.  
4. Quantisation $Q = 16$ bits.

Plug into the IQ bandwidth formula:
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{\text{IQ}} &= 2 \times 128 \times 1200 \times 30.72 \times 10^{6} \times 16 \\
              &= 2 \times 128 \times 1200 \times 30.72 \times 10^{6} \times 16 \\
              &= 2 \times 128 \times 1200 \times 30.72 \times 10^{6} \times 16 \\
              &= 2 \times 128 \times 1200 \times 30.72 \times 10^{6} \times 16 \\
              &= 2.4 \times 10^{12}\ \text{bits/s} \\
              &= 2.4\ \text{Tb/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the Open Fronthaul link must provision **≈ 2.4 Tb/s** one‑way. To meet the **≤ 250 µs** latency, a **fiber** link with **≤ 50 km** distance (assuming light speed ≈ 200 km/ms) is typical, and hardware must support **line‑rate** forwarding with sub‑microsecond queuing.

*If split 7.1* (precoded symbols) were used, the bandwidth would drop by roughly the **spatial multiplexing factor** (≈ 8 for 8 users), yielding ≈ 300 Gb/s, well within current Ethernet‑based solutions.

### 6. Common Mistakes & Practical Tips

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Remedy |
|---------|--------------|--------|
| **Confusing A1 with E2 direction** – treating A1 as a real‑time control path. | A1 is *policy* (seconds‑scale), E2 is *sub‑second* control. Mis‑matching leads to missed latency targets. | Keep A1 messages asynchronous; use E2 for time‑critical RC actions. |
| **Using O2 for management traffic** – sending NETCONF over eCPRI. | O2 is optimised for high‑throughput, low‑latency user‑plane; management traffic should go via O1. | Separate management (O1) and user‑plane (O2) channels. |
| **Neglecting latency budget of split 7.2** – assuming any Ethernet switch will do. | Serialization and queuing can easily exceed 250 µs, breaking HARQ timing. | Deploy cut‑through switches, enable priority queuing, and verify end‑to‑end delay with a test pattern. |
| **Assuming all O‑RUs support any split** – buying a low‑cost RU and expecting split 7.2. | Hardware may only support split 8 (RF‑only) or split 7.1. | Verify the RU’s *functional split capability* in the O‑RU YANG model before procurement. |
| **Over‑provisioning fronthaul bandwidth without considering compression** – leading to unnecessary cost. | Many vendors support **block‑floating‑point (BFP)** or **entropy coding** to halve the bit‑rate. | Evaluate compression options in the O‑RU/YANG `<compression>` leaf and include them in the bandwidth calculation. |

By mastering these interfaces, engineers can design modular, vendor‑agnostic RANs that exploit the full potential of O‑RAN’s open ecosystem.
