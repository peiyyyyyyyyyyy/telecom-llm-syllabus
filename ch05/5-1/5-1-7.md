# 5.1.7 UE 量測量：RSRP、RSRQ、SINR

所屬：[5.1 資料的種類](../5-1.md)

## 正文

In a 5G‑NR network the UE continuously evaluates the quality of the down‑link carrier it is receiving. The three fundamental physical‑layer metrics—SS‑RSRP, SS‑RSRQ and SS‑SINR—are derived from the secondary synchronization signal (SS) and are the basis for cell selection, hand‑over decisions and link‑adaptation. Because they are defined on a linear power scale (watts), they can be directly compared across frequency ranges and antenna configurations, and they are later converted to dBm/dB for reporting to higher layers.

### Definitions and formulas
**SS‑RSRP (Reference Signal Received Power)**  
SS‑RSRP is the linear average of the power contributions of the resource elements (REs) that carry secondary synchronization signals. If the UE averages over $N_{\text{RE}}$ REs, each contributing power $P_i$ (in W), the metric is  

$$
\text{RSRP}= \frac{1}{N_{\text{RE}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{RE}}} P_i .
$$

$N_{\text{RE}}$ is implementation‑dependent but must satisfy the accuracy requirements of the specification. The reference point is the antenna connector for frequency range 1 and the combined signal of the antenna elements of a given receiver branch for frequency range 2; when receiver diversity is used the reported value shall not be lower than any individual branch [TS 38.215 §5.1.1].

**SS‑RSRQ (Reference Signal Received Quality)**  
SS‑RSRQ combines the RSRP with the carrier‑wide Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI). It is defined as  

$$
\text{RSRQ}= \frac{N \times \text{RSRP}}{\text{RSSI}},
$$

where  

* $N$ = number of resource blocks (RBs) that form the NR carrier RSSI measurement bandwidth,  
* RSSI = linear average of the total received power (in W) observed over the measurement bandwidth, including co‑channel serving and non‑serving cells, adjacent‑channel interference and thermal noise.  

Both numerator and denominator are measured over the same set of RBs. The reference point follows the same rule as for RSRP, and with receiver diversity the reported RSRQ shall not be lower than any branch [TS 38.215 §5.1.3].

**SS‑SINR (Signal‑to‑Interference‑plus‑Noise Ratio)**  
SS‑SINR is the linear average signal power of the REs carrying the SS divided by the linear average power of noise plus interference. In formula form  

$$
\text{SINR}= \frac{\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\text{RE}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{RE}}} P_i}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\text{RE}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{RE}}} I_i},
$$

where $P_i$ are the signal powers (as in RSRP) and $I_i$ are the corresponding noise‑plus‑interference powers (both in W). When SS‑SINR is used for L1‑SINR reporting, the measurement time restriction of the SMTC window does not apply; otherwise it is confined to the SMTC window. The reference point again follows the range‑dependent rule, and with diversity the reported value shall not be lower than any individual branch [TS 38.215 §5.1.5].

### Worked example
Assume a UE operating in frequency range 1 measures 10 REs that belong to the SS block. Each RE delivers $2\times10^{-9}$ W.  

*RSRP*  

$$
\text{RSRP}= \frac{1}{10}\times 10 \times 2\times10^{-9}=2\times10^{-9}\; \text{W}.
$$

The UE also measures the carrier RSSI over a bandwidth that contains $N=50$ RBs, obtaining a total average power of $5\times10^{-6}$ W.  

*RSRQ*  

$$
\text{RSRQ}= \frac{50 \times 2\times10^{-9}}{5\times10^{-6}}=
\frac{1\times10^{-7}}{5\times10^{-6}}=0.02\;(\text{linear})\;\approx -16.99\;\text{dB}.
$$

Finally, the UE measures the average noise‑plus‑interference power over the same 10 REs as $1\times10^{-6}$ W.  

*SINR*  

$$
\text{SINR}= \frac{2\times10^{-9}}{1\times10^{-6}}=2\times10^{-3}\;(\text{linear})\;\approx -27\;\text{dB}.
$$

These linear values are what the UE internally uses; they are later converted to dBm (RSRP) and dB (RSRQ, SINR) for RRC reporting.

### Practical notes and common mistakes
* **Measurement bandwidth mismatch** – RSRQ requires that the same set of RBs be used for both RSRP and RSSI. Using a different bandwidth for RSSI yields an incorrect RSRQ value.  
* **Antenna‑branch handling** – For frequency range 2 and for diversity configurations, the specification mandates that the reported metric shall not be lower than any individual branch. Engineers sometimes mistakenly average the branch values, which can underestimate the true link quality [TS 38.215 §5.1.1, §5.1.3, §5.1.5].  
* **SMTC window restriction** – When the metric is not used for L1 reporting, the measurement must be confined to the SS/PBCH Block Measurement Time Configuration (SMTC) window. Forgetting this restriction can lead to non‑compliant measurement reports.  
* **Resource‑element count** – The UE may choose any number of REs for the averaging, but it must still meet the accuracy requirements. Assuming a fixed number (e.g., all REs in a burst) without checking the implementation can cause a mismatch with the network expectations.  

Understanding these nuances ensures that the UE‑side measurements are both compliant with 3GPP specifications and reliable for network‑level algorithms such as hand‑over and load balancing.
