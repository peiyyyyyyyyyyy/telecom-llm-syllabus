# 5.1.2 績效指標（KPI）

所屬：[5.1 資料的種類](../5-1.md)

## 正文

In a 5G New Radio (NR) network, **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)** are quantitative metrics that operators use to assess whether the user‑plane is meeting service‑level expectations. Among the KPIs defined for a gNB, the most fundamental are the air‑interface packet delay measurements, because they directly reflect the perceived latency of applications such as voice over IP, gaming, and real‑time control. Accurate delay KPIs enable performance assurance within the integrity area of the user‑plane connection and guide optimisation actions when thresholds are breached [TS 28.552 §5.1.0]​.

### Delay Measurements
The specification distinguishes three delay KPIs that are **aggregated per Managed Object class (e.g., NRCellDU)** rather than per UE or per DRB:

* **Average DL air‑interface delay** – arithmetic mean of the time a packet spends on the downlink radio link [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.1]​.  
* **Distribution of DL air‑interface delay** – histogram of packet delays, each bin counting how many packets fall into a predefined delay range [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.2]​.  
* **Average UL air‑interface delay** – arithmetic mean of the uplink radio‑link delay [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.3]​.

All three can be filtered optionally by **PLMN ID**, **QoS level (mapped 5QI or QCI)**, and **S‑NSSAI**, allowing operators to isolate performance for specific slices or services [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.1‑1.3]​.

### Formulas
For a given filter (e.g., a specific PLMN/QoS/S‑NSSAI), let  

* $N$ – total number of RLC SDU packets successfully transmitted,  
* $t_i^{\text{sent}}$ – time the last part of packet $i$ is sent to the UE at the MAC layer,  
* $t_i^{\text{ack}}$ – time the corresponding HARQ ACK (UM) or RLC ACK (AM) is received,  

The **average downlink delay** $D_{\text{DL}}$ (in 0.1 ms units) is  

$$
D_{\text{DL}} \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(t_i^{\text{ack}} - t_i^{\text{sent}}\bigr) \;\; \text{[0.1 ms]} .
$$

The same expression applies to the uplink, substituting the appropriate uplink timestamps [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.1‑1.3]​.

The **distribution** is built by evaluating the same per‑packet delay and incrementing the bin whose range contains the result. Even if a packet undergoes retransmission (AM mode), **only the original transmission contributes** to the histogram [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.2]​.

All delay values are reported as **real numbers** representing multiples of 0.1 ms [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.1‑1.3]​.

### Worked Example
Assume a gNB cell (NRCellDU) serves a slice identified by PLMN = 00101 and QoS = 5QI = 9. During a 10‑second interval the following data are collected for downlink packets:

| Packet | $t^{\text{sent}}$ (ms) | $t^{\text{ack}}$ (ms) |
|--------|-----------------------|-----------------------|
| 1      | 100.0                 | 100.5                 |
| 2      | 101.0                 | 101.6                 |
| …      | …                     | …                     |
| 500    | 149.9                 | 150.4                 |

The sum of all per‑packet delays is  

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{500}(t_i^{\text{ack}}-t_i^{\text{sent}}) = 250\;\text{ms}.
$$

Using the formula:

$$
D_{\text{DL}} = \frac{250\;\text{ms}}{500} = 0.5\;\text{ms}.
$$

Since the KPI reports in 0.1 ms units, the reported value is  

$$
0.5\;\text{ms} = 5 \times 0.1\;\text{ms} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{DRB.AirIfDelayDl}=5.0 .
$$

If the operator also wants the distribution, each packet’s 0.5 ms delay falls into the bin “0.4–0.6 ms”, so that bin’s counter is incremented by 500 packets [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.2]​.

### Practical Notes & Common Mistakes
1. **Filtering Misunderstanding** – The measurement can be *optionally* filtered per PLMN/QoS/S‑NSSAI, but the specification does **not** require every deployment to instantiate all possible filter combinations. Forgetting to configure the intended filter may lead to aggregated values that mask slice‑specific issues [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.1‑1.3]​.

2. **Per‑UE vs. Aggregated Reporting** – Unlike the L2 definitions in TS 38.314, the KPI definitions in this document are **aggregated per Managed Object** (NRCellDU) and do **not** mandate per‑UE or per‑DRB reporting. Attempting to retrieve per‑UE values from the KPI database will therefore return “not available” [TS 28.552 §5.1.0]​.

3. **HARQ Disabled for NTN** – For Non‑Terrestrial Networks (NTN) where HARQ feedback is disabled, the downlink delay KPI is unavailable for UM mode. Ignoring this condition can cause “measurement missing” alarms [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.1‑1.2]​.

4. **Retransmission Counting** – In the distribution KPI, a packet that required retransmission still contributes **only once** to the histogram. Counting it multiple times would artificially inflate the tail of the delay distribution [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.2]​.

5. **Unit Misinterpretation** – The KPI values are expressed in 0.1 ms increments, not in raw milliseconds. Displaying the raw number without scaling may mislead operators into believing the network is 10 × faster than it actually is [TS 28.552 §5.1.1.1.1‑1.4]​.

By adhering to the exact definitions and being mindful of these pitfalls, engineers can reliably use the delay KPIs to monitor and optimise the user‑plane performance of 5G NR deployments.
