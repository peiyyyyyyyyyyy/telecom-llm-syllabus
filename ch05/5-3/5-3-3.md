# 5.3.3 告警過濾與告警風暴處理

所屬：[5.3 告警](../5-3.md)

## 正文

In a live 3GPP network an enormous number of **AlarmInformation** objects can be generated by the autonomous fault detection mechanisms described in TS 32.111‑1 §4.1.1.  Each alarm carries a set of attributes (alarmId, alarmRaisedTime, perceivedSeverity, probableCause, …) defined in TS 32.111‑2 §5.5.1.  When many alarms arrive simultaneously – a so‑called *alarm storm* – operators may be overwhelmed and may miss the root cause of a service disruption.  Alarm filtering is the process of selecting a subset of alarms that are most relevant for immediate operator action, based on the attributes supplied by the fault detection function.  Proper filtering reduces operator fatigue, speeds up fault localisation, and prevents unnecessary escalation of transient faults that disappear after the next sampling interval [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.1].

### Key attributes useful for filtering

The specification enumerates the following attributes that can be used as filter criteria:

| Attribute | Meaning | Typical filter use |
|-----------|---------|--------------------|
| **alarmId** | Identifies one AlarmInformation in the AlarmList | De‑duplicate repeated reports |
| **alarmRaisedTime** | Date and time when the alarm is first raised by the alarmed resource | Select newest or oldest alarms |
| **alarmChangedTime** | Last date and time when the AlarmInformation is changed | Detect rapidly changing alarms |
| **alarmClearedTime** | Date and time when the alarm is cleared | Remove cleared alarms from view |
| **perceivedSeverity** | Relative level of urgency for operator attention (Critical, Major, Minor, Warning, Indeterminate, Cleared) | Prioritise critical alarms |
| **probableCause** | Qualifies the alarm and provides more information than eventType | Group alarms by common cause |
| **specificProblem** | Vendor‑specific further qualification | Fine‑grained filtering when vendor data is available |

All of these attributes are defined in TS 32.111‑2 §5.5.1 and are mandatory for every alarm generated by a network entity [TS 32.111-2 §5.5.1].

### Simple filter expressions

A filter can be expressed as a logical predicate on the above attributes.  For example, the following predicate selects alarms that are **still active**, have a **severity of Major or higher**, and share the **same probableCause**:

$$
\text{Active} \land (\text{perceivedSeverity} \in \{\text{Critical},\text{Major}\}) \land (\text{probableCause}=C)
$$  

where **Active** means *alarmClearedTime* is not set.  The predicate uses only values that are defined by the standard, therefore it can be implemented directly in any OSS/EMS that consumes the 3GPP alarm model.

### Worked example

Assume a network element has generated the following three alarms (simplified view):

| alarmId | perceivedSeverity | probableCause | alarmRaisedTime | alarmClearedTime |
|---------|-------------------|--------------|-----------------|------------------|
| 101 | Critical | 5 (Power failure) | 2026‑11‑23 08:15:00 | – |
| 102 | Minor | 5 (Power failure) | 2026‑11‑23 08:16:10 | – |
| 103 | Major | 12 (Software crash) | 2026‑11‑23 08:16:45 | – |

An operator wishes to see only the **active** alarms of **severity Critical or Major** that are caused by **Power failure** (probableCause = 5).  Applying the predicate above yields:

* **Alarm 101** satisfies all three conditions (Critical, active, cause = 5).  
* **Alarm 102** fails the severity test (Minor).  
* **Alarm 103** fails the probableCause test (12 ≠ 5).

Thus the filtered list contains only alarm 101.  The operator can now focus on the power‑related fault without being distracted by the unrelated software crash.

### Handling an alarm storm

When a large number of alarms are raised in a short interval, the following practical steps, derived from the attributes defined in the standards, are commonly employed:

1. **Temporal de‑duplication** – Use *alarmId* and *alarmRaisedTime* to collapse multiple reports of the same fault.  
2. **Severity‑based throttling** – Forward only alarms with *perceivedSeverity* ≥ Major to the operator console; lower‑severity alarms are logged for later analysis.  
3. **Cause aggregation** – Group alarms by *probableCause*; if many alarms share the same cause, present a single aggregated entry.  
4. **ADAC vs. ADMC distinction** – ADAC faults have a clear clearing condition and will generate *alarmClearedTime*; ADMC faults never clear automatically and therefore must be flagged for manual operator intervention [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.1].

These steps exploit only the information that the specification guarantees is present in every alarm.

### Common mistakes and practical notes

| Mistake | Why it is a problem | Correct practice |
|---------|---------------------|------------------|
| **Filtering out all non‑Critical alarms** | Transient Minor or Warning alarms may indicate early symptoms of a larger fault; discarding them can delay detection. | Keep a separate log of lower‑severity alarms and review them periodically. |
| **Ignoring ADMC faults** | ADMC faults cannot be cleared automatically; treating them as cleared leads to stale alarm entries. | Explicitly flag alarms with *nature* = ADMC and require manual acknowledgement. |
| **Using only *alarmRaisedTime* for de‑duplication** | Two distinct faults can occur at the same second; relying solely on time may merge unrelated alarms. | Combine *alarmId* (unique per AlarmInformation) with *alarmRaisedTime* for robust de‑duplication. |
| **Assuming every fault has a clearing condition** | The specification states that “For some faults there is no clearing condition … these are referred to as ADMC faults.” [TS 32.111-1 §4.1.1] | Check the *nature* attribute before assuming an alarm will clear on its own. |

Finally, note that the 3GPP specifications do not prescribe a specific algorithm for *alarm storm* mitigation; they only define the attributes that enable a network operator to design a suitable filtering strategy.  Any additional logic (e.g., rate‑limiting, statistical burst detection) is therefore outside the scope of the referenced standards.

## 練習題

**1.** Which attribute uniquely identifies a single AlarmInformation object within an AlarmList?

- A. alarmRaisedTime
- B. alarmId
- C. perceivedSeverity
- D. probableCause

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The specification defines *alarmId* as the identifier for one AlarmInformation in the AlarmList.

</details>

**2.** What term does TS 32.111‑1 §4.1.1 use for faults that have no automatic clearing condition and require manual operator intervention?

- A. ADAC fault
- B. ADMC fault
- C. Transient fault
- D. Persistent fault

<details><summary>看答案</summary>

正解：B。The specification calls these faults *ADMC faults* and states they cannot be cleared by the network entity itself.

</details>
